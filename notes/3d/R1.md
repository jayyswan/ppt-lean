## Findings summary

1. **The frame is NOT the bounding box of the visible pixels.** It is the *projection of the 2-D "visual bounds" rectangle(s)*
   of the shape: take the axis-aligned bounding rectangle of the flat 2-D appearance (path plus stroke plus glow, shadow,
   reflection, depth ...), push its corners through the same 3-D camera as the shape (same pivot as M2), and take the bounding
   box of the projected corners; then add a constant pad. For a rectangle the ink and the frame agree (to 0.4 pt). For an oval,
   triangle, pentagon, block arc, heart, line ... the frame is the bbox of the projected *bounding rectangle*, which is far larger
   than the ink (examples: oval 245.8 x 166.8 frame against ink 1573 x 782 px at 600 dpi, i.e. 188.8 x 93.8 pt; triangles 11 to 60 pt
   off the outline-bbox). So the lead "frame bounds the visual edges" is true only where the ink touches the corners of its own
   bounding rectangle. [Measured, 22 presets + 60 random rectangles]
2. **Exactness.** Over 60 random rectangles (50 to 500 pt wide, X/Y/Z rotations in +-60/+-60/+-90, perspective fov 20 to 120 and
   orthographic) `frame - exact projected bbox = +0.725 pt` (sd 0.051, range 0.61 to 0.84) in both axes. The pad is a constant: it does
   not depend on shape size (5 to 1000 pt), angle, fov, stroke width (0 to 40), window zoom (30 to 400 %), or ExportBitmapResolution
   (72 to 1200). The quantum is 1 px at a fixed 600 dpi = 0.12 pt. [Measured]
3. **Idealization category: (b).** Pad of +0.725 pt (systematic, constant, cancels in differences and shrinks relatively as 1/size)
   with +-0.06 pt (+-0.5 px) random quantization jitter that depends on the sub-pixel phase of the shape position. The error does *not*
   shrink with zoom, display scaling (only 150 % tested) or export DPI (the EMF device is a fixed 600 dpi reference printer
   DC); it shrinks only by making the shape larger (relative error 0.7/size pt). For *unrotated* shapes the same frame is the exact
   geometric visual bbox to 0.04 to 0.2 pt (vector EMF, rounded to pixels), i.e. (b) with a 1-pixel quantum and no systematic pad.
4. **Position carries no information.** The pasted picture is always centred on the slide centre (verified for shape centres over the
   whole slide); a picture larger than the slide is scaled down (aspect kept) so that COM sizes are then scaled, but the EMF header keeps
   exact device pixels. Only **width and height** (and aspect) are read-outs.
5. **Unrotated shapes with effects give read-outs of their own** (item 6): glow radius r adds exactly 2r to each dimension, for any
   shape type and independent of glow transparency; a hard shadow adds its offset on one side; shadow blur b grows the extent by b
   beyond the shadow; Size% scales the shadow box about its centre; soft edge adds nothing; reflection adds `h + offset` below
   (blur b adds b on three sides) *independent of the reflection's Size* (as long as Size > 0). The frame measures **geometric extents of
   the effect, not visible pixels**.
6. **Content (item 4).** *Rotated (3-D) shape:* EMF with an EMF+ stream holding one 600-dpi RGBA PNG (`DrawImagePoints`) plus a GDI
   fallback (1-bpp AND mask + 16-bpp DIB): **raster only**. *Unrotated shape:* a tiny (0.9 to 1.5 KB) **vector** EMF+ (path in EMU, pen
   and brush). `Picture.Ungroup()` of the vector EMF gives a group; a second ungroup gives exact **vector** Rectangle/Oval(13 nodes)/Freeform
   shapes (quantized to 0.25 pt) *plus an AutoShape rectangle equal to the frame*; for a rotated shape ungroup gives that frame
   rectangle plus two raster Pictures. So the frame becomes a real, alignable rectangle (Align verified; smart-guide snapping not
   observable by COM, [Open]).
7. **Pivot conflict (M2 vs M4): resolved.** Neither "control hull" nor "true curve bbox" is universal. Closed Bézier paths, and open ones
   with *no visible line*: bbox of the true curve. Open Bézier with a *visible line*: bbox of (curve plus the **first** control point P1, never
   P2) plus a stroke inflation. See section 8.

## 1. What the frame bounds: component rectangles projected [Measured]

Model used throughout: `u = c + d/(d-Z) (X,Y)`, `(X,Y,Z) = Rz(-rev) Rx(-lat) Ry(-lon) (x-cx, y-cy, z)`, `d = 452.22 / tan(fov/2)`, `d' = max(d, Zmax+1.3)`
(M2). COM `Perspective=True` writes `legacyPerspectiveFront` (which ignores `rev`; my fits use rev = 0 there, confirmed on shapes with Z = +-90);
`Perspective=False` writes `legacyObliqueFront` (affine, `rev` honoured). COM RotationY = lat, RotationX = -lon, RotationZ = -rev.

Exact vs measured, `frame - model` (pt), model = projected bbox of the flat rectangle (pivot = its centre):

| case | n | frame - model |
|---|---|---|
| rect 100/200/400 wide, 5 rotations, persp + ortho (e1) | 15 | 0.63 to 0.85 both axes (flat unrotated: 0.04 to 0.20) |
| 60 random rectangles (e7) | 60 | mean 0.725 / 0.726, sd 0.051, min 0.61, max 0.84 |
| rect + stroke 0.25 to 40, fill / no fill (e3) | 24 | 0.65 to 0.83 (frame grows by exactly the stroke width, times the local scale) |
| oval + stroke 0 to 40 (e3) | 24 | **same frame as the rect** (oval ink bbox not used) |
| tri, right tri, pentagon freeform (e4) | 9 | vs bbox quad 0.60 to 0.82; vs outline 11 to 60 off |
| 22 presets (e21): rect oval triangle rtriangle diamond pentagon star5 arc blockarc chord pie wave moon heart lightning cloud rightarrow donut frame cross smiley curlybrace | 22 | against *ink-bbox quad* 0.60 to 1.04; against xfrm quad: blockarc -9/-51, chord -54/-13, heart +2.2, wave -0.3 |
| tiny 5x3, 10x6, 20x12, 50x30; big 1000x600 (e10) | 5 | 0.61 to 0.78 |

| picture (PNG, frame) | 1 | +0.71 / +0.66 |
| group of rect + oval (e22/e23) | 1 | **union of the projected child bbox rectangles about the group-centre pivot**: 266.72 x 92.76 (+0.72 = 267.48 x 93.48 measured); neither the group's own bbox quad (274 x 167) nor the child outlines (262 x 79) |

Where does the 0.725 come from? [Measured plus Heuristic] The raster is a 600-dpi RGBA PNG whose alpha box is the 3-D render of the rectangle
dilated by ~0.14 pt per side (M2's "edge dilation"); the PNG adds a 1-px margin all round; COM reports `(bounds_w + 2) px / 600 * 72`
(e.g. EMF header `bounds = (0,0,1669,1084)`, PNG 1670 x 1085, alpha box 1668 x 1083, COM 1671 x 1086 px = 200.52 x 130.32 pt). So 0.725 pt =
about 2.7 px ink dilation + 2 px margin + 1 px inclusive count + floor/ceil rounding; the decomposition is [Heuristic]. The header
`rclFrame` (0.01 mm units) agrees with COM size; `rclBounds` is the raster pixel box (rotated) or the path bounds plus pen (vector).

Stroke: closed shapes: frame = rectangle grown by w/2 per side (hence by w), then projected. Default `AddShape` outline is 1.5 pt (not 1 pt) and
fits (frame 99.00 x 66.84 vs model 98.36 + 0.725 = 99.08 and 66.14 + 0.725 = 66.87).

## 2. Quantization, scaling, display settings [Measured]

* Quantum = 1/600 in = 0.12 pt, from the fixed EMF reference device (`rclDevice = 5100 x 6600 px`, `rclMillimeters = 216 x 279`: a letter-size
  600-dpi printer DC), not the screen (which is 144 dpi / 150 %).
* Window zoom 30, 66, 100, 200, 400 %: identical frame (1669 x 1084 px bounds) [e8].
* `HKCU\...\PowerPoint\Options\ExportBitmapResolution` (originally unset): set to 72, 300, 600, 1200 and deleted afterwards (restored to unset):
  identical frame, identical bitmap size [e8]. (Caution: I changed this value for a few seconds in a PowerPoint shared with other streams.)
* Windows display scaling was not varied (stayed 150 %, `LogPixels` 144) [Open]; GPU/other machines not tested [Open].
* Sub-pixel jitter: moving the shape by 0.00, 0.01, ..., 0.12 pt changes the frame by +-1 px (0.12 pt) in a repeating pattern (1671/1672 px wide, 1086/1087
  high) [e9]. Same position gives bit-identical frames: 5 pastes identical; reopening e7.pptx and pasting again: 25/25 identical sizes [e24].
* Size scaling: pad constant from 5 x 3 pt (frame 5.40 x 3.96, relative error 15 %) to 1000 x 600 pt (relative 0.06 %). A frame wider than the slide
  is scaled by the paste to fit: 2000 x 1200 pt on a 4000-pt slide came back 4000 x 677 pt, while the header shows the real 70436 x 11921 px. Raster size
  grows as 8.33 px/pt squared: 1000 x 600 pt gives a 72 MB EMF, 2000 x 1200 pt 221 MB. [Measured]
* Convergence: the absolute error does not converge to 0; only differences or ratios cancel the constant pad (difference of two frames: error of
  quantization only, +-0.12 pt), and big shapes make the relative error negligible.

## 3. EMF content (item 4) [Measured]

Rotated rect/oval/etc.: header `rclBounds = (0,0,w-1,h-1)` px, GDI records `SAVEDC, INTERSECTCLIPRECT, BITBLT (SRCAND? mask), STRETCHDIBITS (1-bpp
mask with rop 0x00EE0086), STRETCHDIBITS (16-bpp colour, rop 0x008800C6 variant), RESTOREDC`, and an EMF+ stream with object 0x4008 (Image, PNG RGBA,
chunked with continuation flag 0x8000 for > 64 KB) and `DrawImagePoints` (0x401b, src rect (-0.5,-0.5,w,h)). No path records.
Unrotated shapes (rect, oval, triangle, hard shadow): `EMR_EXTCREATEPEN`, `POLYLINE16`/path comments and an EMF+ `FillPath/DrawPath` with an
EMF+ path object of exactly the control points in EMU (e.g. the cubic example: 4 points, types 0,3,3,3) plus a `BITBLT` background; 0.9 to 1.6 KB.
Raster even without 3-D: blurred shadow, glow, soft edge, inner shadow, reflection, Depth/bevel set (even unrotated); hard shadow (blur 0) stays vector.
Ungroup: see summary item 6 (flat: exact vector copy, 0.25 pt grid; shadow becomes its own Freeform of its outline; rotated: frame rectangle + 2 raster
Pictures). Ungroup does not bake the 3-D transform; there is no vector projected geometry anywhere (consistent with probe C).

## 5. Open lines, caps and joins [Measured; flat (unrotated) views]

Setup by COM; cap/join set by an XML patch of `<a:ln cap= >` (not reachable by COM; reachable in Format Shape > Line, Cap type / Join type).
Frame minus ink (ink = true stroked-outline bbox from an 8 px/pt export), w = 14 or 20:

| | straight line (L 200, w 20) | polyline / closed shapes |
|---|---|---|
| round cap | exact (+0.2) | exact (+0.1 to 0.35) |
| flat cap, miter or round join | frame = ink + w/2 along the line (+10.24, +0.16): see formula below | exact (+0.14 to 0.56) |
| flat cap, bevel join | exact (+0.28) | exact |
| square cap (any join) | ink + 8.6 in both axes | exact (+0.2 to 1.2) |

Default line (no explicit join) with flat cap, straight line, length L, weight w, angle th: `W = |dx| + (w/2)(1 + |sin th|)`, `H = |dy| + (w/2)(1 + |cos th|)`
(27 cases, w = 4, 10, 20, th = 0..90: fits to 0.05 pt; the true stroke band is `|dx| + w |sin th|`, so the excess is `(w/2)(1 - |sin th|)`) [Wolfram arithmetic
check: th = 30, w = 10: 7.5 + 100 sqrt3 = 180.71 vs measured 180.96 incl. 0.25 pad]. So even unrotated, a straight flat-cap line is *over*-bounded by a
join/cap-dependent amount (GDI+-style pen-bounds approximation, mechanism [Heuristic]). Rotated, the frame of a line is the projection of this over-bounded
rectangle.

## 7. Usability: snapping and alignment (item 5)

* After paste the picture is a normal Picture/shape. Align Left/Right/Top/Bottom/Centre relative to other objects work on the frame edges
  (COM `ShapeRange.Align` used as a stand-in for the ribbon command) [Measured].
* Ungroup yields a plain rectangle with exactly the frame (0.25 pt grid) [Measured].
* Smart-guide snapping while dragging cannot be observed through COM [Open; R3 covers snapping]. [Heuristic] Smart guides snap to edges of any shape, so a
  second shape resized by dragging to the picture's edges inherits its width/height; but since the paste position is the slide centre, only extents (not
  placement relative to the original shape) are transferable.
* The pad of 0.725 pt cannot be removed by UI; it cancels if two frames are compared (differences, ratios of large frames).

## 8. Side task: Bézier pivot conflict (M4 hull vs M2 curve bbox) [Measured]

**Decisive test (e13 to e16, e25).** Open cubic Bézier built by `BuildFreeform` (msoSegmentCurve), `P = (300,420),(220,120),(520,60),(700,380)`: control-hull bbox 480 x 360,
true curve bbox 413.3 x 254.0 (top 166, left 286.7); second S-curve `(300,300),(380,60),(600,520),(700,200)`; and a reversed copy. Line weight 0 (invisible), 0.25, 1, 5, 20.
Frames pasted flat (to see the bounds) and with 3-D (25,35,fov 60) and (-30,-30,fov 45), compared against all pairs {quad, pivot} from {curve, curve+P1, curve+P2,
hull} with and without +w/2.

| situation | frame / pivot | error (pt) |
|---|---|---|
| open Bézier, no line, 3 curves x 3 cameras | quad = pivot = true curve bbox | <= 0.14 (hull: 180 to 340 off) |
| open Bézier, visible line 0.25 | quad = pivot = bbox(curve plus first handle P1) plus w/2 | 0.02 to 0.24 (curve alone: 50 to 130 off; hull: 40 to 60 off) |
| same, line 5 | curve plus P1 plus w/2 | 1.7 to 4.3 (stroke inflation on curves is larger than w/2, rule [Open]) |
| reversed copy (P1 now (520,60)) | still the *first* handle | 0.02 to 0.14 |
| closed 2-segment Bézier, fill, no line / line 0.25 / line only | true curve bbox (336.2 x 338.5) in all three | 336.36 / 336.60 / 336.60: exact |

Unrotated random test: 13 open stroked cubics: frame width/height = ptp(curve union {P1}) to 0.4 pt in every case (e15); P2 never included. The control hull
(M4's rule) and the curve bbox (M2's rule) are therefore each right for a subset: M2's blob was closed or unstroked; M4's freeform was an open stroked path whose first
handle sat at the hull's top edge (its y = 100 against curve top 148), which makes "hull" and "curve plus P1" coincide in one coordinate and look like the hull.
Verdict: **pivot = bbox of the curve alone when the path is closed, or open without a visible line; open and stroked: bbox(curve plus first control point P1)
plus a stroke-dependent inflation.** Pivot and frame quad use the same box (alternative pairs misfit by 6 to 41 pt in perspective). Slide-render pivots were not
re-measured independently (only via frame sizes, which are second-order sensitive to the pivot in perspective). UI note: `BuildFreeform` places control points exactly;
by UI, the Curve tool or Edit Points handles are dragged, so exact control points are not UI-placeable (flag).

## 9. Idealization category

* Rotated shapes: **(b)** approximation of the ideal "projected bbox of the visual-bounds rectangle(s)": constant +0.725 pt (sd 0.05), 0.12 pt quantum, no
  convergence under zoom, display DPI or ExportBitmapResolution; relative error 0.7/size (0.07 % at 1000 pt). A pad-cancelling use (differences of frames) leaves only
  +-0.12 pt.
* Unrotated shapes: **(a)/(b)**: exact geometric bbox up to the 0.12 pt pixel quantum for round-cap/polyline/closed shapes; straight flat/square-cap lines are
  over-bounded (cap/join rule in section 5), glow/shadow/reflection extents are exact rules (geometric extents).
* The quantity measured is **not** the ink bbox, so it cannot be described as "the visual edges of the rotated shape" except for rectangles, and its corners are
  corners of the projected *rectangle* (a lower-information read-out than the outline, but exactly modellable).

## Other things noticed

* **The frame follows the projected rectangles of components, which also explains pivots:** shadow, glow, reflection and the group's children each enter both the
  pivot (centre of the union of component rectangles) and the frame; depth does not enter the pivot but enters the frame (M2 consistent).
* `Paste` of a shape (dt = 11) also lands at the slide centre; PasteSpecial raster formats give different frames for the same rotated 100 x 60 rect with 1.5 pt line:
  EMF 99.00 x 66.84, PNG (6) 100.81 x 68.65, Bitmap (1) 101.99 x 69.74, GIF (4) 102.00 x 69.75, JPEG (5) 101.40 x 69.00 (R2's territory; not analysed).
* COM `ThreeD.Perspective = 0` gives `legacyObliqueFront` with `rev` honoured (ortho projection of the rotated plane); `Perspective = -1` gives `legacyPerspectiveFront`, which
  ignores Z rotation (rev), also in the pasted frame (60 random shapes with Z up to +-90 fit with rev = 0).
* The unrotated flat EMF of an oval is `lt 1172` bytes; its frame is the bounding rectangle (200.04 x 120.12), not smaller.
* Ungroup quantizes to 0.25 pt (379.25, 209.13, 201.5 x 121.75 for a 201.6 x 121.68 picture), losing ~0.1 pt.
* The PowerPoint instance is shared by about 20 presentations from other streams; the clipboard is therefore a shared resource. I verified repeated pastes
  (identical in 5 of 5) and, for safety, kept every Copy-Paste in one call with a 0.3 to 0.5 s pause; no contamination was seen but it cannot be excluded for single runs.
* The bash tool in this environment collapses `\\` to `\` inside heredocs: Python path strings must use `os.path.join` (cost me a SaveCopyAs failure).
* Hypothesis (not tested): since the frame equals the projection of the *bounding rectangle*, a rectangle with no outline and a shape whose ink touches all four bbox
  sides (oval, diamond, rounded shapes) give the same frame as the filled rectangle; shapes can be given a deliberately larger invisible bounding rectangle by adding a
  zero-opacity or no-line rectangle in a group (group frame = union of children's rectangles), which yields frames of chosen quads (needs a test) [Open].
* [Open] shadow Size < 100 % extent rule (+15 in y, +0 in x for 50 %); the stroke-inflation rule for stroked open curves (x about w, y about w/2 in one example);
  origin of the 0.725 constant and its split into dilation and margin; behaviour at other display scalings, other machines, GPU on/off; whether the UI paste (as opposed to
  COM `PasteSpecial`) also centres the picture; whether the lone-line inflation is the same on the screen render.
