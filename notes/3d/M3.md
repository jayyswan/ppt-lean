## Summary

1. **Extrusion = stack of z-slices of the flat region.** [Measured] The rendered silhouette equals the union over z in
   [-depth, 0] of the flat region (fill and stroke) pushed through the camera at height z (one homography per slice; affine
   for orthographic). IoU 0.985-0.995 at 2 px/pt, the residual being the antialiased boundary. Front face stays at z = 0; the
   body extends **away from the viewer**, in the same pt as the plane. Ortho and perspective, convex and non-convex
   (star, donut with hole, moon, heart).
2. **"Silhouette = front outline + back outline + common tangent segments"** is exactly right for convex shapes (hull of
   the two projected quads, IoU 0.9976, rectangle ortho) [Measured]. For non-convex shapes the same boundary pieces appear but the
   silhouette is the full Minkowski-type sweep: it can close holes (donut) and bridge concavities [Measured + Proof sketch].
3. **Bevel = one fixed unit profile per type, swept along the inward offset of the outline.** [Measured] z/h = f_type(s/w)
   is the same curve for (w,h) = (30,20), (60,10), (15,40), (60,40), for all 12 types, so w and h are plain affine scale factors.
   The bevel occupies z in [-h, 0] (the original outline lies at z = -h, the flat top at z = 0). Silhouettes of bevelled solids =
   stack of layers eroded by s(z): IoU 0.988-0.993, ortho and perspective.
4. **The offset is a true parallel curve on smooth outlines and a mitre (straight-skeleton) offset at polygon corners, reflex
   ones included.** [Measured] Ellipse plateaus match the analytic erosion to 0.5 % of area, even when w exceeds the tip radius of
   curvature; the plus shape matches the mitred polygon (17570 vs 17600 pt^2) and not the Euclidean erosion (19167). Past the
   medial axis the bevel becomes a roof (star: 5 pyramids).
5. **Contour** (`contourW`) is a stroke of width cw around the solid: in the front view exactly a band **centred on the outline**
   (bbox +- cw/2, IoU 0.998 with the cw/2 dilation); it is scene geometry (scales with perspective and foreshortening); in
   tilted views it is *not* the dilation of the silhouette (IoU 0.91) but is fitted (0.979) by the in-plane cw/2-dilated region swept over a
   z-range shifted slightly backward. [Measured; z-range Open]
6. **Material and lighting never change the non-white silhouette** (14 materials x 3 rigs): same pixel set within 0.8 %,
   except Wire frame (edges only). They can however make walls nearly white (Powder, Translucent powder under some rigs), so a
   threshold-based "visual edge" on a white slide can lose the wall of a light extrusion. [Measured]
7. `ThreeD.Z` translates the solid along the **shape-local normal**. [Measured]

## 1. Extrusion

### 1.1 Layout [Measured]
Rectangle 200x120 centred (480,270), depth 60, ortho, (X,Y) = (20,30): hull(front quad, back quad at z = -60) vs rendered mask:
IoU 0.9976 (mismatch 1079 px of mask at 4 px/pt, about 0.27 px mean along the perimeter = antialias). Back at z = +60: 0.55;
at -30: 0.85. A top view (COM Y = 90) with depth 40 occupies y in [230,270], i.e. y = 270 + z, z in [-40,0]. Depth 0 viewed
edge-on renders nothing. `ThreeD.Depth` is in pt (`extrusionH` EMU, 12700 per pt).

### 1.2 Slice model [Measured]
Silhouette = union over z in [-d,0] of H_z(F); H_z = camera applied to the plane at height z; F = flat filled+stroked region.
IoU against the render at 2 px/pt:

| shape | ortho (20,30) | perspective fov 90 (20,30) |
|---|---|---|
| star5 | 0.988 | 0.992 |
| donut | 0.990 | 0.992 |
| moon | 0.985 | 0.991 |

The arc is worse because the preset arc's frame, not its drawn bbox, is the projection centre.
In perspective the projection centre stays at the flat bbox centre at z = 0; deeper slices project smaller.

### 1.3 Closed or open, filled or unfilled [Measured]
- Filled, no line: coloured solid; side wall shaded.
- Unfilled closed outline: the **stroke band** is extruded into a wall (ring with visible hole); wall takes the line colour.
- Open stroke or line: extruded to a ribbon (parallelogram) of width = stroke weight, line colour.
- Fill plus stroke: extruded outline is fill union stroke band (outer boundary = stroke outer edge).
`ExtrusionColorType` 1 = automatic, 2 = custom (`<a:extrusionClr>`).

### 1.4 Boundary geometry [Proof sketch]
Ortho: H_z(p) = H_0(p) + z v, v = projection of the z-axis, so the silhouette is H_0(F) + [-d v, 0] (Minkowski sum with a
segment). Its boundary consists of arcs of the front outline, arcs of the translated back outline and segments parallel to v
that touch the outline where the tangent is parallel to v. Perspective: H_z(p) slides along the line from H_0(p) toward the
vanishing point V of the z-axis; the connecting segments lie on lines through V tangent to the outline, so they are the common
tangents of the front and back images. Convex image: the hull. Non-convex: tangency pieces and sweep can fill concavities and
holes, as seen for the donut.

### 2.2 z layout [Measured]
Front plane z = 0. Top bevel occupies z in [-hT, 0]; body [-(hT+d), -hT]; bottom bevel [-(hT+d+hB), -(hT+d)], mirrored (its widest point is

the silhouette is exactly the flat outline and the bevel only shades the interior.

### 2.4 How the offset is built [Measured]
Front view, type 'angle' (planar facets):
- plus shape 280x240 (arms 160 and 120), w = h = 40: the flat plateau is the mitred inset polygon (predicted 17600 pt^2, measured 17570;
  disc erosion would be 19167). Creases run diagonally from every vertex, reflex ones too: straight-skeleton / wavefront offset.
- ellipses 200x60 (w 20, 10), 200x100 (w 30), circle 120 (w 40): plateau area vs analytic parallel-curve erosion
  (cKDTree distance to the 40000-point ellipse polyline): 1976.06 / 1985.5, 5361.75 / 5353.3, 3997.0 / 4004.1; circle radius 20 exact.
  The case w = 20 exceeds the tip curvature radius b^2/a = 9, and the plateau tip still follows the true parallel curve.
- When the offset exceeds half the local width the bevel runs up a roof to the medial axis and stops (star = five pyramids,
  200x60 oval with w = 40 = a ridge). [Measured, shading images]
- Rounded rectangle: diagonal creases from the straight sides into the round corners.
So: bevel = loft of (w,h)-profile along the wavefront offsets of the outline; offsets of smooth arcs are parallel curves,
polygon vertices get mitres.

### 2.5 Silhouettes of bevelled solids [Measured]
Model: layer at height z in [-h,0] = F eroded by s(z) (s = inverse measured profile); body layers = F; each pushed through H_z.
Rectangle 200x120, w 30, h 20, depth 40: circle ortho (-40,30) 0.988; circle perspective fov 90 0.991; angle ortho 0.988; angle
persp (50,-35) 0.989; convex persp (-40,30) 0.993. Ignoring the bevel (body only) gives 0.95-0.986. In tilted views the front plateau at
z = 0 can poke out beyond the swept base outline, so bevel height changes the silhouette. Perspective only changes H_z.

### 2.6 Bevel and stroke [Measured]
With fill and a 16 pt stroke the bevel acts on the **outer boundary of the stroke band** (the frame is rounded; the fill is a flat
inset face). Unfilled: the ring is bevelled. Hairline unfilled: only a ribbon is extruded.

## 3. Contour

Encoding `<a:sp3d contourW="EMU"><a:contourClr .../>`; COM `ContourWidth` (pt), `ContourColor`.
- **Front view (ortho, no rotation):** a band **centred** on the outline: bbox grows by cw/2 per side (cw 10 -> 5; 20 -> 10); the
  fill shrinks by cw/2 (200 -> 180 wide). Mask equals the dilation of the outline by cw/2: IoU 0.9978 (oval, rectangle).
  So here it is exactly an outward parallel curve with round corners. [Measured]
- **Tilted:** a stroke on the solid's edges (front outline, back outline, depth edges), round joins. The silhouette is not the
  dilation of M0 (IoU 0.911; bbox growth 1.5, 8, 6.7, 2 pt for left, right, top, bottom at cw 10, (X,Y) = (-40,40), d = 30).
  Sweeping the in-plane dilation (by 5) over z in [-d-6, -2] gives IoU 0.979; ranges [-d-cw/2, 0] 0.952, [-d,0] 0.907, [-d,+cw/2] 0.868.
  The best range sits at the scan boundary (a <= 6, b >= -2 were the limits), so the exact rule is [Open].
- **Perspective:** rect 400x100 rotated X = -55, fov 100, depth 10, cw 12: the vertical band width goes 8.5 pt (far) -> 18.75 pt
  (near), ratio 2.2, with the face vertical extent 82 -> 177 (2.15). The contour is scene geometry, not a screen-space stroke. [Measured]
- It enlarges the object's visual bbox in the front view by exactly cw/2 per side [Measured].

## 5. ThreeD.Z (Distance from ground)
Z = +50, -50, +60, +100, -100 moves the solid by that many pt along the shape-local normal (+ toward viewer): IoU 0.987-0.991 with the
slice model shifted by Z vs 0.04-0.40 for the opposite sign; ortho and perspective. The projection centre stays the flat bbox
centre (so in perspective the vanishing geometry does not move). [Measured]

## 6. Perspective, in one line
Every effect is a per-slice homography H_z(F_z) with F_z = F (extrusion), F eroded by s(z) (bevel) or the in-plane dilation (contour).
Bevel profile and contour width are world quantities, so they scale with perspective. [Measured]

## Other things noticed
- COM RotationY tilts about the horizontal axis, RotationX turns about the vertical axis (lon = -X): "Y = 90" views a plate from the top.
- Depth 0 plate viewed edge-on renders nothing; with depth > 0 you get the outline profile, a handy way to read a bevel profile.
- Bevel offsets are mitred at polygon corners (not rounded), including reflex corners, unlike a Euclidean erosion.
- The preset arc projects about its frame centre, not the visible arc's bbox centre.
- An extruded donut closes its hole partly; bbox of a non-convex solid still equals the bbox of the sweep.
- Boundary of the render is 0.1-0.3 px outside the analytic silhouette (antialias); error shrinks with export resolution.
- WireFrame bevels show no bevel lines and no front-face outline inside; unfilled-stroke extrusions colour the wall with the line colour.
- Wall tessellation: the Wire frame of an ellipse shows ~190 straight generators; silhouettes of curved walls are polygons of that mesh (error below noise at 4 px/pt, not measured).
- The bevel on a filled+stroked shape is applied to the stroke band, so the flat fill face is inset by the stroke width plus bevel.
- Unlike rotation, the bevel/contour widths in the dialog are in pt and are stored in EMU; no rounding anomalies seen.

## Open
- Hidden parts of bevel profiles (cross, divot, riblet, artDeco undercuts).
- Exact contour z-range in tilted views; contour on bevelled solids.
- Closed forms for non-circular profiles; the ECMA spec gives none.
- X4 consequence (idea): boxes of bevelled or extruded solids are maxima of the homography over a few profile-offset outlines
  (front plateau at z = 0, base outline at z = -h, back outline at z = -(h+d)); with ortho, the bbox is attained at a vertex of the
  hull of those outlines when convex. For a bevel of width w the plateau is an inward offset (parallel curve) of the outline: new curves
  (offset of conic) appear only on the plateau, not on the widest base outline.
