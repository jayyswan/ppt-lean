## 0. Findings summary

1. **One framework covers every row [Proof].** A route is a carrier curve `t ↦ (X(t), Y(t))` and a
   partner curve `G(x, y) = 0`; the crossing polynomial is `N(t) = G(X(t), Y(t))`. Split the dials
   into the jointly linear ones (spanning a space `Λ` of crossing polynomials, `F = dim Λ`) and the
   rest `V`. The crossing at a generator `τ ∈ K` is `N(τ) = 0`, i.e. 9 linear equations on `F`
   unknowns, so **`c_lin = 10 − F` conditions on `(τ, V)`** (N2's `n + 1 − F`, now with trivial
   identities `N ≡ 0` removed from `F`). The W1/N4 count `r` (rank of dials → 9 crossing roots,
   reparametrisations included) gives `c_τ = 9 − r` conditions on the generator alone.

2. **At n = 9 no drawable pair has 10 jointly linear dials [Proof sketch, modulo G2/R2's
   catalogue].** The maximum is `F = 9` (one linear condition), for rational dials only. It is
   attained by three rows, all against a rational sextic graph: Bézier with `X` cubic (D1), Bézier
   with `X` quadratic (new, row E), and the text-warp egg (new, row F). With P-valued dials only
   the maximum is `F = 7` (Bézier × P-quartic graph, 3 linear conditions). So pure linear algebra
   cannot close T9Q or T9P with these curves; every route pays at least one condition.

3. **Row E is the cleanest T9Q target found [Proof + Computed, exact].** Cross the cubic Bézier
   `(t², Y(t))` (`Y` cubic) with `y = g(x)`, `g ∈ ℚ[x]` of degree ≤ 6. Then
   `N(t) = Y(t) − g(t²)` has support `{0,1,2,3,4,6,8,10,12}` and the construction exists iff

   > `s_{(4,3,2,1)}(τ₁, …, τ₉) = 0`,

   a Schur polynomial of degree 10 in the conjugates of `τ`. Equivalently, by the dual
   Jacobi–Trudi formula in the coefficients `e_k` of `χ_τ`:

   > `det [[e₄,e₅,e₆,e₇],[e₂,e₃,e₄,e₅],[1,e₁,e₂,e₃],[0,0,1,e₁]] = 0`.

   - It involves only `e₁ … e₇` and is **linear in `e₇`**.
   - For centred `τ` (`e₁ = 0`) it reads `e₃(e₇ − e₃e₄ + e₂e₅) = e₅²`, so it **contains**
     `{p₁ = p₃ = p₅ = 0}`, the locus of the old frontier (SUMMARY §1).
   - A ℚ-point there is enough for E; the odd engine needed `p₇ = 0` as well.
   - Real points are abundant: for each of 42 real 9-tuples tested, the shift polynomial
     `s(x + h𝟙)` has 2–10 real roots `h`.
   - **Positive control:** an explicit totally real S₉ nonic is solved by E (§3.2).

4. **Test-field caution [Proof].** `q₁ = ∏_{i=−4}^{4}(x − i) + 1`, the nonic used for D1's
   count, is `x·Q(x²) + 1`. At `τ = β` it satisfies `e₁ = e₃ = e₅ = e₇ = 0`, so the odd engine
   already solves it, and so does E: `f(h) = s(β + h)` splits over ℚ into
   `h(h ± 1)(2h ± 1)(2h ± 3)`. More generally, **any `∏(x − aᵢ) + c` is degenerate for E**: the
   condition sees only `e₁ … e₇`, which the constant `c` does not move. The same field `q₂` with
   other `aᵢ` also hits (`h = 1, −2, −½, −7/2`). Searches for E, and for any route whose
   condition involves only the top coefficients, need a field with lower-order perturbations.
   D0 uses `q₃` (§3.3).

5. **D1's count, rechecked [Computed, exact].** For `q₁`, `τ = β`, `X = t³ + at² + bt`, the curve
   in `(a, b)` has total degree **20**, not 19, and is irreducible over ℚ (degree 18 in `a`, 15 in
   `b`). Since `q₁` is degenerate (item 4), D1 should redo its counts on `q₃`.

6. **Box search for E on a non-degenerate field [Computed, exact].** For `q₃` (totally real, S₉,
   no odd symmetry) and every `τ = Σ_{k=1}^{8} c_k β^k`, `c ∈ {−1, 0, 1}⁸` (3280 classes up to
   sign), with the shift `h` free over all of ℚ: **no hit**. Every `f_τ(h)` is irreducible of
   degree 10. The same code run on the control field finds its solution (`τ = β`, `h = 0`),
   which has height 1 in the same coordinates. This is a null at height 1, nothing more.

7. **Ranking (§4).**
   - **T9Q:** E, then D1, then H (two rational sextic graphs, one rotated 90°). H has
     `c_lin = 2` but a 3-dimensional family of rational sextics for *every* generator, the
     largest fibre in the census. The egg (F) is dominated by E: same `F`, degree 26 instead of
     10, and no shift.
   - **P dials (T9P, or a P-valued generator):**
     - Bézier × egg: `c_τ = 0`, finite fibre; but at most 8 real crossings found.
     - Rational sextic × rotated P-quartic: `c_τ = 0`; fixing the sextic leaves a codim-4 locus
       of generators over L.
     - Bézier × Bézier: `c_τ = 1`, 9 real crossings attained.
     - Bézier × P-quartic: `c_lin = 3`, `c_τ = 1`.
   - Below these come D4 (P-quartic × rotated P-quartic: `c_τ = 1`, `D4`'s count of 6
     confirmed) and the odd engine (`c_lin = 4`).

## 1. Framework

`K = ℚ(β)`, `[K:ℚ] = 9`, `τ ∈ K` (or `K ⊗ L`, `L = P ∩ ℝ_alg`), `τ₁ … τ₉` its conjugates.

- **Crossing family.** Carrier `(X(t), Y(t))` polynomial or rational in `t`; partner
  `G(x, y) = Σ c_m m(x, y)`. With `V` fixed, `Λ_V = span{∂N/∂u : u linear}` (numerators after
  clearing a common denominator). `F = dim Λ_V`, computed exactly.
- **Joint linearity** arises in three forms:
  - (A) partner coefficients only, carrier fixed;
  - (B) partner `y = g(x)` with `g` in a linear family `𝒢`, plus carrier dials entering `Y` only
    (`X` fixed). Then `Λ = 𝒴 + 𝒢∘X`, and `F = dim 𝒴 + dim 𝒢 − dim(𝒴 ∩ 𝒢∘X)`;
  - (C) a fixed-slope line partner with every carrier dial.

  (B) dominates at degree ≥ 9.
- **Field of the dials.** A rational sextic graph has rational coefficients. If a linear dial
  must be rational, the 9 equations over L have rational unknowns, which generically fails
  unless `τ` and every other dial are rational. So rows containing a ℚ-sextic serve T9Q with a
  rational generator only.
- **Counts, measured uniformly.** At a random rational dial vector, take 9 simple roots of `N`
  (real ones first) and form the Jacobian `∂τ_j/∂(dials) = −(∂N/∂u)(τ_j)/N′(τ_j)`:
  - `r` is its rank, and `c_τ = 9 − r`;
  - `s` is the rank after appending `dV`, i.e. the dimension of the solution set in `(τ, V)`;
  - the fibre over a fixed generator is `s − r`. "Effective" removes dials that act trivially
    for fixed `τ` (e.g. `P ↦ aP + b` when the partner absorbs it).

  Precision: 90–150 digits; every singular-value gap is at least 10⁶ (typically 10⁻⁵ against
  10⁻¹¹⁵; the finite-difference rows 10⁻² against 10⁻⁴⁷).
- **Real capacity** is the largest number of real roots `N` can have in the family. It is an
  upper bound by Descartes' rule (sparse rows) or the degree (dense rows), and is marked
  "attained" when a member with ≥ 9 real roots is exhibited. A totally real `K` needs 9.

- `c_τ` counts conditions on the generator up to the reparametrisations the row has: the shift

  enter only as square conditions. Rotated eggs and rotated power laws are nonlinear and not

## 3. Row E in detail

### 3.1 Why the condition is a Schur polynomial [Proof]

`Λ_E = span(1, t, t², t³) + span(g(t²) : deg g ≤ 6)` has the 9 monomials
`{0,1,2,3,4,6,8,10,12}` (the trivial identities `g` affine, `Y = g(t²)`, are the overlap
`{1, t²}`). A nonzero `N ∈ Λ_E` vanishes at `τ` iff the 9 vectors `τ^e` (`e` in the support) are
dependent in `K`. In embedding coordinates the determinant is the generalised Vandermonde
`a_{λ+δ}(τ) = s_λ(τ)·∏_{i<j}(τ_j − τ_i)` with exponents `(12,10,8,6,4,3,2,1,0) = λ + δ`, so
`λ = (4,3,2,1)`. The Vandermonde factor vanishes only when `τ` is not a generator. The ratio was
checked to 20 digits at `τ = β + β²` in `q₁`. On a random rational line in `K` the determinant
has degree 46 = 10 + 36, and it factors over ℚ as 10 × 36 [Computed, exact]. Dual Jacobi–Trudi
(`λ′ = λ`) gives the 4 × 4 determinant in §0.3, checked against the bialternant at a rational
point.

**Drawability.** `cubic_bezier` with control points giving `X = t²`, `Y` cubic (rational), any
parameter interval (an affine reparametrisation of `t` keeps the control points rational), and
`poly_graph g`. `inter_x` returns `τ(β)²`, which generates `K` when `K` has no proper subfield
(odd degree, primitive group). Trivial-solution check: the kernel must not lie in `{g affine}`;
that is exactly the removal of the Vandermonde factor.

**Recovery.** `β` is a polynomial over ℚ in `τ²` of degree ≤ 8.

### 3.2 Positive control [Computed, exact]

Choose rational `e₁ … e₆, e₈, e₉` near those of a real-rooted target and solve the linear
condition for `e₇`. This gives

```
χ = t⁹ − (39/5)t⁸ − (963/7)t⁷ + (4789/5)t⁶ + (56480/9)t⁵ − (70507/2)t⁴ − (1129969/10)t³
    + (3513710115473/8471400)t² + (2548871/4)t − 1383374/3.
```

- `χ` is irreducible with 9 real roots.
- Frobenius types include (4,5) (so a 5-cycle; primitive because 5 ∤ |S₃ ≀ S₃| = 1296; Jordan
  then gives A₉) and (1,8) (odd), so **Gal = S₉**.
- In `K = ℚ[t]/χ` with `τ = t`, the 9 × 9 matrix has a 1-dimensional kernel, giving
  - `Y(t) = t − (331513547075986763/15890448241302697090)·t³`,
  - `g(x) = 145025000660232/112546555997611 − (29087462520562991763/11350320172359069350)x`
    `+ (1799580610528482/2813663899940275)x² − (81614444162908/1688198339964165)x³`
    `+ (5894234503784/3939129459916385)x⁴ − (11037608556/562732779988055)x⁵`
    `+ (10165680/112546555997611)x⁶`.
- `Y(t) − g(t²) = χ·ρ` with cubic cofactor
  `ρ = −12(847140t³ + 6607692t² − 15877885t − 26208567)/112546555997611`.
- `N` has 12 real roots.

So the Bézier `(t², Y(t))` crosses `y = g(x)` at `x = β²`, and every real root of this totally real
S₉ nonic is P-constructible by this route. (The E family is unirational: fix `e₁ … e₆, e₈, e₉` and
solve for `e₇`; it is an 8-parameter family of nonics.)

### 3.3 Box search on a non-degenerate field [Computed, exact]

- `q₃ = ∏_{a∈{−9,−6,−4,−1,0,2,5,7,10}}(x − a) + 3x⁶ + 5x⁵ − 2x⁴ + x³ + 2x + 1`
  `= x⁹ − 4x⁸ − 148x⁷ + 461x⁶ + 6268x⁵ − 13296x⁴ − 81715x³ + 88440x² + 151202x + 1`.
  It is irreducible and totally real; Frobenius (4,5) at 43 and (1,8) at 29 give S₉.
- At `τ = β` the shift polynomial is irreducible of degree 10.
- For each `τ = Σ_{k=1}^8 c_k β^k`, `c ∈ {−1,0,1}⁸`, first nonzero coefficient positive (3280
  classes; `τ ↦ −τ` maps `f_τ(h)` to `f_τ(−h)`): `χ_τ` by resultant, `f_τ(h) = s_λ(τ + h)` from the
  Jacobi–Trudi determinant, exact `FactorList` over ℚ.
- **Result: every `f_τ` is irreducible of degree 10; no rational shift.**
- **Control:** the same code on the control field finds exactly `c = (1,0,…,0)`, `h = 0`, in
  its 601-tuple window.
- On `q₁` and `q₂` the same search hits at `τ = β` (§0.4); those fields are degenerate.

### 3.4 Geometry of the E locus [Proof / Heuristic]

- `Z_K = {τ ∈ K : s_λ(τ) = 0}` is a cone (degree 10) over a hypersurface in `ℙ⁸`, defined over ℚ
  and twisted by `K`. Since `10 > 9`, it is of general type (just: `K_Z = O(1)`). Rational points
  are not expected to be Zariski dense [Heuristic], as for every other frontier variety.
- **What is new:** codimension 1 (the old odd route needed 4; N2's overshoot needed 2), and an
  explicit equation that is linear in `e₇`.
- In centred coordinates the equation is `e₃·A = e₅²` with `A = e₇ − e₃e₄ + e₂e₅`. So
  `Z_K ∩ {e₁ = 0}` is the union over `μ ∈ ℚ` of the codim-2 loci `{e₅ = μe₃, A = μ²e₃}`, together
  with `{e₃ = e₅ = 0}`. A ℚ-point on any one of them suffices.
- For fixed `τ`, the shift gives a one-variable problem: a rational root of the degree-10
  `f_τ(h) ∈ ℚ[h]`.
- **P-points do not help E:** `g` must be rational, so `τ` and the shift must be rational.

## 4. Ranking and recommended order

**T9Q (rational generator).**
1. **E**: one equation of degree 10 on `K`, containing `{p₁ = p₃ = p₅ = 0}`. Next steps:
   - search the codim-2 pencil `{e₅ = μe₃, A = μ²e₃}` for small `μ`;
   - look for low-degree subvarieties beyond `{p₁ = p₃ = p₅ = 0}`;
   - extend the box to height 2 (about 2.2 × 10⁵ classes, roughly 1 hour in Wolfram at the
     measured rate).
2. **D1** (cubic `X`): E is its boundary `x₃ → 0`, and D1 has one more dimension (a curve of `X`
   per generator). Redo the degree-20 count on `q₃`, not `q₁`.
3. **H** (two rational sextics, one rotated): two conditions, but a 3-fold of rational sextics `P`
   for every generator, i.e. `β ∈ span_ℚ(1, P(β), …, P(β)⁶)`. Worth one measurement of the
   fibre 3-fold's degree and structure.
4. F, C/L, K: dominated.

**P-valued dials (T9P, and T9Q through P).**
1. **M2 (Bézier × egg)**: `c_τ = 0` with a finite fibre. But at most 8 real crossings were found
   in 1500 samples [Heuristic: the egg is convex, so the cap is probably 8]. That serves P-nonics
   with `s ≥ 1` (7 real roots), not totally real ones.
2. **I** (rational sextic × rotated P-quartic): fix a rational `P`; the good generators over L form
   a 5-dimensional locus of codimension 4. That is the right shape for Lemma A if its degree is
   small. Not measured.
3. **M1, G, J**: one condition each on the generator.

This suggests replacing PLAN-degree9's default order with **E (a D1 sub-stream), then D1 on
`q₃`, then H**. D3 (towers) is unchanged.

## 5. Reproduction

The census function (Wolfram), as run:

```mathematica
census[name_, expr_, lin_, non_] := Module[{vars, vals, nv, FF, poly, deg, rts, sel, dN, JJ, sv, rk, Jext, sv2, sk},
  SeedRandom[Hash[name]]; vars = Join[non, lin];
  vals = Thread[vars -> Table[RandomInteger[{-30, 30}]/RandomInteger[{1, 7}], Length[vars]]];
  nv = Thread[non -> (non /. vals)];
  FF = MatrixRank[PadRight[#, 60] & /@ Table[CoefficientList[Expand[D[expr, lin[[i]]] /. nv], t], {i, Length[lin]}]];
  poly = Expand[expr /. vals]; deg = Exponent[poly, t];
  rts = t /. NSolve[poly == 0, t, WorkingPrecision -> 100];
  sel = Take[SortBy[rts, {Abs[Im[#]], Re[#]} &], 9]; dN = D[expr, t] /. vals;
  JJ = Table[-(D[expr, vars[[j]]] /. vals /. t -> z)/(dN /. t -> z), {z, sel}, {j, Length[vars]}];
  sv = SingularValueList[N[JJ, 90], Tolerance -> 0]; rk = Count[sv, _?(# > 10^-40 &)];
  Jext = Join[JJ, Table[If[non[[a]] === vars[[b]], 1, 0], {a, Length[non]}, {b, Length[vars]}]];
  sk = Count[SingularValueList[N[Jext, 90], Tolerance -> 0], _?(# > 10^-40 &)];
  {name, deg, FF, rk, sk}];
(* row E *)
census["E", y0 + y1 t + y2 t^2 + y3 t^3 - Sum[g[k] (t^2 + a t)^k, {k, 0, 6}], Join[{y0, y1, y2, y3}, Table[g[k], {k, 0, 6}]], {a}]
```

The other rows change only `expr`, `lin`, `non` (the definitions are in the log). The E box
search uses

```mathematica
sl[e_] := Det[{{e[4], e[5], e[6], e[7]}, {e[2], e[3], e[4], e[5]}, {1, e[1], e[2], e[3]}, {0, 0, 1, e[1]}}];
fpoly[qq_, tau_] := Module[{cp = Resultant[qq, t - tau, x], cps},
  cp = Expand[cp/Coefficient[cp, t, 9]]; cps = Expand[cp /. t -> t - h];
  Expand[sl[Function[k, If[k == 0, 1, (-1)^k Coefficient[cps, t, 9 - k]]]]]];
```

and tests whether `FactorList[fpoly[q3, τ]]` has a factor of degree 1 in `h`.

## Other things noticed

- WARP §4's "egg × sextic, rank 10, 7-dimensional condition variety" treated the sextic's
  coefficients as free P values. With a drawable (rational) sextic the egg route is row F: one
  condition on `τ_s`, a hypersurface of degree 26. Conversely, the square root in the egg's
  `y` is automatic: a crossing with `ξ, y ∈ K` puts the egg parameter `s` in `K`.
- `N2`'s fixed-field test `(x − 1)…(x − 9) − 1` has the same defect as `q₁`. After `x ↦ x + 5` it is
  odd − 1, so `τ = β − 5` solves the odd engine, and also N2's overshoot `W` (cofactor constant).
  N2's box `{−1, 0, 1}⁹` missed it only because the coordinate −5 lies outside. Test fields of
  the form `∏(x − aᵢ) + c` should be avoided for any route.
- Bézier × Bézier reaches 9 real crossings (`y = T₃(x)` against `x = T₃(y)` gives `T₉(x) = x`).
  NOTES left this "—".
- Axis-parallel egg × egg has crossing degree 8, not 16 (both are double covers of the x-line);
  WARP's 16 needs a rotation.
- The pattern "supports with 9 monomials give a single Schur-polynomial condition `s_λ(τ) = 0`
  with `|λ| = Σe − 36`" applies to every monomial row. A drawable support with smaller `|λ|`
  would be strictly better than E. None was found: the alternatives with `F = 9` (cubic `X`,
  egg) are not monomial, and every other monomial support from the catalogue has `F ≤ 8`.
