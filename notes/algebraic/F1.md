### Stage A

1. **The solution variety is `W_m = Ĉ^m/(S_d × S_m)`, not a twist [Proof sketch, §2.2].** `S_d`
   acts diagonally, `S_m` permutes the factors. It is defined over ℚ, finite of degree
   `(d!)^{m−1}` over `Sym^m ℙ¹ = ℙ^m`, and its ℚ-points over an irreducible `h` are exactly the
   solutions with that `h`. This corrects the plan's "a twist of `Ĉ^m/S_d`". A twist appears
   only once `L` (or `K`) is fixed.
2. **Descent criterion [Proof, §2.1].** `(g, λ)` is a solution iff `B_h = ℚ[x]/(N_h)` has a
   degree-`d` subalgebra `K` with `K·ℚ(λ) = B_h`. When `B_h` is a field this is a G-invariant
   block system with `d` blocks, each meeting every fibre once. If the fibre group over `ℚ(λ)` is
   primitive and `m < d`, every block system with blocks of size `m` is automatically of this
   kind. The test needs no assumption about automorphisms of `A_λ`.
3. **The engine list collapses [Proof, §2.4].** `tri(D)` is `pp(D−1, 1)` after `x ↦ 1/x`,
   `λ ↦ −1/λ`, with the same `K`; and `pp(p, q) = pp(q, p)` after `x ↦ 1 − x`. The engines of
   degree `d` are exactly `pp(p, q)` with `p > q ≥ 1`, `gcd(p, q) = 1`, `p + q = d`. Checked on
   certified solutions: the `pp(3, 1)` and `tri(4)` solutions correspond under `λ ↦ −1/λ` with
   isomorphic `K` [Computed, exact].
4. **`Ĉ` for `tri(5)` is Bring's curve [Proof sketch, §2.3].** The ordered roots of
   `x⁵ + λx − λ` satisfy `p₁ = p₂ = p₃ = 0`, which gives an `S₅`-equivariant birational map

5. **`W₂` is of general type for all 8 engines with `7 ≤ d ≤ 9` [Computed, exact; §2.3].** So
   Bombieri–Lang predicts that `m = 2` solutions in those degrees lie on finitely many curves
   plus finitely many points, as the plan assumed. **For `d = 5, 6` general type is not shown,
   and at `d = 5` `W₂` may be rational.** For `tri(5)`, `p_g = q = 0` and `K_S² = 2 > 0` on the minimal resolution `S`, so

## 1. Objects (as in the plan, with one convention fixed)

An engine is `g = A/B` with `A, B ∈ ℤ[x]` coprime and `deg A = d > deg B`. Its fibre polynomial is
`F_λ = A − λB`.

- `pp(p, q)`: `A = x^p (1 − x)^q`, `B = 1`. Finite branch values are `0` and
  `λ* = p^p q^q/(p + q)^{p+q}`.
- `tri(D)`: `A = x^D`, `B = 1 − x`, so `F_λ = x^D + λx − λ`. Finite branch values are `0` and
  `λ* = −D^D/(D − 1)^{D−1}`.

For `h ∈ ℤ[T]` irreducible of degree `m`, write `N_h = Σ_k h_k A^k B^{m−k}`, and
`B_h = ℚ[x]/(N_h) = A_λ` viewed over ℚ (`n = dm`).

## 2. Structure of the solution set

### 2.1 Descent criterion

Let `L = ℚ(λ)`, with `λ` not a branch value, so that `A_λ = L[x]/(F_λ)` is étale of degree `d`
over `L`.

**Proposition 2.1 [Proof].**
(a) There is an étale ℚ-algebra `K` with `K ⊗ L ≅ A_λ` as `L`-algebras iff `B_h` contains a
ℚ-subalgebra `K₀` of dimension `d` with `K₀ · L = B_h`.
(b) If `B_h` is a field with Galois group `G` acting on its `n` embeddings, such subfields `K₀`
correspond to G-invariant partitions of the embeddings into `d` blocks, each block meeting each
fibre of `Hom(B_h, ℚ̄) → Hom(L, ℚ̄)` in exactly one point.
(c) If moreover the Galois group of `A_λ` over `L` acts primitively on the `d` roots (e.g. it is
`S_d`) and `m < d`, then every G-invariant partition into blocks of size `m` meets each fibre
once.

*Proof.*
(a) Given `K`, the image of `K ⊗ 1` is a subalgebra `K₀` of dimension `d`. It and `L` generate
`B_h`, because `K ⊗ L → A_λ` is onto. Conversely, the multiplication map `K₀ ⊗ L → B_h` is an
`L`-algebra map; it is onto because `K₀ · L = B_h`, and both sides have dimension `dm`, so it is
an isomorphism.
(b) This is the Galois correspondence. Subfields of degree `d` correspond to block systems with
`d` blocks. `K₀ · L = B_h` means `Stab(block) ∩ Stab(fibre) = Stab(point)`, i.e. each block meets
each fibre in one point.
(c) Let `Bl` be a block of size `m` and let `F_j` be the fibre over `λ_j`. The stabiliser
`G_{λ_j}` preserves `F_j` and permutes the blocks, so `Bl ∩ F_j` is a block for `G_{λ_j}` acting on
`F_j`. That action is the Galois action on the roots of `F_{λ_j}`, which is primitive, so
`|Bl ∩ F_j| ∈ {0, 1, d}`. The value `d` would force `m ≥ d`. So `Bl` meets each fibre at most once,
and the image of `Bl` in `Hom(L, ℚ̄)` is a block of size `m`, i.e. everything. ∎

The plan's version ("isomorphisms are unique, the cocycle is automatic") is not needed: (a) is
the whole criterion. One `h` can carry more than one `K` in principle (several block systems);
`certify(..., want_all=True)` returns all of them.

### 2.2 The parameter variety `W_m`

Let `Ĉ → ℙ¹` be the Galois closure of `g`, with group `S_d`. The arithmetic monodromy group lies
in `S_d` and contains the geometric one, which is already `S_d` (P1′ §4.2), so the two are equal.
Hence `Ĉ` is geometrically irreducible over ℚ and `S_d` acts on it over ℚ.

**Proposition 2.2 [Proof sketch].** Let `W_m = Ĉ^m/(S_d × S_m)`, with `S_d` acting diagonally
and `S_m` permuting factors, and `π: W_m → Sym^m ℙ¹ = ℙ^m`.
- `π` is finite of degree `(d!)^m/d! = (d!)^{m−1}`, and `dim W_m = m`.
- Let `h` be irreducible of degree `m` with no root a branch value. The ℚ-points of `W_m` over
  `h` correspond bijectively to the fields `K₀` of Proposition 2.1, i.e. to the solutions with
  that `h`.

*Sketch.* A geometric point of `Ĉ` over `λ_j` is a labelling `ℓ_j: {1..d} → F_j` of the roots.
A point of `Ĉ^m` over `(λ_1, ..., λ_m)` is a tuple of labellings. Its `S_d`-orbit is the
partition of all `dm` roots into the `d` transversal blocks `{ℓ_1(i), ..., ℓ_m(i)}`, and `S_m`
forgets the order of the `λ_j`. Over such `h`, `S_d × S_m` acts freely: a permutation in `S_m`
fixing the tuple must fix the distinct `λ_j`, and then `τ ∈ S_d` fixes a labelling, so `τ = 1`. So
ℚ-points of the quotient over `h` are the Galois-stable orbits, i.e. the G-invariant transversal
partitions. ∎

So the plan's "twist" is not needed: `W_m` is one variety over ℚ. Fixing `L` (resp. `K`) gives
the twisted curves of P1′ §4.2.

### 2.3 Kodaira dimension of `W₂`

Write `X = Ĉ × Ĉ` and `Γ = S_d × ⟨ι⟩`, so `W₂ = X/Γ`. Every `(σ, ι)` with `σ² = 1` fixes the
curve `{(P, σP)}` pointwise, so `X → W₂` is ramified along those curves. The quotient is smooth
there; all other non-free points have both coordinates over branch values.

**Method [Computed, exact; `scripts/F1_kodaira.py`].**
- `p_g(S) = dim (Λ² V)^{S_d}`, where `V = H⁰(Ĉ, K)` and `ι` acts on `V ⊗ V` by minus the swap.
  The character of `V` comes from the Eichler trace formula. Checks: `⟨χ_V, 1⟩ = 0`, and
  `χ_V(1) = g`. `q(S) = 0`.
- `e(W₂) = |Γ|⁻¹ Σ_γ e(X^γ)`. Fixed-point counts are `|C(μ)|/e_b` per branch point and exponent.
- `K_{W}² = (K_X − R)²/|Γ|`, with `R = Σ_{σ²=1} {(P, σP)}`. The intersection numbers used are
  `Δ_σ² = 2 − 2g`, `K·Δ_σ = 4g − 4`, and `Δ_σ·Δ_τ = |Fix(σ⁻¹τ)|`.
- Every singular point is found with its local group in `GL₂`, written as monomial matrices in
  standard coordinates. Reflections are removed (diagonal reflection groups `μ_{n₁} × μ_{n₂}`, or
  `G(n, p, 2)` after conjugating an antidiagonal reflection to the swap). The remaining cyclic
  group gives a type `1/N(1, q)` and its Hirzebruch–Jung resolution.
- `K_S²` of the minimal resolution `S` is computed two ways: Noether
  (`12χ(O_S) − e(S)`, `e(S) = e(W₂) + Σ r_x`) and locally (`K_W² − Σ δ_x`). **They agree in every
  case.** That is the check on the local analysis.
- If `K_S² > 0` and `p_g ≥ 1`, then `S` is of general type: `κ ≥ 0`, and the minimal model has
  `K² ≥ K_S² > 0`, which excludes `κ = 0, 1`.

**`Ĉ` for `tri(5)` is Bring's curve [Proof sketch].** A point of `Ĉ` over `λ` is an ordering
`(x₁, ..., x₅)` of the roots of `x⁵ + λx − λ`. Its `e₁ = e₂ = e₃ = 0`, so `p₁ = p₂ = p₃ = 0`, and
`(x₁ : ... : x₅)` lies on Bring's curve `B = {Σx = Σx² = Σx³ = 0} ⊂ ℙ⁴` (genus 4). The map is
`S₅`-equivariant. It is injective on generic points: if two orderings differ by a scalar `c`,
then `x⁵ + λc⁻⁴x − λc⁻⁵` has the shape `x⁵ + μx − μ` only for `c = 1`. Both curves have genus 4, so
the map is an isomorphism. So `W₂(tri(5)) = (B × B)/(S₅ × C₂)`.

- C1 at `d = 5` therefore distinguishes the two in data, though not as a proof. Deciding `κ`

  `P₂ = h⁰(W₂, 2K_W)`. That `h⁰` is the next computation, if wanted [Open].

### 2.4 Redundancies in the engine list [Proof]

- `pp(p, q)(1 − x) = pp(q, p)(x)`: same `λ`, same `K`.
- `pp(D − 1, 1)(1/x) = (x − 1)/x^D = −1/tri(D)(x)`. If `λ ≠ 0`, the fibre algebras of `tri(D)` at
  `λ` and of `pp(D − 1, 1)` at `−1/λ` are isomorphic over `ℚ(λ)` via `x ↦ 1/x`. No root is `0`,
  since `F_λ(0) = −λ ≠ 0`. So the solutions correspond with the same `K`.
- In general `g = x^p(1 − x)^q` has divisor `p[0] + q[1] − (p + q)[∞]`, and Möbius maps permuting
  `{0, 1, ∞}` permute the exponent triple `(p, q, −p − q)` and replace `g` by `±g^{±1}`. So the
  engines of degree `d` (including P1′'s `pp(p, q)` with mixed signs) are indexed by
  `p > q ≥ 1` coprime with `p + q = d`:
  - d = 5: pp(4,1), pp(3,2);
  - d = 6: pp(5,1);
  - d = 7: pp(6,1), pp(5,2), pp(4,3);
  - d = 8: pp(7,1), pp(5,3);
  - d = 9: pp(8,1), pp(7,2), pp(5,4).
- C1 therefore has 11 engines, not 11 plus 5 `tri`. C2's "tri(d), pp(⌈d/2⌉, ⌊d/2⌋)" is
  `pp(d − 1, 1)` plus the balanced one (`pp(5, 4)` at d = 9, `pp(4, 3)` at d = 7, `pp(3, 2)` at
  d = 5; at d = 6 and d = 8 the balanced pair is not coprime, so `pp(5, 1)` alone and
  `pp(5, 3)` respectively).

### 3.2 `cycle_table` and `filter`

`cycle_table(g, ℓ)` gives, for each residue `λ ∈ 𝔽_ℓ`, the sorted factor degrees of `A − λB` mod
`ℓ`. Residues are excluded (inconclusive) if they are a branch value mod `ℓ` or the fibre is not
squarefree of degree `d` there.

**Lemma 3.2 (filter soundness) [Proof].** Let `ℓ ∤ lc(h)` with `h` squarefree and split
completely mod `ℓ`, roots `ρ_1, ..., ρ_m`, and suppose every fibre `F_{ρ_j}` is squarefree of
degree `d` mod `ℓ`. If `(g, λ)` is a solution, the factor-degree multisets at all `ρ_j` coincide.
*Proof.*
- `ℓ ∤ disc h`, so `ℓ` splits completely in `L` and `ℤ[λ]` is `ℓ`-maximal. The primes
  `𝔩_j | ℓ` correspond to the `ρ_j`, with `L_{𝔩_j} = ℚ_ℓ`, and `λ ↦ ρ_j` under `𝒪_{𝔩_j} → 𝔽_ℓ`.
- `F_λ` is monic up to a unit (`pp`: leading coefficient `±1`; `tri`: monic) and squarefree
  mod `𝔩_j`. By Hensel, `A_λ ⊗ L_{𝔩_j}` is unramified with residue degrees equal to the mod-`ℓ`
  factor degrees at `ρ_j`.
- `A_λ ⊗ L_{𝔩_j} ≅ K ⊗ ℚ_ℓ` for every `j`, so the degrees are the decomposition type of `ℓ` in
  `K`, independent of `j`. ∎

`filter` returns `reject` at the first disagreeing prime (a proof of non-solution), `pass` after
12 agreeing usable primes, and `few` if the prime list (all primes 11..1193) runs out first.

### 3.3 `certify` (exact)

1. `N_h` is checked irreducible over ℚ; if not, the status is `reducible` (not decided here).
   This happens at small height, e.g. `λ = φ` for `pp(5, 4)`, where `x = φ` is a root.
2. **Orbitals.** For `c = 2, 3, ...`, take `Q_c(T) = Res_x(N_h(x), N_h(T − cx))` of degree `n²`,
   required squarefree. Its roots are `r_j + c·r_i` over ordered pairs. It is factored over ℚ
   (FLINT). Each pair `(i, j)` is assigned to the unique factor with a root ball overlapping the
   pair's ball, using Arb balls at increasing precision. Each pair value is a root of exactly one
   factor, so a unique overlap is a proof. This gives the G-orbitals exactly.
3. **Fibres.** The fibre of each root is found by matching `A(r)/B(r)` to the root balls of `h`
   (unique overlap).
4. **Blocks.** The search runs over unions of orbitals through root 0 that leave fibre 0 and give
   `m − 1` further points in distinct fibres. Each union is kept if it defines a G-invariant
   equivalence relation with transversal classes (a combinatorial check on the orbital matrix).
5. **K.** For a block system, `θ_B = Σ_{i∈B} (lc·r_i)^k` (`k = 1, 2, ...`) are algebraic integers
   fixed by the block stabiliser. So `∏_B (t − θ_B) ∈ ℤ[t]`, read off balls that each contain a
   unique integer. The first `k` with `d` distinct `θ_B` gives an irreducible monic `f_K`. Its
   root generates `K₀`; Proposition 2.1 (a)–(b) does the rest.

Ancillary exact routines:
- `has_root_in(f, base)`: Trager. `Res_y(base(y), f(T − cy))` is squarefree, and a factor of
  degree `deg base` means `f` has a root in `ℚ[y]/(base)`.
- `fields_isomorphic`: `has_root_in` for equal-degree fields.
- `galois_certificate`: Dedekind patterns prove `S_d`, either from primitivity (`d` prime, or a
  `(d − 1)`-cycle) plus a transposition, or from primitivity plus a 3-cycle or a `p`-cycle with
  `p ≤ d − 3` (Jordan), with `A_d` versus `S_d` decided by the discriminant. Otherwise it returns
  the pattern set, labelled cycle statistics only.
- `signature`: Arb root isolation plus a conjugation argument.

Cost: at `n = 18` (`m = 2`, `d = 9`) the resultant takes 0.3 s and the factorisation of degree
324 takes 0.1 s. `n = 60` (stage B if `m = 12`) means degree 3600. If needed there, the hinted
check `has_root_in(f_K, N_h)` (degree `5n`) is exact and cheap.

## 5. Stage B: Bring–Jerrard calibration

### 5.1 Construction [Proof sketch; `scripts/F1_bring.py`]

For `K = ℚ(β)` with `f` monic, put `y = a₀ + a₁β + ... + a₄β⁴`.
- `Tr y = 0` fixes `a₀`. Then `Q(a) = Tr y²` and `C(a) = Tr y³` are a quadratic and a cubic form in
  `a₁, ..., a₄`.
- **Point.** Take a zero `P` of `Q`: rational if one has height ≤ 4, otherwise
  `P = e_i + t e_j` with `t` quadratic.
- **Line.** The lines on `Q = 0` through `P` lie in the tangent plane. With one coordinate of `V`
  zeroed, one set to 1, and one solved from `B(P, V) = 0`, the last coordinate `r` is a root of a
  quadratic.
- **Cubic.** On the line `a = sP + V`, `C = 0` is a cubic in `s`.
- **Engine.** Over `L = ℚ(t, r, s)` (a 2·2·3 or 2·3 tower, hence solvable), `y` has
  characteristic polynomial `y⁵ + Ay + B`, with `A = −Tr y⁴/4` and `B = −Tr y⁵/5` (Newton with
  `e₁ = e₂ = e₃ = 0`). Then `y = cx` with `c = −B/A` gives `x⁵ + λx − λ`, `λ = A⁵/B⁴`.
- **`h`.** The minimal polynomial of `λ` comes from successive resultants over the relations for
  `s`, `r`, `t`, factored over ℚ. The factor containing the chosen `λ` is identified by ball
  arithmetic, or is the whole eliminant when that is irreducible.
- For each field six coordinate patterns are tried, and the smallest `(m, height)` is kept.

`L` is solvable by construction, so so is `ℚ(λ) ⊆ L`. The Galois group of `ℚ(λ)` is recorded only
as cycle-pattern statistics (no PARI).

### 5.3 What the calibration says

- **Size.** The heights are not artefacts of a careless choice. Tschirnhaus coefficients of height
  ≤ 4 produce `A, B` of degree 4 and 5 in them, and `λ = A⁵/B⁴` has degree 20, so heights of 10³⁰
  or more are expected. Smaller solutions may exist for other reasons; these are upper bounds, not
  minima [Heuristic].
- **Shape.** A Bring–Jerrard `λ` lives in a 2·3 or 2·2·3 tower, the plan's C4 shape for `m = 6`.
  But the heights put stage-B solutions out of reach of any box enumeration of `h`. **C4 should
  enumerate by the tower** (small zeros of `Q`, lines, cubic roots), not by `h`. That is
  Bring–Jerrard itself, and it reaches every quintic field [Heuristic: it recovers the known
  mechanism, not a new one]. Recommendation: drop C4 as planned, or redefine it this way.
- **No negative follows.** A stage-C null would say nothing about whether small solutions exist
  at `d ≥ 9`. The calibration says only that the known mechanism at `d = 5` is not small.
