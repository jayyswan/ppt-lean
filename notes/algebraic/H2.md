# H2: real Tschirnhaus over F_{<n}: which conditions can be met

Stream H2 of `pptc/PLAN-algebraic.md` (wave 2). No `.lean` edits. Labels: [Proof], [Cited], [Wolfram],
[Heuristic], [Open]. Every obstruction is scoped to the route it blocks; nothing concludes a negative about
the target. Backend: Wolfram for all numbers (no Python). Citations are taken from G1.md and NOTES, not
re-opened; treat them at G1's verification level.

Notation. q in Q[X] of degree n, K = Q[X]/(q), K_R = R^r x C^s, r + 2s = n. For tau in K_R, p_j = Tr tau^j =
sum y_i^j + 2 Re sum z_l^j in root coordinates. V_k = {p_1 = ... = p_k = 0} in P^{n-1}; O_m =
{p_1 = p_3 = ... = p_{2m-1} = 0} (odd shape). F = F_{<n}. One "step" = one real root of a polynomial of
degree <= n-1 over the current field (F is closed under these by induction).

## 0. Findings summary

1. **Lemma A (real Brauer / slicing) [Proof].** If W over F has degree d < n and a smooth real point p, then
   F-points are dense near p in W(R). Brauer's bound (r-1)!+1 is exactly this lemma for V_{r-1}. So with
   s >= floor(k/2), p_1..p_k can be killed by one real root of degree k! whenever k! < n, and O_m whenever
   (2m-1)!! < n. (Section 2.)
2. **Real existence is exactly Descartes [Proof + Wolfram].** V_k has a smooth nonzero real point iff
   s >= floor(k/2) (n >= k+1). The real ceiling for power-sum chains is k_R(s) = 2s+1; a totally real field
   allows only p_1. For k even and s = k/2 - 1, p_k > 0 on V_{k-1}(R). General vanishing patterns: a block of
   z zero coefficients costs z (z even) or z-1 (z odd) of the 2s nonreal roots, so isolated single zeros (odd
   shape, N2's overshoot supports) are free. The Wolfram grid at n = 9 agrees exactly. (Section 3.)
3. **Cost, not realness, binds for large n.** Killable power sums with steps of degree < n:
   k_A(n) = max{k : k! < n} = 3 (7 <= n <= 24), 4 (25..120), 5 (121..720) [Proof, Wolfram]. Linear-space
   methods improve this to the complex thresholds of G1: k = 4 at n >= 9, 5 at n >= 41, 6 at n >= 121 [Cited].
   Real linear-space methods need more s: a real line in V_2 is an isotropic 2-plane, so s >= 2 (r >= 1) or
   s >= 3 (r = 0); real isotropic subspaces have dimension <= s (<= s-1 if r = 0) [Proof]. Engines need n-5
   (P quartic) to n-7 (Q sextic, overshoot) conditions, so the count is enough only for n <= 9 (power-sum
   shapes). (Sections 4, 7.)
4. **N2's overshoot locus W_O is expensive in the right currency.** dim 6 for every n, codim n-7, degree
   h_{n-7}(0,1,3,...,13) = 1428, 32340, 630042, 11115258, 182959876 for n = 9..13 [Wolfram]. Lemma A never
   applies; codimension (n+1-F) is the wrong currency, degree against n is the right one. (Section 5.)
5. **Square lift [Proof for the algebra; verdict Heuristic and route-scoped].** After a Mobius move putting
   the target root alone above c, f(y^2+c) has degree 2n, exactly 2 real roots, s' = n-1 [Wolfram]. All
   realness obstructions vanish (k_R(s') = n'-1). But: tau' = a + b y with b = 0 is not a Tschirnhaus element
   (square char poly); the pure-odd subspace tau' = b y kills all odd p_j free but gives chi(t) = chi_{b^2u}(t^2),
   the degree-n problem again; mixed tau' needs ~2n conditions in ambient dimension 2n while the auxiliary
   degree bound stays n. Conditions/ambient-dimension stays ~1, so the lift buys realness and sells cost.
   (Section 6.)
6. **No regime found where the count suffices for all n** except overshoot with offset towers of depth ~ n/3,
   where the count is met and the cost is not controlled (H1's territory). Bookkeeping law: an engine with f
   free parameters needs f >= n - c(n), c(n) = number of cheap conditions ~ log n / log log n, so f must be
   n - O(log n). (Section 7.)

## 1. The cost model

A step is one real root of degree <= n-1. The recovery step (root of tau(x) - y, degree <= n-1) is also
cheap. The only step of degree >= n is the engine crossing, which carries A_n. "Conditions achievable" means
achievable by chains of such steps. The induction gives degree < n even for lifted problems (Section 6).

## 2. Points on varieties of degree < n

**Lemma A [Proof].** Let W in P^N be defined over F, p in W(R) a smooth point of a component W_0 of dimension
m, d = deg W < n. Then every neighbourhood of p in W(R) contains points of W(F).

Proof. Choose a Q-rational linear space L of codimension m, close to p and transversal to T_pW_0 (such L form
a Zariski-dense open set in a rational Grassmannian; Q-points are dense; position and transversality are
open conditions). By the implicit function theorem L meets W_0 in a real point p' near p, isolated in L cap W.
L cap W is a zero-dimensional scheme over F of degree <= d. Take a Q-linear form theta separating the points
of its reduction (Zariski-generic). The minimal polynomial of theta on the reduced algebra has degree <= d <
n and theta(p') is a real root of it. An isolated point is fixed by every automorphism fixing theta(p'), so its
coordinates lie in F(theta(p')); as F is closed under real roots of degree < n, they lie in F. Shrink the
neighbourhood for density. QED.

Remarks.
- Non-degeneracy (tau primitive, entries distinct) is open, so Lemma A returns a good tau.
- Known F-point p0 of multiplicity 1 on W: slice through p0, residual degree d-1. A known F-rational curve of
  degree e inside V_{k-1} reduces the last step to one equation of degree k e (lines: degree k).
- Cubic hypersurfaces with an F-point are unirational (tangent-secant: a line through p0 in the tangent
  hyperplane meets X again in the F-point t = -B/C). Fano complete intersections are classically unirational
  for large ambient dimension (Predonzan, Kollar; [Open: exact citations, not opened]). Unirationality gives
  many points once one is found, never the first, which is where the cost sits.

**Corollary.** With s >= floor(k/2):

| n | Lemma A: max k | complex Wolfson [Cited G1] | need n-5 | need n-7 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 9 | 3 | 4 | 4 | 2 |
| 10 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 3 |
| 13 | 3 | 4 | 8 | 6 |
| 25 | 4 | 4 | 20 | 18 |
| 41 | 4 | 5 | 36 | 34 |

k!: 6, 24, 120, 720; (2m-1)!!: 1, 3, 15, 105, 945, 10395 [Wolfram]. So O_m by slicing: m <= 2 for n <= 15,
m = 3 for 16 <= n <= 105, m = 4 only from n = 106.

Calibration with the repo [Cited NOTES, Certificate.lean]: the n = 7, 8 odd route (m = 3, degree-15 locus >
n) was done by a chain of degrees (3, 2, 6, 5); the n = 9 power chain (k = 4, degree 24 > 9) by (2, 3, 5, 2,
6, 4). So linear-space tricks beat Lemma A by a wide margin at small n; Lemma A is the floor.

[Wolfram] Illustration at n = 9, s = 1 (q = (x-1)...(x-7)(x^2+1)): a random Q-rational P^3 slice of V_3 has
exactly 6 solutions (Bezout) but 0 real ones; real points of V_3 exist (Section 3), so the slice must be
taken near a real witness, as the lemma prescribes.

## 3. Real existence: which conditions have real solutions

**Theorem R1 [Proof].** For n >= k+1, V_k(R) contains a nonzero point iff s >= floor(k/2); smooth ones exist.

Necessity. If e_1 = ... = e_k = 0, chi_tau = t^n + (lower terms from t^{n-k-1} down). Real roots <= V(P(t)) +
V(P(-t)) <= sum over gaps g between consecutive present exponents of w(g), w(g) = 1 (g odd), <= 2 (g even,
and 0 if the flanking coefficients have equal sign). The first gap has g >= k+1, loss g - w(g) >= k (k even)
or >= k-1 (k odd). So 2s >= k resp. k-1.

Sufficiency and smoothness. The k+1 vertices of a regular (k+1)-gon centred at 0 have p_1..p_k = 0 and
contain 1 (k even) or 2 (k odd) real vertices, i.e. floor(k/2) conjugate pairs; fill the other n-k-1
coordinates with zeros, arranged as real zeros and (for larger s) conjugate pairs z = 0, so the pair count
is exactly s. The Jacobian (j w_i^{j-1}) has rank k when >= k distinct values occur (here k+2), the same over
R and C. QED.

[Wolfram] n = 9, min of sum_j p_j^2 on the unit sphere over 10 random starts:

| k | s | min |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | 0, 1, 2 | 1.0, 3e-17, 6e-17 |
| 4 | 1, 2, 3 | 0.072, 1e-16, 6e-17 |
| 5 | 1, 2, 3 | 0.074, 2e-16, 1e-16 |

Exactly s >= floor(k/2). Consistent with THREE-PAIRS section 3 and G1 R1.

**Sign refinement [Proof].** k even, s = k/2 - 1: the t^{n-k} coefficient must be negative, so p_k > 0 on
V_{k-1}(R)\0. At s = k/2 both signs occur at smooth points. So the final equation p_k = 0 for k even needs a
sign change, which a real line need not supply (n = 9, s = 2: p_4 > 0 on every sampled real line in V_3
[Cited NOTES result 3]). For k odd the last equation on a line is an odd-degree binary form and has a real
root.

**Theorem R2 (patterns) [Proof of necessity].** For a support E (present exponents including n), a block of
z consecutive zeros costs c(z) = z (z even), z-1 (z odd, needing a prescribed sign alternation); necessarily
sum c(z) <= 2s. Totally real: every block has z = 1 (N4's gap theorem). Consequences:
- odd shape e_1 = e_3 = ... = e_{2m-1} = 0: cost 0, any s. Smooth real points exist for n >= 2m-1:
  +-a_1, ..., +-a_{m-1}, 0 and zeros; the Jacobian rows w^{2l}, l = 0..m-1, have rank m when >= m distinct
  squares occur [Proof];
- y^n = quartic: a block of n-5 zeros, s >= floor((n-5)/2);
- N2's overshoot supports (a), (b), (c): all gaps <= 2, no obstruction.

**Real isotropic structure [Proof].** The trace form on K_R has signature (r+s, s), on p_1 = 0 it is
(r+s-1, s). Maximal real isotropic subspaces of {p_1 = p_2 = 0} have dimension s (r >= 1) or s-1 (r = 0),
and are built over F by degree-2 steps: pick rational u, v with Q(u) > 0 > Q(v), isotropic vector
sqrt(-Q(v)) u + sqrt(Q(u)) v, pass to P^perp/P, repeat. Consequences:
- real lines in V_2 = isotropic 2-planes: s >= 2 (r >= 1) or s >= 3 (r = 0). So line schemes need more s
  than the existence bound floor(k/2) once k >= 3: at s = 1, p_1 = p_2 = p_3 = 0 is reachable by one sextic
  (Lemma A, n >= 7) but has no real lines;
- Hilbert's 3-plane in {p_1 = p_2 = 0} needs s >= 4 (G1 R3).

## 4. Polar cones and linear spaces, tracked

A line P0 + t u lies in V_{k-1} iff Tr(P0^m u^i) = 0 for 1 <= i, i+m <= k-1 (k(k-1)/2 conditions; i-th degree
class has k-i members). Then p_k(P0 + tu) has degree k in t.

| k | cone degree prod i^{k-i} | necessary n >= k(k-1)/2 + 2 |
|---|---|---|
| 3 | 2 | 5 |
| 4 | 12 | 8 |
| 5 | 288 | 12 |
| 6 | 34560 | 17 |
| 7 | 24883200 | 23 |
| 8 | 1.25e11 | 30 |

[Wolfram]; k = 4 is the degree-12 curve of THREE-PAIRS. The dimension bound is only necessary. The real cost
is a point on the cone, whose degree explodes; THREE-PAIRS attacks it with a second layer (quadric pencil,
conic, degrees 5, 2, 6, 4 < 9). Iterating gives Wolfson's F(r). Odd shape: lines through P0 in O_{m-1} impose
(m-1)^2 conditions (n >= (m-1)^2 + 2), and the Fano variety of lines on O_{m-1} has expected dimension
2n - 6 - (m-2)(m+1) [Wolfram]: m = 3: 4, 8, 12, 16 (n = 7, 9, 11, 13); m = 4: -2, 2, 6, 10; m = 5: -10, -6,
-2, 2; m = 6: -20, -16, -12, -8. So m = 4 at n = 9 has the 2-dimensional family found in NOTES; m = 5 needs
n >= 13. Points on that Fano variety are again high degree (Schubert 344,925 at n = 9 [Cited NOTES]).

Real requirements by stage [Proof unless noted]: quadric stage s >= 1; isotropic line stage s >= 2 (r >= 1)
or s >= 3; odd-degree stage always has a real root; even-degree stage needs a sign change, which lines fail to
give at (k, s) = (4, 2) [Cited], so conics are the first curves that work, at the price of the 24-solution
system.

## 5. (b), (c): odd shape and overshoot

Odd shape. Real: free (R2). Degrees (2m-1)!!. The full odd chi needs m = (n-1)/2 and a drawable Q of degree
(n-1)/2, so the exact engine exists only for n <= 9 (P) or 13 (Q); beyond, overshoot. Points must lie off the
pairing P^{m-1}s (A_n filter); Lemma A gives generic points automatically, but only when (2m-1)!! < n.

Overshoot (N2). W_O: 1, tau, tau^3, ..., tau^13 dependent in K. dim 6, codim n-7, degree (Porteous)
h_{n-7}(0,1,3,5,7,9,11,13) = 1428, 32340, 630042, 11115258, 182959876 for n = 9..13 [Wolfram]. Real points
exist in every totally real K (N2).
- Lemma A: never applies.
- Pure counting says 3 conditions at n = 10 and 4 at n = 11 fit k_W(n) = 4, but W_O is determinantal with
  generators (8x8 minors) of weight ~49, not the weights 1..k of power sums, so that comparison is meaningless
  [Heuristic]. It would become meaningful for a sparse support where chi | N follows from p_1 = ... = p_k = 0
  with small k; none known [Open].
- The dimension of W_O is F-2 = 6 for all n, the codimension n-7 is paid in degree. W_O is unirational as a
  family of polynomials chi (N2), not of elements tau of a fixed K [Open whether the fibre problem has
  structure].

## 6. The square lift and f(g(y))

Algebra [Proof; Wolfram check]. u = alpha - c, K' = K(y), y^2 = u, tau' = a + b y, a, b in K.
chi_{tau'}(t) = Res_x(q(x), (t-a)^2 - b^2 (x-c)); p_j(tau') = 2 Tr_K( sum_{i even} C(j,i) a^{j-i} b^i u^{i/2} )
(verified against roots of chi for a sextic, j = 1..4).

Realness. With a Mobius map (free in F) putting the target alone above c: g = f(y^2+c) has 2 real roots,
s' = n-1 [Wolfram: sextic, c = 4: 2 real of 12]. Without it, m = min(#roots above, #below the target) via
y^2 = c - alpha. With m real roots above c: r' = 2m, s' = n-m. Then k_R(s') = 2s'+1 >= n'-1. For
g = f(y^m + c), m odd: r' = r, s' = (mn - r)/2, degree mn, no real obstruction either.

Shape of a good tau'.
- b = 0: chi is a square; tau' not primitive. Not an option at all, so "avoid being even" is automatic.
- a = 0: conjugation y -> -y is a rational involution, all odd p_j vanish identically, chi(t) = chi_v(t^2)
  with v = b^2 u; p_{2j}(tau') = 2 p_j(v). v has the sign pattern of u, so p_1(v) = 0 is attainable even for
  totally real f, but p_2(v) = sum v_i^2 > 0 when s = 0, so p_4(tau') > 0: the realness of the base field comes
  back [Proof]. Group: chi_v(t^2) has Galois group inside C_2 wr S_n: A_n filter untouched.
- mixed: honest degree-2n Tschirnhaus element, only a Z/2 grading of the conditions.

Cost. Ambient n' = 2n, auxiliary bound n. A sparse engine needs 2n-5 conditions, overshoot (F = 8) 2n-7.
Lemma A in K' needs k'! < n, unchanged by doubling the ambient dimension. Wolfson-type schemes have
auxiliary degrees depending on k alone (27, 12, 288, ...) and need ambient room F(k+1) (9, 41, 121, ...);
with n' = 2n the room is there only for k' <= 4, but k' ~ 2n-5. So the lift cannot reduce the condition count
for sparse or overshoot engines [Proof for Lemma A, Heuristic for Wolfson-type schemes]. What the larger room
does give: isotropic spaces of dimension up to s' = n-1 and a rich real cascade; useful only for engines needing
few conditions but many nonreal pairs (e.g. the y^9 engine with s >= 2 at lifted degree 9, which would need
n = 4.5).

## 7. Capacity against need; regimes

Need: c_P(n) = n-5; c_Q(n) = n-7 (F = 8, overshoot); towers: n - (4+3j) for depth j (G2).
Capacity: k_R(s) = 2s+1; k_A(n); k_W(n) [Cited]; k_line(n,s) (lines: s >= 2, n >= k(k-1)/2 + 2).

| n | k_A | k_W | k_R at s = floor(n/2) | c_P | c_Q |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 9 | 3 | 4 | 9 | 4 | 2 |
| 10 | 3 | 4 | 11 | 5 | 3 |
| 11 | 3 | 4 | 11 | 6 | 4 |
| 13 | 3 | 4 | 13 | 8 | 6 |
| 25 | 4 | 4 | 25 | 20 | 18 |
| 41 | 4 | 5 | 41 | 36 | 34 |
| 121 | 5 | 6 | 121 | 116 | 114 |

Regimes.
1. Large s removes the real obstruction but not the cost; capacity is ~log n/log log n for every s. Large s
   suffices only where c_P(n) <= k_W(n), i.e. n <= 9, with the real methods needing s >= 3 (THREE-PAIRS).
2. Square lift: no gain (Section 6).
3. Special structure: generic S_n has none; hand-made structure (the lift's involution) gives pairings that
   leave the A_n filter intact, with group C_2 wr S_n.
4. Overshoot towers: depth j has f = 4+3j parameters at degree (j+2)2^j (G2), so with j >= (n-4)/3 no
   condition is needed at all and the count is met for every n. But then "chi | N_theta" is an n-equation,
   n-unknown system with no visible structure (cf. the S_20, S_107 computations in NOTES); the count
   suffices and the cost is uncontrolled. This is the only regime where the number of conditions suffices for
   all n; it belongs to H1.

Bookkeeping law [Proof as an identity, route-scoped]: an engine with f free parameters leaves n - f
conditions; Lemma A / Wolfson-type chains pay c(n) ~ log n of them cheaply; so f >= n - c(n) is needed, i.e.
f = n - O(log n), while G2's catalogue gives f ~ 3 log_2 D. An all-n route in this framework needs either
degree exponential in n (overshoot towers) or a family with linear parameter growth.

## 8. Leads (all [Open])

1. Real Wolfson, k = 5 at n >= 41: real planes on the quadric-cubic variety, needing s >= 3; real Hilbert
   27-lines step (Schlafli: 3, 7, 15 or 27 real lines).
2. Real points of the Fano variety of lines on O_{m-1}, m = 4, n = 9, 11 via conic bundles.
3. Rational-curve version of Lemma A (degree-e curve, one equation of degree k e < n) and unirational
   parametrizations of V_k, O_m over F.
4. Overshoot supports whose divisibility follows from few power sums.
5. Engines even in t combined with the lift's pure-odd subspace (only p_j(v) remain; no easier by count).

## Other things noticed

- Lemma A is the real Brauer bound, so Wolfson's F(r) < B(r) measures what linear-space methods gain over
  slicing. At n = 9, k = 4 the gain is from degree 24 down to steps of degree <= 6.
- In the totally real case everything beyond p_1 in the power-sum chain is blocked; every s = 0 route must
  use odd shapes or non-power-sum conditions. This is why overshoot loci are not complete intersections of
  power sums.
- Theorem R2 restates N4's gap theorem in the cost-of-zero-blocks form; N4's 55 supports are exactly the
  cost-0 ones.
- The lift supplies the one genuine Galois-rational pairing found so far (y -> -y). Worth a look for even
  engines (even exponent power law against an even-Q graph), though the group stays C_2 wr S_n.
- Citations (Wolfson, Sutherland, Brauer, Predonzan, Kollar) come from G1 or memory and were not re-opened.
