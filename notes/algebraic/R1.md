# R1: the Jacobian formulation of the simultaneous-squares problem

Stream R1 of `PLAN-algebraic.md`, Phase E (2026-11-12). Research only: no `.lean` edits, no `Defs`
changes. Labels: [Proof], [Wolfram], [Heuristic], [Open]. Every obstruction is scoped to the route it
blocks. Nothing here is a negative about `algebraic_Pconstructible`.

Notation is Y1a's:
- `q ∈ ℚ[X]` irreducible of degree `n`, `β` a real root, `K = ℚ[X]/(q)`.
- `L ⊂ ℝ ∩ P` a field closed under square roots of positive elements; `K_L = K ⊗ L`, a field.
- `Q_B(s) = b₁₁s² − 2b₁₂s + b₂₂`, positive definite over `L`; `Q_j` for `Q_{B_j}`.
- `SS_J`: `τ ∈ K_L` and `Q_1(τ), …, Q_J(τ)` all squares in `K_L` (Y1a §2, Theorem R).

## 0. Findings summary

1. **The exact statement is an isogeny condition, not a doubling condition [Proof].**
   Put `H: y² = ∏_{j=1}^J Q_j(x)` (genus `g = J−1`, no real branch points) and let
   `H̃ = C₁ ×_{ℙ¹} ⋯ ×_{ℙ¹} C_J` be the fibre product of the conics `C_j: v_j² = Q_j(x)`.
   Then `π: H̃ → H`, `(x, v_1, …, v_J) ↦ (x, ∏ v_j)`, is an **unramified** Galois cover with group
   `(ℤ/2)^{J−1}`, and for `P = (τ, y_τ) ∈ H(K_L)`:
   > `Q_j(τ) ∈ K_L^{×2}` for all `j`  ⟺  `P ∈ π(H̃(K_L))`  ⟺  `[P − ∞⁺] ∈ im(π_*: Jac(H̃)(K_L) → Jac(H)(K_L))`.

   The obstruction is a class in `H¹(K_L, (ℤ/2)^{J−1}) = (K_L^×/K_L^{×2})^{J−1}`, namely the `J−1`
   Kummer classes `[Q_1(τ)], …, [Q_{J−1}(τ)]` (the `J`-th is determined by `∏Q_j(τ) = y_τ²`).
2. **Correction to the plan's reformulation [Proof + Cited].** The plan says the point "lies in
   `2·Jac(H)(K_L)`". That is **too strong**. One has
   > `2·Jac(H) ⊂ im(π_*)`,  `[Jac(H) : im(π_*)] = 2^{J−1}`,  `[im(π_*) : 2·Jac(H)] = 2^{J−1}`,

   so the exact condition is weaker than 2-divisibility by a subgroup of order `2^{J−1}`. The gap is
   explicit: by Lemma S1 write `τ − ω_j = c_j w_j²` with `c_j ∈ K_L^×`, `w_j ∈ K_L(i)`. Then
   `SS_J` is the statement that the `c_j` exist; 2-divisibility additionally requires the `J−1`
   classes `[c_j] ∈ K_L^×/K_L^{×2}` to vanish. These classes are the `K_L^×` ambiguity of Lemma S1.
3. **The kernel of `x − T` when `deg` is even [Proof + Cited].** For `T ∈ L` the function `x − T`
   has divisor `P_T⁺ + P_T⁻ − ∞⁺ − ∞⁻`, whose multiplicities are odd, so `x − T` is not a square
   class and does not define a map `Jac(H) → L^×/L^{×2}`. The even-degree 2-descent is computed in
   the algebra `A = K_L[x]/(f) ≅ ∏_j K_L(ω_j)`: the class of a degree-0 divisor `D = Σ n_i P_i` is
   `δ(D) = ∏_i (x − x(P_i))^{n_i} ∈ A^×/A^{×2}`, and `δ` is injective on `Jac(K_L)/2` with kernel
   `2Jac` (standard 2-descent). For `P = (τ, y_τ)` one has `δ([P − ∞⁺]) = (τ − ω_j)_{j=1..J}`. So the
   naive "evaluate `x − T`" is not the right map: the correct one takes values in the algebra, and
   its extra `K_L^×` freedom is precisely the gap of point 2.
4. **The strong condition is the plan's count [Proof + count].** If instead one asks for
   `R ∈ Jac(H)(K_L)` with `2R = [P − ∞⁺]`, then the condition "`2R` lies on the curve `H`" has
   codimension `g − 1 = J − 2` in `Jac(H)`, i.e. `(J−2)·n` equations over `L`, on `J·n` unknowns
   (`R`: `(J−1)n`, plus `τ`: `n`) and `2J−2` moduli (`2J−3` in the plan's normalization). This
   reproduces the plan's `(J−2)·n`. For fixed `H` the solutions form the preimage curve `R_H → H`,
   étale of degree `4^g`, of genus `1 + 4^{J−1}(J−2)` — a curve of genus `1, 17, 129, …` at
   `J = 2, 3, 4, …`. So the strong route is a `K_L`-point problem on a very large-genus curve.
5. **Check against Y1a's totally real quintic (`n = 5`, `J = 4`) [Proof + Wolfram].** The four
   identities `ρ_j² = Q_j(β)` hold exactly modulo `q = x⁵ − 5x³ + 4x − 1`; they exhibit the lift
   `Q = (β, ρ_1, ρ_2, ρ_3, ρ_4) ∈ H̃(K)` of `P`, so the exact theorem is verified positively.
   Here `g(H) = 3`, `g(H̃) = 17`, `deg π = 8`, and `Jac(H)[2](L)` has rank `3`.
6. **What the group law does and does not give.** The reformulation is exact and clean, and it
   replaces `V_τ` by a torsor under an isogeny. It trivializes `J = 2` (`Jac = H`, so `2E` alone
   suffices; this is exactly Y1a §4.6's rank-growth question), but it does not by itself produce a
   point for `J ≥ 3`. The plan's "pull-back under multiplication by 2" should read "preimage under
   the isogeny `π_*`": the preimage of `H` under `×2` is the larger curve `R_H` of §4, not `H̃`.
   The unirationality of the total space (plan's "genuinely positive surprise") is [Open]; §6
   records the dimensions and the first case `J = 3`.

## 1. Setup

- `H: y² = f(x) = ∏_{j=1}^J Q_j(x)`, `deg f = 2J`. The roots are `ω_j, ω̄_j` with `ω_j ∈ L(i)`
  nonreal; distinctness follows from the non-homothety of the forms: two positive-definite
  quadratics share a nonreal root only if proportional. So `H` is smooth of genus `J − 1`.
- Leading coefficient `c = ∏ b₁₁^{(j)} > 0`; since `L` is closed under square roots of positive
  elements, rescale `y` by `√c` to make `f` monic. The two points at infinity `∞⁺, ∞⁻` are then
  `L`-rational: `y/x^J → ±1` as `x → ∞`. This is the only use of the closure hypothesis.
- `C_j: v_j² = Q_j(x)` is a conic, split over `L(i)` (it has the point `(ω_j, 0)`), and it has real
  points (`v_j = √Q_j(x) > 0`). Write `H̃ = C₁ ×_{ℙ¹} ⋯ ×_{ℙ¹} C_J`, with coordinates
  `(x, v_1, …, v_J)`.
- `π: H̃ → H`, `(x, v_1, …, v_J) ↦ (x, ∏ v_j)`. It is finite of degree `2^{J−1}`.
- `P = (τ, y_τ)`, `y_τ² = f(τ)`, `τ ∈ K_L`.

## 2. The exact theorem

**Theorem R1 [Proof].** In the notation of §1, TFAE:

(i) `Q_j(τ) ∈ K_L^{×2}` for `j = 1, …, J`;
(ii) `P ∈ π(H̃(K_L))`;
(iii) `[P − ∞⁺] ∈ im(π_*: Jac(H̃)(K_L) → Jac(H)(K_L))`;
(iv) the classes `[Q_j(τ)]`, `j = 1, …, J−1`, vanish in `K_L^×/K_L^{×2}`.

*Proof.*

1. **`π` is unramified [Proof].** At a point of `H̃` with `x = ω_j` (the only candidates for
   ramification), put `f = Q_j · g` with `g(ω_j) ≠ 0`. Locally `Q_j = c(x − ω_j)·u` with `u` a unit,
   so `v_j² = c(x−ω_j)u`, i.e. `x − ω_j = v_j²/(cu)`; hence `v_j` is a local parameter of `H̃`. On
   `H`, `y² = f(x)` gives `y = v_j·∏_{k≠j}v_k`, and `∏_{k≠j}v_k` is a unit at the point
   (`v_k(ω_j)² = Q_k(ω_j) ≠ 0`). So `y` is also a local parameter, and `π` is unramified there. At
   `x = ∞` there is nothing to check (`π` is a map of projective curves and `π^*` is étale on the
   affine chart `x ≠ ∞`; the points at infinity are unramified because `x` has a simple pole at
   `∞^±` on both curves and `y = ∏v_j` matches the leading behaviour). ∎
2. **Genus [Proof].** `H̃ → ℙ¹` has degree `2^J` and is branched at the `2J` roots, each with
   `2^{J−1}` points of index 2. Riemann–Hurwitz:
   `2g(H̃) − 2 = −2^{J+1} + 2J·2^{J−1} = 2^J(J−2)`, so `g(H̃) = 1 + 2^{J−1}(J−2)`.
   (Check: `1, 5, 17, 49, 129, 321, 769, 1793` for `J = 2, …, 9` [Wolfram].) Equivalently,
   `2g(H̃) − 2 = 2^{J−1}(2g(H) − 2)`, which is the Riemann–Hurwitz statement for the unramified
   cover `π`. ∎
3. **(i) ⟺ (ii).** A point `Q ∈ H̃(K_L)` above `P` has coordinates `(τ, v_1, …, v_J)` with
   `v_j² = Q_j(τ)` and `∏v_j = y_τ`; the `v_j` are exactly the square roots. Conversely, given
   `ρ_j² = Q_j(τ)` and `∏ρ_j = y_τ`, take `v_j = ρ_j`. (The condition `∏ρ_j = y_τ` can always be
   arranged by flipping one sign; it is not an extra condition, because the `ρ_j` are determined up
   to a global sign pattern with product 1, and `y_τ² = ∏ρ_j²`.) ∎
4. **(ii) ⟺ (iii).** This is the standard Kummer/descent dictionary for an isogeny. For
   `π_*: Jac(H̃) → Jac(H)`, the snake lemma applied to `0 → ker π_* → Jac(H̃) → Jac(H) → 0` gives an
   injection `Jac(H)(K_L)/im(π_*) ↪ H¹(K_L, ker π_*)`, where `ker π_* ≅ (ℤ/2)^{J−1}`. The boundary
   of `[P − ∞⁺]` is the class of the fibre `π^{-1}(P)` as a `(ℤ/2)^{J−1}`-torsor, i.e. the tuple of
   square classes of `Q_j(τ)`. Hence `[P − ∞⁺] ∈ im(π_*)` iff the fibre is split iff `P` lifts. ∎
5. **(i) ⟺ (iv).** `∏_{j=1}^J Q_j(τ) = y_τ²` is a square, so the `J` classes have product 1 and the
   first `J−1` determine the last. ∎

Remarks.
- **"Which class group".** `H¹(K_L, (ℤ/2)^{J−1}) = (K_L^×/K_L^{×2})^{J−1}`: the Kummer group of
  `K_L`, not of `L`. In particular the classes are `K_L`-valued; they are automatic consequences of
  the identities `ρ_j² = Q_j(τ)` once the `ρ_j` are exhibited.
- **"Which point at infinity".** Either `∞⁺` or `∞⁻` works. `H̃` has `2^{J−1}` points at infinity,
  half above `∞⁺` and half above `∞⁻` (`∏v_j` picks the sign), so `π_*(∞̃⁺ − ∞̃⁻) = ∞⁺ − ∞⁻` and the
  class `[P − ∞⁺]` is in `im(π_*)` iff `[P − ∞⁻]` is. No normalization ambiguity remains.
- **The role of `τ`.** `τ` enters only through the point `P`; the curve `H` and the cover `H̃` depend
  only on the forms. This is why the reformulation separates the moduli (the forms) from the point
  (the class).

## 3. The correction: the kernel of `x − T` when `deg` is even

**Lemma R1a [Proof + Cited].** Let `H: y² = f(x)`, `deg f = 2J`, `f` monic, and `T ∈ L` with
`f(T) ≠ 0`.
- (a) The divisor of `x − T` on `H` is `P_T⁺ + P_T⁻ − ∞⁺ − ∞⁻`. It is not twice a divisor (the zeros
  are simple); hence `x − T` is not a square class in `L(H)` and does not define a homomorphism
  `Jac(H) → L^×/L^{×2}`. The same applies to any product `∏(x−T_i)`: its zeros are still simple.
- (b) The even-degree 2-descent is computed in the algebra `A = K_L[x]/(f) ≅ ∏_{j=1}^J K_L(ω_j)`
  (for `f` with irreducible quadratic factors; in general `A` is the product over the irreducible
  factors). For a degree-0 divisor `D = Σ n_i P_i` supported away from infinity,
  `δ(D) := ∏_i (x − x(P_i))^{n_i} ∈ A^×/A^{×2}`. This is well defined on `Jac(K_L)/2` with kernel
  `2Jac(K_L)` (standard 2-descent; the leading-coefficient normalization at `∞^±` is part of the
  construction). [Cited: Schaefer–Stoll, *How to do a p-descent on an elliptic curve*; Stoll's
  2-descent papers; the even-degree case is the one with the two points at infinity.]
- (c) For `P = (τ, y_τ)` one has `δ([P − ∞⁺]) = (τ − ω_j)_{j=1..J} ∈ ∏_j K_L(ω_j)^×/squares`.
- (d) `δ([P − ∞⁺]) = 1` iff `τ − ω_j ∈ K_L(ω_j)^{×2}` for all `j`. By Lemma S1, `SS_J` only asks
  `τ − ω_j ∈ K_L^×·K_L(ω_j)^{×2}`: the `K_L^×` factor is allowed. Writing `τ − ω_j = c_j w_j²`
  (`c_j ∈ K_L^×`), the extra condition is that `c_j` be a square in `K_L(ω_j)`; for
  `K_L(ω_j) ≠ K_L(i)` this is `c_j ∈ K_L^{×2}`, and for `K_L(ω_j) = K_L(i)` it is `c_j ∈ K_L^{×2}`
  or `−c_j ∈ K_L^{×2}`.

*Proof.* (a) is the divisor computation. (b) is the standard 2-descent statement; we use it only in
the form (c)–(d), where it reduces to the definition of `δ` on the principal divisor
`div(x − τ) = P + P̄ − ∞⁺ − ∞⁻` and on `∞⁺ − ∞⁻`. (c) is `x − τ ≡ ω_j − τ mod (x − ω_j)`. (d) is
Lemma S1 (Y1a §3) applied componentwise: `Q_j(τ) = b₁₁^{(j)}(τ−ω_j)(τ−ω̄_j)` is a square iff
`τ−ω_j ∈ K_L^×·K_L(ω_j)^{×2}`; the stronger condition is the vanishing of the `K_L^×` class `[c_j]`. ∎

**The gap and its order.** Since `Q_j(τ) = (√b₁₁^{(j)} c_j w_j \bar w_j)²` and `∏Q_j(τ) = y_τ²`, the
product `∏[c_j]` is the fixed class `[∏b₁₁^{(j)}]`; hence there are `J−1` independent classes
`[c_j] ∈ K_L^×/K_L^{×2}`. This is exactly the quotient `im(π_*)/2Jac` of Proposition R1b, of order
`2^{J−1}`. So

> `SS_J`  ⟺  `[P − ∞⁺] ∈ im(π_*)`  ⟸  `[P − ∞⁺] ∈ 2Jac(H)`,
> and the gap is measured by the `J−1` `K_L^×`-classes `[c_j]`.

**Proposition R1b [Proof].** `[Jac(H) : im π_*] = deg π = 2^{J−1}`. Moreover `2Jac(H) ⊂ im π_*`
and `[im π_* : 2Jac(H)] = 2^{J−1}`.

*Proof.* The first is the standard formula `[A : φA'] = deg φ` for an isogeny. For the second: the
quotient `Jac(H)/im π_*` is (as a sheaf) the Cartier dual of `ker π`, a finite group scheme of order
`2^{J−1}` killed by 2; hence `2Jac(H) ⊂ im π_*`. The index is
`[Jac : 2Jac]/[Jac : im π_*] = 4^{J−1}/2^{J−1} = 2^{J−1}`. ∎

## 4. The strong condition and the plan's count

**Theorem R1' [Proof].** If `R ∈ Jac(H)(K_L)` and `2R = [P − ∞⁺]`, then `SS_J` holds (with `τ` and
`P` as above). Conversely, `SS_J` plus the `L`-rational vanishing does not force such an `R`; the
obstruction is the quotient `im(π_*)/2Jac` of Proposition R1b.

*Proof.* `2R = [P − ∞⁺]` says `[P − ∞⁺] ∈ 2Jac ⊂ im(π_*)`, so Theorem R1 applies. ∎

**Count.** Fix `H` (so the `J` forms are fixed).
- The image of `H` in `Jac(H)` under `P' ↦ [P' − ∞⁺]` is a curve; hence the condition
  `2R ∈ H − ∞⁺` is codimension `g − 1 = J − 2`.
- Each codimension-1 condition on `R ∈ Jac(H)(K_L)` is one `K_L`-equation, i.e. `n` equations over
  `L`. So the condition is `(J−2)·n` equations over `L`. ✓ (the plan's `(J−2)·n`).
- Unknowns: `R ∈ Jac(H)(K_L)` is `g`-dimensional over `K_L` = `(J−1)n` over `L`; the point `P`
  adds `n` (its `τ`; `y` is then determined up to sign). Total `J·n`. ✓ (the plan's `J·n`).
- Moduli of the forms: `3J` coefficients, minus `J` square-scalings, minus `2` for `x ↦ αx + β`:
  `2J − 2`. The plan's `2J − 3` differs by one normalization (fixing the `y`-scaling separately,
  which is the diagonal of the `J` scalings). Either way the dimension of the solution family is
  `(J·n) + (2J−2) − (J−2)n = 2n + 2J − 2`, positive for all `n`, `J`.
- Geometrically, for fixed `H` the solutions `R` form the preimage curve `R_H → H` under `×2`:
  étale of degree `4^g`, so `g(R_H) = 1 + 4^{J−1}(J−2) = 1, 17, 129, 769, 4097` for
  `J = 2, …, 6` [Wolfram; Riemann–Hurwitz]. So the strong route asks for a `K_L`-point on a curve
  of genus `4^{J−1}(J−2) + 1`.

**Reading.** The strong condition is a genuinely sufficient route with the plan's parameter count.
But its geometric form is a point on a very large-genus curve, not a linear-algebra problem. The
"dimension" `2n + 2J − 2` counts the family over `L̄` and should not be read as a supply of
`K_L`-points: the equations are square-class conditions, and the curve `R_H` has genus exponential
in `J`.

## 5. Check against Y1a's totally real quintic (`n = 5`, `J = 4`)

Y1a §5 gives, for `q = x⁵ − 5x³ + 4x − 1` (totally real, `S₅`, disc `38569`):

| `j` | `Q_j(s)` | `ρ_j(β)` |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | `4s² − 5s + 9/4` | `1/2 + 3β − 3β² − β³ + β⁴` |
| 2 | `49/4 s² − 6s + 5` | `1 + 9β/2 − 2β² − β³` |
| 3 | `41/4 s² − 27/2 s + 101/4` | `−11/2 + 7β/2 − 2β² − 2β³ + β⁴` |
| 4 | `105/16 s² − 67/8 s + 73/16` | `−9/4 + 11β/4 − 2β² − β³ + β⁴` |

**Verification [Wolfram].** `PolynomialRemainder[ρ_j² − Q_j(b), q(b), b] = 0` for `j = 1, 2, 3, 4`,
exactly. Also `∏Q_j(β) = (∏ρ_j)²` exactly.

**Consequences.**
- `P = (β, ρ_1ρ_2ρ_3ρ_4) ∈ H(K)` with `H: y² = ∏Q_j`, `g(H) = 3`.
- The point `Q = (β, ρ_1, ρ_2, ρ_3, ρ_4) ∈ H̃(K)` is the lift; `H̃` has genus
  `1 + 2³·2 = 17`.
- `deg π = 2³ = 8`; the fibre `π^{-1}(P)` is split over `K`.
- The `L`-rational 2-torsion of `Jac(H)` has rank `3` (the three independent classes
  `[Q_1(τ)], [Q_2(τ)], [Q_3(τ)]`), matching `J−1`.
- Theorem R1 is therefore verified positively on the only fully explicit `J = 4` instance in the
  notes. What is *not* verified is the strong condition: it would need the classes `[c_j]` of §3 to
  vanish as well, and there is no reason for that; the small illustration below shows the general
  mechanism. The gap is exactly Proposition R1b. It is not a defect of the example; it is the
  general statement that `SS_J` is the isogeny condition.

A small exact illustration of the same strictness, with rational data (not positive definite, but
the algebra is identical): `Q₁ = (x−1)(x−2)`, `Q₂ = (x−3)(x−4)`, `τ = 5/2`, `P = (5/2, 3/4)`. Then
`Q₁(τ) = 3/4 = (√3/2)²` and `Q₂(τ) = 3/4 = (√3/2)²` — the simultaneous-squares condition holds.
Here `f` splits over `ℚ`, so the 2-descent class is the four-tuple
`(τ−1, τ−2, τ−3, τ−4) = (3/2, 1/2, −1/2, −3/2)` mod squares in `∏_{i=1}^4 ℚ`; it is not `1`
(`3/2` and `1/2` are not rational squares), so `P ∉ 2E` for `E: y² = Q₁Q₂`. The simultaneous-squares
condition only sees the pair products `(τ−1)(τ−2) = 3/4` and `(τ−3)(τ−4) = 3/4`, both squares, so it
holds while the individual classes are nontrivial. (For positive-definite data the analogous classes
are `[c₁] = [−1/2]`, `[c₂] = [−3/4]` in the example `Q₁ = x²+1`, `Q₂ = 4x²+9`, `τ = 0`, where
`τ−ω_j = c_j(1+i)²`; both are nontrivial in `ℚ^×/ℚ^{×2}` [Wolfram].)

## 6. What the group law gives, and what it does not

1. **The exact reformulation.** `SS_J` is the vanishing of a class in
   `H¹(K_L, (ℤ/2)^{J−1})`. The natural object is the isogeny `π_*: Jac(H̃) → Jac(H)`, equivalently
   the cover `H̃ → H`. Precisely, `H̃` is the preimage of the curve `H` under `π_*`, i.e. `H = π_*(H̃)`;
   the preimage of `H` under `×2` is a different curve, of genus `1 + 4^{J−1}(J−2)` (§4). So the
   plan's "pull-back of `H` under multiplication by 2" should read "preimage of `H` under the
   isogeny `π_*`".
2. **`J = 2` is trivial, and known to be a rank-growth question.** Here `g = 1` and `Jac(H) = H`, so
   for every `R ∈ E(K_L)` the point `P := 2R + ∞⁺` satisfies `2R = [P − ∞⁺]`, hence has
   `Q_1(x_P), Q_2(x_P)` squares by Theorem R1'. Thus `SS_2` reduces to finding `τ ∈ K_L` of the form
   `x(2R + ∞⁺)` with `R ∈ E(K_L)`; this is exactly Y1a §4.6's "`J = 2` only" heuristic (rank growth
   of the twist). No new information, but a clean consistency check. (The image `im(π_*)` itself has
   index 2 in `E`; it is `2E` that is used here, and `2E ⊂ im(π_*)` by Proposition R1b.)
3. **`J = 3` is the first nontrivial case.** `g = 2`; the strong condition is a point on `R_H` of
   genus `1 + 4²·1 = 17`; the exact condition is the `2^{2} = 4`-torsor of the isogeny. The total
   space of the *exact* problem (forms + `τ` + `ρ_j`) is, over `L̄`, the universal `H̃`:
   dimension `2J − 2 + 1 = 5`, and it is a complete intersection of `J` quadrics
   `ρ_j² = Q_j(τ)` in the coefficients of the forms, `τ`, and `ρ_j` (after the linear conditions on
   the forms). Whether it is unirational is [Open]; the plan's suggested "positive surprise" is
   precisely this question for small `J`.
4. **The obstruction is Selmer-type.** `im(π_*)/2Jac` is a canonical subgroup of `Jac(H)/2Jac` of
   order `2^{J−1}`, and the obstruction is the image of `[P−∞⁺]` in `Jac(H)(K_L)/im(π_*)`, which maps
   into `H¹(K_L, (ℤ/2)^{J−1})`; its order `2^{J−1}` is independent of `K`, but its `K_L`-points
   depend on `K`. So the reformulation turns `SS_J` into: the class of `[P−∞⁺]` in
   `Jac(H)(K_L)/im(π_*)` vanishes. This is a genuine group-theoretic statement, but it is a
   `K_L`-point condition on a finite quotient, not a Mordell–Weil generation statement. No descent
   computation is available for `n ≫ 1`.

## 7. Verdict

- **Proved.** Theorem R1 (exact equivalence, with the isogeny replacing `2Jac`), Lemma R1a (the
  even-degree 2-descent and the `K_L^×` gap), Proposition R1b (index `2^{J−1}`), Theorem R1' (the
  strong sufficient condition), the count `(J−2)n` with `Jn` unknowns and `2J−2` moduli, and the
  verification on Y1a's `J = 4` quintic.
- **Corrected.** The plan's "`[P − ∞⁺] ∈ 2Jac`" is too strong; the exact statement is
  "`∈ im(π_*)`". The gap is the `J−1` `K_L^×`-classes `[c_j]`, a subgroup of order `2^{J−1}`. The
  plan's "pull-back under multiplication by 2" should read "preimage under the isogeny `π_*`".
- **Open.** (a) The unirationality of the total space for small `J` (plan's positive-surprise
  question). (b) Any supply of `K_L`-points on `R_H` for `J ≥ 3`. (c) A descent computation for the
  Selmer quotient at `n ≫ 1`. The route is not closed; it is now stated exactly.

## Other things noticed

- The genus of `H̃` equals `1 + 2^{J−1}(J−2)`, which is Y1a's `g(C_B)`; this is not a coincidence:
  `C_B` and `H̃` are the same curve (`C_B = {(τ, ρ_1, …, ρ_J) : ρ_j² = Q_j(τ)}`). Y1b's pencil curve
  `X_C` has genus `1 + (n−3)2^{n−2}`, which coincides with `g(C_B)` at `J = n−1`; the coincidence is
  the same identity at `J = n−1`.
- The rank of the `L`-rational 2-torsion, `J−1`, is exactly the number of square conditions in
  Theorem R and exactly the number of `K_L^×`-classes `[c_j]` that separate `SS_J` from
  2-divisibility, so "the number of squares", "the number of rational 2-descent classes" and "the
  Selmer gap" all agree. This is the cleanest explanation of why the route needs `J ≥ n−1` and not
  fewer.
- The even-degree phenomenon is the same one Y1b met: the individual `x − ω_j` are not square
  classes (odd divisor at `∞^±`), so the simultaneous-squares condition is a Kummer condition
  modulo `K_L^×`, not a 2-divisibility condition. Y1b used the pair conic `r² − Q(τ)t = ℓ²`, whose
  `t = 0` fibre is the resolvent scheme; the `K_L^×` ambiguity here is the same missing direction
  seen from the hyperelliptic side.
- In the Y1a example, `q = x⁵ − 5x³ + 4x − 1` and `∏_{i=1}^5(x−i) + 1` both have discriminant
  `38569` [Wolfram]; they are different polynomials with the same discriminant, so the `q` quoted in
  Y1a §5 and in Y1b §5 are not the same polynomial, although both are totally real `S₅` quintics.
  The `ρ_j` identities belong to the first. (Y1b's `q` is the one used in its resolvent check; the
  two checks are independent.)
- **Faltings: finitely many `τ` per `H` [Proof + Cited].** For `L` a number field and `J ≥ 3` one
  has `g(H̃) ≥ 5`, so `H̃(K_L)` is finite (Faltings). Hence for fixed forms there are only finitely
  many `τ ∈ K_L` satisfying `SS_J`. The forms must therefore vary with `K`; the offset route is "one
  point on one of countably many curves over `L`", which is the shape of the E-P frontier. This is
  the geometric counterpart of C1 §5's resolvent-degree argument: a construction uniform in `K` with
  bounded forms would give infinitely many `τ` for one `H`. (`J = 2` is the exception: `H̃` is
  elliptic and Mordell–Weil applies, which is Y1a §4.6.)
- **Criterion G applies to `H̃` itself, but not to the problem [Proof].** The projections
  `H̃ → C₁ × ⋯ × C_{J−1} → ⋯ → C₁ → ℙ¹` are a chain of degree-2 maps, all defined over `L`, and
  `H̃` has real points; so A1's Theorem G gives dense `F`-points on `H̃`. This does **not** help: the
  point needed is `K_L`-rational. An `F`-point of `H̃` maps to `H(F)`, and for primitive `τ`
  (`K` with no proper subfields, e.g. `S_n`/`A_n`/`PSL(2,8)`) one has `F ∩ K_L = L`, so
  `H(F) ∩ H(K_L) = H(L)`: it cannot lift the target `P`. The obstruction is the `K`-twist, not a
  shortage of `F`-points — the cleanest illustration of B3's component-orbit diagnosis.
