# H1: incidence cost for engine families, general n

Stream H1 of `pptc/PLAN-algebraic.md`. Labels: [Proof], [Wolfram], [Heuristic], [Open]. Every obstruction is scoped
to the route it blocks; nothing here concludes a negative about the target. Backend: Wolfram for every number
(no Python). Exact-arithmetic checks are stated with their ranges.

## Findings summary

1. **Structure theorem (twist of a fibre product) [Proof].** Let W be a family of crossing polynomials C_p (D = deg C_p),
   M = dim of its parameter space, and I_K = {(tau, p) : C_p(tau) = 0 in K}. Over C, K tensor C = C^n, so
   I_K is the n-fold fibre product of the "root curve" {(x, p): C_p(x) = 0} over the parameter space, i.e. the variety of
   ordered n-tuples of roots of a common C_p. Its dimension is **M, independent of n** (n equations on n + M
   unknowns). K enters only as a Galois twist (indices permuted by Gal(q) in S_n). An F-point of I_K is exactly a
   p in F^M such that F[x]/(C_p) has a factor algebra isomorphic to K tensor F. Consequently:
   - *Conditions on tau* = n - r, where r = rank of the map p -> monic C_p (including the free shift) = dimension of the
     image locus of good tau. Since r <= M is bounded for every drawable family (G2: M is about 3 log2 D), **the conditions
     grow linearly in n**, with degree growing at least factorially. The dimension count "I is nonempty of positive dimension"
     is true but says nothing: the cost sits in the degree and the arithmetic of the twist.
   - **Rational parameters.** poly_graph coefficients and power-law (a, b) are rational. Then the vector c of
     coefficients is a Q-point; the only freedom left is the field of tau. By the subgroup-index argument
     (A_n filter in incidence language, [Proof]): if Gal(C_c/Q) = S_D with D > n, no chain of index-<n steps reaches
     a subgroup fixing an n-block, because S_D has no subgroup of index < D except A_D. **So c must lie in the
     reducibility locus of the family** (a Hilbert-thin set); generic points are useless for every overshoot route with rational parameters.
2. **Trinomials (power law x^m against a line) are completely resolved [Proof + Wolfram].** The good-tau locus is a cone over a
   curve Gamma_{n,m} in P(K) of
   - degree (m-2)(m-3)...(m-n+1) (at least (n-2)!); for n = 9, m = 9: 5040; the full determinantal (Porteous) degree is
     (m^{n-1}-1)/(m-1) = sum_k S(n,k) (m-2)^{falling k-2}, checked in Wolfram for 44 pairs (n, m) with 3 <= n <= 13;
   - geometric genus (formula in section 2) 47881 for n = m = 9 (table in section 2). All are >= 2 for n >= 4, so by Faltings each number field
     has finitely many points; no line, conic or rational curve exists inside it; and at most 3 real roots, so it serves
     n - 2s <= 3 only. **Cost: huge degree, no rational structure.**
3. **Linear-support families (power law or odd shape times a graph) [Proof + Wolfram].** With F free coefficients and exponent set E:
   the good-tau cone has dimension F - 1, codimension n + 1 - F; **its main component has degree d_E(n)** obtained from the Porteous
   class h_{n-F+1}(E) by removing the block-diagonal components (Stirling inversion, section 3). For the rational sextic with m = n it
   is the classical CI p_1 = ... = p_{n-7} = 0 of degree (n-7)!; overshoot (m > n) buys realness at the price of much larger
   degrees (N2's n = 9 locus: Porteous 1428, but the main component has degree 210).
4. **Realness [Proof, Descartes].** Any linear family with F free coefficients serves a totally real K only if n <= 2F - 2; the odd
   shape t P(t^2) - a reaches n <= 2F - 3 and then needs >= (n-1)/2 conditions. With rational graphs (F <= 8) this stops at **n <= 13**;
   with P-coefficient quartics (F = 6) at **n <= 9**. The gap theorem of N4 does not apply to overshoot (D > n): the true capacity is the sum
   of w(gap) = 1 (odd) / 2 (even) and only needs to be >= n. Real points of the overshoot locus exist for all n <= 13
   (rank 7 verified for n = 9..13, section 4). **Beyond n = 13 (rational) every sparse linear family is blocked for s = 0**; dense nonlinear
   families (Bezier, offsets) have no Descartes limit but have bounded r.
5. **No family with cost below n found.** For all measured families r <= 9 (Bezier x power law: r = 7 for m = 3..8 [Wolfram]), so the conditions are n - r. The
   only places with low-degree structure are the m = n sparse shapes, whose condition set is the complete intersection CI(1, 2, ..., n-F+1):
   lines exist in it iff the expected Fano dimension 2(n-2) - sum (d_i + 1) >= 0, i.e. n <= 12 for F = 8 and n <= 9 for F = 6 [Wolfram]. This
   reproduces exactly the thresholds of G1 (n <= 12 for the sextic engine) as a statement about lines in CI, and gives the first point at which the incidence
   route loses its linear structure: **n = 13 (rational sextic) and n = 10 (P quartic)**.
6. **A reformulation worth recording [Proof].** The good chi form a unirational family S_E of dimension F - 1 (choose the cofactor rho freely, solve a linear system for chi). The problem
   is then "find s in F^{F-1} with F[x]/chi_s isomorphic to K tensor F": a specialization/inverse-Galois question for an (F-1)-parameter family, which cannot be versal for
   n > 2F - 2 (essential dimension ed(S_n) >= n/2, Buhler-Reichstein, cited in G1). This is a scoped statement about versality of the family, not about individual K.

## 1. Setting and notation

- q of degree n, K = Q[X]/(q), tau in K tensor L with L in F_{<n}; chi_tau its characteristic polynomial; Sigma = Gal-closure of K with group G (S_n or A_n for a generic q).
- A family W of crossing polynomials: C_p(x), p in A^M. Good set: S_W = {tau : chi_tau | C_p for some p}. Fibre over tau in the parameters: linear if W is a linear space, nonlinear otherwise.
- **Dimension count [Proof].** I_K has n + M unknowns and n equations (C_p(tau) = 0 in K), so dim I_K = M when the equations are independent (checked in each family by the fibre-product description, which makes independence automatic over C). The image in K has dimension r, the rank of p -> monic C_p including the shift; conditions n - r.
- **Convention.** N2 counts n + 1 - F conditions for a linear family with F coefficients (cone dimension F - 1 without shift); N4 counts 9 - r with the shift included in r. The two agree when the shift is not already in the span. I use "dimension of the good-tau locus in K" and give conditions = n - dim.

## 2. Trinomials: the power law y = x^m against a line (and rational exponents p/q: same with m = p, line exponent q)

Equation: c tau^m = a tau + b in K. By scale and shift the cone of good tau is {tau : 1, tau, tau^m dependent}.

- **Divided-difference form [Proof].** For all triples, h_{m-2}(tau_i, tau_j, tau_k) = 0 (complete homogeneous symmetric polynomial of degree m - 2 in three of the n embeddings). Hence n - 2 equations of degree m - 2 in n unknowns: dimension 2 (affine cone), a curve Gamma_{n,m} in P^{n-1}.
- **Components [Proof].** One component per set partition of the n embeddings (tau_i = tau_j on blocks); those with k blocks are the curves Gamma_{k,m} (k >= 3) or lines (k = 2). Over Q only Galois-stable partitions are rational; for a primitive G only the generic one and the constants survive.
- **Degree of the generic component [Proof sketch + Wolfram check].** Parametrize by the roots of y^m - A y - 1: the cover C~ of the A-line has N = m^{falling n} sheets (S_m monodromy). The hyperplane section has N/(m-1) zeros (poles at A = infinity); the mu_m action (A, tau) -> (zeta^{-1} A, zeta tau) is free away from infinity and acts totally ramified at every place over infinity, so deg Gamma = N/(m(m-1)) = (m-2)(m-3)...(m-n+1). The identity
  sum_{k=2}^n S(n,k) (m-2)^{falling (k-2)} = (m^{n-1} - 1)/(m-1) is exactly the Porteous count of the whole degeneracy locus; Wolfram confirms it for all n in 3..13 and m in {n, n+1, n+3, 2n} (44 cases, all True). This is an independent check of the degree formula and of "all multiplicities are 1".
- **Genus [Proof by Riemann-Hurwitz; checks].** Branch data: m simple branch points A^m = const (each a transposition on the labels), and A = infinity ((m-1)-cycle), then quotient by mu_m with fixed places = all N/(m-1) places over infinity, giving
  2g(Gamma~) - 2 = [ -3N + m(N - Q)/2 + N(m-2)/(m-1) ] / m, with Q = (m-2)^{falling n}.
  Checks: n = 3 gives the smooth plane curves of degree m - 2 (g = 0, 1, 3 for m = 4, 5, 6); n = 4, m = 5 gives degree 6, genus 4 (a canonical curve) [Proof: the formula matches those independent facts].
- **Table [Wolfram].** deg = (m-2)^{falling (n-2)}, genus g:

- **Fibres.** Over tau: linear (a, b). Over (a, b): the roots of the trinomial in K, a finite set of size at most m^{falling n}. Over the invariant A^m: the n-tuples. No fibration with fibres of degree < n visible.
- **Rational structure.** Tangent cone at the constant point [1]: u^2 in span(1, u) forces u in a quadratic subfield, so no branch of primitive tau passes near [1] (second-order computation, [Proof]). Gamma has genus >= 2 for n >= 4, so no rational curve and no lines. [Proof]
- **Realness.** A trinomial has at most 3 real roots (at most 4 as a polynomial in t^2 or with rational exponents). So the family serves n - 2s <= 3 only. Scoped to this route.
- **Profile against n [Wolfram].** conditions n - 3; degree >= (n-2)!; genus > 4.7e4 at n = 9 and growing about factorially. No cost below n.

## 3. Linear-support families: degrees and realness

For a rational (or P) polynomial family N = sum_{e in E} c_e x^e with F = |E| free coefficients, the good-tau cone is
{tau : rank [tau^e]_{e in E} < F}, of dimension F - 1.

- **Porteous class [Proof].** Codimension n + 1 - F, class h_{n+1-F}(E) (complete homogeneous symmetric polynomial in the exponents) as a locus in P(K). This counts every block-diagonal component.
- **Main component [Proof of the decomposition, Wolfram of the numbers].** Components are indexed by set partitions with k >= F - 1 blocks; k = F - 1 gives a linear space (degree 1). So h_{n+1-F}(E) = sum_{k >= F-1} S(n,k) d_E(k), and d_E(n) follows by inversion. Consistency checks: trinomials reproduce (m-2)^{falling (n-2)}; E = {0..F-2, m} with n = F gives d = m - F + 1 (the divided difference h_{m-F+1}, a hypersurface); m = n gives the complete intersection p_1 = ... = p_{n-F+1} = 0 of degree (n+1-F)! (n = 13, E = {0..6, 13}: 720 = 6!; also the odd shape at n = 13: 10395 = 1*3*5*7*9*11, the product of the odd-power-sum degrees). Both are exact matches.
- **Table of d_E(n) (main component degree), n = 9..13 [Wolfram].**

  Porteous totals (including diagonal components), for comparison: odd overshoot n = 9: 1428 (N2's number), n = 10..13: 32340, 630042, 11115258, 182959876. So N2's 1428 is a count with excess; the main component has degree 210.
- **Reading.** At m = n all these loci are complete intersections of degrees 1, 2, ..., n+1-F (the census sparse shapes). Degrees stay smallest there. Overshoot shifts to larger degrees but is the only way to realness.
- **Fibre structure [Proof].** Over tau the parameters are linear (kernel of an n x F matrix). Over the cofactor rho (degree D - n, D - n free coefficients) the chi are an affine linear space of dimension n + F - 1 - D (N2's "4 + 3" at n = 9): so the good chi form a unirational variety of dimension F - 1. The cost is the Tschirnhaus-incidence of that variety with the twisted tau-variety (degree n! cover): finding s with F[x]/chi_s isomorphic to K tensor F.
- **Lines in the m = n loci [Wolfram].** For CI(1, 2, ..., k) in P^{n-1} with k = n + 1 - F, the expected dimension of the Fano scheme of lines is 2(n-2) - sum (d_i + 1):
  - F = 8 (rational sextic): n = 9..14: 9, 7, 4, 0, -5, -11. Planes (l = 2): 9, 2, -10, -28, -53, -86.
  - F = 6 (P quartic): n = 9..14: 0, -4, -9, -15, -22, -30. Planes: -16, -34, -59, ...; 3-planes: -49, -101, ...
  So sparse sextic lines vanish at n = 13 and quartic lines at n = 10, matching G1's thresholds.
- **Lines in the overshoot loci [Heuristic].** A line tau = a + t b lies in the cone iff a polynomial syzygy sum_j c_j(t) (a + tb)^{e_j} = 0 exists in K[t]; this is n (e_max + deg c + 1) equations for 2n + F (deg c + 1) unknowns, overdetermined for e_max >= 3. So the overshoot loci are not expected to contain lines (not measured; for the odd locus at n = 9 the search found only the point tau = 1 in a box of height 1).
- **Capacity and realness [Proof].** By Descartes, the real roots of N are at most sum over adjacent exponents of w(gap) (1 odd, 2 even) <= 2(F-1); equality needs all gaps even, so N = t^k G(t^2) with symmetric roots (needs D >= 2n and a coprimality condition). Hence n <= 2F - 3 for the useful odd shape. Real points exist: for n <= 13 take any n of the 13 real roots of T_13(t) - 1/3 as tau (rank of the n x 8 matrix [tau^{0,1,3,...,13}] is 7 for n = 9..13 [Wolfram, 25-digit threshold]), so there is no sign obstruction up to n = 13. Beyond, F = 8 gives no totally real tau. To reach n = 2F - 3 one needs F = (n+3)/2 free coefficients, i.e. a drawable graph of degree about n/2 (G2: ℚ degree <= 6, P degree <= 4). **Escape from the gap theorem: yes, for D > n, but the capacity bound n <= 2F - 3 replaces it.**
- **Profile [Wolfram + Proof].** Odd shape at best: F = (n+3)/2, conditions (n-1)/2, degrees 1*3*5*...*(n-2) at D = n; but only for n <= 13 with current graphs.

## 4. Nonlinear families

For these the parameters enter nonlinearly. I use r = rank of the monic crossing map (including the free shift), measured where possible.

| family | root variable | params M | r | conditions n - r | notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| power law x^m vs cubic Bezier | Bezier parameter s | 9 | **7 for m = 3..8 [Wolfram, exact]** | n - 7 | D = 3m; fibre over (tau, X) is linear in Y; reduces to X^m in span(1, tau, tau^2, tau^3) with X in that span |
| power law vs affine image of rational sextic | s | 7 | 5 (G2: 5 P parameters + the rational f) | n - 5 | I = {(tau, W): W in U_tau = span(1, tau, f(tau)), W^m in U_tau}; dimension 5 for every n; fibres over tau nonlinear |
| parabola offset (rational sextic, G2: M=7) vs line | u | 7 | 5 (G2: 5/6) | n - 5 | codim 1 in monic sextics; nonic by a known point |
| parabola offset vs conic through 3 known points (N4-2) | u | 7 | 7 | n - 7 | D = 12 - 3 = 9: only for n = 9 |
| offset x circle (G2 lead) | u | 4 (d + circle 3) + outer Aff (G2: M=6 as offset x ellipse) | not measured | >= n - 6 | circle = ellipse by outer Aff; D is 10-12; crossing is nonlinear in the parameters |
| two-graph crossing y = P(x), x = R(y), both rational sextic | x | 14 rational | 12 (composition) | n - 12 | **new, see below** |
| conic vs power law | x | 6 | 6 | n - 6 | linear in the six coefficients, support {0,1,2,m,m+1,2m} (table in section 3) |

- **New observation: graph against rotated graph [Proof of the algebra, Open as an engine].** y = P(x) and x = R(y) with P, R rational degree <= 6 (rotating by 90 degrees is a constructor) cross in the roots of x - R(P(x)), degree 36, 14 rational parameters, rank 12 (composition modulo the affine ambiguity). The condition is the fixed-point equation tau = R(P(tau)) in K: write sigma = P(tau), then tau in span_Q(1, sigma, ..., sigma^6). It is *unconditional for n <= 7* and has n - 12 conditions in principle (so none for n <= 12 by the dimension count), but all 14 parameters are rational, so by the rational-parameter remark of Summary 1 a Q-point of a thin locus is required (unresolved). Capacity 36 real roots: no Descartes limit. This is the cleanest candidate I found for a family whose count of free (rational) parameters beats the sparse ones; I did **not** verify the rank 12 numerically and did not check that the solution set has Q-points. [Open]
- **Fibres.** For all rows with a linear dependence on some parameter subset (Y in the Bezier row; (c, d, g) in the sextic image row; the cofactor rho in the linear rows) the parameter space fibres over a lower-dimensional base with linear fibres; the base carries the whole cost.
- **Real points for s = 0.** Dense rows have no Descartes limit; N4 measured 9 real roots for N4-1/N4-2 at n = 9 and at most 7 for the offset Bezier x line. Nothing new measured here for n > 9.

## 5. Questions asked, answered by route

- *Is I usually of positive dimension?* Yes: dim = M, always (Section 1). It is the cheapness of an F_{<n}-point that is the question.
- *Degree.* See tables: trinomial (n-2)!..., sparse sextic at m = n: (n-7)!, odd overshoot 210 .. 10395 (n = 9..13), conic six-term 4.5e5 .. 2.7e10.
- *Low-degree or rational components.* Only the block-diagonal components (linear spaces/lines of constant-on-blocks tau), which are not primitive elements and so useless; plus the point [1].
- *Linear subspaces.* Expected lines exist in CI(1..n-7) iff n <= 12 (Fano dimension), in CI(1..n-5) iff n <= 9; none for overshoot loci (syzygy count).
- *Fibres into varieties of degree < n.* The only fibration seen is I -> (cofactor rho) with linear fibres, which is fine for the fibre but leaves the base (a twist-incidence with the n!-degree cover) with no lower-degree structure.
- *Cost profile.* Conditions n - r with r in {3, 5, 6, 7, 8 (sparse linear), 12 (composition, unverified)}; degrees at least factorial; no family with cost bounded below n.

## 6. What this suggests for the plan (route-scoped)

- Sparse linear families cannot go beyond n = 13 for s = 0 (rational) or n = 9 (P-quartic): the capacity bound is a theorem (Descartes).
- To get a general-n route the number of free rational parameters has to grow linearly. Candidates: the two-graph fixed-point engine above (14 rational parameters but a thin locus), deeper compositions of graphs (x = R(P(x)) with more levels), or offset/graph towers (G2: +3 per level, degree exponential).
- The rational-parameter thin-set point suggests treating the problem arithmetically: the vector of rational parameters must hit the reducibility locus of the family. That joins H3.

## Other things noticed

- N2's "1428" is the Porteous total; the main component of the n = 9 overshoot locus has degree 210. The same Stirling-inversion trick corrects N4's degrees if any were read from Porteous counts.
- The identity sum S(n,k)(m-2)^{falling (k-2)} = (m^{n-1}-1)/(m-1) is new to me and holds in all 44 cases checked; I did not prove it in closed form.
- The trinomial genus formula reproduces smooth plane curves for n = 3 (g = (m-3)(m-4)/2) and a canonical genus-4 curve for n = 4, m = 5. That makes it a solid check on the covering-monodromy argument.
- For m = n the "overshoot" loci coincide with the classical sparse CI; so the census engines are exactly the first rung of the overshoot ladder.
- G1's note that RD counts parameters rather than auxiliary degree is consistent with the Fano-dimension thresholds found here: the first obstruction for the sextic engine at n = 13 shows up as the Fano dimension turning negative.
- The mu_m symmetry of the trinomial family acts by the inverse of the (m-1)-cycle at infinity (shift by one among the large roots); I did not see this documented anywhere. It makes C~ -> Gamma~ totally ramified at every place over infinity.
