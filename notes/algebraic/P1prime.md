## 0. Verdict

1. **The plan's "(or L ⊂ F)" makes the statement vacuous [Proof].** With L ranging over all real
   subfields of F, (T_K) is equivalent to β ∈ F (take L = ℚ(β)). A conjecture with content must fix
   a base class 𝓛 of fields already known to lie in F. §2 gives the chain of classes, from Taylor's
   (totally real solvable) to the inductive class F_{<n}, and shows that for Gal ⊇ A_n no class in
   the chain splits K (Lemma D).
2. **Minimal conjecture (§3).** (T_β; 𝓛): there are L ∈ 𝓛, a finite real extension M ⊇ L(β), an
   engine e and λ ∈ L^m with M = L(x₀) for a real root x₀ of the member f_{e,λ}. The engines are
   trinomial, power × power, Chebyshev trinomial, and five-term sine. The object is M, a real
   field *containing* β, not K_L. Theorem X uses this freedom: M = L(√(β − c)).
3. **Three equivalent forms [Proof] (§4):** twisted variety, specialisation, dependence. For the
   power × power engine the twisted variety is a **curve**: an M-twist of the Galois closure
   Ĉ_{p,q} of the three-point map x ↦ x^p(1 − x)^q. H1's trinomial curve Γ_{K′} (genus 47881 at
   D = 9) is the case (p, q) = (D, −1); the genus formula reproduces 47881 exactly [Proof, Python
   arithmetic]. This is the cleanest single object for P2′.
4. **Real points needed (§5).** For trinomial, power × power and five-term the condition on the
   real place reduces to existence: any L-point gives a drawable crossing, and only the one real
   place σ₀ of L given by L ⊂ ℝ matters. A twisted variety has real points at σ₀ iff the number r of
   real places of M over σ₀ is at most the engine's real capacity: 2 or 3 for the trinomial,
   **3 for power × power with gcd(p, q) = 1** [Proof; Python check], every r for the sine engines.
   The Chebyshev cosine form alone has a genuine semialgebraic constraint (x₀ ∈ [−1, 1]), and the
   five-term form removes it.
5. **Local conditions are free in a strong form (§6, Lemma U) [Proof].** After a totally real
   solvable base change L, every factor of K_L/L is unramified at all finite primes and split
   above any prescribed finite set.
6. **The three induction measures proposed in the plan all fail (§7) [Proof].** The relative
   discriminant is already trivial (Lemma U). Heights on F accumulate at 0, so they are not
   well-founded. Geometric gonality is the same for every K, and the F-gonality is
   self-referential. The only well-founded measure in sight is the degree n via F_{<n}, and
   Lemma D shows that it can only enlarge the base field, never simplify K.

## 1. The engines

Each engine is a family f_{e,λ}(x) over ℚ with parameters λ in a rational base B_e, and a domain
D_e ⊂ ℝ such that **every real root x₀ ∈ D_e of a nonzero member with λ ∈ F^m lies in P.**

| e | family | base | D_e | source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| tri(D), D ≥ 2 | x^D + ax + b | (a, b) | ℝ ∖ {0} | Theorem X: `power_law` × line |
| pp(p, q), p, q ∈ ℤ∖0 | x^p(1 − x)^q − λ (denominators cleared) | λ | ℝ ∖ {0, 1} | C1 §4: `power_law` x^p × affine image of x^{−q} |
| cheb(p, q), p > q ≥ 1 | T_p(x) + a·T_q(x) + c | (a, c) | [−1, 1] | Engine S (C1 §1) |
| five(p, q) | G(t) of C1 §1.2, in t = tan(θ/2) | (A : B : C : D : E) | ℝ | Engine S, five-term |

Notes [Proof].
- *pp is drawable on all of ℝ ∖ {0, 1}.* On (0, 1) draw y = x^p (`power_law 1 p`) and
  y = λ(1 − x)^{−q} (`power_law 1 (−q)`, `scale_x` by −1, `translate_x` by 1, `scale_y` by λ). On
  x < 0 or x > 1 use the same curves with `scale_x`/`scale_y` by −1 to fix signs, since p and q are
  integers. Crossings of distinct real-analytic graphs are isolated, so `restrict` to a rational
  window and `inter_x` apply.
- *tri ⊂ pp.* If τ^D + aτ + b = 0 with ab ≠ 0, then u = −aτ/b satisfies u^D(1 − u)^{−1} =
  −(−a/b)^D·b (C1 §4). So tri(D) is pp(D, −1) after an L-affine change of variable. If a = 0 the
  root is a radical; if b = 0 then τ = 0.
- *cheb ⊂ five.* Take B = D = 0; then x = cos θ = (1 − t²)/(1 + t²) and L(x) ⊆ L(t).
- *gcd(p, q) = d > 1 in pp.* Then x^p(1 − x)^q = (x^{p/d}(1 − x)^{q/d})^d, so the step is a radical
  over a pp(p/d, q/d) step. For d = 2 this is the square lift built into the engine.

## 2. The base class, and why it must be named

**Remark V (vacuity) [Proof].** Suppose (T_K) allows L to be any real subfield of F. If β ∈ F,
take L = ℚ(β) and any member with a root in L, e.g. x₀ = β itself for tri. Conversely, any L ⊂ F
with β ∈ L(x₀) gives β ∈ F. So "(T_K) with L ⊂ F" is the target restated. The content of E-P is the
choice of a class 𝓛 of fields **already proved** to lie in F.

**The chain of classes.** Every class below consists of real number fields contained in F. Each is
contained in the next, so the corresponding conjecture (T_β; 𝓛) gets weaker down the list.

| 𝓛 | fields | known ⊂ F by | (T_β; 𝓛) implied by |
|---|---|---|---|
| TR-solv | totally real solvable | X1 | Taylor's question (X1 §3.3; C1 §3) |
| R-solv | real solvable; only the place L ⊂ ℝ is constrained | X1 | TR-solv |
| E | subfields of E | (E-deg8), (E-solv) | R-solv |
| F_{<n} | real fields generated over E by real algebraic numbers of degree < n | induction on n | E |
| 𝓛_∞ | closure of E under real engine steps | definition | F_{<n} for every n ⟹ the target |

The last row is self-referential: (T_β; 𝓛_∞) for all β is equivalent to "the engine closure of E is
all of ℝ_alg", i.e. the target restricted to these engines. So the only non-circular forms are
TR-solv, R-solv and E, plus the inductive F_{<n}: (T_β; F_{<n}) for every β of degree n ≥ 9 implies
the target by induction on n.

**Lemma D (no class in the chain splits K) [Proof, using X1's section lemma].** Let n ≥ 9 and
Gal(Ω/ℚ) ⊇ A_n. For every L ⊂ F_{<n}, L ∩ Ω ⊆ ℚ(√disc q). Hence q stays irreducible over L, so
K_L = L(β) is a field of degree n, with group A_n or S_n over L.
- *Proof.* L is finitely generated, so its Galois closure L̂ lies in a compositum of Galois closures
  of towers whose steps are abelian, of degree ≤ 8, or of degree < n. So every composition factor of
  Gal(L̂/ℚ) is abelian, a section of S_8, or a section of S_k for some k < n. Let N be the Galois
  closure of L ∩ Ω. Then N ⊆ Ω ∩ L̂ and Gal(N/ℚ) is a quotient of Gal(Ω/ℚ) ∈ {A_n, S_n}. The
  normal subgroups give N ∈ {ℚ, ℚ(√disc), Ω}. If N = Ω, then A_n is a composition factor of
  Gal(N/ℚ), hence of its cover Gal(L̂/ℚ). That is impossible: A_n is not a section of S_m for
  m < n (X1, proved), it is not abelian, and n ≥ 9 > 8. So L ∩ Ω ⊆ N ⊆ ℚ(√disc). A_n is
  transitive, so q is irreducible over ℚ(√disc), and then over L by linear disjointness. ∎
- *Consequence.* Enlarging the base field (solvable, degree ≤ 8, or inductively everything of
  degree < n) never reduces the problem to a smaller field. Any induction gains only a larger field
  over which to find a point on the same twisted variety. This is X1's "base case blocked" in
  degree form.

## 3. The conjecture

**Conjecture (T_β; 𝓛).** There are
- a field L ∈ 𝓛 (a number field, L ⊂ ℝ),
- a finite extension M ⊂ ℝ of L with β ∈ M,
- an engine e from §1, and parameters λ ∈ B_e(L),

such that M = L(x₀) for some x₀ ∈ D_e with f_{e,λ}(x₀) = 0 and f_{e,λ} ≢ 0.

**Headline form:** (T; R-solv), meaning (T_β; R-solv) for every real algebraic β of degree ≥ 9.
It is implied by a positive answer to Taylor's question (S = {∞}) and is strictly weaker in its
hypotheses on L (one real place, not all).

**Theorem 1 (sufficiency) [Proof].** If 𝓛 consists of subfields of F, then (T_β; 𝓛) implies β ∈ P.
*Proof.* L ⊂ F, so λ ∈ F^m. Then x₀ ∈ P by the engine property, and β ∈ M = L(x₀) ⊂ F. ∎
(Recovery of β from x₀ is X1's gcd step. It needs only M = L(x₀), which is part of the statement.)

What the statement allows beyond the plan's wording:
- **M ⊋ L(β) is allowed.** Theorem X takes M = L(√(β − c)) of degree 2n; any real M containing β
  serves. Larger M means higher degree but more fields to choose from.
- **Overshoot.** The fibre of f_{e,λ} may have degree > [M:L]; only one factor needs to be M.
- **One real place.** Only σ₀ (the given L ⊂ ℝ) is constrained; other real places of L are free.

## 4. Three equivalent forms

Fix L ⊂ ℝ, M ⊂ ℝ finite over L, d = [M:L], and an engine e.

**(I) Twisted variety.** Let

    Y_e^{M/L} = {(τ, λ) ∈ R_{M/L}𝔸¹ × B_e : f_{e,λ}(τ) = 0 in M ⊗_L (−),  disc χ_τ ≠ 0,  τ ∉ Z_e},

where χ_τ is the characteristic polynomial of τ over L and Z_e is the excluded locus (τ = 0 for
tri; τ ∈ {0, 1} for pp). Y_e^{M/L} has an L-point whose real coordinate τ ∈ M ⊂ ℝ lies in D_e.

**(II) Specialisation.** There is λ ∈ B_e(L) such that M is L-isomorphic to a factor of the
specialisation algebra L[x]/(f_{e,λ}), by an isomorphism sending x to a real number in D_e under
M ⊂ ℝ.

**(III) Dependence.** There is τ ∈ M with M = L(τ), τ ∈ D_e (as a real number), satisfying:
- tri(D): 1, τ, τ^D are L-linearly dependent, with nonzero τ^D-coefficient;
- pp(p, q): **τ and 1 − τ are multiplicatively dependent modulo L^×**: τ^p(1 − τ)^q ∈ L^×;
- cheb(p, q): 1, T_q(τ), T_p(τ) are L-linearly dependent with nonzero T_p-coefficient. Equivalently
  (C1 §4) the conjugate points (T_q(τ_i), T_p(τ_i)) of the Lissajous curve are collinear;
- five(p, q): with w = (1 + iτ)/(1 − iτ) ∈ M(i), so that |w| = 1, the numbers
  1, Re w^p, Im w^p, Re w^q, Im w^q are L-linearly dependent, not all coefficients of the w-terms
  zero.

**Theorem 2 [Proof].** For fixed (L, M, e), (I) ⟺ (II) ⟺ (III). Consequently (T_β; 𝓛) holds iff
for some L ∈ 𝓛, some real M ∋ β finite over L and some e, any of (I)–(III) holds.
- (II) ⇒ (III): take τ = the image of x. Then M = L(τ) and f_{e,λ}(τ) = 0, which is the dependence
  relation with coefficients read off λ.
- (III) ⇒ (I): M = L(τ) means the minimal polynomial of τ has degree d, so χ_τ equals it and is
  separable. The relation is f_{e,λ}(τ) = 0 for the λ it defines.
- (I) ⇒ (II): disc χ_τ ≠ 0 forces L[τ] = M (M is a field, so χ_τ separable ⇒ τ has d distinct
  conjugates ⇒ degree d). f_{e,λ}(τ) = 0 means χ_τ | f_{e,λ}, so M ≅ L[x]/(χ_τ) is a factor.
- The "M ⊇ L(β), M ⊂ ℝ" clause is the same in all three, and x₀ := τ under M ⊂ ℝ. ∎

The proofs are bookkeeping. The point is to fix the objects exactly, including the containment
M ⊇ L(β), overshoot (divisibility, not equality) and the domain D_e.

### 4.1 Form (I) as twisted Galois closures [Proof]

In the exact case deg_x f_{e,λ} = d (no overshoot), the variety of ordered d-tuples of distinct
roots of a member is the Galois closure Ŷ_e → B_e when the monodromy group is S_d: S_d acts simply
transitively on orderings. Then Y_e^{M/L} is the twist of Ŷ_e by the S_d-torsor of M/L (the
cocycle G_L → S_d of the étale algebra M). L-points of the twist over unramified λ correspond to
λ ∈ B_e(L) with fibre algebra ≅ M. This is the twisting lemma for non-Galois covers in Dèbes's form
[Cited from memory: P. Dèbes, *Galois covers with prescribed fibers: the Beckmann–Black problem*,
Ann. Sc. Norm. Sup. Pisa 28 (1999); not re-opened]. With overshoot, use ordered d-tuples of
distinct roots among the deg f roots, which is irreducible when the monodromy is d-transitive.

### 4.2 The canonical curve: the power × power engine [Proof]

pp(p, q) has a one-dimensional base, so Y_pp^{M/L} is a **curve**. Inside R_{M/L}𝔾_m, of dimension d,
the condition τ^p(1 − τ)^q ∈ L is d − 1 linear conditions on the coordinates. Write g = x^p(1 − x)^q
and Ĉ_{p,q} for the Galois closure of g: ℙ¹ → ℙ¹.
- **Monodromy S_d when gcd(p, q) = 1 and p, q > 0.** g is a polynomial of degree d = p + q with
  g^{−1}(0) = {0, 1} (multiplicities p, q). Suppose g = h∘k with deg k = e, 1 < e < d. If h^{−1}(0)
  has two points, k is totally ramified over each, so e | p and e | q. If it has one point, h is
  totally ramified there, so (d/e) | p and (d/e) | q. Either way gcd(p, q) > 1. So g is
  indecomposable, hence its monodromy is primitive (Ritt). It contains a transposition (the simple
  critical point x* = p/(p + q)), so it is S_d. For tri = pp(D, −1) the monodromy is S_D (X1 §3.3).
- **Three branch points.** g is branched only over 0, ∞ and λ* = g(x*) = p^p q^q/(p + q)^{p+q} ∈ ℚ.
  So Ĉ_{p,q} is a Belyi curve with an S_d-action, and Y_pp^{M/L} = (Ĉ_{p,q})^{M} is its M-twist.
- **Genus.** With e₀, e_∞ the orders of the local monodromy over 0 and ∞,
  2g − 2 = d!·(1 − 1/e₀ − 1/e_∞ − 1/2). For p, q > 0 coprime: e₀ = pq, e_∞ = d. For tri(D):
  e₀ = D, e_∞ = D − 1.
  - D = 9: g = 1 + 9!·(1/2 − 1/9 − 1/8)/2 = **47881**, H1's genus of Γ_{K′} at n = m = 9. So H1's
    trinomial curve is exactly this twisted Belyi Galois closure [Proof; arithmetic in Python].
  - (p, q) = (5, 4): g = 61489.
- **Intermediate covers.** The quotient of the twist by H ⊂ S_d is defined over L for every torsor
  with image S_d only when H is normal: H ∈ {1, A_d, S_d}. Ĉ/A_d → ℙ¹ is a double cover branched at
  λ* and at the one of {0, ∞} whose local monodromy is odd, so it has genus 0. Its twist is the
  discriminant conic {disc f_λ ∈ δ_M·L^{×2}}, which always has an L-point (over a branch point).
  This matches the plan's insight: nothing abelian is visible on the cover itself.

**Canonical instance for P2′.** The target follows if for every real β of degree ≥ 9 there are a
real solvable L, a real M ∋ β (M = L(β) if β has ≤ 3 real conjugates at σ₀, else M = L(√(β − c)))
and a coprime (p, q) with an **L-point on the M-twist of the Belyi curve Ĉ_{p,q}**. That is a
countable family of twists of explicit curves defined over ℚ, with one point needed among them.

### 4.3 Form (II) and the literature

In specialisation language, (T_β; 𝓛) says: **the union of the engine families is "potentially
parametric" for M over 𝓛.** After base change to some L ∈ 𝓛, one real field M ∋ β is a
specialisation of one member of the union. It differs from the notions in the literature in four
ways:
- Beckmann–Black asks, for a given Galois G-extension, for *some* regular G-cover specialising to
  it. Here the covers are a fixed countable list.
- Parametricity (Dèbes; König–Legrand) asks a *fixed* cover to specialise to *every* G-extension
  over a *fixed* field. Here base change is allowed, and one field per β is enough.
- The group is not fixed: one engine carries S_d for every d.
- A real-place condition is imposed.

Over a fixed number field, non-parametricity results for covers of ℙ¹ come from Faltings applied to
the twisted curves [Cited from memory: König–Legrand, non-parametric sets of regular realisations
over number fields; not re-opened]. That argument does not survive base change to a varying L,
which is exactly the freedom (T) uses (X1 §3.3 remark on Faltings).

## 5. Exactly which real points are needed

Let r be the number of real places of M over σ₀ and s the number of complex ones (r + 2s = d).
The fibre at a real λ has some number of real roots; call the maximum over real λ the engine's
**real capacity** c_e.

**Proposition 5.1 [Proof].** For tri, pp and five, every L-point of Y_e^{M/L} gives a drawable
crossing. There is no condition at the real place beyond existence, and none at real places of L
other than σ₀. Y_e^{M/L} has an ℝ-point at σ₀ iff some real member has ≥ r real roots and ≥ s
nonreal pairs (= r and = s in the exact case).
- An L-point gives τ ∈ M with M ⊂ ℝ, so x₀ = τ is real. D_e is ℝ minus the excluded points, which
  the twisted variety already removes. So the root lies in D_e.
- At σ₀, M ⊗_{L,σ₀} ℝ = ℝ^r × ℂ^s, and τ must send the real factors to real roots and the complex
  factors to conjugate pairs. That is the stated condition. ∎

**Proposition 5.2 (capacities).**

| e | c_e | patterns (r, s) | label |
|---|---|---|---|
| tri(D) | 2 (D even), 3 (D odd) | exact: r ≤ c_e, r ≡ D mod 2 | [Proof] (Descartes; X1 §3.3) |
| pp(p, q), gcd = 1 | **3** | r ≤ 3 | [Proof] below; [Python] all coprime pairs with abs(p), abs(q) ≤ 7, no exceptions |
| pp(p, q), p, q both even | 4 | | [Proof]; this is the built-in square lift |
| cheb(p, q) | p | exact q = 1: every r ≥ 1, r ≡ p mod 2, distinguished root in [−1, 1] | [Proof sketch] below |
| five(p, q) | 2p | every r + 2s ≤ 2p | [Proof sketch] (C1 §3) |

*pp, gcd(p, q) = 1 [Proof].* g′/g = p/x − q/(1 − x) vanishes only at x* = p/(p + q) (nowhere if
p + q = 0). So g is monotone on each of (−∞, 0), (0, 1), (1, ∞) except the one containing x*, where
it is unimodal. On (0, 1), g > 0. On (−∞, 0), g has sign (−1)^p, and on (1, ∞) sign (−1)^q.
- For λ > 0 the roots lie in (0, 1) and in the outer intervals where g > 0. Both outer intervals
  qualify only if p and q are both even, which gcd = 1 excludes. So there are at most two intervals,
  one of them possibly unimodal: ≤ 3 roots.
- For λ < 0, (0, 1) contributes nothing. The two outer intervals give ≤ 2 + 1 = 3. ∎

The control p = q = 2 at λ = 0.01 gives 4 real roots [Python], as it should.

*cheb with q = 1 [Proof sketch].*
- r = p: |a| + |c| < 1 (C1 §1.3).
- Intermediate r: for small a ≠ 0 the local minima of T_p + a x have the distinct values
  −1 + a·x_k, at x_k = cos(kπ/p). As c increases, the root pairs at those minima leave the real line
  one at a time, and the surviving real roots stay in (−1, 1).
- r = 1 (p odd): for a > max_{[−1,1]} |T_p′| = p², T_p + a x is increasing on [−1, 1] and has positive
  derivative outside, so it has one real root. Choose c to put that root near −c/a ∈ (−1, 1).

**Consequences.**
- **The square lift is needed only for r ≥ 4 at σ₀.** tri and pp(coprime) serve every M with ≤ 3 real
  places over σ₀ directly (M = L(β)). For r ≥ 4, use M = L(√(β − c)), which has r′ ∈ {1, 2}
  (X1 §3.3), or use a sine engine.
- **pp gives no realness gain over tri.** Coprime pp has capacity 3, the same as tri(D) for D odd.
  Its gain is the freedom in (p, q): more curves Ĉ_{p,q}, one point needed.
- **Taylor ⇒ (T) needs no density argument for tri, pp or five.** The real condition is mere
  existence of an ℝ-point (Proposition 5.1), so plain PSC existence suffices. C1 and X1 invoked
  "a solvable point close to the real point". That step is needed only for cheb, whose domain
  [−1, 1] is a semialgebraic condition at one real place; five removes it.

## 6. Local conditions: Lemma U

**Lemma U [Proof].** Let K be a number field and S a finite set of primes. There is a totally real
solvable M₀ such that, for every real solvable L ⊇ M₀, every factor of K ⊗ L is unramified over L
at all finite primes and split completely at every prime above S.
- Let S′ = S ∪ {p : p ramified in K}. For p ∈ S′, let K″_p be a finite Galois extension of ℚ_p
  containing every completion of K at p. Lemma LS (P1) gives a totally real solvable M₀ with every
  completion M₀,u (u | p ∈ S′) containing K″_p.
- Then K ⊗ M₀,u = ∏_i K_{p,i} ⊗_{ℚ_p} M₀,u splits completely, because M₀,u contains the Galois
  closure of each K_{p,i}. Primes outside S′ are unramified in K, hence in every K ⊗ L.
- Base change preserves splitting and unramifiedness. ∎

Consequence. WLOG M/L is unramified at every finite prime: the only "ramification" is at infinity
(the complex places of M over real places of L). Combined with Lemma LS this is everything local
that base change can do for free. Whatever obstruction remains is global (P2′).

## 7. The induction measures proposed in the plan

The plan asks for a measure μ(β) for which the engine step decreases (insight (i) of E-P).
Candidates and verdicts:

1. **Conductor or discriminant of K_L/L after optimal solvable base change.** Always trivial at
   finite primes, by Lemma U [Proof]. It is constant, not decreasing, so it is useless as a measure.
2. **Minimal height of a point on the twisted cover.** F contains 2^{1/k} with absolute height
   (log 2)/k → 0 [Proof]. Heights on F are not well-ordered, so they need a degree bound to give an
   induction (Northcott), and base change to L ∈ 𝓛 leaves the degree unbounded. Also no map relates
   the twisted covers of different β, so nothing makes the height decrease [Proof for
   non-well-foundedness; Heuristic for the rest].
3. **Gonality of the cover over F.** For fixed (e, d) all the varieties Y_e^{M/L} are twists of one
   ℚ-variety Ŷ_e. Every geometric invariant (genus, Kodaira dimension, geometric gonality) is
   therefore independent of β [Proof]. The F-gonality depends on F, which is the unknown, so it is
   circular as an induction measure. The E-gonality is computable in principle, but values ≤ 8 close
   β directly (Theorem G) and B3 found ≥ 12 uniformly in K. [Proof for independence; Heuristic for
   the rest]

**What remains.** The degree n through F_{<n} is well-founded. By Lemma D it never splits K, so an
induction on n gains only the size of the base field, never a simpler K. A useful self-referential
measure would have to be arithmetic and attached to the pair (β, engine). None is in sight. Record
insight (i) as **untested beyond these three; not closed**.

## 8. Hand-off to P2′ and P3′

- **P2′ should work with §4.2:** twists (Ĉ_{p,q})^M over real solvable L, M = L(β) or L(√(β − c)),
  coprime (p, q). Facts it can use:
  - Ĉ_{p,q} is a Belyi curve with an S_d-action defined over ℚ, so Jac(Ĉ_{p,q}) decomposes into
    S_d-isotypic pieces, and the twist acts piece by piece [Heuristic: the natural place to look for
    a descent obstruction];
