**Notation.**
- q ∈ ℚ[x] has degree n, K = ℚ[x]/(q), G = Gal(q), and s is the number of conjugate pairs.
- τ ∈ K ⊗ L. Its characteristic polynomial is χ_τ, with elementary symmetric functions e_k and power sums
  p_k = Tr τ^k.
- E = ℚ^solv ∩ ℝ (real solvable numbers) and F = P ∩ ℝ_alg. Then E ⊆ F [Proof, X1 §2.2].
- O_m = {p_1 = p_3 = … = p_{2m−1} = 0} ⊂ P(K) = P^{n−1} is the odd shape. V_k = {p_1 = … = p_k = 0}.
- **Wooley.** T. D. Wooley, *Solvable points on smooth projective varieties*, Monatsh. Math. 180 (2016)
  391–403, arXiv:1501.01789. Read on ar5iv. Its statements are reproduced below as summarized from that
  reading. The proofs of Cor. 4.3–4.6 were read only as paraphrase, so they are [Cited].

## 0. Findings summary

1. **A real Wooley theorem is true, and the right hypothesis is just "a smooth real point" [Proof, modulo
   Wooley Cor. 4.5].** Theorem RW (§2.3):
   - Setting: L ⊂ ℝ is a number field, and X/L is smooth and geometrically integral, with X(ℝ) ≠ ∅ and
     dim X ≥ exp(2^{D+2}(log D)²), where D = deg X.
   - Conclusion: X has a point over a real quadratic extension L′ = L(√a) ⊂ ℝ.
   - So X(E) ≠ ∅ if L ⊆ E, and X(F) ≠ ∅ if L ⊆ F. For L = ℚ, Wooley's Cor. 4.3 gives ℚ-points once
     dim ≥ D^{2^D}.
   - The trick: choose a so that every other real place of L becomes complex in L′. Then the only archimedean
     condition left is the given real point.
   - Even-degree sign problems are absorbed into "X(ℝ) ≠ ∅". Smoothness of the real point cannot be dropped
     (§2.4, an example).
2. **The diagonal layer is exactly real [Proof] (§2.1).**
   - Over E, a *system* of diagonal forms of one degree d has a nontrivial E-zero iff it has a nontrivial real
     zero. For odd d, N > r variables suffice for r forms (ϕ_{d,r}(E) = r, sharp).
   - So E is a **Birch field**, and Birch's theorem (odd-degree systems in many variables have nonzero zeros)
     holds over E [Cited: Birch 1957, as stated by Lampert–Snowden arXiv:2406.18498]. The same is true over ℚ.
3. **Nondegeneracy is free [Proof] (Lemma N, §3.1).** Suppose G ⊇ A_n with n ≥ 5, L is any field with
   Gal(KL/L) ⊇ A_n (true for L = E), and τ ∈ K ⊗ L is nonzero with trace zero.
   - Then τ has n distinct conjugates, and no two conjugates satisfy τ_i = −τ_j.
   - So every nonzero point of V_k or O_m over ℚ, E or F is a usable Tschirnhaus element. The pairing
     P^{m−1}'s and the constants never contain such points.
   - This removes the "points must lie off the pairing loci" caveat of N2 and H2 for A_n and S_n fields.
4. **Dimension is invariant, and Wooley-type theorems are dimension-hungry [Proof] (§4).**
   - Every reformulation of the engine incidence I_K has the same dimension M = dim I_K (H1): auxiliary
     variables, quadric or cubic rewriting, cofactor variables, fibre products. Padding with dummy variables
     gives cones, which are singular, and Wooley needs smoothness.
   - Weil restriction multiplies the dimension by ℓ and raises the degree to the power ℓ. That makes every
     Wooley bound strictly harder.
   - A good-τ locus of codimension c has degree ≥ c + 1. So any Wooley-type route needs
     n − 2 − c ≥ 2^{2^{c+1}} (Thm 1.1), or ≥ exp(2^{c+3}(log(c+1))²) (Cor. 4.5/RW).
   - **Even one condition (c = 1) needs n ≥ 19 (Thm 1.1) or n ≥ 2184 (RW) [Wolfram].** Two conditions need
     n ≥ 260 (Thm 1.1).
5. **Honest numbers at n = 9, 10, 12 (§4.2 table) [Wolfram].**
   - Every existing route misses its Wooley bound. The margins range from a factor of about 3 (n = 9, sextic
     engine; Hasse–Minkowski does that case anyway) up to bounds like 10^{10^{1500}}.
   - The trinomial surface of Theorem X has dimension 2 and degree (n−2)! (or (2n−2)! after the square lift).
     Its bound is 10^{10^{1517}} at n = 9.
   - Rewriting does not help (point 4). **Verdict: no route of item 2 brings (SR) or MI-1 into Wooley's range,
     for any n ≥ 10.** This is scoped to the cited theorems and to the engine families measured so far.
6. **The odd-degree route (§5).**
   - **Odd-weight lemma [Proof].** If a symmetric engine condition is cut out by odd-weight polynomials in the
     e_k, the engine's image contains the whole even space L_even. So its rank is ≥ ⌊n/2⌋.
   - An exact sparse engine with only odd conditions must contain every exponent ≡ n (mod 2).
   - **Theorem O (new rational engines, n = 9…13) [Proof, conditional on one rational point].**
     - With a *rational* τ, `poly_graph` Q of degree ≤ 6 against `power_law` y = c·x^{∓1/2} realises
       χ_τ = t·Q(t²) − c (n odd ≤ 13) and χ_τ = Q(t²) + c·t (n even ≤ 12).
     - The conditions are odd only: p_1 = p_3 = … = 0, with m = ⌊(n−1)/2⌋ forms.
     - Smooth totally real points exist [Wolfram, n = 10…13]. So **realness is free for totally real q**.
     - Lemma N makes every nonzero ℚ-point good. Every root of every S_n/A_n polynomial of degree 9…13 is in P
       **if O_m(ℚ) ≠ {0}** for its field.
   - This revives `poly_graph` for totally real q up to degree 13, but only conditionally:
     - O_m is singular at the pairing points, so Wooley's Cor. 4.6 (smooth, odd degree) does not apply.
     - Its dimension (4–6) misses even the weakest bound by a factor of 10^{10^{30}}.
     - Birch-type bounds are far beyond 13 variables.
     - The circle-method exponent n − Σ deg is negative: −7, −6, −14, −13, −23 for n = 9…13, against +4 for
       H5's decic engine [Heuristic]. So rational points on O_m are heuristically **rarer** than on MI-1's V′_K.
   - N2's odd overshoot locus is cut out by odd forms (maximal minors of degree Σe = 49) [Proof]. But there are
     C(n,8) of them, of degree 49, in n variables, so Birch's hypotheses fail by far.
   - **Theorem D [Proof modulo Davenport 1963].** For n ≥ 17 and G ⊇ A_n there is a nonzero *rational* τ with
     p_1 = p_3 = 0, and it is a good Tschirnhaus element. No drawable engine leaves only those two conditions,
     so this is a capacity statement and does not close anything.
7. **Verdict (§6).**
   - The X2 programme gives two clean conditional theorems:
     - Theorem X′: Theorem X with (SR) replaced by a dimension/degree hypothesis that RW discharges.
     - Theorem O.
   - It also names the exact missing ingredient for the Wooley route: **(BD) a drawable family whose number of
     P-valued parameters is unbounded while the crossing conditions have bounded degree in those parameters.**
   - With (BD), RW closes every n [Proof of the implication]. No constructor combination is known to give
     (BD). Offset towers grow M but their parameter degree grows with depth [Heuristic, from W1's j = 1 data].
   - **For which n each route works:**
     - real-Wooley on existing engines: none ≥ 10;
     - Theorem O: n ≤ 13, conditional on O_m(ℚ) ≠ {0};
     - Theorem D: capacity only (n ≥ 17).

## 1. Wooley's theorems, as needed here [Cited]

Wooley's results, with D = deg X:

- **Thm 1.1.** K is algebraic over ℚ, and X/K is smooth and geometrically integral with dim X ≥ 2^{2^D}. Then
  X has a point over K^sol, the compositum of all solvable extensions.
  - The introduction says this comes from "a tower of field extensions, each of degree no larger than deg(X)".
  - The body uses only extractions a^{1/j}.
- **Thm 4.1.** Under dim X ≥ (2(m+1))^{2^D}, there is a projective linear space of dimension m over K^sol.
- **Thm 4.2.** Over ℚ_p: dim ≥ D^{2^D} gives ℚ_p-points.
- **Cor. 4.3.** Over ℚ: a real point and dim ≥ D^{2^D} give a ℚ-point.
  - The Hasse principle comes from Browning–Heath-Brown, arXiv:1403.5937, Thm 1.1, which needs
    dim ≥ (D−1)2^D − 1.
  - For odd D, X is a complete intersection (CI) of odd-degree hypersurfaces, so the real point is automatic
    (Borsuk–Ulam, or Lang).
- **Thm 4.4.** Over K algebraic over ℚ_p: dim ≥ exp(2^{D+2}(log D)²).
- **Cor. 4.5.** L is an algebraic extension of ℚ. If X has points at all infinite completions and satisfies the
  same exp bound, then X has an L-point.
  - The Hasse principle is Frei–Madritsch, *Forms of differing degrees over number fields*, Mathematika 63
    (2017) 92–123, arXiv:1412.6419 (verified bibliographically). It is a number-field theorem, so I use
    Cor. 4.5 only for number fields L.
- **Cor. 4.6.** K is totally real, and D is odd ⇒ K-point.
- **The structure of the proof.**
  - Harris puts X in a linear space of dimension ≤ dim X + D − 1.
  - Bertram–Ein–Lazarsfeld (JAMS 4 (1991)) and Browning–Heath-Brown show X is a complete intersection once
    dim X > 2D(2D − 3) (Wooley's Lemma 2.3).
  - Brauer diagonalisation follows (Lemmas 3.1–3.5).
  - The key bound is Lemma 3.3: V_d(r; 𝕂) ≤ 2 r_d^{2^{d−1}} (ψ_d + 1)^{2^{d−1}−1}, where ψ_d is the sup of the
    diagonal invariants ϕ_i, i ≤ d.
  - Over K^sol, ϕ_j = 1, via (x_0, x_1) = (a_1^{1/j}, (−a_0)^{1/j}).

The two facts that matter for us:
- every bound is **doubly exponential in deg X**;
- every theorem needs **smoothness**.

## 2. Item 1: a real Brauer/Wooley

### 2.1 Diagonal systems over E [Proof]

E-facts:
- (E1) If a ∈ E, a > 0 and k ≥ 1, then a^{1/k} ∈ E.
- (E2) If a ∈ E and k is odd, the real a^{1/k} is in E.

Reason: ℚ^solv is closed under radicals. A radical extension of a solvable Galois extension has solvable Galois
closure, because the compositum of solvable Galois extensions is solvable. The root is real.

**Proposition D.** Let E′ ⊆ ℝ be any field with (E1) and (E2), and let A = (a_{ji}) ∈ E′^{r×N}. Consider the
diagonal system Σ_i a_{ji} x_i^d = 0, for j = 1..r.
- (a) d odd: a nontrivial E′-zero exists if N > r. That bound is sharp: a generic system in N = r variables has
  only x = 0, even over ℝ.
- (b) d even: a nontrivial E′-zero exists iff a nontrivial real zero exists, iff the cone
  {y ∈ ℝ^N : y ≥ 0, Ay = 0} is nonzero.

*Proof.*
- Substitute y_i = x_i^d.
- (a) Ay = 0 has a nonzero solution y ∈ E′^N by linear algebra. Put x_i = y_i^{1/d}, using (E2).
- (b) The polyhedral cone C = {y ≥ 0, Ay = 0} is defined over the ordered field E′.
  - If C ∩ ℝ^N ≠ 0, then C has an extreme ray. An extreme ray is the solution line of N − 1 linearly independent
    equations chosen from the rows of A and the coordinate hyperplanes, so it is spanned by a vector of E′^N.
  - This is the usual Fourier–Motzkin/vertex argument, valid over any ordered field.
  - Put x_i = y_i^{1/d} ∈ E′, using (E1).
  - The converse is trivial. ∎

Consequences:
- ϕ_{d,r}(E) = r for odd d: E is a **Birch field** in the sense of Lampert–Snowden (N_E(d) = 2 for odd d).
- ϕ_d(E) = ∞ for even d, because of positive definite forms. So Wooley's Lemma 3.3 is unavailable verbatim:
  ψ_d = ∞ as soon as d ≥ 2.
- The same holds for F, and for any real field with (E1) and (E2).

**Birch over E [Cited].**
- Birch (*Homogeneous forms of odd degree in a large number of variables*, Mathematika 4 (1957) 102–105)
  proved: over a Birch field, any system of odd-degree forms in sufficiently many variables has a nonzero zero.
  I use the statement as given by Lampert–Snowden, *Two improvements in Birch's theorem on forms*,
  arXiv:2406.18498, Def. 1.1 and Thm 1.2.
- Lampert–Snowden Thm 1.3: over a Birch field the Zariski closure of Z(K) has bounded codimension.
- Lampert–Snowden Thm 1.4: Z(K) is Zariski-dense once the collective strength is large.
- So all of this holds over E (and over ℚ, the original case).
- **No explicit constant** is given there. The constants are existence statements.

### 2.2 Why a sign-tracked Brauer does not come out of the diagonal layer alone [Proof of the obstruction to the method]

- Brauer's reduction (Wooley Lemma 3.2) diagonalises a form of degree d by choosing vectors on which all
  *mixed* terms vanish.
- For F(sx + ty) the mixed terms are forms G_k(x, y) of degree k in y and d − k in x, for 1 ≤ k ≤ d−1. So even
  degrees in the new vector appear even when d is odd.
- With x fixed, the even-degree mixed forms G_k(x, ·), k even, change sign together under x ↦ −x (d − k odd).
  That is one sign for all of them, not one each. So their joint indefiniteness cannot be arranged in general by
  this freedom.
- For odd systems Birch's argument circumvents this. For even forms no hypothesis on the *input* system that is
  weaker than "the lower-order systems have real zeros" propagates through the induction.
- I did not find a published sign-tracked Brauer. The literature search below found only the Birch-field
  framework and the real-closed remark of Lampert–Snowden §6.
- So I take an arithmetic route instead (§2.3). It gives a cleaner theorem and a stronger conclusion: points
  over a quadratic extension, not over a tower.

### 2.3 Theorem RW (real Wooley) [Proof, modulo Wooley Cor. 4.5 for number fields]

**Theorem RW.**
- *Hypotheses.*
  - L is a number field with a fixed embedding σ_0 : L ⊂ ℝ.
  - X/L is smooth, projective and geometrically integral, with X(ℝ) ≠ ∅ (via σ_0).
  - dim X ≥ B(D) := exp(2^{D+2}(log D)²), where D = deg X.
- *Conclusion.* There is a ∈ L, σ_0(a) > 0, such that L′ = L(√a) ⊂ ℝ and X(L′) ≠ ∅.
- *Corollaries.*
  - If L ⊆ E then X(E) ≠ ∅. If L ⊆ F then X(F) ≠ ∅.
  - If L = ℚ, Cor. 4.3 already gives X(ℚ) ≠ ∅ under dim X ≥ D^{2^D}.

*Proof.*
1. By weak approximation for the real places of L, choose a ∈ L with σ_0(a) > 0 and σ(a) < 0 for every other
   real embedding σ.
2. The real embeddings of L′ = L(√a) are exactly the two extensions of σ_0 (√a ↦ ±√σ_0(a)). Every other
   archimedean place of L′ is complex.
3. Both real places of L′ restrict to σ_0 on L. So the base changes of X along them are X^{σ_0}, which has real
   points.
4. Complex places impose nothing.
5. X_{L′} is still smooth, geometrically integral, of the same dimension and degree.
6. So Wooley's Cor. 4.5, with L′ a number field, gives X(L′) ≠ ∅. Its finite places are handled by Thm 4.4 and
   the Hasse principle by Frei–Madritsch.
7. L′ is real (via σ_0), and L′ ⊆ ℚ^solv if L ⊆ ℚ^solv, so L′ ⊆ E. For F: √a ∈ F by `sqrt_Pconstructible`. ∎

Remarks:
- When L has only one real place, L′ = L.
- The result is stronger than a solvable statement: one real quadratic step. So for the targets in this
  programme, *in the high-dimension regime realness costs nothing and solvability is not even needed*.
- The odd-degree case of Cor. 4.6 is recovered without "totally real". For odd D the real point is automatic
  (Borsuk–Ulam, as Wooley notes), and RW needs only the one real place.

### 2.4 Necessity of the hypotheses [Proof]

- *A real point is necessary*: trivially, since E ⊂ ℝ.
- *A smooth real point is necessary in general.*
  - Let P(x, y) be a binary quintic form over ℚ with Galois group S_5 and exactly one real root [x_0 : 1]. Take
    X = {P(x, y)² + z² = 0} ⊂ P².
  - X(ℝ) = {[x_0 : 1 : 0]}, which is a singular point, and x_0 ∉ E because its Galois group is not solvable.
    So X(E) = ∅.
  - (Low-dimensional, but the same construction cones up to any dimension: P(x,y)² + z_1² + … + z_k².)
- *High dimension is necessary* for the generality of RW: Lemma A (H2) is the only low-dimensional substitute,
  and it needs deg X ≤ 4 (over E) or ≤ 8 (over F).

So the honest real statement has two regimes:

| regime | hypothesis | field | source |
|---|---|---|---|
| low degree | smooth real point, deg X ≤ 4 (E) or ≤ 8 (F) | E, F | Lemma A (H2) [Proof]; E is closed under real roots of degree ≤ 4 (Ferrari) |
| high dimension | smooth X, real point, dim ≥ B(deg X) | real quadratic over L | RW [Proof mod Cited] |
| odd systems (any field with odd radicals) | many variables | E, ℚ | Birch [Cited] |

## 3. Lemmas used by every route

### 3.1 Lemma N: nondegeneracy is free [Proof]

**Lemma N.**
- *Hypotheses.*
  - G = Gal(q) ⊇ A_n, n ≥ 5.
  - L is a field with K ⊗ L a field whose Galois group over L contains A_n. This holds for every L ⊂ ℚ̄ with
    L ∩ Ω solvable over ℚ, where Ω is the splitting field. In particular it holds for L = E, because the
    normal subgroups of S_n are 1, A_n, S_n.
  - τ ∈ K ⊗ L, τ ≠ 0, Tr τ = 0.
- *Conclusion.*
  - (i) The conjugates τ_1..τ_n are distinct.
  - (ii) τ_i ≠ −τ_j for all i, j, including i = j.

*Proof.*
- (i) A repeated conjugate means L(τ) ⊊ KL. A_n is primitive, so KL/L has no intermediate fields. Hence τ ∈ L,
  and Tr τ = nτ = 0 gives τ = 0.
- (ii) Let R = {(i, j) : τ_i = −τ_j}. It is stable under the Galois action, since g(τ_i) = τ_{g(i)}.
  - If (i, i) ∈ R, transitivity makes every τ_i = 0.
  - If (i, j) ∈ R with i ≠ j, 2-transitivity of A_n (n ≥ 4) puts every ordered pair in R. Then
    τ_1 = −τ_2 = τ_3 = −τ_1, so τ = 0. ∎

Consequences:
- The degenerate loci of N2 (constants) and H2/NOTES (pairing P^{m−1}'s, the singular locus of O_m) contain no
  nonzero trace-zero point over ℚ, E or F.
- **Any nonzero rational or E-point of V_k or O_m is a good Tschirnhaus element** for A_n/S_n fields. Fields
  with other groups are handled by towers (H5, N2).

### 3.2 Totally real definiteness [Proof, restated from H3]

- For s = 0, every real field L ⊂ ℝ and τ ∈ K ⊗ L: K ⊗_ℚ ℝ ≅ ℝ^n, so p_2(τ) = Σ τ_i² > 0 unless τ = 0.
- Every route with an even power-sum condition fails for totally real q, whatever the parameter field.
  Theorem X avoids this by the square lift; Theorem O avoids it by having odd conditions only.

## 4. Item 2: trading degree for dimension

### 4.1 Invariance of dimension [Proof]

Let I_K ⊆ P(K) × A^M be the engine incidence of H1. It is the twisted fibre product, of dimension M.

- **Auxiliary variables.**
  - Suppose the new variables are regular functions of the old ones, e.g. σ_j = τ^j ∈ K with the quadrics
    σ_j = τσ_{j−1}. Then the new variety is isomorphic to I_K.
  - #variables − #equations is unchanged. For V_k as quadrics: nk variables, n(k−1) quadrics plus k linear
    forms, excess n − k.
  - Brauer-type results for quadric systems need many more variables than forms. Over a quadratically closed
    field, r quadrics in ≥ 2^r variables, by halving isotropic subspaces. Over E with sign conditions it is
    worse. Here N = nk against r = n(k−1) quadrics: excess n − k, far below 2^r.
  - Cubic rewriting is the same.
- **Overshoot cofactor.** χ_τρ = N is linear in (ρ, c) for fixed τ. Adding ρ adds D − n variables and D − n
  equations. The incidence {(τ, [c])} is a determinantal variety of the same dimension.
- **Padding.** Dummy variables turn X into a cone over X, which is singular at its vertex. All of Wooley's
  results and BHB need smoothness. So padding is excluded by hypothesis, not by an accident of the bounds.
- **Weil restriction.** For L of degree ℓ, R_{L/ℚ} of a CI with r forms in N+1 variables is a CI with ℓr forms of
  the same degrees in ℓ(N+1) variables. Its dimension is ℓ(m+1) − 1 and its degree D^ℓ.
  - Wooley's inequality ℓ(m+1) − 1 ≥ 2^{2^{D^ℓ}} gets strictly harder with ℓ for D ≥ 2.
  - The points it produces are ℚ^sol-points of R_{L/ℚ}X, i.e. points of X over solvable extensions of L, which
    Wooley gives directly.
  - [Wolfram] For the n = 10 sextic route (m = 6, D = 6): ℓ = 1, 2, 3 give dim 6, 13, 20 against
    log₁₀ log₁₀(2^{2^{D^ℓ}}) = 1.28, 10.3, 64.5.
- **Codimension lower bound.**
  - An irreducible nondegenerate Y ⊂ P^N has deg Y ≥ codim Y + 1 [Cited: the classical bound for varieties
    of minimal degree, e.g. Eisenbud–Harris, "On varieties of minimal degree", 1987].
  - If the good-τ locus Y ⊂ P^{n−2} (trace zero) has c conditions, Wooley's Thm 1.1 needs
    n − 2 − c ≥ 2^{2^{c+1}}, and RW needs n − 2 − c ≥ B(c+1).
  - [Wolfram] The smallest n: c = 1 needs n ≥ 19 (Thm 1.1) or n ≥ 2184 (RW); c = 2 needs n ≥ 260 (Thm 1.1).
  - Asymptotically c ≤ log₂ log₂ n − 1 (Thm 1.1), or about log₂ log₂ n / 2 (RW).
  - With power-sum conditions, deg = k!, and k = 3 already needs n > 2^64.

**Bookkeeping law, Wooley version [Proof].** A route through a Wooley-type theorem needs a drawable family whose
good-τ locus has codimension c ≤ log₂ log₂ n in K, i.e. rank ≥ n − log₂ log₂ n. This is strictly stronger than
H2's law (n − O(log n / log log n), via Lemma A and Wolfson). In the low-codimension regime the Wooley route is
dominated by Lemma A, whose degree threshold is k! < n rather than n ≥ 2^{2^{k!}}. Its one advantage is that
it needs no degree induction (solvable or quadratic steps only).

- **Reading.**
  - The nearest miss is n = 9 with the quadric (dimension 6 against 16). That case is classical: Hasse–Minkowski
    or Meyer. It shows Wooley's bounds are far from sharp for quadrics, but no comparably sharp theorem is known

  - The next nearest is MI-1 (dim 6, deg 6). BHB's CI-specific thresholds are 29/37 variables (H3), against 9.
  - Everything else misses by towers of exponentials. **Rewriting cannot close the gap (§4.1).**

### 4.3 What would make the Wooley route work: (BD) [Proof of the implication]

**(BD).** For each n there is a drawable family with P-valued parameters p ∈ A^M, crossing x-coordinate
polynomial C_p, such that:
- for a fixed rational τ ∈ K with distinct conjugates, the fibre Φ_τ = {p : C_p(τ) = 0 in K} (a ℚ-variety, n
  equations) is smooth and geometrically integral, has a smooth real point, and has degree ≤ δ^n with δ
  independent of M;
- M can be taken arbitrarily large.

Then:
- choose M with M − n ≥ B(δ^n);
- RW gives p ∈ Φ_τ(E′) for a real quadratic E′ ⊂ E;
- the crossing y_0 = τ(β) is in P;
- the gcd recovery (H3) gives β.

Comments:
- Linear-support families have δ = 1, which is the trivial case "graphs of degree ≥ n with P coefficients" of
  PLAN §2.4.
- Offset towers have unbounded M, but δ grows with the depth (W1: degree 8 in d at j = 1, and the curve degree
  (j+2)2^j) [Heuristic].
- No combination of the constructors in `Defs.lean` is known to satisfy (BD) [Open].

## 5. Item 3: the odd-degree route

### 5.1 Odd-weight lemma [Proof]

An engine condition depends only on χ_τ, so it is a polynomial f(e_1, …, e_n). Since τ ↦ λτ scales e_k by λ^k,
its degree in τ is its weight.

**Lemma W.** Let S ⊆ {monic χ of degree n} be the engine's image (closed, weighted-conic). If the ideal of S is
generated by polynomials of odd weight, then S ⊇ L_even = {e_k = 0 for all odd k}. In particular
dim S ≥ ⌊n/2⌋, so the engine's rank is ≥ ⌊n/2⌋.

*Proof.* Every monomial of odd weight contains some e_k with k odd, so it vanishes on L_even. L_even ≅ A^{⌊n/2⌋}
(coordinates e_2, e_4, …). ∎

**Corollary (sparse supports) [Proof].**
- For a coordinate engine S = {e_k = 0, k ∈ A}, the conditions are odd iff A ⊆ odd. By Newton's identities,
  p_k for odd k is (−1)^{k−1}k e_k plus monomials each containing an odd-index e of smaller index.
  - [Wolfram] At n = 10: e_1 = e_3 = e_5 = e_7 = 0 forces p_1 = p_3 = p_5 = p_7 = 0, and the lower-order part of
    p_9 vanishes there.
- Then the support of χ contains every exponent n − k with k even, i.e. every exponent ≡ n (mod 2). The family
  needs ≥ ⌊n/2⌋ + 1 free coefficients.
- This is H1's realness bound n ≤ 2F − 3, recovered from parity alone.

**Overshoot (N2's support a).**
- The locus "1, τ, τ³, …, τ^{13} dependent" is cut out by the maximal minors of [τ^e]_{e∈E}. They are
  homogeneous of degree Σ_{e∈E} e = 49 [Wolfram: Σ = 49].
- In general the minors have degree Σe, which is odd iff E has an odd number of odd exponents [Proof].
- So W_O *is* cut out by odd forms. But there are C(n, 8) of them, of degree 49, in n ≤ 13 variables.
  - Birch's theorem needs vastly more variables than forms. Even over ℝ an odd system needs N > #forms for the
    dimension count.
  - Lemma W does not apply: the minors are not symmetric, and the image S of overshoot is defined by the
    condition "χ divides something", not by odd e-polynomials.

### 5.2 Theorem O: rational odd engines for n = 9…13 [Proof of the construction; conditional on one rational point]

Fix n ∈ {9, …, 13}. Let m = (n−1)/2 for n odd and m = (n−2)/2 for n even. Let O_m ⊂ P(K) be the CI of
p_1, p_3, …, p_{2m−1}.

**Theorem O.** Suppose G ⊇ A_n and O_m(ℚ) ≠ ∅, i.e. there is a nonzero rational τ ∈ K with
p_1 = p_3 = … = p_{2m−1} = 0. Then every real root β of q is P-constructible, whatever s is (including s = 0).

*Proof.*
1. **τ is good.** By Lemma N, τ has distinct conjugates.
2. **The shape of χ_τ.** By Newton (§5.1) e_1 = e_3 = … = e_{2m−1} = 0, and χ_τ ∈ ℚ[t].
   - n odd: χ_τ = t·Q(t²) − c, with Q ∈ ℚ[x] monic of degree (n−1)/2 ≤ 6 and c = e_n ∈ ℚ. Here c ≠ 0 because τ
     is invertible.
   - n even: χ_τ = Q(t²) + c·t, with Q ∈ ℚ[x] monic of degree n/2 ≤ 6 and c = −e_{n−1}. If c = 0 then χ_τ is
     even, contradicting Lemma N(ii). So c ≠ 0.
3. **The engine.** Let y_0 = τ(β) ∈ ℝ, a root of χ_τ, so y_0 ≠ 0. Put x_0 = y_0² > 0.
   - n odd: Q(x_0) = c/y_0 = ±c·x_0^{−1/2}.
   - n even: Q(x_0) = −c·y_0 = ∓c·x_0^{1/2}.
   - The sign is that of y_0, and both signs are available as rational coefficients.
   - So (x_0, Q(x_0)) lies on `poly_graph` Q (rational, degree ≤ 6) and on `power_law` y = a·x^b with
     a = ±c ∈ ℚ and b = ∓1/2 ∈ ℚ.
   - Isolate the crossing with `restrict` on a rational window. It is a simple crossing because y_0 is a simple
     root (step 1). Then apply `inter_x`: x_0 ∈ P.
4. **Recovery.** y_0 = ±√x_0 ∈ P, and β is the unique common root of q(x) and τ(x) − y_0 (gcd, H3 §1). ∎

**Realness [Wolfram + Proof].**
- Odd conditions carry no Descartes obstruction (H2 R2). Smooth totally real points exist.
- [Wolfram] For n = 10, 11, 12, 13, take the real roots of T_10 + t/10, T_11 − 1/3, T_12 + t/10, T_13 − 1/3.
  Each has n real roots, all odd power sums up to 2m−1 vanish (|p| < 10^{−38}), and the Jacobian has full rank m
  (smallest singular value 0.18, 0.044, 0.046, 0.011).
- So O_m(ℝ) has smooth points in every totally real K (choose any field ordering), and also for every s.

**Cost, honestly.**

| n | m | dim O_m | deg (2m−1)!! | circle-method exponent n − Σ deg [Wolfram] |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 9 | 4 | 4 | 105 | −7 |
| 10 | 4 | 5 | 105 | −6 |
| 11 | 5 | 5 | 945 | −14 |
| 12 | 5 | 6 | 945 | −13 |
| 13 | 6 | 6 | 10395 | −23 |

For comparison, H5's ℚ-sextic engine V_{n−7} has exponents +6, +4, +1, −3, −8.

- **Wooley Cor. 4.6 (K = ℚ, odd degree) does not apply, for two independent reasons [Proof]:**
  - O_m is singular. At the pairing point (a, −a, 0, …, 0) only two squares occur, so the Jacobian rows
    (2l−1)x^{2l−2} have rank ≤ 2 < m.
  - dim ≤ 6 against B(105) ≈ 10^{10^{33}}.
- Birch's theorem: no explicit constant. The constant for a single cubic over ℚ is ≥ 10, because of p-adic
  anisotropic cubic forms in 9 variables [Cited, classical; not re-opened]. Here there are 4–6 forms of degrees up
  to 11 in ≤ 12 variables.
- [Heuristic] Negative exponents mean that a rational point, if one exists, is not expected from height
  counting. O_m(ℚ) ≠ ∅ for every S_n field of degree 9–13 is therefore **not** a reasonable conjecture as
  stated. It is the exact ingredient Theorem O needs [Open].
- Its advantage over MI-1 is realness only: it allows s = 0. Its disadvantage is arithmetic: the exponent is
  negative where MI-1's is +4.
- **N2's n = 9 version** uses a P-quartic Q and needs only an F-point. Theorem O at n = 9 is the rational
  analogue. For n ≥ 10 a rational τ is needed, because `poly_graph` is over ℚ.

### 5.3 Theorem D: what odd few-form conditions buy [Proof, modulo Davenport]

- Davenport, *Cubic forms in sixteen variables*, Proc. Roy. Soc. A 272 (1963) 285–303 [Cited from memory, not
  re-opened]: every rational cubic form in ≥ 16 variables has a nontrivial rational zero.
- For n ≥ 17, p_3 restricted to the trace-zero hyperplane is a cubic form in n − 1 ≥ 16 variables, so there is a
  nonzero rational τ with p_1 = p_3 = 0.
- By Lemma N it is a good Tschirnhaus element when G ⊇ A_n.
- Heath-Brown, *Cubic forms in 14 variables*, Invent. Math. 170 (2007) 199–230, lowers this to n ≥ 15 if that
  cubic is nonsingular. Nonsingularity was not checked.
- Using it would need an engine that leaves exactly {e_1 = e_3 = 0}, i.e. a drawable family of rank n − 2
  containing every other coefficient. None exists, so Theorem D measures **capacity**, not progress.
- With p_5 added, no theorem with a usable explicit bound is known to me.

## 6. Item 4: verdict

**Theorem X′ (a variant of X1's Theorem X) [Proof, conditional].**
- *Setting.* β is real algebraic of degree n ≥ 9, with the square lift K′ = ℚ(√(β − c)) as in X1 §3.3 (one or
  two real places).
- *Hypothesis.* There is a drawable family whose P-valued parameters range over an affine space, whose good locus
  Y_{K′} (or the parameter fibre Φ_τ over some rational τ) is smooth and geometrically integral over ℚ, has a
  smooth real point, and satisfies dim ≥ B(deg).
- *Conclusion.* β ∈ P.
- *Proof.*
  - RW gives a point over a real quadratic E′ ⊂ ℝ.
  - The point must also have distinct conjugates. Lemma N does **not** cover K′: its group lies in C2 ≀ S_n,
    which is not 2-transitive on 2n points, and K′ has the subfield K. So nondegeneracy is an **extra
    hypothesis** here: Y_{K′} ∩ Δ must have no E′-point, where Δ is the degenerate locus. An alternative is a
    density version of RW, which is [Open].
  - On the base field K (no lift), Lemma N gives nondegeneracy for free.
  - The engine crossing and gcd recovery are as in X1 §3.3 steps 3–4.
- *Status.* The hypothesis is a dim/deg condition, so RW discharges it. **It is not satisfied by any family
  measured so far, for any n ≥ 9** (§4.2 table). It would be satisfied for all n under (BD) (§4.3).

**Answer to the question asked.** Yes, the existence problem can be recast so that a Wooley-type theorem yields
*real* points over P-reachable fields. RW does this, with real quadratic extensions, which is better than
solvable. The only hypothesis beyond Wooley's own is a smooth real point. But no available engine produces a
variety in Wooley's range:
- the incidence dimension is fixed by the engine (§4.1);
- the degree is at least the number of conditions plus one;
- Wooley's dimension requirement is doubly exponential in the degree.

The odd route revives rational `poly_graph` engines for every real-root pattern up to n = 13 (Theorem O), at
the price of a rational point on a singular odd complete intersection with negative height exponent. No
odd-degree theorem (Birch, Cor. 4.6) reaches it. All negatives above are scoped to the theorems and engine
families named.

## 7. Other things noticed

- **Lemma N simplifies earlier notes.**
  - The "avoid the pairing P³'s" caveat (NOTES-general, N2, H2) is vacuous for rational, E- or F-points when
    G ⊇ A_n.
  - The degenerate point N2's box search found (τ = 1) is excluded by the trace-zero normalisation alone.
  - Pairing points matter only over fields containing a piece of the splitting field, i.e. for searches over
    number fields L with L ∩ Ω ≠ ℚ.
- **Proposition D** shows that over E the diagonal layer of Brauer is *exactly* the real layer: a linear program
  over an ordered field. A future sign-tracked Brauer should be phrased as "every auxiliary system has a real
  zero", and the difficulty is that this is not inherited.
- **Even n in Theorem O** uses the exponent +1/2 (`power_law` with b = 1/2) and the support {0, 1, 2, 4, …, n}.
  Descartes capacity is n (gaps 1, 1, 2, …, 2) [Proof], so totally real fields fit. This even-n odd engine
  (χ = Q(t²) + ct) does not appear in N2's table, which listed t·P6(t²) − a and P6(t²) − a·t^m with m odd ≤ 11
  for overshoot. At exact degree it is the m = 1 case of N2's support (c), and it is new as an exact engine for
  n = 10, 12.
- **Theorem O with P-quartic Q** (N2's n = 9 engine) needs F-points, not rational ones. With E- or F-points the
  even-n analogue would be n = 8 only, which is already done.
- **RW and Taylor's question are complementary.** RW settles the high-dimension, any-degree case of "real
  solvable points (in fact quadratic) on varieties with a smooth real point". Taylor's question concerns all
  dimensions. (SR) sits at dimension 2, the opposite extreme.
- **Wolfram notes.** Large factorials inside `2^D` abort the kernel. Compute log₁₀ log₁₀ of the bounds
  directly (§4.2). `NSolve[..., Reals, WorkingPrecision -> 40]` on Chebyshev perturbations gives clean real
  witnesses.
