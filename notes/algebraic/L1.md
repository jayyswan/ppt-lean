# L1 literature search: PAC/PRC/Northcott for bounded-degree towers
Status: done

## Verdict
(a) (PRC_8) is [Not found] in the literature: no paper treats PAC/PRC/ample properties of fields
    closed under bounded-degree (<=8) real roots. Taylor's question (totally real solvable) and
    "is Q^solv PAC" remain OPEN as far as I can find [Cited: Bary-Soroker-Fehm arXiv:1106.1310;
    Jarden-Razon; Fried-Jarden Problem 10.16(a) via arXiv math/0305226; Rawson JTNB 2024 as
    still-open context]. Not verified against current 2025-26 literature beyond search snippets.
(b) Northcott gives NO obstruction. [Cited: Fehm, "Three counterexamples concerning the Northcott
    property of fields", Atti Accad. Naz. Lincei 2018, arXiv:1708.06599; restated as Prop 1.2 in
    Checcoli-Fehm arXiv:2401.03730]: "There exists a Galois extension K/Q such that K is pseudo-
    algebraically closed and has the Northcott property." (This was against expectation; motivated
    by Amoroso-David-Zannier.) So "Northcott => not PAC" is FALSE. Also, correction to the premise:
    Bombieri-Zannier (2001) proved Northcott for the compositum of all ABELIAN extensions of
    degree <=d of a number field; for Q^(d) (all extensions of degree <=d) Northcott is OPEN for
    d>2 (yes for d=2) [Cited: Checcoli-Widmer arXiv:1111.5060; Checcoli-Fehm 2401.03730].
    Checcoli-Widmer: for Galois K/Q of finite exponent, K^(d) has the Bogomolov property.
    [Inferred] Our E (iterated real towers deg<=8 + all solvable reals) is not contained in
    Q^(d) (unbounded degree, solvable numbers), so none of the Northcott/Bogomolov results apply
    to it anyway; and E is not known to have Northcott, and ought NOT to (it contains Q^solv-real,
    which contains elements of tiny height relative to degree... not checked).
(c) Obstruction-free but also tool-free: the only known PAC/PRC subfields of Q-bar are built by
    Galois-theoretic/Hilbertian-style compositum constructions (Fehm; Moret-Bailly; Green-Pop-
    Roquette for Q^tr). Nothing for degree-bounded towers.

## Details by question
Q1/Q2. [Cited: arXiv:1708.06599] method: K = compositum of finite Galois N_i/Q chosen so each
  variety X_i gets a point over N_i, hence X_i(K) nonempty => PAC. Jarden: no number field has a
  Galois extension that is PAC over it except Q-tilde (stated in search snippet of 1708.06599 /
  Jarden refs) [verify exact statement before citing]. Gal-Grizzard arXiv:1210.4217: compositum of
  all degree-d extensions of a number field contains all lower-degree ones iff d<5 [snippet; this is
  about Q^(d) structure, not PAC]. Checcoli-Fehm "Fields of algebraic numbers with bounded local
  degrees" arXiv:1012.0984 (Checcoli-Fehm-ish; actually Checcoli, Bounded local degrees) concerns
  fields with bounded local degrees (Q^(d) is one). [Not found]: any statement that Q^(d) or the
  iterated degree-<=d tower is/ isn't PAC, ample, or PRC. Iterated vs compositum: Q^(d) is the
  compositum in one step (Galois-theoretically a fixed-ish finite-exponent-like object); iterated
  tower closure has unbounded degree and, for d>=5, contains Q^solv-ish pieces; I found no study.
Q3. State of play: Pal (Ambrus Pal, "Solvable points on projective algebraic curves", Canad. J.
  Math 2006): every curve of genus 0,2,3,4 over a number field has a solvable point; genus >=40
  fails over some LOCAL fields. Ciperiani-Wiles: genus 1 over Q has solvable points. Rawson,
  "Some obstructions to solvable points on higher genus curves", JTNB 2024, arXiv:2211.10367:
  assuming Bombieri-Lang, for genus>=5 rational points on C^n/G (G solvable) are not Zariski dense
  => suggests (conditionally) obstructions, not a proof that Q^solv is non-PAC. Also arXiv:1501.01789
  "Solvable points on smooth projective varieties" (Gomez-Gonzalez? author not verified).
  Q^solv PAC: still open [Cited: arXiv math/0305226 quoting FJ Problem 10.16(a)]. Note these are
  about complex/non-real solvable points; real/totally real variant = Taylor Q5.5.
  [Inferred] Rawson's Bombieri-Lang conditional result hints that even Q^solv might be non-PAC
  (it would predict curves of genus>=5 with no solvable points of bounded Galois-group type, though
  Q^solv allows unbounded solvable groups, so not a contradiction).
Q4. Origami (Alperin arXiv math/9912039; Oller Marcen arXiv:0709.3270; arXiv:1408.0880): real
  origami field = smallest subfield of R closed under real cube roots and sqrt of positives;
  elements = those whose splitting field has degree 2^r 3^s. [Not found]: any study of PAC/PRC
  or points on varieties over origami/conic/neusis/Pierpont fields. Such fields are tiny
  (Galois groups {2,3}-groups), so they are certainly not PAC (a PAC field needs projective Galois
  group; also not of interest for E). [Inferred]
