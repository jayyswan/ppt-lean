# X1: Galois-class induction (the user's proposal), the realness problem, and a conditional reduction

Stream X1 of `pptc/PLAN-algebraic.md`. Research only: no `.lean` edits, no commits. Labels: [Proof], [Cited],
[Wolfram], [Heuristic], [Open]. Every obstruction is scoped to the route it blocks. Nothing here is a negative
about the target. Backend: Wolfram for every computation (no Python).

Notation. `F = P ∩ ℝ_alg` (a real field). `F(i)` is its complex companion. For a field E ⊂ ℂ and a finite group
G, a *G-extension* of E is a Galois extension M/E inside ℂ with Gal(M/E) ≅ G. c is complex conjugation. μ(T)
is the minimal index of a proper subgroup of a simple group T, i.e. its minimal permutation degree.
`R(N)` means: F is closed under real roots of polynomials of degree ≤ N over F. The repo proves R(8).
`H_ℂ(N)` means: F(i) is closed under all roots of polynomials of degree ≤ N over F(i).

## 0. Findings summary

1. **The right object is complex, and with it the tower lemma is a one-line proof [Proof].**
   - A conjugation-stable field M ∋ i satisfies M = (M ∩ ℝ)(i). So "α ∈ P for all real α in a class" is the
     same as "F(i) contains the class".
   - **Tower lemma.** Let 𝒞 be a class of simple groups that is closed under simple sections. If F(i) has
     *no G-extension* for every G ∈ 𝒞, then F(i) contains every algebraic number whose Galois closure over ℚ
     has all composition factors in 𝒞. Hence A(𝒞) ⊆ P. Realness disappears from the statement; it moves into
     the hypothesis "F(i) has no G-extension".
   - The hypothesis H(n) is equivalent to `ℚ^{𝒞_{≤n}} ⊆ F(i)`. The form that inducts is the *relative* one,
     H_ℂ(n) (F(i) closed under degree ≤ n). H_ℂ(n) implies A(𝒞_{≤n}) ⊆ P and R(n). Conversely,
     **R(n²) implies H_ℂ(n)**, because Re z is a real root of a degree-n² resolvent (§2.6).
2. **The cyclic class is done [Proof; Lean-feasible from existing lemmas].**
   - F(i) is closed under complex n-th roots, via `sqrt`, `arccos_Pconstructible_of_mem_Icc`, `rpow`, and
     `cos`/`sin`. Hence it has no cyclic extensions (Kummer).
   - So **ℚ^{solv} ⊆ F(i), i.e. every real number with solvable Galois closure is in P**. This makes W3's
     sketch rigorous.
   - Also H_ℂ(4) holds: F(i) is closed under roots of degree ≤ 4 over F(i).
3. **The base case A(𝒞_{<9}) ⊆ P is *not* reachable from the repo by real towers [Proof, route-scoped].**
   - 𝒞_{<9} = {cyclic} ∪ {A5, A6, A7, A8, PSL(2,7)} [Proof + Wolfram]. PSL(2,8) and PSL(3,4) are *not*
     sections of S8.
   - A *c-chain lemma* (§2.3) says exactly which (group, conjugation) configurations the repo's tools (real
     roots of degree ≤ 8, plus solvable steps) reach. Three configurations fail:
     - **swap.** A chief factor T×T whose two copies are exchanged by c. Every real-tower route then needs a
       nonsolvable step of degree ≥ min(μ(T)², |T|), which is ≥ 25 for T = A5.
       - Explicit [Wolfram + Proof]: u = 2 Re z, z a root of `y⁵+y+1+2i`, has an irreducible degree-25
         minimal polynomial over ℚ. Its Galois closure has composition factors A5, A5 and C2's. Every real
         algebraic tower to u needs a step of degree ≥ 25.
     - **A8 with c fixed-point-free.** Example: `x⁸+28x+98` has Galois group A8 and no real roots
       [Wolfram + Proof]. Every real element of its splitting field outside ℚ has degree ≥ 15 [Proof mod ATLAS].
     - **PGL(2,9) with c outer** (A6 chief factor): degree ≥ 10 [Proof mod standard subgroup facts].
   - The cases that *are* covered: every simple chief factor T ∈ {A5, …, A8, PSL(2,7)} on which c acts
     trivially, by an inner involution with a fixed point, or by an outer involution of S5, S6, S7, S8 or
     PGL(2,7) (§2.4).
4. **What is precisely missing for the base case: complex roots [Proof of the reduction].**
   - The missing statement is H_ℂ(5)…H_ℂ(8), i.e. complex roots of degree 5–8 polynomials over F(i).
   - For degree 5 this collapses to a one-complex-parameter statement:
     **BR5: the roots of z⁵ + z + c lie in F(i) for every c ∈ F(i).** The reason is that Bring–Jerrard
     needs only radicals, and radicals are free.
   - For real c, BR5 already holds [Proof].
   - The repo proves nothing about real parts of complex roots (searched; §2.5).
5. **Inductive step, obstacle (a), W1 squares [Proof of the structure; verdict scoped].**
   - The condition is "Q(m) is a square in K ⊗ L". It is a K-point condition on a genus-1 curve E_B, i.e. it
     asks that E_B(K_F) be bigger than E_B(F). Enlarging the parameter field L (to solvable numbers, or to
     F_{<n}) does not touch it.
   - Working in K(√Q) instead is fine for the A_n filter (C2 ≀ S_n has only C2 and A_n factors), but each
     square-rooted layer doubles the ambient dimension, so the count becomes 3j + 4 ≳ 2^{j'}·n. The extra
     depth brings new square conditions of its own, so the rearrangement does not close (§3.1).
   - What would dissolve it is Mordell–Weil growth E(K_F) ≠ E(F) with explicit points, which is [Open].
6. **Inductive step, obstacle (b), MI-1 [Proof + Wolfram; verdict scoped].**
   - Over F, the variety V'_K has F-points *unconditionally* (Lemma A with degree 6 ≤ 8, smooth real points

7. **Headline (task 3c): a conditional theorem for the full target [Proof, conditional on one named open
   question].**
   - Combine three ingredients:
     - the trinomial engine `power_law x^{2n}` × line;
     - the square lift y² = β − c, which leaves at most 2 real conjugates;
     - the proved fact ℚ^{solv} ∩ ℝ ⊆ P.
   - Then **every real algebraic number is P-constructible provided the twisted trinomial surface I_{K'} has
     a point over ℚ^{solv} ∩ ℝ**.
   - This holds if **Taylor's question** (as stated by Bary-Soroker–Fehm, arXiv:1106.1310, §3.2) has a
     positive answer: is ℚ_{tot,S} ∩ ℚ^{solv} PSC, taken for S = {∞}, i.e. do totally real solvable numbers
     satisfy the local-global principle for smooth varieties with a real point?
   - No Tschirnhaus cost and no degree induction are involved. The whole target reduces to the arithmetic of
     *solvable real points*, which is exactly the user's intuition ("solvable is free").
   - Status of that question: open. The complex analogue "is ℚ^{solv} PAC?" is also open (Fried–Jarden
     Problem 11.5.9(a)). The unconditional analogue for *all* totally real numbers is a theorem
     (Moret-Bailly 1989; Green–Pop–Roquette 1995), but its fields are not known to lie in P.

## 1. Formulating the hypothesis

### 1.1 Classes

- 𝒞₀ = cyclic groups of prime order.
- 𝒞_{<n} = simple groups that are composition factors of subgroups of S_m for some m < n. Always add 𝒞₀:
  cyclic factors of every prime order are free (§2.2).
- 𝒞^μ_{≤d} = simple T with μ(T) ≤ d, i.e. T embeds transitively in S_d.
- A(𝒞) = real algebraic α whose Galois closure L/ℚ has every composition factor in 𝒞 ∪ 𝒞₀.
  ℚ^𝒞 = the same for complex α. ℚ^𝒞 is a field, conjugation-stable and containing i, and A(𝒞) = ℚ^𝒞 ∩ ℝ
  [Proof: groups with factors in a class are closed under subgroups (for section-closed classes), quotients,
  products and extensions].

**Which Galois group?** The normal closure over ℚ is the right invariant for *membership* (H(n) ⟺ ℚ^{𝒞_{≤n}}
⊆ F(i)). For *proofs* the relevant object is relative: the composition factors of Gal(LF(i)/F(i)), a subgroup
of Gal(L/ℚ). Its factors are sections of the factors of Gal(L/ℚ) [Proof: intersect a composition series with
the subgroup; Jordan–Hölder]. That is why the class must be section-closed. Realness enters only through the
pair (Γ, c), with Γ = Gal(L(i)/ℚ) and c ∈ Γ (§2.3). A tower of real fields is the wrong object: the real
subfield L^c is not Galois, and the c-chain lemma below is what replaces it.

### 1.2 Sections versus embeddings

**Fact S.** A nonabelian composition factor T of a subgroup H ≤ S_n has μ(T) ≤ n. So 𝒞_{<n+1} = 𝒞^μ_{≤n}.
[Proof modulo the Schreier conjecture (CFSG).] By induction on n:
- *H intransitive.* H is a subdirect product of its constituents H₁ ≤ S_{n₁}, H₂ ≤ S_{n₂}, so H/(H∩H₂) ≅ H₁
  and H∩H₂ ≤ H₂.
- *H imprimitive.* The kernel of the action on blocks is intransitive; the block action has degree < n.
- *H primitive.* Let N = T₀^k be a minimal normal subgroup.
  - Affine case: the point stabiliser acts faithfully on n − 1 points.
  - Otherwise T₀ acts nontrivially, hence faithfully, on the n points, so μ(T₀) ≤ n.
  - The centraliser C_H(N) is 1 or ≅ N.
  - H/(N·C_H(N)) embeds in Out(T₀) ≀ S_k. Out(T₀) is solvable (Schreier), and k ≤ n/4 because N contains an
    elementary abelian 2-group of rank 2k ≤ n/2.

For n ≤ 8 there is a direct proof (§1.3).

### 1.4 Target statements

- H(n): A(𝒞_{<n+1}) ⊆ P. Equivalently ℚ^{𝒞_{≤n}} ⊆ F(i).
- **H_ℂ(n)** (relative, the one that inducts): F(i) is closed under roots of polynomials over F(i) of degree
  ≤ n. Equivalently, by Fact S, F(i) has no T-extension for any T with μ(T) ≤ n.
- Implications [Proof]:
  - H_ℂ(n) ⇒ H(n), by the tower lemma.
  - H_ℂ(n) ⇒ R(n), since a real root lies in F(i) ∩ ℝ = F.
  - R(n²) ⇒ H_ℂ(n) (§2.6).
  - target ⟺ H_ℂ(n) for all n ⟺ R(n) for all n.
- **Why the relative form is needed.** H(n−1) speaks only about ℚ-Galois classes. The step to H(n) needs
  old-group extensions (say A5) of fields that already contain new-group elements (say A9). That is a
  statement about F(i), not about ℚ^{𝒞_{<n}}. An induction H(n−1) ⇒ H(n) without the relative form has no
  hypothesis to use there.

## 2. The tower lemma and realness

### 2.1 Tower lemma [Proof]

**Lemma.** Let 𝒞 be section-closed and E ⊂ ℂ a field with no G-extension for any G ∈ 𝒞. If α ∈ ℚ̄ has
Galois closure L/ℚ with composition factors in 𝒞, then α ∈ E.

*Proof.*
- LE/E is Galois with group H ≤ Gal(L/ℚ), and the factors of H lie in 𝒞 (section-closed).
- If H ≠ 1, take a maximal normal subgroup N ◁ H. Then H/N is simple, so it lies in 𝒞. Its fixed field is
  an H/N-extension of E, which contradicts the hypothesis.
- So H = 1 and L ⊆ E. ∎

**Realness reformulation [Proof].**
- If M ⊂ ℂ is conjugation-stable and i ∈ M, then M = (M∩ℝ)(i), via z = Re z + i Im z.
- So for conjugation-stable N ∋ i: N ⊆ F(i) ⟺ N ∩ ℝ ⊆ F.
- "F(i) has no G-extension" is therefore equivalent to: for every finite Galois N/F with i ∈ N and a quotient
  of Gal(N/F(i)) isomorphic to G, the real elements of N are in F.

### 2.2 The cyclic class: ℚ^{solv} ⊆ F(i) [Proof; Lean-feasible]

Take z = a + bi ∈ F(i), z ≠ 0.
- r = √(a²+b²) ∈ F (`sqrt_Pconstructible`).
- θ = ±arccos(a/r) ∈ P (`arccos_Pconstructible_of_mem_Icc`, sign of b).
- Then r^{1/n}, cos(θ/n), sin(θ/n) ∈ P (`rpow_Pconstructible`, `cos_Pconstructible`, `sin_Pconstructible`,
  Basic.lean:1762, 2652–2657). They are algebraic, so they lie in F.
- So w = r^{1/n}(cos(θ/n) + i sin(θ/n)) ∈ F(i) with wⁿ = z.
- ζ_n = cos(2π/n) + i sin(2π/n) ∈ F(i) (`pi_Pconstructible`), so all n roots lie in F(i).

Consequences:
- F(i) contains μ_∞ and is radically closed.
- By Kummer theory every C_p-extension of F(i) is F(i)(ω^{1/p}) = F(i). So there are no cyclic extensions.
- The tower lemma gives ℚ^{solv} ⊆ F(i): **every real algebraic number with solvable Galois closure is in P.**
- Also H_ℂ(4): the Galois group of a degree ≤ 4 polynomial is solvable, so the tower lemma applies over
  F(i) itself.
- Not stated in the repo (no theorem mentions ℂ, radicals or real parts; checked with `rg`). The proof uses
  only existing lemmas plus Mathlib's Kummer/Galois theory; the formalization cost lies in the latter.

### 2.3 The c-chain lemma: what real towers can and cannot reach [Proof]

**Setting.**
- α is real algebraic, L/ℚ is the Galois closure of ℚ(α, i), Γ = Gal(L/ℚ), and c ∈ Γ is conjugation.
- A *real algebraic tower* is a chain of real fields ℚ = E₀ ⊂ E₁ ⊂ … ⊂ E_k ∋ α. Each step E_{j+1} = E_j(θ_j)
  is one of:
  - (i) θ_j is a real root of a polynomial over E_j of degree ≤ 8; or
  - (ii) the Galois closure of E_{j+1}(i)/E_j(i) is solvable. This covers the radical steps of §2.2.
- Intermediate fields may lie outside L.

**Lemma (c-chain).** Such a tower exists iff there is a chain Γ = Γ₀ ≥ Γ₁ ≥ … ≥ Γ_k with c ∈ Γ_k ⊆
Stab_Γ(α), all Γ_j ∋ c, where each step either
- has index ≤ 8, or
- has solvable *step group*: the image of Γ_{j} acting on Γ_j/Γ_{j+1}.

*Proof.*
- (⇐) The fixed fields L^{Γ_j} are real because c ∈ Γ_j. Each step is (i), by a primitive element of degree
  equal to the index, or (ii).
- (⇒) Let L̃ be the Galois closure of the whole tower, with Γ̃ ↠ Γ. Project the chain of stabilisers of E_j.
  - The surjection of Γ̃_j-sets Γ̃_j/Γ̃_{j+1} → π(Γ̃_j)/π(Γ̃_{j+1}) is equivariant. So the projected index is
    no larger, and the projected step group is a quotient of the original step group.
  - c ∈ Γ̃_j projects to c. ∎

(The rational nonic theorem adds a step of index 9 at the base ℚ. It changes nothing below.)

**Swap lemma.** Suppose N ◁ Γ with N ≅ T×T (T nonabelian simple) and c exchanges the two factors, and α is
not fixed by N. Then every c-chain contains a step of index ≥ min(μ(T)², |T|) whose step group contains a copy
of T×T. In particular, real algebraic towers with steps (i), (ii) cannot reach α when μ(T)² > 8 and |T| > 8,
i.e. always.

*Proof.*
- Let Γ_j be the first group of the chain not containing N.
- The core of Γ_j in Γ_{j−1} meets N in a normal subgroup of N normalised by c. That is 1 or N, so it is 1.
  Hence the step group contains N ≅ T×T, and it is nonsolvable.
- The index satisfies [Γ_{j−1}:Γ_j] ≥ [N : N∩Γ_j]. Here D = N∩Γ_j is a proper c-stable subgroup of T×T.
- Normalise coordinates so that c acts by (a,b) ↦ (b,a). Then the projections satisfy π₂(D) = π₁(D) =: A.
  - If A ≠ T, then [T×T : D] ≥ [T:A]² ≥ μ(T)².
  - If A = T, Goursat (with T simple) gives D = T×T, which is excluded, or a graph, of index |T|. ∎

**Inner-involution variant.** Suppose N ≅ T ◁ Γ is simple, c ∉ N, and c acts on N as conjugation by an
involution g₀ (or trivially). Then the first step leaving N has index ≥ min{[T:A] : A < T proper, normalised
by g₀}. For A ≠ T the condition "normalised by g₀" is the same as g₀ ∈ N_T(A). For maximal A it is g₀ ∈ A.

**Verdict on the base case [Proof, route-scoped].** A(𝒞_{<9}) ⊆ P does **not** follow from R(8) plus

configuration avoids the three bad rows. The scope is real algebraic towers. Derivations through
transcendental intermediates (other than radicals) are not covered by the lemma, and nothing here says P
misses u.

### 2.6 Reductions that are proved

- **R(n²) ⇒ H_ℂ(n) [Proof].**
  - Let g ∈ F(i)[X] have degree n and root z. Then Res_y(g(y), ḡ(2x − y)) ∈ F[x] has degree n² and real
    root Re z. (Its coefficients are fixed by conjugation, which swaps g and ḡ.)
  - Then Im z: z − Re z is a root of g(X + Re z). So y = Im z is a common real root of the real and imaginary
    parts of g(Re z + iY), which are not both 0. That is a degree ≤ n real polynomial over F.
  - So **R(N) ⇒ A(𝒞_{≤⌊√N⌋}) ⊆ P**. Under the plan's real induction, the Galois-class parameter field
    available at degree N contains A(𝒞_{≤√N}), but not A(𝒞_{<N}) (swap witnesses).
- **H_ℂ(5) ⟺ BR5 [Proof; classical algebra].**
  - Over the quadratically closed, radically closed field F(i), Bring–Jerrard reduces any quintic to
    z⁵ + az + b:
    - kill e₁ (linear);
    - take a line on the quadric {e₁ = e₂ = 0} in 4 variables (F(i) is quadratically closed);
    - solve a cubic on it by Cardano.
  - Scaling with λ⁴ = a gives z⁵ + z + c. If a = 0, the equation is a radical.
  - So H_ℂ(5) holds iff the roots of z⁵ + z + c are in F(i) for every c ∈ F(i).
- **BR5 for real c [Proof].** c ∈ F: z⁵ + z + c has one real root (R(5)) and 2 conjugate pairs, so
  c = (12)(34) fixes a point. By the c-chain lemma S5 > S4 > solvable reaches every real element of the
  splitting field. The gap is genuinely complex c: two real parameters.
- For n = 6, 7, 8 the classical reductions over a radically closed field leave 2, 3 and 4 complex parameters
  ("resolvent degree" bounds; see G1 for citations, not re-opened here) [Cited via G1].

## 3. The inductive step with an enlarged parameter field

Setting: q ∈ ℚ[x] of degree n with group S_n or A_n, K = ℚ[x]/(q), and K_F = K ⊗ F, a field because q stays
irreducible over F (otherwise R(n−1) already gives β). The enlarged parameter field is either
F_𝒞 = A(𝒞_{<n}) or, more honestly, whatever is proved: F ⊇ ℚ^{solv} ∩ ℝ, plus R(8).

### 3.1 (a) W1's offset-tower square conditions

W1 §3: layer j of the backward chain needs |v|² = u(m)ᵀ B u(m) to be a square **in K**, with m ∈ K and the
entries of B in the parameter field L.

- **Roots in L do not help [Proof].**
  - The square root must be of a K-element. With m ∈ L the layer is constant across the n crossings, and the
    K-freedom of that layer (n dimensions) is lost; W1's count r = 3j + 4 needs m to vary in K.
  - Over K_L the condition is a K_L-point (m, s) of the genus-1 curve E_B: s² = u(m)ᵀBu(m), defined over L,
    with m ∉ L.
  - E_B has L-points: for positive definite B and m ∈ L, s = √(uᵀBu) lies in L, which contains real square
    roots. So E_B is an elliptic curve over L, and the need is **E_B(K_L) ≠ E_B(L)** with control over the
    points (an L-point of the trace-zero part of the Weil restriction R_{K_L/L}E_B).
  - Torsion does not supply such points [Heuristic]: K_L/L is primitive of degree n ≥ 9 with group A_n or
    S_n, while torsion fields have groups inside GL₂(ℤ/N), whose nonabelian factors are only A5 and PSL(2,p)
    (H4). For n ≥ 9 this makes a coincidence of fields implausible, but it was not proved here.
  - So it is a Mordell–Weil growth question over the non-finitely-generated field L [Open]. Enlarging L from
    ℚ to ℚ^{solv} or F changes neither its nature nor its degree.
- **Working in K' = K_L(√Q(m)) is harmless for the Galois class, but not for the count [Proof, given W1's
  ranks].**
  - The Galois closure of K' over L has group ≤ C2 ≀ S_n, whose factors are C2's and A_n. That is exactly
    the engine's allowed load.
  - But K' has dimension 2n. Each square-rooted layer doubles the ambient dimension, so a tower with j'
    square-rooted layers needs effective rank ≥ 2^{j'}·n (less a few cheap conditions) against W1's 3j + 4.
  - For n ≥ 10 the depth needed with j' = 0 is already j ≈ n/3, and from depth 2 every non-conformal layer
    carries a square condition (W1). Square-rooting even one layer requires 3j + 4 ≥ 2n, i.e. j ≥ (2n−4)/3,
    and all the extra layers bring their own square conditions.
  - Verdict: the rearrangement does not dissolve the obstacle.
- **The other rearrangement.**
  - One can interpolate B from (m, s) ∈ K² instead: W1's "F-linear dependence of four K-vectors".
  - This is a determinantal locus of codimension n − 3. Its Porteous degree is the complete homogeneous
    symmetric polynomial h_{n−3}(6, 4, 4, 2) ≥ 6^{n−3}, so Lemma A never applies [Proof of the lower bound
    for the Porteous class; Heuristic that it equals the true degree].
- **What would dissolve it:** explicit growth E_B(K_L) ≠ E_B(L), e.g. from a family of B for which
  u(m)ᵀBu(m) is a square times a conic (W1's wish 1) [Open]. A PSC-type hypothesis on L (§3.3) would also
  dissolve it, because the Weil restriction is a geometrically integral L-variety with smooth real points.
  That is only a remark, though: §3.3 does not need towers at all.

### 3.2 (b) MI-1 at degree 10

- **F-points of V'_K exist unconditionally [Proof].**
  - V'_K = {p₂ = p₃ = 0} ⊂ P(trace-zero K) is smooth of degree 6 (H5).
  - Its real points exist for s ≥ 1. The real cone {p₂ = 0} has signature (a, b) = (r+s−1, s) with a ≥ 2,
    so C_ℝ \ 0 ≅ S^{a−1}×S^{b−1}×ℝ_{>0} is connected and contains x and −x. The odd form p₃ therefore
    vanishes on it by the intermediate value theorem. This makes H5's sketch a proof.
  - Lemma A (H2) needs closure under degree ≤ deg V' = 6, which R(8) gives. So F-points are dense near every
    real point.
  - So with F-parameters, χ_τ = y¹⁰ − G_τ(y) with G_τ ∈ F[y] of degree ≤ 6 is available for every decic K
    with s ≥ 1.
- **What remains is the rationality of G_τ up to the drawable affine group [Proof + Wolfram].**
  - The drawable partner is c·G((x−b)/a) + ex + d with G ∈ ℚ[x] (`poly_graph`) and a, b, c, d, e ∈ P. So
    G_τ mod linear terms must lie in the 3-dimensional (a, b, c)-orbit of a rational sextic.
  - Two invariants decide this: after the shift that kills h₅,
    J₁ = h₄³/(h₆h₃²) and J₂ = h₄h₂/h₃².
  - [Wolfram] Both are invariant under (a, b, c), and both have weighted degree 30 in τ
    (h_k = ±e_{10−k}(τ)).
  - So the problem is: for some j ∈ ℚ², find an F-point on W_j = V'_K ∩ {J₁ = j₁} ∩ {J₂ = j₂}, a 4-fold
    of degree ≤ 6·30·30 = 5400 [Bézout bound; actual degree not computed].
  - Lemma A needs degree ≤ 8 now (< 10 given R(9)). Not applicable.
  - The real points of V'_K have J values filling an open set of ℝ², so F-points with J arbitrarily close to
    rational exist, but never provably equal [Heuristic].
- **Verdict.**
  - The enlarged field solves the "p₂ = p₃ = 0" half of MI-1 outright. It leaves two rationality
    conditions on a high-degree fibre.
  - The structural reason: `poly_graph` imposes ℚ-conditions, and no enlargement of the parameter field
    helps a condition of the form "x ∈ ℚ". Solvable τ (coordinates in a solvable L) gives χ_τ ∈ L[y] and
    meets the same two conditions.
  - What does help is an engine whose every parameter slot accepts P-values. The trinomial engine of §3.3 is
    one: `power_law` with a rational exponent against a line with P-coefficients.

### 3.3 (c) A formulation that needs only points over solvable real fields

**Theorem X (conditional) [Proof, conditional on (SR)].**

Let β be a real algebraic number. Choose:
- a rational Möbius map making β the largest real conjugate (H2 §6);
- a rational c strictly between the two largest real conjugates;
- y₀ = √(β − c);
- K' = ℚ(y₀), of degree D (2n, or n when q(y² + c) = h(y)h(−y) splits). The only real roots of q(y² + c)
  are ±y₀, so K' has exactly 1 or 2 real places;
- I_{K'} = {(τ', a, b) : τ' ∈ K' ⊗ A¹ with distinct conjugates, χ_{τ'}(x) = x^D + ax + b}, a ℚ-variety.

Hypothesis **(SR)**: I_{K'} has a point with coordinates in ℚ^{solv} ∩ ℝ. Under (SR), β ∈ P.

*Proof.*
1. *Geometry.*
   - Over ℚ̄, K'⊗ℚ̄ ≅ ℚ̄^D, and I_{K'} becomes the variety of ordered D-tuples of distinct roots of a common
     trinomial.
   - The monodromy of x^D + ax + b is S_D. Fix a ≠ 0 and vary b: the map x ↦ −(x^D + ax) has D − 1 simple
     critical points with pairwise distinct critical values (1 − 1/D)·a·x. So the monodromy is transitive and
     generated by transpositions, hence it is S_D [Proof, standard lemma].
   - So the ordered-roots variety is irreducible, and I_{K'}, a twisted form of it, is geometrically
     integral.
   - It is smooth, being étale over the (a, b)-plane at simple roots.
2. *A smooth real point.*
   - K'⊗ℝ = ℝ^{r'} × ℂ^{(D−r')/2} with r' ∈ {1, 2}, and r' ≡ D mod 2.
   - A real trinomial with exactly r' real roots exists: for D even, b < 0 gives 2; for D odd, a > 0 gives 1
     (x^D + ax is then monotone). For generic parameters all roots are simple.
   - Send the real places to the real roots and the complex places to the conjugate pairs. This defines
     τ' ∈ K'⊗ℝ by interpolation, and it is a smooth real point.
3. *Using an (SR)-point.*
   - (SR) gives (τ', a, b) over L ⊆ ℚ^{solv} ∩ ℝ ⊆ F (§2.2).
   - x₀ = τ'(y₀) is real and a root of x^D + ax + b.
   - Draw `power_law` y = x^D (rational exponent; `scale_x` by −1 for x < 0) and the line y = −ax − b
     (P-coefficients). Isolate x₀ with `restrict` on a rational window and apply `inter_x`. So x₀ ∈ P.
4. *Recovery.*
   - Let q̃ be the minimal polynomial of y₀. gcd(q̃(Y), τ'(Y) − x₀) over P(x₀) is linear because the
     conjugates of τ' are distinct (Euclid, as in H3).
   - So y₀ ∈ P and β = Möbius⁻¹(y₀² + c) ∈ P. ∎

Remarks:
- The engine solves a degree-D trinomial over L whose Galois group is that of K', at most C2 ≀ S_n. So the
  A_n filter is respected. Realness is handled by the square lift.
- **No Tschirnhaus conditions, no degree induction and no `poly_graph` are needed.** All difficulty sits in
  (SR).
- I_{K'} modulo scaling is a cone over H1's trinomial curve Γ_{K'}. Its genus is ≥ 2 (H1 gives 47881 at
  n = m = 9), so by Faltings each *number field* contributes finitely many points up to scaling. (SR) needs
  points over infinitely many solvable real fields as K' varies.
- Weaker hypotheses that also suffice:
  - points over F (any proved subfield of P) instead of ℚ^{solv} ∩ ℝ;
  - for q with ≤ 3 real roots, points of I_K for K itself, without the lift (a real trinomial can have
    1, 2 or 3 real roots of the right parity).

**(SR) and the literature.**
- **PSC fields and Taylor's question.** Source: Bary-Soroker–Fehm, *Open problems in the theory of ample
  fields*, arXiv:1106.1310 (2011), §2.1 and §3.2. I read the full text via pdftotext.
  - "a field K ⊆ ℚ_{tot,S} is called PSC if every smooth ℚ-variety that has a point over ℚ_p for each
    p ∈ S (with ℚ_∞ = ℝ) has a K-rational point".
  - "ℚ_{tot,S} is PSC, [MB89, GPR95]".
  - "Taylor … ask[ed] in [Tay04] whether also ℚ_{tot,S} ∩ ℚ^{solv} is PSC". A positive answer "would have
    'extremely important consequences'".
  - The references, as listed by BSF (not opened): L. Moret-Bailly, *Groupes de Picard et problèmes de
    Skolem II*, Ann. Sci. ÉNS (1989); B. Green, F. Pop, P. Roquette, *On Rumely's local-global principle*,
    Jber. DMV 97 (1995); R. Taylor, *Galois representations*, Ann. Fac. Sci. Toulouse (2004).
  - For S = {∞}, ℚ_{tot,S} is the field of totally real numbers. Totally real solvable numbers lie in
    ℚ^{solv} ∩ ℝ, and I_{K'} is a smooth, geometrically integral ℚ-variety with a real point.
  - **So a positive answer to Taylor's question for S = {∞} implies (SR), hence, by Theorem X, that every
    real algebraic number is P-constructible.** [Cited for the question; Proof for the implication.]
  - The unconditional theorem for ℚ_{tot,∞} (Moret-Bailly; Green–Pop–Roquette) gives totally real points
    of I_{K'}. But their fields have uncontrolled Galois groups and are not known to lie in P, so this is
    only a reformulation of the target.
- **Is ℚ^{solv} PAC?** BSF §3.2: "it is a long-standing open question whether ℚ^{solv} is PAC, equivalently,
  whether every absolutely irreducible ℚ-variety admits a solvable rational point". Also: "Frey proved that
  ℚ^{ab} is not PAC [FJ08, Cor. 11.5.7]", and even ampleness of ℚ^{solv} is open. It is listed as
  Fried–Jarden, *Field Arithmetic*, 3rd ed., Problem 11.5.9(a) [per a search summary, not opened].
  - A positive answer gives *complex* solvable points. That is not enough: the crossing must be real, and a
    complex point would need a complex trinomial engine (BR_n, §2.6).
  - So the PAC question is the complex shadow of (SR), not a substitute for it.
- **Known points over solvable extensions.**
  - M. Çiperiani, A. Wiles, *Solvable points on genus one curves*, Duke Math. J. 142 (2008) 381–464: a genus
    one curve over ℚ, with semistable Jacobian and points everywhere locally, has a point over a solvable
    extension of ℚ.
  - A. Pál, *Solvable points on projective algebraic curves*, Canad. J. Math. 56 (2004) 612–637. Curves of
    genus 0, 2, 3 and 4 over any field have solvable points. There are curves of genus ≥ 40 over some local
    fields without solvable points [abstract and snippet only]. Over ℚ_p every finite extension is
    solvable, so no ℚ_p obstruction arises.
  - T. D. Wooley, *Solvable points on smooth projective varieties*, Monatsh. Math. 180 (2016) 391–403
    (arXiv:1501.01789). A smooth geometrically integral X over a field algebraic over ℚ has a solvable point
    once dim X ≥ 2^{2^{deg X}}. Wooley says the higher-genus-curve case remains unclear.
  - None gives *real* solvable points. None applies to I_{K'}, which has dimension 2 and degree ≥ (n−2)!, and
    sits over a curve of huge genus.
- **Galois-group results.** Shafarevich's conjecture (G_{ℚ^{ab}} free; BSF Conj. 3.1) and Iwasawa's theorem
  on solvable extensions (recalled, not checked) concern Galois groups, not points. They are not used.
- **Status.** (SR) is open. Taylor's question, which is stronger, is an open problem posed by others for
  unrelated reasons (potential modularity). No evidence either way is offered here. A numerical probe would
  search Γ_{K'}, for a small K', over real quadratic and then real cyclic cubic fields. Not attempted.

**Relation to the user's induction.** Theorem X realises the intuition "measure parameters by Galois class,
not degree":
- the parameters have *solvable* class, which is proved to lie in P, and unbounded degree;
- the one engine step carries A_n;
- realness, which breaks the Galois-class induction at swap configurations (§2.3), is absorbed by the square
  lift.

The inductive step is not needed at all. The price is an arithmetic statement about points over solvable
real fields, in place of points over ℚ (MI-1) or over K (W1).

**The open statements, cleanly.**
1. **(SR)** For every number field K' of degree D with one or two real places, there are τ' ∈
   K' ⊗ (ℚ^{solv}∩ℝ) with distinct conjugates and a, b ∈ ℚ^{solv}∩ℝ with χ_{τ'} = x^D + ax + b.
   *This implies the full target.*
2. **(BR_n)** For every c ∈ F(i), the roots of zⁿ + z + c lie in F(i). n = 5 is H_ℂ(5).

   crossings.
3. **(MI-1)**, unchanged, and **(W1-MW)**: E_B(K_F) ≠ E_B(F) for suitable real B. *Route ingredients only.*

## 5. Other things noticed

- **The two filtrations are offset by a square.** The real-degree filtration R(N) and the Galois-class
  filtration H_ℂ(n) are cofinal, with R(n²) ⇒ H_ℂ(n) ⇒ R(n). The swap witnesses show the square is sharp for
  real towers (min(μ², |T|)). So "Galois class" is not cheaper than degree for real algebraic constructions.
  It is cheaper only for the cyclic class, because the radical steps (arccos/cos) are transcendental and are
  not real algebraic towers.
- **u = 2 Re z** (degree 25, A5 ≀ C2-type) is a concrete real algebraic number of small Galois class that no
  repo theorem reaches. It is a good test for any future complex-root capability. Its polynomial is in §2.4.
- **x⁸ + 28x + 98** (A8, totally imaginary) is the cleanest octic witness. The octic theorem says nothing
  about it (no real roots), and its real parts need degree ≥ 15 by real towers.
- **A Lean corollary** `solvable_real_algebraic_Pconstructible` (§2.2) would mostly need Mathlib's
  Kummer/solvable-tower machinery. The P-specific content is a single lemma: F(i) is closed under complex
  n-th roots.
- **The trinomial engine** is the only engine found whose every *free* parameter accepts P-values at every
  degree. The exponent D is a fixed integer, since `power_law` takes rationals only; the P-valued slots
  are the line's slope and intercept. That is what makes Theorem X possible. `poly_graph` partners import ℚ-conditions that no field
  enlargement removes.
- **Wolfram quirks.**
  - `FiniteGroupData["Simple"]` lists 19 nonabelian groups up to order 40320 and misses PSL(2,8).
  - `ProjectiveSpecialLinearGroup` is not a built-in; use
    `FiniteGroupData[{"ProjectiveSpecialLinearGroup",{n,q}},"PermutationGroupRepresentation"]`.
  - `IrreducibleQ` is undefined; use `FactorList`.
- **Sources.**
  - Opened: arXiv:1106.1310 (full text); arXiv:1501.01789 (abstract and an ar5iv summary); Çiperiani–Wiles and
    Pál (bibliographic data and abstract via search).
  - Not opened: Taylor 2004, Moret-Bailly 1989, Green–Pop–Roquette 1995, Fried–Jarden, and the ATLAS. The
    ATLAS is used for the A8 subgroup index 15 and the PGL(2,9) subgroup facts.
