# Z8: F-points, not ℚ-points (audit of every route)

Stream Z8 of `pptc/PLAN-algebraic.md`. Research only: no Lean, no git, no PowerPoint. Labels: [Proof],
[Proof sketch], [Wolfram], [Heuristic], [Open]. Every obstruction is scoped to its route. Nothing here is
a negative about the target.

Notation.
- F = P ∩ ℝ_alg. F contains:
  - real roots of degree ≤ 8 polynomials with F-coefficients, so every real tower of such steps;
  - every real number with solvable Galois group over F, via complex radicals (X1 §2.2; the argument works
    over any subfield of P, because F(i) is closed under complex n-th roots).
- K_F = K ⊗ F. s is the number of conjugate pairs.
- "F-point" means coordinates in F. "K_F-point" means coordinates in K_F, i.e. an F-point of a Weil
  restriction.

## Verdict

**Over-constraints found: yes, five. Routes closed: none. Improved: three, by reformulation.**

1. **Theorem O for n ≥ 10 (X2 §5.2) [Proof].** X2 says "for n ≥ 10 a rational τ is needed, because
   `poly_graph` is over ℚ". That overstates it.
   - The drawable partner is λ·Q(μx+ν) + e·x + κ: an x-shift, shear, both scales and a y-offset, all with
     P-values. Against the pinned power law x^{∓1/2} it still gives χ_τ = Q̃(t²)·t^{0/1} ± c.
   - So only deg Q − 4 affine invariants of Q̃ must be rational:
     - 0 at n = 9 (this is N2's Lemma Q);
     - **1 at n = 10, 11**;
     - 2 at n = 12, 13.
   - Each invariant can be fixed to a chosen rational value, which makes it an algebraic condition over ℚ.
   - So Theorem O at n = 10 needs an **F-point** on O₄ ∩ {one algebraic condition}, not a ℚ-point.
     - The special orbit u⁵ − u³ (shifted u²-coefficient = 0) keeps **totally real** points [Wolfram].
     - X2's negative circle-method exponents (−6, −14, …) are heuristics for ℚ-points only. They say
       nothing about F-points.
2. **Z5's "genus ≥ 2 ⇒ finitely many points (Faltings) ⇒ the curve route is dead" (Z5 Verdict, §5).**
   - Theorem R allows L = F (Y1a notation), and F is not a number field.
   - Every F-curve of genus 2–5, and every F-curve with an F-map of degree ≤ 8 to ℙ¹, has F-points dense in
     a real arc [Proof]. So Faltings is irrelevant.
   - The correct residual is "an F-curve of low F-gonality on V_τ". I exclude most of it
     (§3 below: Castelnuovo, plus the fact that every L-rational quadric in V_τ's system is smooth).
   - What remains is genus 3 in degree 8 spanning ℙ⁵, and genus 4–5 in degree 9–16. These are [Open], and
     dimension counts are against them [Heuristic, weak].
   - The route stays closed in practice, but on weaker grounds than Z5 states.
3. **The X1 / SUMMARY wording "rationality conditions from `poly_graph` are the one kind no field
   enlargement helps"** is too strong.
   - Once a rational shape G₀ is fixed, the orbit condition is algebraic over ℚ, so F helps in principle.
   - X1 does use the fibres W_j correctly. Only the slogan is off.
   - For MI-1 the special orbits (y⁶ + y³, y⁶ ± y⁴, y⁶ ± y²) cut the fibre's Bézout degree from 5400 to
     ≤ 2016. That is still > 8.
4. **(SR) in Theorem X is stated over ℚ^solv ∩ ℝ.**
   - Under the strengthened fact, (SR_F) suffices: a point of I_{K'} over F ⊋ ℚ^solv ∩ ℝ.
   - F contains real roots of A₅…A₈ octics, so it is strictly larger.
   - Taylor's question is sufficient, not necessary. X1 lists "points over F" as a weaker hypothesis; the
     headline should use it.
5. **N1a's "no rational certificate centre, so it lives over ℚ(√d)"** is a Lean-engineering constraint,
   not a mathematical one.
   - The THREE-PAIRS chain is an F-construction: all steps have degree ≤ 6, and the square roots of
     positive discriminants that obstruct the rational centre (N1a O1) are free in F.
   - The only real issue is a certified real open region, which N1a item 5 (box certificates) addresses.

**New reductions [Proof of the reduction].** In both, the last condition has odd degree, so a real root
always exists.
- **Totally real nonics** (N2's P-quartic odd engine = Theorem O at n = 9) follow from **one F-rational
  line in X₃ ∩ X₅ = {p₁ = p₃ = p₅ = 0} ⊂ ℙ⁷** off the pairing ℙ³'s.
  - On such a line p₇ is a binary septic, so it has a real root. Lemma N makes that root good.
  - The good lines form a surface: the expected Fano dimension is 2(7−1) − 4 − 6 = 2.
- **MI-2** (P-coefficient decics, engine y¹⁰ = quartic, s ≥ 2) follows from **one F-line in
  V₃' = {p₂ = p₃ = p₄ = 0}**, which H5 says form a 2-dimensional family. p₅ on that line is a binary quintic.
- Both turn "an F-point on a degree-105 or degree-120 variety" into "an F-point on a Fano surface of lines".
  Neither surface has a visible low-degree structure [Open].

**Elliptic functions (task ii) are unnecessary where they would apply and useless where they are needed
[Proof].**
- Over F, a genus-1 curve with a real point already has dense F-points. A line through an F-point of ℙ²
  meets a plane cubic in a real cubic, which has a real root of degree ≤ 3. Rank and elliptic functions
  play no role.
  *[Correction, 2026-11-17 (`RA.md` §2.3): this needs a model of degree ≤ 8 over F, i.e. an F-rational
  divisor of degree ≤ 8. A plane cubic is the degree-3 case. Without one, existence needs more; RA's
  Lemmas G1/G2 (unchecked) cover period 2^a·3^b and CM Jacobians.]*
- The routes that actually need genus-1 points need **K_F-points**: W1's E_B(K_F), and Theorem R's V_τ is of
  Weil type. A K_F-point is an F-point of an n-dimensional Weil restriction. Its conjugate coordinates
  cannot be produced by evaluating real functions.
- Also, H4 shows the repo has sn/am only at u = F(c, φ) with φ ∈ P, plus dyadic multiples and sums. The
  theorem "am at an arbitrary P-argument" is missing. So the premise "elliptic functions at P-arguments are
  drawable" is not established in general.

Rule that came out of the audit [Proof]:
- A `poly_graph` of degree d used with all P-affine freedom (λ, μ, ν, e, κ) leaves max(0, d − 4) rational
  invariants.
- If the x-shift ν is unavailable (partner pinned at the origin and not shiftable with it), it leaves
  max(0, d − 3).
- In Theorem O the shift *is* available, because Q(μ·t² + ν) is still a polynomial in t².
- [Wolfram] The Jacobian of (λ, μ, ν, e, κ) ↦ coefficients has rank 5 for d = 4, 5, 6. That leaves 0, 1 and 2
  invariants.

## 2. Exploit (i): linear slices, fibrations, low-degree structure

**Toolkit over F** [Proof unless marked]:
- (a) Lemma A: a variety of degree ≤ 8 with a smooth real point has dense F-points.
- (b) Any F-variety with a dominant F-map of degree ≤ 8, or with solvable monodromy, to an F-variety with
  dense F-points, provided some fibre over a real point has a real point. Specialisation only shrinks the
  group, so a chain of index ≤ 8, or solvability, survives.
- (c) Every F-curve of genus ≤ 5 with a real point:
  - hyperelliptic: double cover of an F-conic;
  - non-hyperelliptic: canonical model of degree 2g − 2 ≤ 8, projected from an F-linear space;
  - genus 1: plane cubic.
- (d) **Lines through a point.** In a cubic hypersurface the lines through an F-point form a CI of degrees
  (1, 2, 3) in direction space, of degree 6, so Lemma A applies. So X₃ ∩ X_d has dense F-points for odd
  d ≤ 7: take an F-line in X₃, then a real root of the odd-degree restriction. In a CI of a quadric and a
  cubic, the lines through P₀ form a degree-12 curve of genus 13 on a dP4; a real singular pencil member of
  signature (2,2) gives a conic bundle, hence an F-map of degree 6 (this is THREE-PAIRS/N1a R3–R6).
- (e) **Odd last step.** If the last condition has odd degree ≤ 7, an F-line in the CI of the others
  suffices.

**Applied:**
- V'_K (MI-1): degree 6, so (a) applies (X1).
- O₄ (n = 9), degree 105:
  - (d) gives F-points on X₃ ∩ X₅ but not on X₃ ∩ X₅ ∩ X₇.
  - (e) reduces the problem to an F-line in X₃ ∩ X₅. Lines through a general point of X₃ ∩ X₅ do not exist:
    8 direction conditions in ℙ⁶.
  - The Brauer/Sylvester obliteration that would build such a line needs far more than 8 variables
    [Heuristic: the direction system for one quintic already contains a quintic].
  - Planes in X₃ (a 5-dimensional family) give plane quintic ∩ septic = 35 points. Lines in X₃ ∩ X₇ form a
    0-dimensional family; lines in X₅ ∩ X₇ form none.
  - F-rational lines in the pairing ℙ³'s do not exist: an F-line would lie in ∩_{g∈H} Λ_{gπ} = 0
    [Proof, as X2].
  - [Wolfram] For q = ∏_{i=−4}^{4}(x−i) + 1 (totally real, S₉), random rational ℙ³-slices of the trace-zero
    ℙ⁷ meet O₄ in 105 points, with **0 real** in both trials (seeds 3 and 12). Lemma A needs a real point
    near the slice, and even that is not automatic.
- V₄' (MI-2), degree 120: reduced by (e) to the Fano surface of V₃'. H5's line-through-P₀ count shows no
  line through a general point.
- W_j and the special-orbit fibres (MI-1): degree ≤ 5400 and ≤ 2016. No fibration found. A rational line ℓ
  in V'_K would carry ℚ-points itself, which is circular. On a linear-section curve of V'_K (degree 6,
  genus 4), J has degree ~5400 onto its image.
- I_{K'} (Theorem X):
  - The only F-maps come from S_D-quotients of the ordered-roots curve: the identity, the A_D-quotient of
    degree D!/2, and the j-line of degree D!.
  - The twisted A_D-quotient (a conic-like δ² = d·disc) has dense F-points, but its fibre has monodromy A_D.
  - No low-degree structure [Proof sketch].
- V_τ (Theorem R):
  - π: V_τ → ℙ² has fibres whose points need Q(τ) ∈ K_F^{×2}. The fibre's Galois group is C₂ ≀ S_n
    restricted to sign classes.
  - Chains from it to the point stabiliser S_n × C₂ must pass a single step of index 2^{n−2} = 128 at n = 9,
    because the heart is irreducible.
  - So (b) fails [Proof, same module fact as Z5].

**Totally real special orbits (n ≥ 10) [Wolfram + Proof]:**
- n = 10, 11 (quintic Q̃): the orbit of u⁵ − u³ (shifted u²-coefficient = 0) is realness-compatible.
  T₅(t² − 2)/16 − t/1000 has 10 real roots, by exact `CountRoots`.
- The orbit u⁵ + u² is not: zero w⁴ and w³ coefficients force Σr = Σr² = 0.
- Sextics (n = 12, 13): all three two-vanishing orbits have Descartes capacity 4 < 6, so the generic fibre
  W_j must be used there.
- Random search over the u⁵ ± u³ / u⁵ + u² families (1500 draws each) found at most 6 real roots. The
  10-root point needs the Chebyshev design, so the real locus is thin [Wolfram].

**Verdict for (i): no route closes.** The F-point problems are honest: O₄ (dimension 4, degree 105), its
Fano surface of lines in X₃ ∩ X₅, V₃'-lines, W_j, I_{K'} and V_τ. None has a degree-≤ 8 or solvable fibration
that I could find.

## 3. Exploit (ii): genus-1 and low-gonality curves

- **Genus 1 is free over F** (§0). For F-point problems, the real question is whether the needed variety
  contains an F-curve of low F-gonality. Genus ≤ 5 always qualifies.
- **V_τ (Theorem R), n = 9, H ⊇ A₉.** Z5 gives (n−8)e ≤ 4g − 4, so g ≥ 2 and e ≤ 4g − 4. Add:
  - [Proof] Every L-rational member Tr(ωρ²) (ω ∈ W_τ^⊥ ⊂ K_L) of the 6-dimensional quadric system is
    smooth of rank 9, because all conjugates of ω ≠ 0 are nonzero. A smooth quadric in ℙ⁸ contains no ℙ⁴.
    So restriction to any L-rational ℙ^r with r ≥ 4 is injective.
  - [Proof sketch] An irreducible L-curve C ≠ ℓ_τ of degree e spanning ℙ^r:
    - r = 2: a conic through a curve of degree ≥ 3 vanishes on the plane, so the plane lies in V_τ. Impossible.
    - r = 3, e ≥ 5: two quadrics through C share a component, so C is planar. So the six restrictions are
      multiples of one quadric, which would put a quadric surface in V_τ. Impossible.
    - r ≥ 4: C must lie on 6 independent quadrics of ℙ^r.
  - With Castelnuovo's bound and Z5's inequality this excludes:
    - genus 2 entirely (e ≤ 4 forces a plane quartic);
    - every e ≤ 7;
    - e = 8 except (r, g) = (5, 3), where a projectively normal genus-3 octic lies on exactly 7 quadrics
      of ℙ⁵.
  - Left open: (8, ℙ⁵, 3) and genus 4–5 with 9 ≤ e ≤ 16.
  - The expected dimension with τ free is g + 5 − 3e < 0 throughout [Heuristic, weak: curves on
    general-type surfaces are obstructed].
- **Does Z5 extend to other fields? Yes [Proof sketch].** It extends to every subfield L ⊂ P, algebraic
  or transcendental.
  - For L ⊂ ℝ with L_alg = L ∩ ℚ̄, L/L_alg is regular, so Gal(KL/L) = Gal(KL_alg/L_alg).
  - The argument is geometric plus H-irreducibility of the heart. A curve over a transcendental field is a
    family over ℚ̄, but nothing in steps 1–4 sees that.
  - The only escape is F ∩ Ω ⊄ ℚ(√d), i.e. F already containing degree-≥ 9 information about β's
    splitting field.
- **O₄, the Fano surfaces, W_j:**
  - There is no symmetry argument like Z5's. F-curves in a K-twist need not be S₉-stable: the twisted
    descent condition ρ(σ)σ(C) = C is not a stability condition.
  - So genus-1 or low-gonality F-curves are [Open].
  - O₄ is of general type, with K = O(7) on the CI in ℙ⁷. The expected dimension of curve families is
    negative, so special low-gonality curves would be unexpected [Heuristic].
