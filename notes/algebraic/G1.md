# G1: Tschirnhaus / resolvent-degree literature, read for P

Stream G1 of `pptc/PLAN-algebraic.md`. Literature only; no Lean edits. Tags: [Cited: ref], [Proof],
[Heuristic], [Open]. Where I could not open a primary source (many classical items) I say
"unverified" and give only what a verified secondary source attributes. Backends: citations from
WebSearch/WebFetch (arXiv abstract/ar5iv pages; the arXiv PDFs came back as binary and were not

## 0. Findings summary

1. **The literature is complex-algebraic and counts parameters, not auxiliary degrees.**
   [Cited: Farb-Wolfson 2019; Wolfson 2020; Sutherland 2021; Heberle-Sutherland 2023]. All of it
   works over C (or a finitely generated C-algebra, or any algebraically closed char-0 field).
   I found **no source** on resolvent degree or Tschirnhaus reduction over R, over Q with
   rational points, or over totally real fields (three targeted searches; one 2025 paper,
   Gomez-Gonzales arXiv:2510.10272, gives "arithmetic formulations" via special points but its
   abstract says nothing about real fields). [Open: absence of a citation is not a negative].

2. **Best known generic bound shape.** `RD(n) <= n - r` for `n >= F(r)` (Wolfson), with
   F(1..7) = 2, 3, 4, 5, 9, 41, 121, improved by Sutherland to `G(r) <= F(r)` and by
   Heberle-Sutherland for r in [13,17] and [22,25]. Brauer: `n >= (r-1)!+1`. In plain words,
   the best generic methods remove only r-1 leading terms, and r grows like (log n)/(log log n)
   at best. The remaining family has n - r parameters. Nobody knows `RD(n)` bounded (Segre's
   problem). [Cited: Sutherland, arXiv:2107.08139, Problem 1.2 and Thm 1.3].

3. **What this gives P, in numbers.** An engine "x^n = (poly of degree d)" has d+1 free
   coefficients and needs `n - d - 2` terms removed (after shift and scale), i.e.
   `r = n - d - 1`. The known thresholds cover this only when `n >= F(n-d-1)`:
   - quartic engine (d=4): n = 9 (F(5) = 9, Hilbert's nonic); fails for n >= 10 (needs F(6) = 41).
   - sextic engine `x^n = P_6(x)` (d=6): n <= 12 (n = 12 needs F(5) = 9 <= 12); fails at n = 13
     (needs F(6) = 41). [Proof: arithmetic from the table; the table entries are Cited.]
   So **no fixed-size sparse engine is reachable for all n by known generic bounds.** This is a
   scoped statement about "reduce to a fixed-term shape with the known bounds", not about the
   target.

4. **Two things the RD literature does not charge for, which matter for P.**
   - *Induction on n is free for auxiliary degree.* RD counts parameters, so it does not require
     the auxiliary equations to have degree <= 8. For P we may use *any* auxiliary degree < n by
     induction, provided the needed auxiliary roots are real and lie in the algebraic part F.
     The Wolfson/Sutherland constructions find points on complete intersections by lines and
     planes and then solve equations of degree controlled by the degrees of the varieties, which
     are < n for the thresholds above [Heuristic: I did not audit each degree]. So *realness*,
     not degree, is the first-order obstruction. [Heuristic]
   - *Realness is unaddressed in the literature and is genuinely restrictive* (§3).

5. **Realness facts that I can prove (route-scoped).**
   - (R1) If f is totally real, no real Tschirnhaus element tau has `p_2 = Tr tau^2 = 0` (positive
     definiteness). So Bring-Jerrard and every route that kills p_2 with a real tau fails for
     totally real f. [Proof]
   - (R2) A Tschirnhaus image of a totally real f under real tau is totally real, so a t-term
     target shape needs `n <= 2(t-1)` (Descartes). For power-law engines (branch x>0 only) the
     bound is `n <= t-1`. [Proof, Descartes; the second clause assumes only positive roots are
     visible to one drawn crossing, which is a Heuristic about the construction].
   - (R3) Real isotropic subspaces of the quadric `{p_1 = p_2 = 0}` have dimension
     `<= s` (r1 >= 1) or `<= s-1` (r1 = 0), s = number of conjugate pairs: by Sylvester's law of
     inertia, Tr(x^2) on K tensor R has signature (r1+s, s), and the trace-zero hyperplane has
     signature (r1+s-1, s). So Hilbert's step "a 3-plane in the quadric" (4-dim isotropic) is
     real only for s >= 4. [Proof]
   - (R4) For n >= 6 and s >= 1 the trace-zero part has dimension >= 5 and signature indefinite,
     so over Q it is isotropic (Meyer). This is the arithmetic reason Q-rational p_2 = 0 works
     with a nonreal root. [Cited: Serre, A Course in Arithmetic, Ch. IV, Meyer's theorem; page
     reference from memory.]

6. **A lead I have not seen in the project notes (the "square lift", [Heuristic]).** For
   f in F[x] of degree n with root alpha and any c in F, `g(y) = f(y^2 + c)` has degree 2n and
   alpha = y^2 + c for y = sqrt(alpha - c). Choosing c just above all but one of the roots makes g
   have only 2 real roots and n-1 conjugate pairs. This removes the Descartes/few-real-roots
   obstruction (R2) and the p_2 obstruction (R1) at once, for any f, at the price of doubling the
   degree. Galois group of g lies in C2 wr S_n, whose only nonabelian composition factor is A_n,
   so the A_n filter of NOTES-general is untouched. It does not by itself solve the cost of
   finding tau. See §6, lead 2.

7. **Closed-form solutions (Umemura, Mellin/Birkeland/Sturmfels, Klein) need transcendental
   inputs P is not known to evaluate** (theta constants at period matrices, A-hypergeometric
   series, hypergeometric inversions). None gives an all-n route inside P as it stands. They
   are listed for G5.

**What the classical results give for P.** They give *Tschirnhaus elements with auxiliary
quadratic / cubic / small-degree equations over C*. For P we need the real versions:
(a) Bring-Jerrard needs p_2 = 0, impossible for totally real f (R1); (b) for s >= 1 the
aux steps are real quadratic/cubic subject to sign conditions on discriminants and indefinite
forms (R3, R4); (c) none of them is stated "for arbitrary polynomial": the 1927/1945/1975 arguments
use *generic* f, and Wolfson/Sutherland make the genericity precise (open dense subsets of the
parameter space). For a *specific* rational f one needs the Hilbert-irreducibility / specialization
step. [Heuristic: standard, not checked in the sources.]

## 2. Modern resolvent degree

- **Farb-Wolfson**, "Resolvent degree, Hilbert's 13th Problem and geometry", L'Enseignement
  Math. 65 (2019), 303-376, DOI 10.4171/LEM/65-3/4-2, arXiv:1803.04063. Lemma 3.2:
  `RD(G) <= ed(G)`; Thm 3.3: `RD(G) <= max RD(G_i)` over simple factors; Thm 8.2:
  `RD(W(E6)^+) <= 3`. [Cited: Sutherland, arXiv:2312.04430, summary]. Hilbert's 13th,
  sextic and octic conjectures are equivalent to enumerative problems [Cited: FW abstract].
  *For P:* RD is invariant under replacing the group by simple composition factors, which is
  the Galois-theoretic form of the A_n filter in NOTES-general. It says nothing real.
- **Wolfson**, "Tschirnhaus transformations after Hilbert", L'Enseignement Math. 66 (2020),
  489-540, arXiv:2001.06515. **Thm 1.1**: with `F` from Definition 5.4, `RD(n) <= n - r` for
  `n >= F(r)`; `B(r)/F(r) -> infinity` with `B(r) = (r-1)!+1` (Brauer). Table: F(1..7) =
  2, 3, 4, 5, 9, 41, 121. Thm 2.12: genericity of Hilbert's cubic surfaces. Base field char 0,
  algebraically closed in the Bertini part; solutions are points over extensions of
  `C(a_1..a_n)`. Merkurjev-Suslin is used to trivialise Severi-Brauer varieties by radicals.
  [Cited: ar5iv rendering of 2001.06515]. *Bookkeeping:* `RD(n) <= n - r` means the general
  monic polynomial is Tschirnhaus-equivalent over a tower of RD-cheap extensions to one with
  r-1 leading terms removed and (after scaling) n - r parameters. Check: Bring r = 4, n = 5
  gives 1; Hilbert r = 5, n = 9 gives 4. [Proof of consistency with the two known cases.]
- **Sutherland**, "Upper bounds on resolvent degree and its growth rate", arXiv:2107.08139.
  Thm 1.3: `RD(n) <= n - m` for `n >= G(m)`; `G(m) <= F(m)` with equality only for
  `m in {1,2,3,4,5,15,16}`; `G(2d^2+7d+6) <= (2d^2+7d+5)!/d!` for `d >= 4`. Thm 3.7:
  `n >= 21 => RD(n) <= n-6`. Thm 3.10: `n >= 109 => n-7`, `n >= 325 => n-8`, and for
  `9 <= l <= 14`, `n > (l-1)!/24 => RD(n) <= n-l`. Prop 2.19: a degree-d subvariety of P^r_K has a
  point over an extension of resolvent degree `<= RD(d)`. Lemma 2.24 (Polar Point Lemma).
  Remark 2.12 (on a non-algebraically-closed field a polar cone may contain no rational points
  other than the vertex). [Cited: ar5iv 2107.08139]. *Remark 2.12 is the exact place where the
  arithmetic/real issue is acknowledged.*
- **Heberle-Sutherland**, "Upper bounds on resolvent degree via Sylvester's obliteration
  algorithm", New York J. Math. 29 (2023), 107-146, arXiv:2110.08670. **Thm 1.1**: `RD(n) <= n-13`
  for `n >= 5,250,199`; `n-m` for `14 <= m <= 17` and `n > (m-1)!/120`; `n-22` for
  `n >= 381,918,437,071,508,901`; `n-m` for `23 <= m <= 25` and `n > (m-1)!/720`. (Thm 4.6
  quotes thresholds off by one: 5,250,198 and ...900. Check against the paper.) Fields: finitely
  generated C-algebras. [Cited: ar5iv 2110.08670]
- **Gomez-Gonzales, Sutherland, Wolfson**, sporadic groups, J. Algebra 647 (2024), arXiv:2310.09375
  (upper bounds for the 26 sporadic groups) [Cited: search summary]. **Gomez-Gonzales**, "Special
  points on intersections of hypersurfaces", arXiv:2510.10272: lower bounds on ambient dimension for
  intersections to have dense "level l" points, proposes improved RD(n), RD(G) bounds
  [Cited: abstract only]. **Sutherland**, summary note, arXiv:2312.04430.
  *For P:* these do not improve the large-n shape in any way that changes finding 3, as far as
  the abstracts show.
- **Essential dimension.** Buhler-Reichstein, "On the essential dimension of a finite group",
  Compositio Math. 106 (1997): `ed(S_n) <= n-3` (n >= 5), `ed(S_{n+2}) >= ed(S_n)+1`,
  `ed(S_n) >= floor(n/2)`; `ed(A_{n+4}) >= ed(A_n)+2`, `ed(A_n) >= 2 floor(n/4)` (n >= 4)
  [Cited: Sutherland 2312.04430 listing "Thm 6.5, Thm 6.7"]. Buhler-Reichstein, "On Tschirnhaus
  transformations", in *Topics in Number Theory* (Kluwer, 1999), 127-142: a Tschirnhaus
  transformation *without auxiliary radicals* (also extended to odd-degree radicals, and odd-degree
  radicals plus sqrt(disc)) cannot reduce the general degree-n polynomial below `floor(n/2)`
  independent coefficients [Cited: Reichstein's page personal.math.ubc.ca/~reichst/tsch-ind.html].
  *For P:* ed bounds Tschirnhaus over the base field itself or with radical towers, not with
  unrestricted degree-<=8 (or degree < n) auxiliary steps, so it is **not** a lower bound for our
  towers. The statement most relevant here: with only P-closure-style odd-degree radicals one
  cannot go below n/2 parameters, so in the radical-only fragment a bounded-term engine is out.
  Scope: that is a statement about that fragment of the route.
- **RD vs ed.** `RD <= ed`, and no lower bound on `RD(n)` beyond trivial ones is known for
  n >= 7 (Hilbert's Sextic/Septic/Octic conjectures are conjectural; Edens-Reichstein show they
  can fail in positive characteristic [Cited: search summary]). [Open].

## 3. Real and arithmetic variants

**Real.** No source found. What I can prove, route-scoped, is R1-R3 above. Additional proven
or classical facts:
- (R5) Over R the real parts of the Hilbert objects: a real smooth cubic surface contains 3, 7, 15
  or 27 real lines (Schlafli); so Hilbert's step "a line on the cubic surface" has real
  points, but the surface is `P ∩ T_123`, itself needing a real isotropic 3-plane by (R3).
  [Cited: classical, Schlafli 1858; not re-verified here.]
- (R6) Bring radical: for real a, `x^5 + x = a` has exactly one real root (monotone), so the
  Bring-Jerrard engine always has a unique real root, which is *the right shape for a single
  root* and wrong for all-real-roots statements. [Proof: monotonic]
- (R7) P contains every real root of `x^n + a x + b` (power law crossed with a line), for all
  n and P coefficients. That is Mellin's trinomial for free. [Proof from the P primitives as
  summarised in the task statement.]

**Arithmetic (over Q or a number field).**
- Meyer: indefinite rational quadratic forms in >= 5 variables are isotropic [Cited:
  Serre, A Course in Arithmetic, Ch. IV]. Gives Q-rational tau with `p_1 = p_2 = 0` for f in Q[x]
  with a nonreal root, n >= 6 (R4). Already used in `Nonic/Nonic.lean`.
- Birch, "Forms in many variables", Proc. Roy. Soc. London Ser. A 265 (1962), 245-263 (asymptotic
  count of integer solutions of R forms of common degree d, in N variables when `N - dim Sing >
  R(R+1)(d-1)2^{d-1}`). Differing degrees: Browning-Heath-Brown, "Forms in many variables and
  differing degrees", J. Eur. Math. Soc. 19 (2017), 357-394, DOI 10.4171/JEMS/668, arXiv:1403.5937.
  Heath-Brown, "Cubic forms in ten variables" (1983): nonsingular rational cubic in >= 10
  variables has a zero (year/title verified via search; journal unverified). [Cited]
  *For P:* these give **Q-rational Tschirnhaus elements with no auxiliary roots at all**, killing
  p_2, ..., p_k simultaneously once the number of variables n exceeds a bound of order
  `k^3 2^k`, **provided** real solvability (p-adic solvability is automatic for N large) and
  provided the singular locus is small. The singular locus of `{p_j(tau) = 0}` is where tau has
  fewer than k distinct conjugates, dimension `<= k-1` for generic K [Heuristic, one-line
  Vandermonde argument]. Proxy threshold (equal-degree Birch bound with R = d = k, Wolfram):
  `N > (k-1) + k(k+1)(k-1)2^{k-1}`: k = 2: 13, 3: 98, 4: 483, 5: 1924, 6: 6725, 7: 21510,
  8: 64519, 9: 184328. [Wolfram, proxy only: not the sharp differing-degree constant.]
  So the arithmetic route kills about `log_2 n` power sums, no better than Wolfson-style routes
  for large n, but costs zero auxiliary equations. [Heuristic.]
- Real solubility is the sticking point: for k >= 2 the real locus of `{p_2 = 0}` is nonempty only
  if s >= 1 (R1), and for several p_j one needs a nonsingular real point, which depends on s
  relative to k [Open: no clean criterion found; the plan's N-streams measure it for n = 9].

**Totally real fields.** Serre, "L'invariant de Witt de la forme Tr(x^2)", Comment. Math. Helv. 59
(1984), 651-676 (year/volume as I recall; the search hit listed it as "Comm. Math. Helv. 27
(1984), 651", which looks garbled; unverified). It concerns the Hasse-Witt invariant of the trace
form, which is positive definite for totally real K, so it controls the *discriminant side*, not the
isotropy needed here. Bayer-Fluckiger-Parimala-Serre, "Hasse principle for G-trace forms", Izv. Math.
2013, abstract not seen. I found nothing that produces a Tschirnhaus element with prescribed
vanishing power sums for a totally real field. [Open; consistent with R1: `Tr(tau^2)` is
definite there, so such a statement could only involve odd power sums or non-power-sum shapes.]

## 4. Sparse / fewnomial

- **What every degree-n polynomial can be reduced to** (generic, over C): n - r parameters with
  r as in §2; trinomial `x^n + ax + b` (2 parameters, 1 after scaling) is reachable for n = 5 only
  (Bring-Jerrard, r = 4); n = 6, 7, 8 have Hamilton forms with 2, 3, 4 parameters, so a trinomial or
  quadrinomial normal form is not reached beyond n = 5 by known results. [Cited: Sutherland,
  Hamilton's bounds; Hilbert's 13th for n = 7 is conjecturally 3 parameters.] [Open for P]
- **Realness limits.** (R2): Descartes: at most t-1 positive and t-1 negative roots for a t-term
  polynomial; this holds for generalised exponents on the positive side. Khovanskii-type bounds
  for systems of fewnomials (Khovanskii, *Fewnomials*, AMS 1991; Bihan-Sottile 2007, Moscow Math. J.
  7, 387-407) are the multi-variable analogue [Cited from memory, unverified]. Consequence for
  P engines built from `power_law` x `poly_graph` of degree d: t <= d + 2 terms, so a totally
  real polynomial of degree > 2(d+1) = 14 (d = 6) can **not** be Tschirnhaus-transformed (real tau)
  into that shape. Scope: Tschirnhaus-into-fixed-shape route.
- **Odd shape** `t Q(t^2) - c`: the number of terms is not fixed (Q has degree (n-1)/2 for n real
  roots), so no Descartes obstruction, but drawing Q of unbounded degree is the G2 bottleneck. The
  only sparse shapes with room for n real roots have a number of terms growing with n. [Proof for the
  count; the P-side claim is the plan's.]
- **The square lift (§0.6)** gives any real algebraic number as the *real* root of a polynomial with
  only 2 real roots, which satisfies the Descartes bound for t >= 3. [Heuristic]; it also removes
  R1. The price (degree 2n) interacts with finding tau. Cheap check I did not do: whether
  Tschirnhaus elements for `f(y^2 + c)` can be taken even in y (then the problem factors through
  `f` and nothing is gained) or must be genuinely odd/mixed. [Open]

## 5. Closed-form solutions of the general equation

- **Hermite 1858, Kronecker 1858, Brioschi 1858**: quintic via elliptic modular functions
  (Bring-Jerrard form). **Klein**, *Vorlesungen uber das Ikosaeder* (1884): quintic via the
  icosahedral equation and a hypergeometric 2F1 with Schwarz triangle (2,3,5). Klein, Math. Ann. 15
  (1879) 251-282: degree 7 and 8 equations with group PSL(2,7) via the Klein quartic (volume and
  pages from memory, unverified). Klein 1905, on general quintic and sextic (Crelle 129): from memory.
  [Cited: Nash and Solanilla-Barreto-Morales, arXiv:1308.0955, as expository sources for the
  quintic; arXiv:2102.01019 "A Brief Account of Klein's Icosahedral Extensions".] *For P:* these need
  inversion of 2F1 / elliptic modular functions (e.g. arc lengths and `arc_of_length` give elliptic
  integrals, so elliptic inversion is plausible for the quintic but is not needed since n <= 8 is
  done). They apply to n = 5, 6, 7 only. [Open for larger n.]
- **Mellin / Birkeland** (trinomials via Gamma / hypergeometric series): Mellin 1915 and Birkeland
  1927 are the standard attributions; I could not verify the originals. [Cited: Bologna 2021 paper
  "trinomial equation the hypergeometric way": line of research founded by Hermite, Kronecker,
  Birkeland, Mellin, Brioschi.] For P the trinomial *is* a drawable crossing (R7), so this adds
  nothing to the engine; it matters only for reduction *to* a trinomial.
- **Sturmfels**, "Solving algebraic equations in terms of A-hypergeometric series", Discrete Math.
  210 (2000), 171-181 (pages from memory; DOI 10.1016/S0012-365X(99)00126-0 verified via MaRDI).
  Series solutions for the n roots of the general equation (Thm 3.2 gives a convergence
  condition; a 2016 note arXiv:1607.04144 says the inequality in Thm 3.2 had a misprint in
  direction). Gives roots as series in the coefficients; convergence only on a domain. *For P:*
  requires evaluating hypergeometric-type series at algebraic points, not a constructor. [Open]
- **Umemura / Thomae**: Umemura, "Resolution of algebraic equations by theta constants", appendix to
  Mumford, *Tata Lectures on Theta II* (Birkhauser, 1984), pp. 261-272: a root of any algebraic
  equation as a Siegel modular function of theta constants, using genus `[(n+2)/2]` hyperelliptic
  curves (Thomae's formula). [Cited: Wikipedia Umemura/Thomae pages; search summary]. Zhivkov: genus
  2 for degree <= 6. *For P:* needs the period matrix of the hyperelliptic curve `y^2 = f(x)` and then
  theta constants. Arc lengths of drawable curves give some abelian integrals, so the period matrix
  might be reachable; theta constants at it are the missing step. [Open; G5.]

## 6. Ranked leads for an all-n route

1. **Match engine parameter count to `n - r(n)`.** Known bounds say a general degree-n polynomial
   is Tschirnhaus-equivalent to one with r(n)-1 terms removed. Fixed-size engines fail for
   n >= 13 (finding 3), but an engine family whose number of free P-parameters grows like
   `n - O(log n)` would be reachable *if* the auxiliary steps are real. This is the G2/G3 question
   (offset/affine towers). Needs: realness of each Wolfson-style step (R3 and R5 style conditions)
   plus a drawable family of that size. [Heuristic]
2. **Square lift, then arithmetic/Wolfson reductions on the nonreal-rich polynomial.** Replace f by
   `f(y^2 + c)` so that the target root is one of 2 real roots and there are many conjugate pairs.
   This unblocks R1 (p_2 = 0 solvable over R), R2 (few real roots suffice) and makes the quadric
   isotropic spaces large (R3: Witt index s). For f in Q[x] it also puts Meyer/Birch (R4) in play.
   Needs: that the Tschirnhaus element is not forced to respect the even structure (otherwise it
   reduces back to f). [Heuristic; [Open] on the last point.]
3. **Birch/Browning-Heath-Brown over Q** to kill about `log_2 n` power sums with zero auxiliary
   equations, then Wolfson-style lines for the next few. Gains a constant number of terms only;
   useful as a supplement to lead 1 and for making the base case rational. [Heuristic]
4. **Transcendental routes** (Umemura/Thomae, Klein, Sturmfels): require theta or hypergeometric
   evaluation at algebraic points; to be tested against `arc_of_length` (G5). Lowest priority.
5. **Bounded-term sparse engines for all n via the generic literature**: scoped out by finding 3
   and R2; not a lead unless Segre's problem (RD(n) bounded) is solved.

## Other things noticed

- RD counts the *number of variables* of each auxiliary extension, not the *degree* of
  auxiliary equations. The plan's §2.5 phrase "bounded degree" should be read as "bounded
  parameter count + any degree < n by induction"; the induction needs the algebraic part F to be
  closed under real roots of degree < n over *F* (not only over Q), which the plan states for
  <= 8 only.
- Wolfson's bookkeeping check (Bring n = 5, r = 4; Hilbert n = 9, r = 5) matches the plan's
  nonic conditions (p_1..p_4 = 0 for `y^9 = quartic`).
- Arithmetic/Birch thresholds above are an equal-degree proxy; the true differing-degree
  constants from Browning-Heath-Brown should be extracted before relying on them.

  numbers, Wiman, Chebotarev, Segre titles, Klein 1879/1905, Mellin 1915, Birkeland 1927,
  Serre 1984, Khovanskii, Bihan-Sottile. Please do not quote these without checking.
