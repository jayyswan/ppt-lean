# H4: transcendental Galois sources (elliptic and abelian division values, modular and theta routes)

Stream H4 of `pptc/PLAN-algebraic.md`. Research only; no `.lean` edits, no commits. Tags: [Proof],
[Cited], [Wolfram], [Heuristic], [Open]. Backend for every number: Wolfram Language (the kernel has no
`FindMinimalPolynomial`, so minimal polynomials were found with `LatticeReduce` on `(1, v, v^2, ...)`
at 300-2200 digits, and a relation is accepted only when its height is far below what a spurious
lattice vector could reach, i.e. residual much smaller than height^(-(n-1))). No Python was used.
Obstructions below are scoped to the route they block; none is a statement about the target.

Notation. `c = k^2` is the repo's parameter (`ellipticF c phi`, `jacobiAm c u`); `K(c) = F(c, pi/2)`,
`K'(c) = K(1-c)`, `tau = i K'/K`, `lambda(tau) = k^2`, `q = exp(-pi K'/K)`.

## 0. Findings summary

1. **What P evaluates, elliptically (a).** For P-constructible `c < 1`: `F(c,phi)`, `E(c,phi)` and
   `Pi(c,n,phi)` (n > 1 or n < 1 under the stated hypotheses) at P-constructible `phi`; hence `K`, `E`,
   `K'`, the ratio `K'/K`, the nome `q = exp(-pi K'/K)` and `theta_{2,3,4}(q)^2 = 2K{sqrt c, 1, sqrt(1-c)}/pi`
   [Proof, Lean: `ellipticF_Pconstructible`, `ellipticE_Pconstructible`, `rpow_two_Pconstructible`,
   `exp_one_Pconstructible`; Wolfram check]. `sn,cn,dn,am` at `u = F(phi)` and at all `Z[1/2]`-combinations
   of such `u` [Proof, Lean: `JacobiHalving`, `JacobiAddition`]. The complete `am` is **not** formalized.
   Everything on this list is a *forward* evaluation. The only inversion P has is `arc_of_length` on one
   drawn curve at a time (ellipse: `E`).

2. **New, small, concrete (b).** `sn(K/3, c)`, `sn(K/5, c)`, `sn(K/7, c)` are P-constructible for all
   P-constructible `c` by *algebra of degree <= 8 over Q(c)*, so the amplitude is P-constructible at
   `K/3, K/5, K/7` and (by the repo's addition theorem) on the `Z[1/2,1/3,1/5,1/7]`-module generated by `K`.
   - `K/3`: `c s^4 - 2c s^3 + 2s - 1 = 0` [Proof from `sn(2K/3) = cn/dn(K/3)` and duplication; Wolfram check].
   - `K/5`: `Q(c)(sn(K/5))` has degree 12 (c = 3/10: irreducible, height 4320), Frobenius cycle types
     consistent with `GL(2,5)/{+-1}` (order 240, composition factors A5, C2, C2) [Wolfram]; it contains
     `lambda(5 tau)` (sextic, irreducible) and is quadratic over `Q(lambda(5 tau))` [Wolfram at c = 3/10;
     generic-c identities are [Heuristic], explicit polynomials in c not written out].
   - `K/7`: cubic over `Q(lambda(7 tau))` [Wolfram at c = 3/10].
   This adds **no new composition factor**: A4, A5, PSL(2,7) are all already reached by towers.

3. **The first new simple group would be PSL(2,11) at level 11 (b).** `lambda(11 tau)` has degree 12
   over `Q` at `c = 3/10` [Wolfram]; its modular equation has group PGL(2,11) acting on `P^1(F_11)` and
   is a degree-12 real-rooted-in-two-places equation that no degree <= 8 step solves. Equivalence:
   `am(K/N)` is P-constructible for all P-constructible `c` iff `lambda(N tau)` is, up to a cyclic step
   of degree `(N-1)/2` [Heuristic, verified for N = 5, 7 only]. So "division of `K` by 11" is a precise,
   [Open] target; it is exactly "solve Klein's level-11 modular equation in P".

4. **Group-theoretic ceiling of the elliptic route (b), [Proof modulo Dickson].** The Galois group of any
   elliptic division value over a field is a subgroup of `GL(2, Z/N)` (extended by `(Z/N)^2` for division
   of a point). By Dickson's classification of subgroups of PSL(2,q), its non-solvable sections are
   `PSL(2,p)` (p prime dividing N, p >= 5) and `A5`. So: **PSL(2,7)** yes (covered), **PSL(2,11), PSL(2,13), ...**
   yes in principle, **PSL(2,8) and PGammaL(2,8) never** from elliptic curves, and **A6 = PSL(2,9) never** (needs q = 9, but only prime q = p
   occurs). In particular no elliptic route ever produces A_n for n >= 6, let alone n >= 9. Elliptic functions
   alone cannot carry the target; they can only add the groups PSL(2,p).

5. **Abelian side (c).** `Gal(Q(J[2]))` for `J = Jac(y^2 = f)` is the Galois group of `f` itself (Thomae,
   Umemura: the 2-torsion field is the splitting field). So A_n, n >= 9, appears exactly via
   hyperelliptic Jacobians of genus `>= 4` whose branch points are the roots of the target `f`.
   P cannot draw such a curve and cannot evaluate Siegel theta constants. Three separate obstacles
   (Section 4): no drawable speed-curve has branch points of degree > 5; real arcs never give the period
   matrix without knowing the roots; theta series are not a constructor. All three are scoped to the
   current constructors (offset towers are [Open]).

6. **Inversion (b).** `K'/K` is available forward. Its inverse `r -> c` is not obtained by `arc_of_length`
   or the box on any one drawable curve: the needed speed would be `pi/(4c(1-c)K(c)^2)` in the position
   variable `c` [Wolfram: derivative of `K'/K` checked], which contains `K(c)`, whereas drawable speeds
   are algebraic (plus `exp`, `sin` bases) in the position [Heuristic, route-scoped]. By Schneider
   (below) the inverse is transcendental at every algebraic non-CM `r`, so no algebraic shortcut exists
   for general `r`; it is algebraic exactly at the multiplier-N points covered by the modular equations.

7. **The Galois catch (d).** The Aₙ filter is a theorem about *towers of bounded-degree algebraic steps*.
   P-constructible algebraic numbers reached through a transcendental intermediate are outside that
   theorem: (i) `cos(2 pi/p)` has unbounded degree (but cyclic group); (ii) `sn(K(c)/5, c)` is an algebraic
   number whose splitting field has an A5-factor group obtained from a transcendental argument. The
   filter still restricts what the *algebraic data* can be; it does not restrict the monodromy of the
   transcendental function. Precise statement in Section 5.

### 1.2 Hyperelliptic periods via arc lengths of drawable curves

For a drawn curve the arc-length integrand is `sqrt(speed^2)`; with an affine image of a graph `(x, g(x))`
by a matrix `[[alpha,beta],[gamma,delta]]`, `speed^2 = (alpha + beta g')^2 + (gamma + delta g')^2 =
C (h + B/C)^2 + D` with `h = g'` and constants P-constructible (`C > 0`, `D > 0`; `D` is the area-squared
term). [Proof, direct computation.]

* **Graphs (`poly_graph`, deg <= 6):** `h` has degree <= 5, the curve `z^2 = C(h - e)^2 + D` has genus
  <= 4, branch points = roots of `h(x) = e +- i w`, i.e. roots of **quintic** polynomials over `Q(i, e, w)`.
  So the Galois group of the 2-torsion field lies in `S5 wr C2`: composition factors C2 and A5 only. [Proof.]
* **Power laws `y = a x^b`, b = p/q:** `z^2 = A + 2B t^s + C t^{2s}` after `x = t^q`; the branch points are
  roots of `t^s = const` (roots of unity times a constant): abelian. Moreover the family has **no moduli**: rescaling
  `t` identifies all constants, so the Jacobian of `z^2 = 1 + t^{2n}` is a single CM abelian variety (Fermat quotient)
  with periods `B(1/(2n), 1/2 - 1/(2n))/(2n)` [Wolfram: `int_0^inf dt/sqrt(1+t^4) = Gamma(1/4)^2/(4 sqrt(pi))` to 78
  digits; `n = 3,5,6` Beta formula to 29 digits]. By the main theorem of complex multiplication the torsion
  Galois groups are abelian over a cyclotomic field [Cited: Shimura-Taniyama, *Complex multiplication of abelian
  varieties and its applications to number theory*, Math. Soc. Japan 1961; not re-read]. So no non-solvable
  Galois group comes out of power laws. [Proof sketch, route-scoped.]
* **Cubic Bezier:** `speed^2` is a quartic sum of two squares: genus 1, giving `F` and `Pi` (already used).
* **Ellipse, sine, exp_two:** genus 1, 1 (`w^2 = (1-s^2)(2-s^2)`), 0.
* **Real periods are not available as arc lengths of integrals between roots.** `speed^2 = x'^2 + y'^2` is a sum of
  two squares, so its real zeros have even multiplicity; it never has simple real branch points. The classical real
  period `int_{e_1}^{e_2} dx/sqrt{f}` between simple roots is therefore not an arc length of any drawable curve; P
  reaches `K` only through the integration-by-parts identities of `Basic`. [Proof for the base curves.]
* Offset towers: `speed(offsetParam) = |1 - d kappa| speed`, genus is unchanged by one offset (SUMMARY, [Proof]);
  non-conformal affine steps in between can raise the genus (G2 says the degree roughly doubles per layer).
  Whether such towers reach hyperelliptic curves with more than five free branch points: [Open].

## 2. Division values (b)

### 2.1 What P gets

Verified identities, all at `c = 3/10` unless said (Wolfram):

* `sn(K/2) = 1/sqrt(1+k')` (the repo's halving), difference `0`; `s = sn(K/3)` satisfies
  `c s^4 - 2c s^3 + 2s - 1 = 0`, residual `0` to 79 digits, real roots `-1.569..., 0.53338...`.
* `s5 = sn(K/5)`: minimal polynomial over Q (degree 12, found by LLL at 500 digits, residual `10^-496`):
  `1000 - 2000x - 4000x^2 + 3000x^3 + 1500x^4 - 4320x^5 + 840x^6 + 3480x^7 - 450x^8 - 900x^9 + 108x^10 + 54x^11 - 27x^12`.
  Irreducible over Q (`Factor` does not split it).
* Frobenius cycle types of that polynomial mod 153 good primes between 19 and 937 (bad primes excluded; counts 35, 29, 19, 14, 14, 13, 12, 9, 8 in the order listed): `{12}, {2,10}, {4,4,4},
  {3,3,3,3}, {2^6}, {1,1,5,5}, {6,6}, {1^4 2^4}, {1,1,2,4,4}`. These contain elements of order 12, 10, 5, 6,
  so the group is not S5 or PSL(2,5)=A5 and is consistent with `GL(2,5)/{+-1}` (order 240) acting on the 12 points
  `(F_5^2 \ 0)/+-1` [Wolfram, Heuristic: cycle statistics are evidence not proof].
* `lambda(5 tau)` (`tau = i K'/K`, computed with `ModularLambda`; check `K'(l)/K(l) - 5K'/K = 0` to 299 digits) has
  minimal polynomial over Q: `729 - 8303891700y + 32756627100y^2 - 43488079200y^3 + 18759289200y^4 + 275171520y^5 + 1000000y^6`
  (irreducible, degree 6).
* `lambda(5 tau)` lies in `Q(s5)`: `1225000 lambda5 = -44886000 + 122887500 s + 93007500 s^2 - ... + 1452330 s^12`
  [Wolfram, relation among `(lambda5, 1, s, ..., s^11)`, height 2.2e8, residual `10^-592`].
* `s5` is quadratic over `Q(lambda5)`: relation `A(t) + B(t) s + C(t) s^2 = 0` with `A,B,C` of degree <= 5 in
  `t = lambda5` and coefficients up to 1.3e7, residual `10^-744` [Wolfram].
* `s7 = sn(K/7)`: cubic over `Q(lambda(7 tau))` (heights up to 7.1e11, residual `10^-1989`, 32 unknowns; the quadratic
  ansatz fails with a spurious height 1e83) [Wolfram]. The degree 8 of `lambda(7 tau)` is the textbook value
  (Jacobi modular equation of degree p+1) [Cited, standard], not rechecked here.
* `lambda(11 tau)` has degree 12 over Q at `c = 3/10` (height 2.4e26, residual `10^-1324`; degree 10, 11 give spurious
  heights 1e122, 1e112) [Wolfram].

Consequence [Heuristic, from the identities above at one `c`, plus the standard generic argument that the
degree-6 subfield and the quadratic step are given by rational functions of `c`]: for P-constructible `c`,
`lambda(5 tau)` is a real root of a sextic with coefficients in `Q(c)` (so in P by `root_Pconstructible_le_six_coeffs`),
and `sn(K/5)` is a real root of a quadratic over `Q(c, lambda5)`, so `sn(K/5) in P`; likewise `K/7` with a degree-8 and a
cubic step (`root_Pconstructible_deg8_coeffs`). The cases that matter for a Lean proof: `K/3` is a one-line quartic
from the addition theorem; `K/5`, `K/7` need explicit polynomials in `c` that this note did not extract.

### 2.2 Which Galois groups

| Level | Field `Q(c)(sn(K/N))` | Group (generic c) | New composition factor? |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3 | degree 4 (quartic above) | solvable [expected, not computed] | no |
| 5 | degree 12 | `GL(2,5)/{+-1}` [Wolfram evidence] | A5 only (already in towers) |
| 7 | degree 24 [expected `(p^2-1)/2`] | `GL(2,7)/{+-1}` | PSL(2,7) (already covered, degrees 7/8) |
| 11 | degree 60 [expected] | `GL(2,11)/{+-1}` | **PSL(2,11)**: new |
| p | `(p^2-1)/2` | `GL(2,p)/{+-1}` | PSL(2,p) |

Generic-group claims for p >= 7 follow from Serre's open-image theorem for non-CM curves over number fields
[Cited: Serre, Inventiones Math. 15 (1972) 259-331 (the surjectivity of `rho_{E,l}` for large `l` over Q); the
statement for `Q(c)` with `c` a transcendental parameter is the elementary monodromy statement
(`SL(2,Z)` surjects on `SL(2,F_p)`) and is [Proof]. For specific algebraic `c` Hilbert irreducibility
gives it for almost all `c`.]

**Dickson ceiling** [Proof modulo Dickson, L. E. Dickson, *Linear groups* (1901), classification of subgroups of
PSL(2,q), as used in Serre loc. cit.; I did not open the primary source]: every non-solvable subgroup of
`PSL(2,F_p)` is `PSL(2,p)` itself or contains A5 as its only non-solvable section; `GL(2,Z/N)` has only
p-group kernels over the residue levels. So the non-solvable sections of any group arising from elliptic
division values are among `{A5, PSL(2,p)}`. Hence:

* PSL(2,7): yes. PSL(2,11): yes if level 11 is solved. PSL(2,13), ...: likewise.
* **PSL(2,8), PGammaL(2,8) = Ree(3): never from elliptic division.** To get PSL(2,F_8) one needs a 2-dimensional
  `F_8`-representation, which arises from `A[2]` of an abelian threefold with real multiplication by a cubic
  field in which 2 is inert (e.g. `Q(zeta_7)^+`, since `2 != +-1 mod 7`). That is an abelian-function
  division value of a *threefold*. I did not find a verified citation for a concrete threefold with this image;
  the Dieulefait-Wiese framework (`PSL_2(F_{p^n})` as Galois groups via modular representations, Trans. AMS 363
  (2011) 4569-4584) concerns positive-density sets of primes `p` and does not state `p = 2, n = 3`. [Open].
* **A_n, n >= 9: never from elliptic functions**, whatever finite sequence of division values and compositions is
  used: A9 is not a section of `GL(2,Z/N)` or of any tower of them and S8 (A9 is larger than |S8|=40320).

### 2.3 Solving modular equations in P

The nome and the period ratio are available, so the modular transformation can be *verified* in P, but a modular
equation has to be *solved*:

* **Level 2, 3, 5, 7** are degree 3, 4, 6, 8 over `Q(c)`: solvable in P (the repo's degree <= 8 theorems).
* **Level 4, 6, 8, ...** by composition.
* **Level 11** (degree 12), **13** (degree 14), ...: needs an unavailable step. The root we want is the one with
  `K'(l)/K(l) = N K'(c)/K(c)`; it is one of the real roots of the level-N polynomial, so any real root that P can
  produce at all gives no problem about *which* root. The problem is *producing* one.
* **Is `r(c) = K'(c)/K(c)` invertible in P?** Forward: yes. Inverse: [Heuristic, scoped]. `arc_of_length` and
  `box_*` invert `s -> int_0^s speed`. `dr/dc = -pi/(4 c (1-c) K(c)^2)` [Wolfram, to 30 digits] so for `r` to be such an
  integral the drawn curve would need speed `pi/(4 c(1-c) K(c)^2)` as a function of its position `c`: the position
  variable would have to enter through `K(c)`, whereas the only transcendental dependence on position in base curves is via
  `exp`/`sin`. Closing this would need a curve whose position-dependence is itself a period. This is the same
  obstruction the repo records for `am` (`F` is not an arc length), strengthened: `r^{-1}` is a *modular* inverse.
  [Open: no proof that no tower of offsets produces such a speed.]
* **Schneider.** If `tau` is algebraic and not imaginary quadratic then `j(tau)` is transcendental [Cited: T. Schneider,
  Math. Ann. 113 (1937); statement as quoted in arXiv:2408.00271 (2024) introduction, primary source not opened].
  So for algebraic non-CM `r`, `c = r^{-1}` is transcendental; an inverse of `r` on algebraic inputs would be a source of
  new transcendentals, not of the algebraic Galois-group data. For `r = sqrt(n)` (singular moduli, class field theory) the
  value is algebraic with solvable (generalized dihedral) group. [Cited: standard CM theory.]
* Barre-Sirieix-Diaz-Gramain-Philibert and Nesterenko (algebraic independence of `q`, `j`, `pi`, `Gamma(1/4)`) say that
  `q = exp(-pi r)` and `c` are not both algebraic when `r` is algebraic non-CM [Cited, via arXiv:2408.00271 context]: the nome
  is a genuinely new transcendental in P.

## 3. Hermite-Klein style routes by analogy (c)

### 3.1 What the classical sources do

* **Quintic** [Cited]: Hermite, *Sur la resolution de l'equation du cinquieme degre*, C. R. Acad. Sci. Paris 46 (1858)
  508-515 (Oeuvres II, 5-12); Kronecker, same title, C. R. 46 (1858) 1150-1152; Brioschi 1858. The roots of the
  Bring-Jerrard quintic are modular functions at `(tau + 16 nu)/5`, `5 tau`; the transcendental input is `tau` determined
  from the coefficient (inversion of the elliptic modular function). In P the sextic `Phi_5` is solved by algebra, so
  P does not need the inversion for the quintic.
* **Degree 7 and 11** [Cited]: Klein, *Ueber die Transformation siebenter Ordnung der elliptischen Functionen*,
  Math. Ann. 14 (1879) (EuDML 156835; pages not confirmed here, I recall 428-471); *Ueber die Transformation elfter Ordnung*,
  Math. Ann. 15 (1879) 533-555 (EuDML 156871). Level 11 gives the degree-11 resolvent with group PSL(2,11)
  (LMFDB 11T5, order 660; modern commentary arXiv:2105.08602). **PSL(2,11) is a proper subgroup of A11.** Klein's
  resolvent solves a one-parameter family, not the general degree-11 equation.
* **General n** [Cited]: Jordan, *Traite des substitutions et des equations algebriques* (Paris, 1870); Thomae, J. reine angew.
  Math. 71 (1870) 201-222, Beitrag zur Bestimmung von `theta(0,...,0)`; Umemura, appendix to Mumford, *Tata Lectures on
  Theta II*, Birkhauser 1984, pp. 3.261-3.272, using Thomae with the period matrix of `y^2 = F(x)`,
  `F = x(x-1)f(x)` (n odd) or `x(x-1)(x-2)f(x)` (n even): each root is a ratio of fourth powers of theta constants.
  [As summarized on the Wikipedia page for Thomae's formula; Umemura's text not opened.] Zhivkov, *Resolution of degree <= 6
  algebraic equations by genus two theta constants*, Humboldt-Universitat zu Berlin preprint no. 11 (2005),
  doi:10.18452/2623.
* Mumford, *Tata Lectures on Theta II*, section 8 of Chapter IIIa for Thomae [Cited via the same page].

### 3.2 What P would need, and what it has

For a target `f` of degree `n` (take the real root we want to reach as one of the branch points):

| Needed | Has? |
|---|---|
| A curve `y^2 = F(x)` with the roots of `f` as branch points | Not as a *drawn* curve: drawn speed-curves have branch points of degree <= 5 (1.2) |
| The period matrix `Omega` of `y^2 = F` (integrals of `x^j dx/y` over a symplectic basis) | No: needs integrals between roots; real arcs give sums of two squares only; hypergeometric structure (`F` trinomial: periods are `_{n-1}F_{n-2}`, Mellin/Sturmfels [Cited: G1]) needs `pFq`, only `_2F_1` is in the repo |
| Siegel theta constants `theta[m](0, Omega)` (rapidly convergent genus-g series) | Only for `g = 1`: `theta^2 = 2K{...}/pi`, `Q`-linear in `K` (forward). No genus >= 2 mechanism, no series operator |
| Thomae-Umemura formulas giving the roots from the theta constants | Algebraic after theta is known |
| The *inverse* map from the coefficients to `Omega` (this is the transcendental content of Umemura/Jordan) | No: the same inversion obstruction as 2.3, for the Siegel modular variety |

All five rows are individually "not a constructor". The first one is the structural obstruction: P's drawn curves have
a fixed shape of branch set. A new curve with more free branch points would have to come from an offset-affine tower
(G2), which this note does not analyze [Open]. A cheaper observation: since Thomae gives `theta^4 in Q(roots)(Omega-periods)`,
genus >= 2 *forward* evaluation is algebraic-in-roots and does not reach new roots; the missing content is entirely the inversion.

### 3.3 Sources of A_n, n >= 6, in the literature, ranked for P

1. `Gal(Q(J[2])) = Gal(f)` for hyperelliptic Jacobians (genus `g = floor((n-1)/2)`), contained in `Sp(2g, F_2)`, which contains
   S_{2g+1}, S_{2g+2} [Proof: Weierstrass points; Cited: Mumford, Tata II]. This is the only classical source with S_n for all n.
2. PSL(2,p) for p prime, from elliptic curves: sections of towers or new (p >= 11).
3. `PSL(2, F_{p^r})` and `PSp(2g, F_p)` from RM/abelian varieties of GL2-type or genus g [Cited: Dieulefait-Wiese, loc. cit.].
   Contains A6 = PSL(2,9) = `Sp(4,2)'`; A8 = PSL(4,2). Not A9+.
4. Siegel modular forms as modular functions on `A_g`: the hyperelliptic locus; Igusa's genus-2 formulas express invariants of binary sextics by theta constants
   [Cited from memory: J. Igusa, Amer. J. Math 1967; not checked].

## 4. Why route-scoped (not a conclusion about P)

* [Proof] Arc lengths of affine images of single base curves have branch sets of degree <= 5 (graphs), or abelian (power laws),
  or genus <= 1. So Thomae-Umemura needs more than `arc_length` on a base curve.
* [Heuristic] Multiple arcs, offsets, boxes and crossings could in principle assemble more elaborate numbers; the repo's theorem
  `crossing_Pconstructible` shows crossings are algebraic and the record of offset-inversion shows towers are triangular.

## 5. The Galois catch (d), stated precisely

**Filter [Proof].** Let `F_0` be the smallest real field containing `Q` closed under real roots of polynomials of degree <= 8 over
itself, equivalently the union of towers `Q = E_0 < E_1 < ... < E_m`, `E_{i+1} = E_i(x_i)` with `x_i` a real root of a degree <= 8
polynomial over `E_i`. The Galois group of the normal closure of `E_m` over `Q` embeds in an iterated wreath product of subgroups
of `S_8`, so every composition factor of it is cyclic or a simple section of `S_8`: `A5, A6, A7, A8, PSL(2,7)`.
(Composition factors of a wreath product `G wr H` are those of `G` and `H`; a simple section of `S_8` has order dividing `8!`, and
an A_n with n >= 9 is larger than `S_8`. For PSL(2,8): its minimal permutation degree is 9, so it is not a section of a subgroup
of `S_8` containing 7 and 9 | order; the subgroup tables for degrees <= 8 (LMFDB transitive groups) agree [Cited, not re-read].)
So **A9, PSL(2,8), PGammaL(2,8), PSL(2,11), ... are not composition factors of any element of `F_0`.**

**Why this does not bound `P ∩ R_alg`.** `P ∩ R_alg` is not known to equal `F_0`. The proof of the filter uses the *whole* derivation as a chain of
number fields. If a derivation passes through a transcendental number `t` (e.g. `t = K(c)`, `pi`, `q`), then:

1. The next step is not a root of a polynomial over a number field, so the bounded-degree hypothesis has nothing to bound.
   Example [Proof, Lean]: `cos(2 pi/p)` is P-constructible for every `p` (via `arccos`/`arc_of_length`), with degree `(p-1)/2`
   (unbounded) but cyclic group, so consistent with the filter. Example [Wolfram]: `sn(K(3/10)/5, 3/10)` is an algebraic number of
   degree 12 with group `GL(2,5)/{+-1}` obtained from the transcendental `K/5`; the algebra reduces to degree <= 6 here, but the
   *derivation* as stated (a division value) is not a tower, and at level 11 the same derivation would produce a group
   (PSL(2,11)) that no tower can.
2. The algebraic number is algebraic over `Q(c)` (the argument's own field), but its relation to `c` comes from an *analytic identity*
   (a modular equation, addition theorem, Thomae formula), not from a crossing of curves. That identity has its own Galois/monodromy group,
   unrelated to the degree of any crossing.
3. Values of transcendental functions at transcendental arguments can be algebraic: `sn(K/5, c)`, `cos(2 pi/7)`, `lambda(5 tau)`, singular values
   `k_r` at `K'/K = sqrt r` (CM, [Cited]). No hypothesis on the degree of the argument's field is available, because `K` is not algebraic.

**What the filter does say.** It says: if a real algebraic number `x` has a Galois closure with a composition factor in `{A_n : n >= 9}`
(for example any root of a degree-9 irreducible polynomial with Galois group `S_9`), then *every* P-derivation of `x` passes through a transcendental
step whose analytic identity has monodromy containing A_n. For the transcendental sources that P has this cuts the possibilities sharply (it requires a
hyperelliptic Jacobian of genus >= 4 with a full branch set, or an equivalent source, since exp, sin and elliptic functions carry only
cyclic and `GL(2)` monodromy), but it is a statement about the routes the constructors supply, not about the target. [Heuristic.]
