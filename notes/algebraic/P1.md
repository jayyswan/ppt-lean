# P1 — (PRC₈) for Fano complete intersections

Status: done (2026-11-06)

Headline.
- Local solubility is free over totally real solvable fields (Lemma LS). So every smooth X in the
  circle-method range has E-points: dim ≥ (D−1)2^D − 1, or N ≥ (d−1)2^d for hypersurfaces. This
  supersedes RW's exp(2^{D+2}(log D)²).
- E is Euclidean, which gives Witt slicing (Theorem W). It covers low-dimensional Fano CIs with ≥ 2
  quadrics, e.g. (2,2,3) for all N ≥ 7.
- Colliot-Thélène's conjecture would cover every Fano CI.
- None of this reaches general type.

Notation. E as in SUMMARY §10. Facts used about E (all [Proof] or in X2/H2):
- (E-deg8) every real root of a polynomial of degree ≤ 8 over E lies in E.
- (E-solv) E ⊇ ℚ^solv ∩ ℝ. Caution: real radicals a^{1/k} for ARBITRARY a ∈ E, and real roots of
  polynomials that are solvable over E (not over ℚ), are not obviously in E (casus irreducibilis:
  cos(arccos(c)/11)). They are in F (P has real roots, cos, arccos, so F(i) is closed under radicals).
  Everything below avoids them unless flagged.
- E is Euclidean (positive elements are squares), so quadratic forms over E are classified by signature
  as over ℝ; Witt index over E = min(p, q) [Proof].
- Lemma A (H2): a variety over E of degree ≤ 8 with a smooth real point has E-points, dense near it.
- Density principle [Proof]: a smooth E-rational variety Y has Y(E) dense in Y(ℝ) (an open dense
  U ≅ open ⊂ 𝔸^m has U(E) dense in U(ℝ); U(ℝ) is dense in the manifold Y(ℝ)).

## (1) Hypersurfaces

### 1.1 Why the resolvent-degree recursion does not transfer [Proof of the obstruction to the method]
- Hilbert/Wolfson/Sutherland (Wolfson, *Tschirnhaus transformations after Hilbert*, Enseign. Math.
  2020, arXiv:2001.06515; Sutherland arXiv:2107.08139, polar cones: Lemma 2.24, Prop 2.19, 2.26;
  Heberle–Sutherland) find k-planes on degree-d hypersurfaces by (i) taking a point via a line section
  ("solving a degree-d polynomial", Sutherland Prop 2.19/Rem 2.20), then (ii) iterated polar cones, CIs
  of type (1,…,d−1) with growing multiplicities. Their auxiliary extensions have degrees 24, 36, 64,
  108, …, 236196 (Sutherland Thm 3.10 proof) and are bounded only in RESOLVENT degree. [Cited]
- Over E both steps fail: (i) a degree-d (d ≥ 9) line section is neither solvable nor of degree ≤ 8;
  (ii) RD is the wrong measure (E needs DEGREE ≤ 8 or solvability, not few parameters). The thresholds
  "RD(n) ≤ n−6 for n ≥ 21, n−7 for n ≥ 109, n−8 for n ≥ 325, n−ℓ for n > (ℓ−1)!/24 (9 ≤ ℓ ≤ 14)"
  [Cited: Sutherland Thms 3.7, 3.10] give no points over E.
- The only point-producing step that avoids solving degree d is Brauer's radical step: an E-line
  through p, q with f(sp + tq) = a s^d + b t^d (q on the polar CI of type (1,2,…,d−1) at p).
  Realness breaks it:
  - real type (p, q real) forces exactly 1 real root (d odd) or 0/2 (d even) on the line [Proof];
  - if f is a small smooth perturbation of a product of d real linear forms, almost every real line has
    d real roots, so real-type binomial lines through most real points do not exist [Proof sketch];
  - the conjugate type f|ℓ = L^d + L̄^d (all d roots real) still yields roots in E (L = ωL̄ with
    ω = e^{iπ(2k+1)/d}, and cos, sin(π/d) ∈ ℚ^solv) [Proof], but its parameter variety, in real
    coordinates, is a system of d−1 real forms of degree d: no reduction [Proof].
  - This matches X2 §2.2: no sign-tracked Brauer.
- Odd d: Birch's odd induction works over E, since E is a Birch field (X2 Prop D: ϕ_{d,r}(E) = r) and
  Wooley's efficient diagonalisation (*An explicit version of Birch's theorem*, Acta Arith. 85 (1998),
  Lemma 2.1, valid over any field) uses ONLY odd-degree auxiliary systems [Cited]. But the bounds are
  tower-type: over ℚ, v_d(ℚ) < ψ^{((d−5)/2)}(d r (m+1)), with ψ^{(n)} iterating ψ^{(n−1)} about 42 log x
  times, ψ^{(0)} = exp (Wooley Thm 1). Over E the base inputs are better, but the recursion has the
  same shape. Numerically useless next to 1.2. [Cited + Heuristic]

### 1.2 Theorem H: the arithmetic route, with local solubility made free [Proof sketch]

**Lemma LS (local solubility is free over totally real solvable fields) [Proof sketch].**
Let S be a finite set of primes and K_p/ℚ_p (p ∈ S) finite extensions. Then there is a totally real
number field M ⊂ ℚ^solv with every completion M_u (u | p ∈ S) containing a copy of K_p.
- Replace K_p by its Galois closure K''_p over ℚ_p. Gal(K''_p/ℚ_p) is solvable (local Galois groups
  are), so ℚ_p = K_0 ⊂ … ⊂ K_r = K''_p with each step cyclic of prime degree.
- Inductively take M_{i+1}/M_i cyclic of prime degree with prescribed localisations: at each u | p
  (where M_{i,u} ≅ K_i) the step K_{i+1}/K_i transported along the isomorphism; trivial (split) at every
  real place; trivial where a tower has already ended. Grunwald–Wang supplies this for prime degree
  (its exceptional case needs 8 | degree) [Cited: Artin–Tate, Class Field Theory, Ch. X Thm 5;
  Neukirch–Schmidt–Wingberg (9.2.8)].
- M = M_r is totally real, its Galois closure is solvable, and M_u ≅ K''_p ⊇ K_p.

**Theorem H.** Let L_0 ⊂ E be a number field (real embedding σ_0) and X ⊂ ℙ^N_{L_0} smooth,
projective, geometrically integral, of degree D, with X^{σ_0}(ℝ) ≠ ∅. Suppose X satisfies the Hasse
principle and weak approximation over every finite extension of L_0 (HP+WA). Then X(E) ≠ ∅, and X(E)
is dense in X^{σ_0}(ℝ). This holds in particular when:
- (a) dim X ≥ (D − 1)2^D − 1 [Cited: Browning–Heath-Brown, *Forms in many variables and differing
  degrees*, JEMS 2017, arXiv:1403.5937, Thm 1.1 over ℚ; Frei–Madritsch, Mathematika 63 (2017),
  arXiv:1412.6419, Thm 1.4 over number fields];
- (b) X is a hypersurface of degree d with N + 1 > (d − 1)2^d [Cited: Birch 1962; Skinner, Compositio
  106 (1997) 11–29 over number fields, singular integral corrected by Frei–Madritsch];
- (c) X is cut out by a nonsingular system (in BHB's sense) with Σd_i = 𝒟 and N + 1 > (𝒟 − 1)2^𝒟
  [Cited: BHB Thm 1.7; Frei–Madritsch Thm 1.3];
- (d) X is cut out by R forms of equal degree d with N + 1 > R + R(R+1)(d−1)2^{d−1} [Cited: Birch,
  Skinner; plus Proof: for a smooth CI the projective Birch singular locus misses X, which is cut by R
  hypersurfaces, so it has dim ≤ R − 1, i.e. affine dim ≤ R];
- (e) two forms of degrees D' > E' ≥ 2 with N + 1 > (2+E')(D'−1)2^{D'−1} + E'2^{E'−1} [Cited: BHB
  Cor 1.8 over ℚ; the number-field version via Frei–Madritsch Cor 1.2 is not checked term by term].

*Proof sketch.*
1. (Realness.) Choose a ∈ L_0 with σ_0(a) > 0 and σ(a) < 0 at every other real place. Then
   L_1 = L_0(√a) ⊂ E, and every real place of L_1 lies over σ_0, where X has smooth real points.
   (This is the RW step of X2.)
2. (Local.) X is smooth and geometrically integral, so X(L_{1,v}) ≠ ∅ outside a finite set of v
   (Lang–Weil + Hensel). For each of the finitely many bad p choose K_p ⊂ ℚ̄_p over which X has points
   above every v | p. Lemma LS gives a totally real M ⊂ ℚ^solv ∩ ℝ ⊂ E with M_u ≅ K''_p. Every completion
   of L = L_1·M above p is then K''_p, which contains the needed field (K''_p is normal over ℚ_p, so every
   embedding lands inside it). Real places of L lie over real places of L_1, since M is totally real.
3. X_L is everywhere locally soluble, with smooth local points. HP+WA over L gives X(L) ≠ ∅, dense at
   the real place L ⊂ ℝ. And L ⊂ E. ∎

Remarks.
- For L_0 = ℚ, step 1 is void: X has points over a TOTALLY REAL SOLVABLE field (a Taylor-type
  statement in the circle-method range). [Proof sketch]
- The degree-≤8 closure is not used. The whole gain over X2's RW comes from replacing Wooley's local
  bound (Thm 4.4: exp(2^{D+2}(log D)²)) by "local is free".

### 2.2 Low dimension: Witt slicing (uses that E is Euclidean + Lemma A) — NEW

**Theorem W [Proof sketch].** Let X ⊂ ℙ^N be a smooth complete intersection over E, x ∈ X(ℝ).
Suppose the ideal of X has generators Q, g_1, …, g_m with Q a quadric that is nondegenerate of real
signature (p, q), min(p, q) ≥ m + 1, and ∏ deg g_j ≤ 8. Then X(E) has points arbitrarily close to x.

*Proof sketch.*
1. Isotropic projective m-planes Λ ⊂ Q through x correspond to isotropic m-spaces W of x^⊥/x (signature
   (p−1, q−1), Witt index ≥ m), so real ones exist.
2. (Transversality.) Λ ∩ X = Λ ∩ {g_j = 0} is transverse at x iff the m functionals dg_j|_{x^⊥/x}
   (independent, X smooth) stay independent on W. The bad W lie in some u^⊥, u ∈ span of the normals.
   For fixed u this has codimension m in OG_m (also when u is isotropic: lift from u^⊥/u). The union over
   ℙ^{m−1} of u's has codimension ≥ 1, so a generic real Λ_0 ∋ x is transverse.
3. Q is defined over E with Witt index min(p, q) ≥ m + 1 over E (E Euclidean). So the isotropic
   Grassmannian OG_{m+1}(Q) is projective homogeneous with an E-point, hence E-rational [Cited:
   Borel–Tits big-cell argument], so its E-points are dense in its real points (density principle).
4. Take an E-point Λ' near Λ_0. By the implicit function theorem (step 2), Λ' ∩ {g_j = 0} has an
   isolated reduced real point y' near x. Since Λ' ⊂ Q, y' ∈ X.
5. The isolated points of Λ' ∩ {g_j = 0} ⊂ Λ' ≅ ℙ^m_E number at most ∏ deg g_j ≤ 8 (refined Bézout) and
   form a Galois-stable set. So y' has residue degree ≤ 8 over E, and is real, so y' ∈ X(E) (E-deg8). ∎

**Finding a good quadric when the ideal has several (Corollary W) [Proof].**
- For any nonzero quadric Q in I(X)_2 we have corank Q ≤ k − 1, where k is the number of generators.
  - Q can replace a quadric generator.
  - If corank Q = c ≥ k, then Sing Q ≅ ℙ^{c−1} meets the other k − 1 generators, at a point where the
    Jacobian drops rank, i.e. a singular point of X. So c ≤ k − 1.
- Assume I(X)_2 contains a nondegenerate member (true if two of the quadrics meet smoothly, and
  generically). Run θ ↦ cos θ·Q + sin θ·Q' from Q to −Q. Then p goes from p_0 to n − p_0 (n = N + 1),
  jumping by ≤ k − 1 at the finitely many degenerate members.
- So some nondegenerate member has min(p, q) ≥ (n − k + 1)/2. Theorem W (with m = k − 1) therefore
  applies whenever N ≥ 3k − 2.
- If a generic pencil of the linear system has only corank ≤ 1 members (generic X), min(p, q) =
  ⌊n/2⌋ is attained, and N ≥ 2k − 1 suffices.

**Lemma C3 (E-lines on cubics) [Proof sketch; generic-point smoothness cited].** Let C ⊂ ℙ^N be a smooth
cubic over E with a real point x_0 at which the second fundamental form II (on T_{x0}C, dim N − 1) has
min(p, q) ≥ 2. Then C contains E-lines.
- Take an E-point p near x_0 (Lemma A, degree 3), general enough. Lines through p in C form
  Z_p = {II_p = III_p = 0} ⊂ ℙ^{N−2}, a CI of type (2, 3) and degree 6 over E. It is smooth for general p
  [Cited: lines through a general point of a smooth hypersurface of low degree form a smooth CI; e.g.
  Harris–Mazur–Pandharipande, Beheshti].
- Real point: with p, q ≥ 2 the real cone {II_p = 0} \ 0 is connected (it is S^{p−1}×S^{q−1}×ℝ_{>0}).
  III_p is odd, so it changes sign on it and has a real zero.
- Lemma A on Z_p gives an E-point, i.e. an E-line through p.

**Coverage of the smallest Fano CIs of degree ≥ 9.**
- A Fano CI of degree ≥ 9 has dim ≥ 4 [Proof]. Fano means Σ d_i ≤ N, and dim = N − k.
  - dim 2: only (2), (3), (2,2).
  - dim 3: hypersurfaces of degree ≤ 4, (2,3), (2,2,2), all of degree ≤ 8.

## (3) Rational connectedness

**Unconditionally, rational connectedness alone gives no E-points by any mechanism found.**
[Proof of the obstruction to the methods; Heuristic that no other method exists]
- Every rational-curve tool needs a first point.
  - Lines and conics through a point, the polar cones, and Kollár's very free curves all start from a
    point.
  - Kollár (*Rationally connected varieties over local fields*, Ann. Math. 150 (1999)): over ℝ, real
    points in one component are joined by real rational curves [Cited]. Moving these to E means finding
    E-points on Hom(ℙ¹, X), whose components have huge degree. That is the same problem again.
  - "RC + one K-point ⇒ dense" holds over LARGE (ample) fields (Kollár), but E is not known to be large.
    E being large is (PRC₈) for curves (A1, Theorem G covers only some curves).
- No fibrations to use.
  - A Fano CI of degree ≥ 9 has dim ≥ 4 (§2.2), hence Picard rank 1 (Lefschetz), hence no conic-bundle
    or del Pezzo-fibration morphisms.
  - Linear projections have fibres that are linear sections of degree D ≥ 9. Lowering the degree needs
    a point of high multiplicity, i.e. a point of X.
- What does work is a covering family of low-degree subvarieties parametrised by an E-rational variety.
  - Theorem W: X ∩ Λ for Λ in an isotropic Grassmannian.
  - Lemma C3: lines in a cubic containing X.
  - These come from the auxiliary hypersurfaces of degree ≤ 3 in I(X). A degree-d hypersurface with
    d ≥ 9 has none, so it gets nothing.

**Conditionally: all of them [Proof, modulo a conjecture].**
- Assume Colliot-Thélène's conjecture: for smooth proper RC X over a number field L, X(L) is dense in
  X(𝔸_L)^{Br} [Cited: Colliot-Thélène, *Points rationnels sur les fibrations*, 2003].
- For a smooth CI of dim ≥ 3:
  - Pic X̄ = ℤH, so H¹(L, Pic X̄) = 0;
  - H²(O) = 0 (Fano), and H³(X, ℤ) is torsion-free, so Br X̄ = 0;
  - hence Br X = Br_0 X, and the conjecture gives HP + WA.
- Theorem H's proof (local solubility free over L = L_0(√a)·M) then gives: **every smooth Fano CI of
  degree ≥ 9 (all of which have dim ≥ 4) over a number field L_0 ⊂ E with a real point at σ_0 has
  E-points, dense in its real points.** [Proof sketch, conditional on CT's conjecture]
- So the unconditional gap is exactly the circle-method gap. No new arithmetic input specific to E
  (degree ≤ 8) is needed for Fano CIs, only HP.

## (4) Comparison with real Wooley (X2 Theorem RW)

- RW: dim X ≥ exp(2^{D+2}(log D)²), with D = deg X, for any smooth X. The bound comes from Wooley's
  local solubility over arbitrary algebraic extensions of ℚ_p (Thm 4.4).
- Theorem H(a) replaces it by dim X ≥ (D−1)2^D − 1 (BHB / Frei–Madritsch), for any smooth X:
  local solubility costs nothing over totally real solvable fields (Lemma LS).
  - D = 9: 4095 versus 10^4294.
  - D = 10: 9215 versus 10^9431.
  - In log scale: 2^D·log D versus e^{2^{D+2}(log D)²}, an improvement from triple-exponential-like
    to single-exponential in D.
  - For CIs, the type-specific bounds (c)–(e) are better still: (3,3): N ≥ 50; (2,2,2,2): N ≥ 44.
- The bounded-degree closure (degree ≤ 8) itself:
  - does NOT improve the high-dimensional bound;
  - its only contributions are Lemma A (degree ≤ 8), Theorem W and Lemma C3 (low dimension, via
    quadrics and cubics in the ideal), and the quadratic twist √a.
  - So for Fano CIs it fills part of the gap between "degree ≤ 8" and the circle-method range: CIs with
    many quadrics (Cor W), and odd (3, e) given Lemma C3's real condition.
- Correction to X2's regime table: the "high dimension" row should read dim ≥ (D−1)2^D − 1 (not
  exp(2^{D+2}(log D)²)), via Theorem H. RW's quadratic step remains step 1 of Theorem H.

## (5) Verdict

**Theorem P1 [Proof sketch; relies on Cited circle-method theorems and Grunwald–Wang].**
Let L_0 ⊂ E be a number field and X ⊂ ℙ^N_{L_0} a smooth Fano complete intersection with a real point
(at the given embedding). Then X(E) ≠ ∅, with E-points dense in X(ℝ), in each of these cases:
- (i) deg X ≤ 8 (Lemma A, H2);
- (ii) X is in the circle-method range: dim X ≥ (deg X − 1)2^{deg X} − 1, or the type-specific bounds
  H(b)–(e). Examples: hypersurfaces with N ≥ (d−1)2^d (4096, 9216, 20480, 45056 for d = 9..12);
  (3,3) with N ≥ 50; (2,2,2,2) with N ≥ 44;
- (iii) X = Q ∩ {g_1..g_m}, with Q a quadric in I(X) of real Witt index ≥ m+1 and ∏ deg g_j ≤ 8
  (Theorem W). In particular:
  - type (2,2,3) and (2,2,4), all Fano N, when I(X)_2 has a nondegenerate member;
  - (2,2,2,2) for N ≥ 10, and for N ≥ 8 generically;
  - (2, e), 5 ≤ e ≤ 8, when the quadric is not of sphere signature;
- (iv) type (3, e), e odd ≤ 7, when some cubic of I(X)_3 has a real point with second-fundamental-form
  index ≥ 2 (Lemma C3).
Conditionally on Colliot-Thélène's conjecture: all smooth Fano CIs with a real point (§3).

**First open cases.**
- (3,3) ⊂ ℙ^6, if the index-2 condition fails or is unproven. This is the smallest-dimensional
  uncovered type: a 4-fold, deg 9, index 1.
- (2,5) ⊂ ℙ^7 with sphere-type quadric.
- (3,4) ⊂ ℙ^7.
- Non-generic (2,2,2,2) ⊂ ℙ^8, ℙ^9.
- Degree-d hypersurfaces with d + 1 ≤ N + 1 ≤ (d−1)2^d (d ≥ 9): the cleanest open family. Nothing
  here touches it below the circle method.

**Can this reach general type (V_τ, S, T)? No [Proof sketch for each method].**
- Circle method (Theorem H): needs dim ≥ (D−1)2^D − 1 (or N ≫ Σ d_i), which forces Fano. V_τ (a surface,
  CI of n−3 quadrics), S (a surface, K = O(13)) and T (a 3-fold, K = O(18)) are far outside.
- RC / CT conjecture: needs rational connectedness. General-type varieties have no rational curves
  through general points.
- Witt slicing: for V_τ (n−3 quadrics in ℙ^{n−1}, dim 2), a 0-dim slice of degree ≤ 8 needs a linear
