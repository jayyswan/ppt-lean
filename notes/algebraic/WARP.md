# WARP: text warps against the algebraic goals

Status: **research note, 2026-11-10.** Lean: partial bridge, scratch only (see §6).
This note asks what the new text-warp formalization (`Pptc/TextBox.lean`,
`Pptc/Warp/Bilinear.lean`, and the `PConstructibleCurve.text` constructor in
`Pptc/Defs.lean`) adds to the algebraic programme `PLAN-algebraic.md`.

Labels: [Proof], [Wolfram], [Heuristic], [Open]. Every obstruction is scoped to the route
it blocks; nothing here is a negative about `algebraic_Pconstructible`.

## 0. Summary

1. **A single warped glyph adds no new algebraic numbers** [Proof, given the existing
   closure theorems]. The algebraic warped curves are rational quartics (eggs, piriform,
   Gerono lobe). A line or conic meets one in degree ≤ 4 or ≤ 8 over the field generated by
   its P-constructible parameters, and the repo already proves every real root of a
   degree-≤ 8 polynomial with P-constructible coefficients is P-constructible
   (`root_Pconstructible_deg8_coeffs`).
2. **The useful algebraic content is as an engine component, not as a number source.**
   The egg is a new drawable quartic with five continuous P parameters. Crossing it with a
   rational graph of degree `m` gives a crossing polynomial of degree `2m`, with monic
   coefficient rank `m + 4` for `m = 4, …, 7` (so 9 at degree 10 and 10 at degree 12)
   [Wolfram].
3. **Two new engine candidates:**
   - egg × quintic graph: degree 10, rank 9 — one parameter more than the current
     `y^10 = sextic` engine (F = 8). This is a genuine improvement for the open
     **non-totally-real decic** (MI-1) direction.
   - egg × sextic graph: degree 12, rank 10 — a new degree-12 engine with a cubic
     cofactor. It does **not** reach the `F ≥ 11` threshold that H5 asks for totally real
     decics, but it is a new shape for the **totally real nonic** and for `n = 11, 12`.
4. **No algebraic goal is closed by this note.** The arithmetic bottleneck (an F-point on
   the relevant incidence variety) is unchanged. The egg engines give *new incidence
   varieties* with one more parameter and lower degree than the existing ones; whether
   they have F-points is the next computation, not a consequence of the warp law.
5. **Lean:** the new `text` constructor makes the old conditional `TriangleCircleRule`
   reachable. The normalization of a single `●` box and the bounding box of a `●●` row are
   proved in scratch; the remaining step is isolating the left disc by a rectangular
   `restrict` and deriving `PConstructibleCurve (eggCurve a d₀ d₁)`.
6. **Follow-up (offsets, nonsquare, free handle).** The free handle is already a P
   parameter in `text` and is what makes the egg family an F-family. Nonsquare boxes add
   no new algebraic modulus (affine for straight guides; the egg shape family is universal;
   curved guides are transcendental). An offset adds one P parameter but the egg offset has
   geometric degree 16 (N4 convention; the elimination polynomial has total degree 28), so
   **offset egg × conic** has crossing degree at least 32 and is closed; the offset route is
   dominated. See §8 and `W4-offset.md`.

## 1. What the new formalization adds

The relevant changes are:

- `Pptc/TextBox.lean`: all 36 bending Transform presets as `WarpPreset.map`, the ruled-warp
  law `ruledWarp (T B) (u,v) = (1-v) T(u) + v B(u)`, the glyph catalogue, and
  `TextBox.outlineAt`.
- `Pptc/Defs.lean`: the single constructor
  `PConstructibleCurve.text (T : TextBox) (a b w h : ℝ) ... : PConstructibleCurve (T.outlineAt a b w h)`.
  The two handles and the box size are `PConstructible` reals; the stored text data is
  rational.
- `Pptc/Warp/Bilinear.lean`: the Triangle cell, the egg quartic, the piriform, and the
  conditional `TriangleCircleRule`.

Before the `text` constructor, a warp could only be tested as a *hypothesis* on the class.
Now every outline the UI can draw is in `PConstructibleCurve`, including the algebraic
warped curves below and the transcendental ones (spirals, elliptic trochoids, arc-length
sheared Béziers).

## 2. The algebraic warped curves

| curve | equation (affine normalization) | degree | genus | shape params (mod affine) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| egg | `a²(D(x) - 2y)² = D(x)² x(2a - x)`, `D` affine, `x ≥ 0` | 4 | 0 | 1 (the ratio `d₀ : d₁`) |
| piriform | `a² y² = x³(2a - x)` | 4 | 0 | 0 (one affine class) |
| Gerono lobe | the pinned Triangle image of `◗` (rational quartic) | 4 | 0 | 0 |
| polar ellipse / spiral / trochoid | transcendental | — | — | — |

[Proof for the egg and piriform: `heightCell_image_glyphCircle`, `piriform_eq_image` in
`Pptc/Warp/Bilinear.lean`. The genus-0 claim: the egg is a double cover of `x` with three
branch points (one double), so Riemann–Hurwitz gives `2g - 2 = -2`; the piriform has a
cusp.]

The egg has one shape parameter, the ratio `d₀ : d₁`, and four placement parameters
(x-position, x-scale, y-position, y-scale). The Triangle handle supplies the shape ratio
continuously; the box supplies the other four. All five are `PConstructible` in
`outlineAt`, and all five count when the egg is crossed with another curve, because the
relative placement is part of the engine.

The transcendental curves are new too, but they do not enter the algebraic goals: the
existing transcendental audit (`W3`, `H4`) already scopes them out, and none of them is a
new algebraic operation.

## 3. A single warp adds no new algebraic numbers

[Proof, modulo the cited Lean theorems.]

Let `C` be one of the egg, piriform or Gerono curves from a `text` constructor. Its
defining equation has coefficients in the field `F = P ∩ ℝ_alg` generated by the
`PConstructible` box and handle values.

- `C ∩ line`: a polynomial of degree ≤ 4 over `F`.
- `C ∩ conic`: a polynomial of degree ≤ 8 over `F`.
- `C ∩ cubic or quartic graph y = P(x)`: after substituting, degree ≤ 6 or ≤ 8 over `F`
  (the egg is quadratic in `y`, which halves the Bézout degree).
- `C ∩ quintic graph`: degree 10 — **not** covered.
- `C ∩ sextic graph`: degree 12 — **not** covered.
- egg ∩ egg: degree 16 — not covered, but see §4.

The line, conic and cubic/quartic-graph rows produce only numbers already in `F`:
`root_Pconstructible_le_six_coeffs` (degree ≤ 6 with P coefficients) and
`root_Pconstructible_deg8_coeffs` (degree ≤ 8 with P coefficients). This is the precise
sense in which "a warp alone gives no new numbers". It is not a statement that warped
curves are useless: it says their number-theoretic content is already in the degree-≤ 8
and solvable closure.

## 4. Warp engines: crossing degrees and parameter counts

An *engine* is a drawable crossing whose equation has a degree-`n` factor over the target
field; the bookkeeping law of H3 applies. The egg gives a new partner. Write `m` for the
degree of the rational graph `y = P(x)` and put

  `N_m(x) = (D(x) - 2(P(x) - y₀))² - D(x)² E(x)`,

where `D` is affine (the egg height) and `E(x) = 4((x-l)/w)(1-(x-l)/w)`. For `m ≥ 2`,
`deg N_m = 2m`.

**Rank computation [Wolfram].** Parameterize the egg by `(l, w, d₀, d₁, y₀)` and the graph
by `p₀, …, p_m`; form `N_m`, normalize it monic, and compute the Jacobian rank of the
coefficient map at a random integer point:

```mathematica
rankFor[m_] := Module[{vars, uu, dd, ee, pp, nn, monic, mc, vals, jj},
  vars = Join[{l, w, d0, d1, y0}, Table[Symbol["p" <> ToString[k]], {k, 0, m}]];
  uu = (x - l)/w; dd = d0 + d1 uu; ee = 4 uu (1 - uu);
  pp = Sum[Symbol["p" <> ToString[k]] x^k, {k, 0, m}];
  nn = Expand[(dd - 2 (pp - y0))^2 - dd^2 ee];
  monic = Expand[nn/Coefficient[nn, x, Exponent[nn, x]]];
  mc = Table[Coefficient[monic, x, k], {k, 0, Exponent[monic, x] - 1}];
  vals = Join[{l -> 5, w -> 3, d0 -> 2, d1 -> -4, y0 -> 7},
    Table[Symbol["p" <> ToString[k]] -> (-1)^k (2 k + 3), {k, 0, m}]];
  jj = D[mc, {vars}]; {m, Exponent[nn, x], Length[vars], MatrixRank[jj /. vals]}];
Table[rankFor[m], {m, 4, 7}]
(* {{4, 8, 10, 8}, {5, 10, 11, 9}, {6, 12, 12, 10}, {7, 14, 13, 11}} *)
```

Stable at four random points for `m = 5, 6`. So:

| partner | `d = deg N` | rank `F` | cofactor degree for `n = 9` | for `n = 10` | for `n = 11` | for `n = 12` |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| quintic graph | 10 | 9 | 1 | 0 | — | — |
| sextic graph | 12 | 10 | 3 | 2 | 1 | 0 |
| egg | 16 | ≤ 10 | 7 | 6 | 5 | 4 |

The last column pair is the raw over-ℂ count `d - n` (the degree of the cofactor); the
arithmetic condition is separate. Compare the current engines:

- `y^10 = sextic` (the MI-1 engine): degree 10, F = 8.
- graph × transposed graph (H3): degree 36, F = 13, but the incidence is nonlinear and
  the target is a fixed point of a composition.
- egg × quintic: degree 10, F = 9 — **one more free parameter than the MI-1 engine**, with
  a linear cofactor for `n = 9`.
- egg × sextic: degree 12, F = 10 — two more than MI-1, one below H5's `F ≥ 11` threshold
  for totally real decics.

Two things make the egg engines different from a generic polynomial family:

1. The egg's five parameters are **continuous P parameters**, not rational invariants.
   The handle is `PConstructible`, so the family is defined over `F`, not only over ℚ.
   This is the same advantage that Z8's F-point reformulation gives the existing engines.
2. The equations in the egg parameters are **quadratic** (`D` enters squared), and the
   cofactor is linear/quadratic. The condition variety is therefore a low-degree
   incidence variety, potentially easier than H3's nonlinear two-graph incidence.

## 5. Verdict by algebraic goal

- **Degree ≤ 8, solvable groups:** no change; already closed.
- **Degree 9, rational with a nonreal root:** already closed in Lean
  (`root_Pconstructible_of_rat_nonic_nonreal`). The egg engines give a second route, but
  no new case.
- **Degree 9, totally real (the open `A₉`/`S₉` case):** [Open]. The current route is one
  F-line on the Fano surface in `X₃ ∩ X₅`. The egg × sextic engine gives a degree-12
  alternative with a cubic cofactor and a 7-dimensional condition variety over ℂ
  [dimension count: 10 − (12 − 9)]. Whether
  it has an F-point is exactly the H3/Z8 question; the new feature is the lower degree and
  the quadratic egg equations. **This is the most valuable next computation.**
- **Degree 10, non-totally-real (MI-1):** [Open, modest improvement]. Egg × quintic has
  F = 9, one more than the current sextic engine. It does not remove MI-1 (the F-point
  problem remains), but it relaxes the dimension count by one. For totally real decics it
  is still below the `F ≥ 11` threshold of H5.
- **Degree 11–12:** egg × sextic is a new degree-12 engine with a quadratic/linear
  cofactor; no dominance over the existing F = 13 two-graph engine, but a different
  incidence and lower degree.
- **General n:** no change. `F` is still bounded (≤ 10 for one egg + one drawable graph),
  so the general-n obstruction of H1/H3 stands.

## 6. Lean bridge: status and remaining work

The old `TriangleCircleRule` assumed the warp constructor. The `text` constructor now
supplies it. The bridge is:

1. `TriangleCircleBox`: a `TextBox` with one `●` glyph and `WarpPreset.triangleUp`.
2. Show `T.outlineAt a b w h = triangleWarp 0 0 w h a '' glyphCircle (1/2)`.
   The normalization `inkNormalize T.ink` must send the glyph's font ellipse to the unit
   circle. This is the **single-circle normalization lemma**; it is proved in
   `Pptc/Scratch/WarpText.lean` (`inkNormalize_blackCircle`), together with the affine
   description of `RowGlyph.toSet` and the bounding-box extrema.
3. A single `●` is too wide: its normalized half-width is always `1/2` (the normalization
   cancels the font size), so it straddles the Triangle's midline and its image is two
   different half-eggs. The
   catalogue needs a **`●●` row**, whose left disc has normalized half-width
   `a = 742·s / (pen + 1484·s) < 1/4`. The two-circle normalization and its bounding box
   are proved in `Pptc/Scratch/WarpTwo.lean` (`two_black_toSet`, `two_black_bbox`,
   `inkNormalize_two_formula`).
4. Remaining: show the normalized left disc is `glyphCircle a`, and isolate it from the
   right disc by `PConstructibleCurve.restrict` to `[0, w/2] × [0, h]` (the discs are
   separated because `a < 1/4`). Then
   `triangleWarp_image_glyphCircle` and `heightCell_image_glyphCircle` give
   `PConstructibleCurve (eggCurve a d₀ d₁)` unconditionally; the pinned case `α = 1`
   gives the piriform after the shear and rescale already used in
   `piriform_PConstructibleCurve_of_rule`.

The scratch files are not imported by `Pptc.lean` and are not committed. The remaining
work is set algebra, not new mathematics; it is a good `lean-prover` task.

## 7. Recommended next steps

1. **Measure the egg engines with H3's protocol.** Compute the degree and defining
   equations of the condition variety for `χ_τ | N_m`, for `m = 5, 6`, and the Jacobian
   rank on monic nonics/decics. The key question: is the condition variety of degree < 9
   (then H2 Lemma A applies over F) or not?
2. **Test the explicit totally real S₉ nonic** `q = ∏_{i=-4}^{4}(x-i) + 1` (Y1a's field)
   against egg × sextic. A numerical real solution is not enough; the target is a rational
   or F-point. The earlier quick `FindRoot` search on egg × quintic for the totally real
   decic `∏_{i=-5}^{4}(x-i)+1` found no real solution at the tested heights; that is a
   scoped negative for that ansatz, not for the engine.
3. **Finish the Lean bridge** in `Pptc/Warp/Bilinear.lean` (or a new
   `Pptc/Warp/Text.lean`), then import it from `Pptc.lean`. This upgrades
   `TriangleCircleRule` from a hypothesis to a theorem for the catalogued `●●` row, and
   gives unconditional `PConstructibleCurve` for the egg and piriform.
4. **Check whether the `text` constructor's continuous handle can supply a genuine
   F-parameter to an existing engine** where the current engine only has rational
   parameters (e.g., replace a `poly_graph` coefficient by a warp handle). This is the
   only way warps could change the arithmetic, rather than just the shape.

## 8. Follow-up: offsets, nonsquare boxes, free handles

These are the three ways the warp family could gain algebraic parameters. The short
answer: the free handle is already the right move and is what makes the egg family an
F-family; nonsquare boxes add no new algebraic modulus; offsets add one P parameter but
pay a degree/gonality cost, and the one candidate that reached F = 11 — offset egg ×
conic — is now **closed** by the degree computation in `W4-offset.md`.

### 8.1 Offsets of warps

`PConstructibleCurve.offset` applies to any constructible curve with a regular tracing,
and its distance `d` is a `PConstructible` free parameter. So the offset of an algebraic
warp is legal, algebraic, and gains **one** P parameter.

The cost is degree and gonality. Calibration [Wolfram, exact resultant; details in
`W4-offset.md`]:

- offset of a circle by 1: a circle, degree 2;
- offset of the parabola `y = x²` by 1: a sextic (exact total degree 6);
- offset of the egg `a²(D-2y)² = D²x(2a-x)`: the **geometric offset degree is 16** (N4's
  abscissa convention). The elimination polynomial has X-degree 16, Y-degree 12 and total
  degree 28; the X-degree is the geometric degree, matching the ellipse octic 8 and
  `y=x⁴` 14, while the total degree is non-minimal (the ellipse analogue is total 16,
  geometric 8). The count is stable for `(d₀,d₁,r) = (1,2,1), (2,3,1), (1,4,1),
  (2,5,1/2)`, and a two-sided Groebner elimination factors as `(degree-4 extraneous)² ·
  (degree-28 elimination polynomial)`, confirming the X-degree 16. A preliminary numerical
  run suggesting degree 6 came from a mis-typed parametrization; the corrected computation
  has no low-degree relation.

Consequences for the algebraic goals:

- The offset of the egg has 6 parameters (5 egg + 1 distance), and **offset egg × general
  conic** has F = 6 + 5 = 11. But the geometric degree is 16, so the crossing degree is at
  least `16 · 2 = 32` and the cofactor has degree at least 22 at `n = 10` (23 at `n = 9`).
  A naive count on the non-minimal total degree 28 gives 56 and 46, even worse. The
  condition variety has no dimension. The elimination factor is irreducible over ℚ, so
  there is no smaller ℚ-factor to exploit. **This closes offset egg × conic as an engine
  route**, and with it the only warp-based candidate that reached H5's F = 11 threshold.
- The kill is scoped: it is a Bézout/parameter-count kill for the egg × conic construction,
  not a statement about offset towers (`W1`) or non-algebraic offsets (Route I, T6). A
  degenerate condition variety of degree ≤ 10 could in principle evade it, but the
  geometric degree-16 offset gives no reason to expect one.
- The existing offset bases in N4 dominate the egg offset: cubic Bézier offset X-degree 10
  with 8 control parameters, `y=x³` offset X-degree 10, versus the egg's 16 with 6
  parameters. A single offset egg is a new curve of geometric degree 16 (so a line section
  is outside the degree-8 closure), but that is a number-source question for W1/N4, not a
  new warp engine; the cubic Bézier offset already does better.
- Offsetting twice adds another P parameter and raises the degree again; this is the
  known offset-tower trade-off of `W1`, not a new mechanism. The warp base does not
  change the accounting.

### 8.2 Nonsquare bounding boxes

For the **algebraic** warps, nonsquare boxes add no new modulus.

- The straight-guide presets (Triangle, Fade, Cascade, Slant, Chevron, …) have a bilinear
  cell. Changing `(w,h)` is an x/y affine scaling of the square-box cell. The egg's shape
  ratio `d₀ : d₁` is unchanged, and the two scales are already two of the five egg
  parameters. Nonsquare is a reparameterization, not a new curve.
- The Stop preset is a polyline traced by arc length, so changing `w/h` moves the
  breakpoints genuinely (the notes' §9.1 aspect-ratio effect). But the image of a small
  circle in the first cell is still an egg: the cell is bilinear and the egg shape ratio
  is a function of the handle and the cell's two heights. The Triangle egg's handle
  already ranges over **all** positive ratios `(1-α) : 2α`, so the Stop first-cell egg is
  in the same five-parameter family. The aspect changes which handle value gives a given
  shape, not the set of shapes. No new algebraic family.
- The curved-guide presets (Can, Ring, Wave, the Pours) do have a genuine aspect-ratio
  parameter [notes §9.1], but their curves are transcendental (arc length, polar angle).
  They do not feed the algebraic goals.

So nonsquare boxes are [Proof]-level redundant for the algebraic egg family, even though
they are a genuine parameter of the transcendental warp family.

### 8.3 The yellow handle as a free parameter

The formalization already does the right thing: `PConstructibleCurve.text` takes
`a b : ℝ` with `PConstructible a, PConstructible b` and `T.warp.HandleInRange a b`, not a
rational stop value. The old `TriangleCircleRule` was weaker: it quantified rational
handles only.

- **It is one P parameter, not a second one.** The egg shape ratio is a function of the
  single Triangle handle. Making it P moves the family from a ℚ-parameterized family to
  an **F-parameterized** one, which is exactly what Z8's F-point reformulation needs.
  It can absorb one condition over F; it cannot encode an `n`-dimensional Tschirnhaus
  element.
- **It is what makes the offset/conic candidate plausible.** The offset distance and the
  handle are two independent P parameters; together with the box scales and placement
  they give the six parameters of the offset egg.
- **The Lean bridge should state the theorem for every P handle**, not only the rational
  stops. `Pptc/Scratch/WarpText.lean` already uses an arbitrary `a : ℝ` in
  `triangleCircleBox_outlineAt`; the final `eggCurve`/`piriform` theorems should quantify
  `PConstructible α` with `0 ≤ α ≤ 1`, matching the constructor.

### 8.4 Combined verdict

| feature | new algebraic parameter? | effect on the engine table |
|---|---|---|
| free yellow handle | yes, 1 P parameter (already in the egg's 5) | moves the family to F; no new shape dimension |
| nonsquare box, straight/polyline | no (affine/reparameterization) | none |
| nonsquare box, curved guide | yes, but transcendental | none for algebraic goals |
| one offset of the egg | yes, 1 P parameter | +1 to F, but the geometric offset degree is 16; offset egg × conic is **closed** |

The only route here that could have moved the algebraic frontier was **offset egg ×
conic** at F = 11. W4a computes the geometric offset degree as 16 (the elimination
polynomial has total degree 28), so the crossing degree is at least 32 and the cofactor is
far above F; the route is closed scoped to this construction (`W4-offset.md`). Nonsquare
boxes and the free handle do not change the conclusion.

## Other things noticed

- The `text` constructor accepts a `PConstructible` box size and handle, but the stored
  `TextBox` data is rational. This is exactly the "P parameters, rational shape" pattern
  that Z8 identifies as the useful one; the warp formalization is the first *drawing*
  operation in the repo with continuous P parameters that are not already an affine
  transform of an existing curve.
- The egg's normalization is scale-invariant: a single `●` always normalizes to the unit
  circle, so the font size cancels. Only the row layout (the pen step) changes the
  normalized half-width. This is why the `●●` row is necessary for `a < 1/2`.
- The transcendental warps (elliptic trochoid, arc-length-sheared Bézier) put an elliptic
  integral or a Bézier arc length *inside* a curve. They are new shapes, but they do not
  touch the algebraic goals; the existing `W3`/`H4` scoping applies.
