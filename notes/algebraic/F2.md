# F2: where families of solutions can live in `W₂`

Written 2026-11-13. Research only; no `.lean` edits. Companion to `F1.md` (read §1–2 there first)
and to `PLAN-forward-search.md` §2 ("the search is a hunt for special subvarieties of `W_m`").
Labels as in the plan. Backend: python-flint 0.9.0 and mpmath, Python 3.12; no Wolfram, PARI or
Sage in this environment.

**Question.** A *family* is a curve `Y ⊂ W₂` of geometric genus ≤ 1 carrying infinitely many
solutions. Bombieri–Lang says that is where the rational points of a general-type `W₂` concentrate.
Which curves can it be? This note answers that for `m = 2`, reducing the search for families at
`d ∈ {8, 9}` to one narrow, explicitly described class.

## Findings summary

1. **Reduction to isomorphism correspondences (Prop. 3.1) [Proof, with a computed inequality].**
   For all five engines with `d ∈ {8, 9}`, a family reaching any `K` whose Galois group contains
   `A_d` lies over a curve `Γ ⊂ ℙ¹ × ℙ¹` along which the two fibre covers are *isomorphic*:
   `λ₁*g ≅ λ₂*g` over `ℚ̄(Γ)`. Then `Y ≅ Γ` birationally and `g(Γ) ≤ 1`. Every other curve in
   `W₂` has genus ≥ 2. The proof is Goursat's lemma plus a Riemann–Hurwitz count whose correction
   terms are computed exactly (`scripts/F2_genus_bound.py`). The count is too crude for `d ≤ 7`.
2. **No graphs of extra automorphisms [Proof, citing Singerman].** `Aut(Ĉ) = S_d` for all 11
   engines, because the triangle groups `Δ(2, pq, d)` are maximal. Graphs of automorphisms all lie
   over the diagonal, so they give only the trivial solutions.
3. **No Hecke-type families for `d ≥ 7` [Proof, citing Margulis and Takeuchi; arithmeticity
   computed].** An isomorphism correspondence that is unramified in the orbifold sense on both
   sides comes from the commensurator of `Δ`. `Δ(2, pq, d)` is arithmetic exactly for the `d = 5, 6`
   engines (`scripts/F2_arith.py`, validated against Takeuchi's list). For every engine with
   `d ≥ 7` the commensurator is `Δ` itself, so the only such correspondence is the diagonal. This
   removes the one mechanism known to produce infinite families of isomorphic fibres, the analogue
   of isogenies between elliptic curves.
4. **At `d = 5` the obvious commensurator element does not give a family [Computed, exact over
   `F_ℓ`].** `Δ(4,4,5)` lies in `Δ(2,4,5)` and in `Δ(2,4,10)`, which gives an explicit genus-0
   correspondence of bidegree (6,6), with `λ₁ = −(s²−6s+25)(s−1)⁴/(3125 s)`. Along it the two
   pullbacks of `tri(5)` are not isomorphic: 4120 of 13375 points mismatch, with no constant
   twist. Separately, `λ₁` is the F20 resolvent, so this correspondence could only ever reach
   solvable quintics. Commensurability alone is not enough; the `S_d` cover must also be
   compatible with it.
5. **Low bidegrees are excluded.**
   - **[Proof]** Correspondences of bidegree `(1, k)`, `(k, 1)` (including Möbius graphs) and
     `(2, 2)` are excluded for all engines.
   - **[Computed, exact over `F_ℓ`]** For the five engines with `d ∈ {8, 9}`, no isomorphism
     correspondence over ℚ of bidegree `(2,3)`, `(3,2)` or `(3,3)` reduces mod any
     `ℓ ∈ {101, 103, 107, 109, 113}` to an admissible curve. That covers every such `Γ` over ℚ
     except those whose corner coefficients `c_{0b}·c_{a0}` vanish mod all five primes.
   - The key input is a fibre-matching lemma (Lemma 5.1). For small bidegree it fixes the Newton
     polygon of `Γ` and forces even multiplicities over `λ*`.
6. **What remains at `d ∈ {8, 9}` [Open].** A family must be a *ramified* isomorphism
   correspondence `Γ` of genus ≤ 1 with bidegree outside `{1, 2, 3}²`. No mechanism for one is
   known. For stage D, Lemma 5.1 cuts the curve-fitting step down to curves of a prescribed support
   (§6).

## 1. Set-up

Notation as in `F1.md` §2: `g` is the engine map, `B = {0, ∞, λ*}` its branch values, `Ĉ` the
Galois closure (group `S_d`), and `W₂ = Ĉ²/(S_d × S₂)`. Write `W₂′ = Ĉ²/S_d` (diagonal action) for
the ordered version, so `π: W₂′ → ℙ¹ × ℙ¹` has degree `d!`.

The local monodromies of `g` are:
- over `0`: an element `u₀` with cycle type `(p, q)`, of order `pq`;
- over `∞`: a `d`-cycle `u_∞`;
- over `λ*`: a transposition `τ`.

As an orbifold, the base is `𝒪 = ℙ¹(2, pq, d)` with `χ′ := −χ(𝒪) = 1/2 − 1/(pq) − 1/d > 0`.

**Family.** An irreducible curve `Y ⊂ W₂` of geometric genus ≤ 1 whose ℚ-points give infinitely
many solutions. It is not contained in the preimage of the diagonal (`λ₁ = λ₂`, the trivial
solutions) or of a line `{λ_i = c}`. Lift `Y` to `Y′ ⊂ W₂′` and let `Γ = π(Y′) ⊂ ℙ¹ × ℙ¹`, with
normalisation `D` and the two coordinate maps `λ₁, λ₂: D → ℙ¹` of degrees `k₁, k₂`. Set
`k = max(k₁, k₂)`.

**Scope.** We consider families reaching at least one `K` with `Gal(K̃/ℚ) ⊇ A_d`, which covers the
generic field. Fields with smaller Galois groups are outside this note.

## 2. Automorphisms [Proof, citing Singerman]

`Ĉ = ℍ/Π` with `Π = ker(Δ → S_d)` normal in `Δ = Δ(2, pq, d)`. Its normaliser in `PSL₂(ℝ)` is a
Fuchsian group containing `Δ`, so it equals `Δ` when `Δ` is maximal, and then
`Aut(Ĉ) = Δ/Π = S_d`.

**Maximality.** Singerman's list of inclusions between triangle groups is cited from memory
(D. Singerman, *Finitely maximal Fuchsian groups*, J. London Math. Soc. 6 (1972)):
- normal inclusions `(t,t,t) ⊂ (3,3,t)` and `(t,t,u) ⊂ (2,t,2u)`;
- non-normal inclusions `(7,7,7), (2,7,7), (3,3,7) ⊂ (2,3,7)`, `(4,8,8), (3,8,8) ⊂ (2,3,8)`,
  `(9,9,9) ⊂ (2,3,9)`, `(4,4,5) ⊂ (2,4,5)`, `(n,4n,4n) ⊂ (2,3,4n)`, `(n,2n,2n) ⊂ (2,4,2n)`,
  `(3,n,3n) ⊂ (2,3,3n)`, `(2,n,2n) ⊂ (2,3,2n)`.

`(2, pq, d)` occurs on the left of none of these:
- it has no repeated entry, because `pq ≠ d` when `p + q = d` and `p, q ≥ 1` are coprime;
- `(2, n, 2n)` would need `d = 2pq` or `pq = 2d`. The first is impossible since `pq ≥ d − 1`. The
  second has integer solutions only for `(p, q) = (4, 4)` and `(6, 3)`, neither coprime.

So `Aut(Ĉ) = S_d`. The graph of `σ ∈ S_d` lies over the diagonal of `ℙ¹ × ℙ¹`, giving only trivial
solutions.

## 3. Reduction to isomorphism correspondences

**Lemma 3.2 (local rigidity) [Proof].** A nontrivial power of one of `u₀`, `u_∞`, `τ` is conjugate
to a nontrivial power of another only when `2 ∈ {p, q}` (say `q = 2`), between `τ` and `u₀^e` with
`p | e`, `e` odd.
- Powers of `u_∞` have all cycles of one length.
- A power of `u₀` has cycles of lengths `p/(p,e)` and `q/(q,e)`. These are equal only if both are
  1, since `p` and `q` are coprime.
- A power of `u₀` is a transposition only if one part is the identity and the other a single
  2-cycle, i.e. `q = 2` (or `p = 2`).

**Euler characteristic.** Give `D` the orbifold structure `D_o` whose order at `y` is the order of
the local monodromy of `λ₁*g`. Riemann–Hurwitz for the Galois closure of the pullback, compared
with that of `Ĉ`, gives

  `−χ(D_o) = k₁ χ′ + Ex₁`, where
  `Ex₁ = R_free + Σ_{y ↦ b ∈ B} (e_y − gcd(n_b, e_y))/n_b ≥ 0`.

Here `R_free` is the ramification of `λ₁` over points outside `B`, and `n_b` is the cone order at
`b`. `Ex₁ = 0` exactly when `λ₁: D_o → 𝒪` is an orbifold covering. **[Proof]**, by direct
substitution into Riemann–Hurwitz.

**Proposition 3.1 [Proof; the inequality is computed exactly].** Let `d ∈ {8, 9}`. If `Y` is a
family as in §1, then `λ₁*g ≅ λ₂*g` over `ℚ̄(D)` and `g(Γ) ≤ 1`. The component of `π⁻¹(Γ)` of
degree 1 over `Γ`, which is birational to `Γ`, is then itself a family. So it is enough to look
for such `Γ`.

*Proof.*
(a) **Monodromy groups.** Let `G_i ⊆ S_d` be the geometric monodromy group of `λ_i*g`.
- Some point of `Y` gives a `K` with group `⊇ A_d` over the solvable `L`. That group lies in the
  arithmetic monodromy group, which normalises `G_i`. So `G_i` is normalised by `A_d`, hence
  `G_i ∈ {1, A_d, S_d}`.
- If `G_i = 1`, then `λ_i` factors through `Ĉ`, and `g(Y) ≥ g(D) ≥ g(Ĉ) ≥ 4`.
- So `G_i ⊇ A_d` for both `i`.

(b) **Goursat.** The monodromy group `G_Γ ⊆ G₁ × G₂` of `π⁻¹(Γ)` is a fibre product over a common
quotient. The quotients of `A_d` and `S_d` are `1`, `C₂`, `A_d`, `S_d`. Either:
- `G_Γ` is the graph of an isomorphism. For `d ≠ 6` that is conjugation by some `σ ∈ S_d`, i.e.
  the covers are isomorphic. The component of `π⁻¹(Γ)` attached to `σ` has degree 1 over `Γ`.
  `Y′` is some component, and every component maps onto `Γ`, so `g(Γ) ≤ g(Y′) ≤ 1`.
- Or `G_Γ ⊇ A_d × A_d`. Then the action on points of `W₂′` over `Γ`, `(a, b)·σ = aσb⁻¹`, has orbits
  of size `N ≥ d!/2`, and the action is faithful.

(c) **Genus count in the second case.** Let `D̄ = D/ι` if `Γ` is swap-invariant, and `D̄ = D`
otherwise. `Y` is a component of degree `N` over `D̄`. By Burnside, the number of orbits of the
local monodromy `w` (of order `o`) at `z` is `N/o + c_z`, where
`c_z = (1/o) Σ_{j=1}^{o−1} fix(w^j)`. So

  `2g(Y) − 2 ≥ N·(−χ(D̄_o)) − Σ_z c_z ≥ (d!/2)·k χ′/2 − Σ_z c_z`.

This uses `−χ(D̄_o) = −χ(D_o)/2` and `−χ(D_o) ≥ k χ′` from the Euler characteristic formula. The
local order at `y` is the order of `(u₁, u₂)`, a multiple of the order of `u₁`.

(d) **Bounding the corrections.** For `w = (u₁, u₂)` in the base group,
`fix(w^j) = |C(u₁^j)|` if `u₁^j ~ u₂^j`, and 0 otherwise. By Lemma 3.2 this is nonzero only at:
- points with `λ₁(y) = λ₂(y) ∈ B` (on the diagonal);
- mixed `(0, λ*)` points when `2 ∈ {p, q}`.

For `w` outside the base group (swap-ramified points, at most `Γ·Δ = k₁ + k₂` of them),
`fix(w^j) ≤ #{x : x² = ab} ≤ I(d)` for odd `j`, where `I(d)` is the number of solutions of
`x² = 1`. This holds because every irreducible character of `S_d` has Frobenius–Schur indicator 1.
Summing, with `Σ_{y ∈ λ₁⁻¹(b)} e₁(y) = k₁`:

  `Σ c_z ≤ k·[ I(d) + Σ_b max_{e₁,e₂} c_b(e₁,e₂)/e₁ + 2·max_e c_mix(e)/e ]`.

(e) **The inequality.** `g(Y) ≥ 2` whenever `d!·χ′/4` exceeds the bracket.
`scripts/F2_genus_bound.py` evaluates both sides exactly:

| engine | `χ′` | `d!χ′/4` | correction bracket | `g(Y) ≥ 2`? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| pp(7,1) | 13/56 | 2340 | 1550 | yes |
| pp(5,3) | 37/120 | 3108 | 1608 | yes |
| pp(8,1) | 19/72 | 23940 | 7762 | yes |
| pp(7,2) | 20/63 | 28800 | 9874 | yes |
| pp(5,4) | 61/180 | 30744 | 7838 | yes |
| pp(6,1), pp(5,2), pp(4,3) | | 240–345 | 374–438 | no (bound too crude) |
| d = 5, 6 | | 1.5–24 | 40–120 | no |

So in case (b, second branch) `g(Y) ≥ 2`, and `Y` is not a family. ∎

**Descent.** In the isomorphism case the isomorphism is unique, because `C_{S_d}(A_d) = 1`. It is
therefore Galois-invariant and defined over `ℚ(Γ)`. For swap-invariant `Γ`, uniqueness also makes
it compatible with the swap. So every ℚ-point of `D̄` off the bad locus, with `λ ∉ ℚ`, is a
solution. A swap-invariant `Γ` with `D̄ ≅ ℙ¹_ℚ`, or an elliptic `D̄` of positive rank, would be an
infinite family. **[Proof sketch]**

## 4. The étale case: commensurators and arithmeticity

**Proposition 4.1 [Proof, citing Margulis].** Suppose `λ₁*g ≅ λ₂*g` and both `λ_i` are orbifold
coverings `D_o → 𝒪` (`Ex₁ = Ex₂ = 0`).
- Write `D_o = ℍ/Δ₁` with `Δ₁ ⊂ Δ` of finite index via `λ₁`. Then `λ₂` is induced by `γ ∈ PSL₂(ℝ)`
  with `γΔ₁γ⁻¹ ⊂ Δ`, so `γ` is in the commensurator `Comm(Δ)`.
- For non-arithmetic `Δ`, `Comm(Δ)` is a lattice containing `Δ` with finite index (Margulis; cited
  from memory). `Δ` is maximal (§2), so `Comm(Δ) = Δ`, hence `γ ∈ Δ`, `λ₂ = λ₁`, and `Γ` is the
  diagonal.

**Arithmeticity [Computed; `scripts/F2_arith.py`, output in `F2_arith_out.txt`].** Takeuchi's
criterion (cited from memory) says `Δ(a,b,c)` is arithmetic iff `σ(β) < 0` for every embedding
`σ ≠ id` of the invariant trace field `ℚ(cos²(π/a), cos²(π/b), cos²(π/c), cos(π/a)cos(π/b)cos(π/c))`,
where `β = Σ cos²(π/e) + 2Π cos(π/e) − 1`. Evaluated at 60 digits:
- it reproduces Takeuchi's list for `(2,3,n)` with `n ≤ 40`;
- it accepts all 26 listed `(2,a,b)` triangles tested;
- it rejects 7 that are not on the list.

| engines | orbifold | arithmetic |
|---|---|---|
| pp(4,1) = tri(5) | (2,4,5) | yes |
| pp(3,2), pp(5,1) = tri(6) | (2,5,6) | yes |
| all eight engines with `d ∈ {7, 8, 9}` | (2,6,7) … (2,20,9) | **no** |

So for `d ≥ 7` there are no étale families (Prop. 4.1). Combined with Prop. 3.1: **at
`d ∈ {8, 9}` every family is a ramified isomorphism correspondence**, meaning `Ex_i > 0` for some
`i`.

**`d = 5`, the explicit commensurator element [Computed; `scripts/F2_d5_hecke.py`].**
- **Construction.** `Δ(4,4,5)` has index 6 in `Δ(2,4,5)` (Singerman) and index 2 in the arithmetic
  `Δ(2,4,10)`. The order-2 element of the latter swaps the two order-4 cone points and lies in
  `Comm(Δ(2,4,5))`.
- **The Belyi map.** The degree-6 map for `Δ(4,4,5) ⊂ Δ(2,4,5)` has passport
  `([2,2,2], [4,1,1], [5,1])`. It is derived in closed form by matching critical points:
  `λ₁(s) = −(s²−6s+25)(s−1)⁴/(3125 s)`, with
  `λ₁ − λ* = −(s³−5s²+15s+5)²/(3125 s)`, checked exactly.
- **The involution.** `α(s) = 25s/(6s−25)` fixes the order-5 point `s = 0` and swaps `3 ± 4i`. Set
  `λ₂ = λ₁∘α`.
- **Result.**
  - Cycle types of `x⁴(1−x) − λ_i(s)` over `F_ℓ`, `7 ≤ ℓ ≤ 400`, disagree at 4120 of 13375 étale
    points.
  - The mismatches occur in every residue class of `ℓ` mod 20, so they are not a constant twist.
  - The pullbacks are not isomorphic: this commensurator element does not preserve the `S₅` cover.
  - Every type seen along `λ₁` is an F20 type, as predicted by the exceptional `S₅ ⊂ S₆`. So
    `D = Ĉ/F20`, and the correspondence could only have reached solvable quintics.
- **Open.** Whether genuine Hecke operators, which are compatible with a congruence `Π`, give
  families at `d = 5, 6`. That needs the congruence structure of `Π`, in the framework of
  Clark–Voight (*Algebraic curves uniformized by congruence subgroups of triangle groups*, cited
  from memory), and is not needed for the target.

## 5. Ramified isomorphism correspondences of small bidegree

Write `Γ: F(λ, λ′) = Σ c_{ij} λ^i λ′^j = 0` of bidegree `(a, b)`, with `deg_λ F = a` and
`deg_{λ′} F = b`. So `k₂ = a` and `k₁ = b`.

**Lemma 5.1 (fibre matching) [Proof].** Let `Γ` be an isomorphism correspondence with
`a, b < min(pq, d)`. If `2 ∈ {p, q}`, assume also `a, b < p` (resp. `q`), the odd member. Then:
- **(N)** `F(0, λ′) = c_{0b} λ′^b`, `F(λ, 0) = c_{a0} λ^a`, and the leading coefficients in `λ` and
  in `λ′` are the constants `c_{a0}` and `c_{0b}`. Equivalently,
  `supp F ⊆ {(0,b), (a,0)} ∪ {(i,j) : 1 ≤ i < a, 1 ≤ j < b}`.
- **(S)** Every root `y ≠ λ*` of `F(λ*, y)` has even multiplicity, and likewise for `F(x, λ*)`.

*Proof.* At a point of `D` over `λ₁ = 0` with exponent `e ≤ b < pq`, the local monodromy
`u₀^e ≠ 1`. The isomorphism forces `λ₂` there to have a conjugate nontrivial local monodromy. By
Lemma 3.2 that means `λ₂ = 0`; the mixed case needs `e ≥ p > b`. So `Γ ∩ {λ = 0} = {(0,0)}`, which
is (N) at 0. The argument at `∞` is the same (`e < d`). At `λ*`, an odd exponent gives `τ ≠ 1`, so
`λ₂ = λ*`. The branches through a point `(λ*, y)` with `y ≠ λ*` therefore all have even exponent,
and their intersection multiplicity with `{λ = λ*}`, which is the multiplicity of `y`, is even. ∎

**Corollary 5.2 [Proof].**
- **Bidegree `(1, k)` or `(k, 1)`, all engines.** Here `λ₁` is an isomorphism, so `D_o = 𝒪` and
  `χ′ = k₂χ′ + Ex₂`. That forces `k₂ = 1` and `Ex₂ = 0`, so `λ₂` is an orbifold automorphism of
  `𝒪`. The three cone orders are distinct, so it is the identity. This includes Möbius graphs.
- **Bidegree `(2, 2)`, all engines.** By (N), `F = c₂₀λ² + c₁₁λλ′ + c₀₂λ′²`, a pair of lines
  `λ′ = tλ` through the origin. By the previous bullet these are not isomorphism correspondences.

**Exhaustive search for `(2,3)`, `(3,2)`, `(3,3)` [Computed, exact over `F_ℓ`;
`scripts/F2_lowbideg.py`, output in `F2_lowbideg_out.txt`].**
- **Reduction to a linear problem.** For `a, b ≤ 3`, condition (S) says
  `F(λ*, y) = (y − λ*)^{b mod 2}(y − h)^{2⌊b/2⌋}` and
  `F(x, λ*) = c_{a0}(x − λ*)^{a mod 2}(x − h′)^{2⌊a/2⌋}`. Over ℚ, `h` and `h′` are rational, being
  double roots of rational cubics with a rational simple root. Normalise `c_{0b} = 1`. Then (N) and
  (S) are linear in the remaining coefficients for each `(h, h′)`, and the script enumerates all
  solutions over `F_ℓ`: 2 curves for `(2,3)`, about `2ℓ²` for `(3,3)`.
- **The test.** At each smooth `F_ℓ`-point with neither coordinate a branch value, the Frobenius
  cycle types of `g − λ` and `g − λ′` must agree. This holds for the reduction of a genuine
  isomorphism correspondence: two finite étale covers that are isomorphic generically are
  isomorphic over the regular locus of the model.
- **Result.** At every `ℓ ∈ {101, 103, 107, 109, 113}`, for each of `pp(7,1)`, `pp(5,3)`,
  `pp(8,1)`, `pp(7,2)` and `pp(5,4)`, no curve passes (75 runs, all null).
- **Control.**
  - With a constant type table every candidate passes, and candidates have 40–120 usable points.
  - With the real tables every candidate fails within its first 4 usable points.
- **Scope.** This is a null for `Γ` defined over ℚ with `c_{0b}c_{a0}` a unit at one of the five
  primes. It covers all genera, not only genus ≤ 1.

## 6. What this means for the search

- **At `d ∈ {8, 9}`, the space of possible families is now a single class.** It consists of
  genus-≤1 curves `Γ ⊂ ℙ¹ × ℙ¹`, not of bidegree `(a, b)` with `a, b ≤ 3`, along which
  `λ₁*g ≅ λ₂*g`, with at least one `λ_i` ramified outside the orbifold structure. Lemma 5.1 applies
  to them up to bidegree `min(pq, d) − 1` (7 for `pp(8,1)`, 8 for `pp(7,2)` and `pp(5,4)`). Above
  that, inessential ramification (exponents divisible by `pq` or `d`) can decouple the fibres.
- **The obvious mechanism is gone.** Isogeny-type (Hecke) families are excluded at `d ≥ 7` by
  arithmeticity alone. Any family would have to come from a ramified pair of maps with the same
  `S_d` pullback. No construction of such a pair is known for any non-arithmetic triangle group.
  The constraints of Lemma 3.2 and Lemma 5.1 are the place to start if one is sought by theory.
- **Stage D curve fitting can be sharpened.** Instead of fitting arbitrary plane curves of degree
  ≤ 6 through `(λ + λ′, λλ′)`:
  - fit `F(λ, λ′)` with support (N);
  - impose (S) as a filter;
  - require agreement of Frobenius types at `F_ℓ`-points of the fitted curve (the test of
    `F2_lowbideg.py`).

  That removes most coefficients and gives an exact rejection test for any candidate fit.
- **Stage C at `d = 5, 6` is not a clean probe for families.** The triangle groups there are
  arithmetic, so Hecke families may exist and would show up as structured `m = 2` solutions. A
  positive result at `d = 5, 6` would therefore say nothing directly about `d = 9`.
- **Scope of this note.** Only `m = 2` and fields with group `⊇ A_d`. Curves in `W_m` for `m ≥ 3`,
  and the Goursat analysis there (the fibre product of `m` covers), are not done. Nothing here
  bears on isolated solutions, which Bombieri–Lang still allows in finite number off the special
  curves.
