## Verdict: NO HIT

No UI-legal composition of the existing primitives adds many P-weighted terms without square roots.
Best trade-offs found (linear-in-P crossing families with no offset):

| family | crossing equation | free linear P-params F | degree |
|---|---|---|---|
| rotated ellipse x power_law y = x^k | A x^2 + B x^(k+1) + C x^(2k) + D x + E x^k + F0 = 0 | 5 (6 monomials, projective) | 2k |
| cubic Bezier (x(t) = t, P ordinates) x power_law y = a x^n | a x^n = c3 x^3 + c2 x^2 + c1 x + c0 | 4 (+ a) | n |
| affine image of any base vs fixed curve (6 affine params) | span {1, x, f(x)} | 2 | deg f |

All have F <= 6 whatever the degree, so they serve the (BD) criterion of X2 section 4.3 only for n <= 5.
(BD) needs F unbounded at bounded degree. Reason in one line: **without offset, the number of P-parameters
entering a crossing is bounded by an absolute constant (<= 28), independent of n; with offset every
parameter drags a square root (Y1a Prop T), and degenerate summands do not exist.**

## (a) Pointwise sums y = f(x) + c g(x), curves depending on curves

**Lemma S (structure) [Proof sketch, structural induction on `PConstructibleCurve`].** Every constructor of
`PConstructibleCurve` takes at most one curve argument plus numbers (`translate_*`, `scale_*`, `rotate`, `restrict`,
`arc_of_length`, `offset`). No constructor takes two curves and returns a curve (no union, no sum, no
envelope of a family). Curves meet only through readouts (`inter_x/y`, `box_*`, `arc_length`), and those return
**numbers**. Hence a curve depends on another curve only through P-numbers, and the unknown-carrying curve is
S = T_k(...T_1(S0(p))) with S0 a base and each T unary.

Consequences [Proof sketch]:
1. `translate`, `scale`, `rotate` compose to one affine map M (6 real parameters, P-valued). `restrict` and
   `arc_of_length` return subsets of their input (arc_of_length: a sub-arc of S), so they change which part
   of the locus is used, not the locus. Between two offsets the whole chain is one affine map plus selection.
2. A pointwise sum of two drawn graphs f and g is therefore impossible. The only built-in "sums" are
   (i) poly_graph = sum of 7 monomials with **rational** weights (cap 6), (ii) cubic_bezier = sum of 4
   Bernstein functions with P weights. Shear y -> y + c x (scale + rotate, any P angle) adds only the single
   function x. Affine images of a graph f give the span {1, x, f(x)}: 2 free weights.
3. Offset by a non-constant amount: not available. `offset` carries one real `d` (`offsetParam` uses the constant d
   times `unitNormal`). [Proof, from Defs.lean]
4. Box/intersection readouts as Bezier control points: legal, but each is a P-number, so it only re-enters as
   one of the <= 8 control coordinates. A Bezier curve is linear in them, but degree 3 is fixed. The crossing with
   a graph y = g(x) has degree 3 deg g in t (nonlinear in the x_i), or deg g after fixing x(t) = t (linear in
   the 4 y_i). So cubic_bezier is the only P-weighted linear combination in the system, with exactly 4 weights.
5. arc_length / arc_of_length: arc length of y = f + c g is the integral of sqrt(1 + (f' + c g')^2): not
   additive and again a square root.

**Bounded-parameter lemma [Proof sketch].** In a crossing of S1 and S2 built without offset, the unknown
enters through <= 6 + 6 affine parameters and <= 8 + 8 Bezier control coordinates (the rational shape params
of power_law/poly_graph are fixed), so M <= 28, independent of n. Hence gcd recovery and Wooley/RW (X2 4.3), which
need M >= n - c, are impossible past tiny n. Sanity count: the two equations x0(t) = (a x1 + b y1 + e)(u),
y0(t) = (c x1 + d y1 + f)(u) are linear in the 6 affine params but impose 2n L-linear conditions once
u in K_L, with 6 unknowns: generic failure for n >= 4 [Heuristic].

## (b) Other Minkowski summands

**Offset is the parallel curve, so the summand is always a circle before the later affine maps [Proof]**
(Y1a Prop T): x(s) = G(s) + sum_j d_j eps_j B_j u(s)/sqrt(Q_j(s)), with B_j = M_j M_j^T where M_j is the product of
all affine maps drawn after layer j. The summand of a layer is the image of a circle under M_j: an ellipse,
support function sqrt(u^T B_j u). Nothing else is generated: a parabola summand would need a non-constant
offset d(s), unavailable.

**Degenerate scale (factor 0) [Proof].** scale_x/scale_y allow the factor 0 (no positivity), so M_j of rank 1 is legal
and B_j = p p^T gives Q = (p.u)^2, sqrt(Q) = |p.u|, and B u/sqrt(Q) = +-p. [Wolfram: p = (2,3), u = (s,-1),
s > 3/2: B u/sqrt(u^T B u) = (2,3) exactly.] So the segment summand is a root-free piecewise-constant term
(support |p.u|), the "zonotope" idea. It is blocked:
1. If M_j has rank 1 then M_0 = M_j N_j has rank <= 1, so the base G and all earlier layers are flattened into
   one line: everything accumulated before the degenerate scale becomes a segment. [Proof] Only layers added
   *after* the degenerate scale keep full rank, and they see a segment.
2. An offset of a segment is a translated segment (parallel curve, no end caps): no stadium. The back-and-forth
   tracing of a degenerate closed ellipse is illegal for offset: not injective on Ico a b, and zero velocity at
   the turns (offset needs nowhere-vanishing C^1 speed). [Proof]
3. [Wolfram: a=3, b=2, d=5/7: parallel curve of the ellipse has ymax = 2.714285... = b+d = 19/7 and xmax =
   3.714285... = a+d = 26/7.] The scale-0 image of an offset ellipse is a segment of half-length b + d on the y axis:
   just the number b + d, an ordinary sum of P-numbers, not a curve sum. Readouts of a tower are
   h_G(e) + sum d_j sqrt(e^T B_j e): square roots of B_j entries again.
So degenerate scaling erases information and never yields a segment summand next to a curved base.
Moreover, even an ideal segment summand would be useless: its term is ±p, constant on each branch,
i.e. a translation, which the affine maps already supply. A useful root-free summand needs a
*non-constant* root-free support term (parabola-like), which needs a non-constant offset. [Proof]

**Base with convenient support function [Proof sketch].** The bases have support G(s): conic (sqrt), poly graph
(inverse of f', algebraic of degree deg f - 1), power law x(s) ~ s^(1/(b-1)) (a monomial with rational exponent). A
parabola gives G(s) rational-linear in s, the Y1a Theorem R base (cost: 1 linear unknown, its scale). A richer
base costs no roots but carries only <= 2 rational shape parameters plus the affine data, never a sum of many
terms. Offset - affine - offset again leaves Prop T unchanged: a tower of ellipses, each B_j with its own sqrt.

## (c) Families linear in many P-parameters at bounded degree

Searched pairs among {ellipse, rectangle edge, poly graph, power law, Bezier, affine images}:
- the P-linear coefficient space of a conic is span{x^2, xy, y^2, x, y, 1}: 5 free parameters (any positive-definite
  P-ellipse is an affine image of the circle);
- a cubic Bezier gives 4 free weights as a graph over t (x(t) = t), or 8 coordinates with only 4 linear;
- nothing gives more than about 6. Best ever seen: F = 5 at degree 2k, or F = 4 at degree n (Bezier x power
  law), the sparse supports {0,1,2,3,n} and {0,1,2,k,k+1,2k} already met as gap engines in N4/Z2.
  [Wolfram: Resultant of a general conic with y = x^4 has degree 8 in x.]
Against (BD): need F -> infinity at bounded degree; here F <= 6, so (BD) fails. Only offsets add parameters
(+1 d, +4 matrix entries per layer; Prop T gives J+2 linear unknowns for J layers), each with an independent sqrt;
Y1a reduces closing n to the simultaneous-squares problem (open).
