# Z7: multi-crossing readout (field embedding instead of a factor)

Labels: [Proof], [Proof sketch], [Wolfram], [Heuristic], [Open], [Citation, from memory]. Every negative is
scoped to *this route* (reading α from several crossings of one engine), never to the target.

## Verdict

- **Multi-crossing readout is never strictly cheaper than single-crossing (factor) readout, for any engine.**
  It is equal in exactly two small sporadic cases (n = 5 and n = 6), both already closed. In every other
  case it is strictly worse.
  - **Orbit Lemma [Proof].** Any readout scheme needs one L-irreducible factor of E_p whose root orbit O
    carries A_n faithfully. So m = |O| ≥ n.
  - If m = n (n ≠ 6), the scheme *is* the factor case: some root z has L(z) = KL, so χ_z | E_p. One
    crossing suffices.
  - For n ≥ 9 the next sizes are m = 2n, then m ≥ C(n,2) (Dixon–Mortimer 5.2B, [Citation]). So any engine of
    degree N < 2n admits *only* the factor case. That covers every current n = 9 engine of degree ≤ 17: the
    odd overshoot (N = 13), Theorem O, Z2's Bézier × a·x⁻².
- **Geometry [Proof sketch].** A scheme with a faithful orbit of size m reads α off the K_O-twist of
  Ord_m(E). Ord_m(E) is the variety of (p, m ordered distinct roots of E_p).
  - Ord_m → Ord_n is finite and dominant, so κ(Ord_m) ≥ κ(Ord_n). Unirationality of Ord_m would imply
    unirationality of Ord_n.
  - Imprimitive (wreath) schemes use a quotient Ord_{2n}/C₂ⁿ instead. It is measured worse below.
  - The weighted-adjunction canonical degree, which is minus the heuristic point-count exponent, increases
    strictly with m.
  - Numbers [Wolfram]:
    - trinomial curves at n = 5, D = 10: factor genus 2689, wreath 13225, twin 16801, pairs 524161;
    - at n = 9, D = 18: 2.4·10⁹ for the factor case, 2.2·10¹² for the wreath.
- **Realness [Proof].** For totally real K, a scheme that needs fewer than n real crossings must have
  m ≥ 2n. The minimal such scheme is G1/X1's square lift, which is Theorem X's own engine. Multi-crossing
  readout adds no new way around Descartes.
- **Best (engine, H) found:** none better than the factor case. The best *non-factor* scheme is the
  wreath C₂ ≀ S_n, i.e. the square lift. Its twisted cover is of general type wherever the factor cover is,
  and it is already in the record (Theorem X).
  - The only genuine ties are n = 5 and n = 6. At n = 5, a sextic engine with Galois group PGL(2,5) gives a
    different twist of the *same* genus-49 trinomial curve (D = 6). At n = 6, the twin sextic field K' gives
    another tie.

Net effect on the project: this assumption-break closes as a *reformulation*. The readout freedom
(rational functions of several real crossings, plus free solvable extensions) is real and legitimate.
But every scheme it allows reduces to, or is dominated by, the factor locus already studied.

## 1. Formalisation

**Readout legitimacy [Proof].**
- Let L ⊂ F = P ∩ ℝ_alg be the field of the engine parameters p, and let R_p be the set of real roots of E_p
  that are crossings. Restrict windows with rational bounds isolate each one, and `inter_x`/`inter_y` read it.
- P is a field, so L(R_p) ⊂ F. F(i) is radically closed (X1: complex radicals via `sqrt`, `arccos`, `rpow`,
  cos/sin), so every real element of a solvable extension of L(R_p) is also in F.
- Readout condition:

  (RO) α ∈ (L(R_p))^solv ∩ ℝ, equivalently Gal(K̃·L(R_p) / L(R_p)) is solvable.

  The strict form, α ∈ L(R_p), is the special case where that group fixes α.
- Caveat: real roots of E_p outside the drawn domain are not crossings. For example, power_law with a
  non-integer exponent lives only on x > 0. R_p means the drawn real roots.

**Group form.**
- G_p = Gal(E_p/L) acts on the N roots, and c ∈ G_p is complex conjugation. R_p corresponds to Fix(c), and
  φ: G_p ↠ Ḡ = Gal(K̃L/L) ⊇ A_n.
- (RO) ⇔ φ(G_p,(Fix c)) is solvable, where G_p,(Fix c) is the pointwise stabiliser. The strict form is
  ⇔ φ(G_p,(Fix c)) ⊆ Ḡ_α.

**Twisted cover [Proof sketch, Dèbes' twisting lemma].**
- Let X → B ⊂ A^k be the Galois closure of the family, with group G. Fix H ≤ G, a surjection φ: H ↠ Γ ≅ Ḡ,
  and the K-torsor ψ_K: Gal_L → Γ.
- Then p ∈ B(L) has its specialisation inside H, compatible with ψ_K, iff p lifts to an L-point of the
  twist W_{H,φ,K} = (X/ker φ) ×^Γ Spec K̃L.
- dim W = k for every H. So the codimension of the condition is not what distinguishes schemes; the degree
  and geometry of W are.
- Readability adds an open, real condition: some c in the real component must satisfy (RO). It depends only
  on (H, φ, c).
- **Factor case:** H = S_n × S_{N−n} and ker φ = S_{N−n}. X/S_{N−n} = Ord_n, and W = I_K =
  {(τ ∈ K⊗L, p) : χ_τ | E_p}.
- **General orbit scheme:** H = H_O × S_{N−m}, where H_O acts on an orbit O of size m.
  - If H_O acts faithfully through Γ, then W = the K_O-twist of Ord_m. Here K_O is the stem algebra of the
    Γ-set O: KL, K^(2), the twin field, and so on.
  - Otherwise (a kernel on O, e.g. the wreath) W is a twist of a quotient Ord_m/Q, and K_O varies with p.
  - For a linear family E_p = Σ_{s∈S} p_s t^s, W is {(g ∈ K_O, p) : E_p(g) = 0 in K_O}. That is m equations of
    weighted degree N in the weighted space (g weight 1, p_s weight N − s).

## 2. Which H are available

**Orbit Lemma [Proof].**
- (a) If (RO) holds and n ≥ 5, some L-irreducible factor of E_p has root orbit O with A_n ⊆ φ(image of G on
  O). Hence m := |O| ≥ n.
  - Proof: split the roots into an orbit O and the rest, with kernels N_O and N'. Then N_O ∩ N' = 1, so
    [N_O, N'] = 1, so φ(N_O) and φ(N') are commuting normal subgroups of Ḡ. They cannot both contain A_n,
    since A_n = [A_n, A_n] would then be trivial. Normal subgroups of Ḡ (n ≥ 5) either contain A_n or are
    trivial, so one of them is trivial.
  - If φ(N') = 1, recurse into the rest. This ends at an orbit with φ(N_O) = 1. Then G/N_O ↠ Ḡ, and the
    kernel K₀ of that map acts on O.
  - If K₀ = 1, Ḡ acts faithfully and transitively on O, and the smallest such set has n points.
  - If K₀ ≠ 1, its orbits are blocks permuted faithfully by Ḡ (or ≤ 2 blocks, which a solvable step
    removes), so m ≥ 2n.
- (b) If m = n and n ≠ 6, then G/N_O ≅ Ḡ acts naturally on O. Point stabilisers are conjugates of Ḡ_α, so
  some root z has L(z) = L(α). That is χ_z | E_p with z ∈ KL, i.e. the factor case, read from one crossing
  z = τ(α), which is real because α is.

**Possible orbit sizes.** These are the indices of core-free subgroups, or of non-faithful blocks:
- n ≥ 9 [Citation, Dixon–Mortimer Thm 5.2B, from memory]: a subgroup of S_n or A_n of index < C(n,2)
  contains A_{n−1} up to conjugacy. So m ∈ {n, 2n} ∪ [C(n,2), ∞).
  - m = 2n with stabiliser A_{n−1} gives the stem field K(√d). Over L(√d), which costs nothing since d > 0
    for totally real K and solvable extensions are free anyway, it is the factor case again.
  - m = 2n with a kernel is the wreath C₂ ≀ S_n, stem K(√b). This is the square lift.
- Sporadic small n (classical):
  - n = 5: PGL(2,5) on 6 points;
  - n = 6: the twin action on 6 points, and 10 bisections;
  - n = 8: A_8 ≅ GL(4,2) on 15 points (S_8 has none, because the two classes are swapped);
  - n = 7: none below 14.

**Readout capacities [Wolfram, pointwise stabilisers in explicit permutation groups].** Minimum number of
real crossings from O that pin α:

| action | m | crossings needed |
|---|---|---|
| S_9 on 2-sets | 36 | 2 ({1,2},{1,3}; the toy example) |
| S_6 on bisections | 10 | 3 |
| S_5 on 6 Sylow-5s (PGL(2,5)) | 6 | 2 (pointwise stabiliser C₄, fixing one natural point) |
| S_6 twin | 6 | 5, i.e. all (pointwise stabiliser orders 720, 120, 24, 6, 2, 1, transitive on the natural points up to 3) |
| wreath C₂ ≀ S_n | 2n | 1 (the square lift) |

Every action is "readable". Readability was never the obstacle.

**Engine-by-engine availability.** A scheme needs N = deg E_p ≥ m.
- Overshoot (rational sextic), Theorem O, Z2 engines, Bézier × power law: N ≤ 17 at n = 9, so only the
  factor case exists.
- Trinomial `power_law D` × line: D = 2n gives the wreath, which is Theorem X itself.
- W2's A/C family (degree 36): m ∈ {9, 18, 36} are possible. It is versal for m = 9 (n + 1 − F ≤ 0); the
  m = 18 and m = 36 schemes need 6 and 24 conditions.
- Offset towers: E_p is the norm of a linear function f_p on the fibre-product curve C_J (Y1a). An orbit
  of points P ∈ C_J(K') with [K':K] = 2^r costs n·2^r linear conditions on the J + 2 parameters. The square
  condition of Theorem R is exactly r = 0, the factor case.
  - Any "multi" scheme is an orbit of size ≥ n·2^r, so again nothing is gained. In Z7's language Theorem R's
    squares are the factor case on C_J, and non-square layers double the orbit.
- Composite engines E = A(B(t)), e.g. even engines Q(t²): the block quotient reduces to the factor case
  for A. Nothing new.

## 3. Geometry of the twisted covers

**Monotonicity [Proof sketch].**
- For faithful schemes, W_O ⊗ L̄ ≅ Ord_m. Ord_m → Ord_n (forget m − n roots) is finite and dominant, so
  κ(Ord_m) ≥ κ(Ord_n) (Iitaka), and Ord_m unirational ⇒ Ord_n unirational.
- Arithmetic caveat [Open, minor]: twists by *different* torsors (K_O versus K) are not related by a
  morphism, so a Brauer–Manin-type accident could in principle favour one. No instance is known.

**Weighted adjunction [Proof sketch; quasi-smoothness assumed].** For a linear family, W_O is a twisted
weighted complete intersection, with

κ-degree(m) = Σ_{j=1..m}(N − m + j) − w_O − Σ_{s free}(N − s).

- w_O = m for faithful orbits; w_O = 3n for the wreath (e₁ weight 1, e₂ weight 2).
- Heuristic point-count exponent = −κ-degree. With w_O = m, κ(m) − κ(n) = (m − n)(N − (m+n+1)/2) ≥ 0.
  Equality holds only for m = N, n = N − 1, which is the n = 5, D = 6 tie.
- Wreath minus factor = n(2N − 3n − 3)/2 > 0 for N ≥ 2n, n > 3.

**Exact genera, trinomial engine [Wolfram, Riemann–Hurwitz].** The cover is over the u = a^D/b^{D−1} line,
with monodromy a transposition, a D-cycle and a (D−1)-cycle. The check reproduces H1's genus 47881 at
D = m = 9.
- D = 5: m = 2, 3, 4, 5 give g = 0, 1, 4, 4. D = 6: m = 2…6 give 0, 3, 19, 49, 49.
- **n = 5 controls (D = 6):**
  - factor m = 5 (E = χ_τ·(t − r)): genus 49;
  - PGL(2,5) m = 6: also genus 49, the same curve with a different twist;
  - search: no sextic trinomial t⁶ + at + b with a ≠ 0, |a|, |b| ≤ 60 passes the PGL(2,5)
    Frobenius-pattern filter (primes 5–409). The x⁶ + b hits are degenerate.
- n = 5, D = 10: factor 2689, wreath (Ord₁₀/C₂⁵) 13225, twin 16801, pairs 524161.
- n = 9, D = 18: factor 2,363,961,601, wreath 2,227,132,897,921, faithful m = 18 1,234,444,603,392,001.

Lemma A (degree < 9 with a smooth real point ⇒ F_{<9}-points) does not apply to any of these. Their
degrees are far above 8, and enlarging the field does not lower κ. So no candidate (engine, H) has a
guaranteed F-point that I_K lacks.

## 4. The classical obstruction, made exact

- The suspicion in the brief is correct. A multi-crossing scheme with orbit type O is a single-crossing
  scheme for the resolvent whose stem field is the field generated by the needed crossings, e.g.
  θ = z₁₂ + 2z₁₃.
- Its cost is governed by m = |O|, not by how many crossings are read. In the special case
  g = Σ_{i∈I} τ(α_i), it is the I-subset-sum resolvent R_I(χ_τ) dividing E_p.
- Comparison with the plain factor case, in every metric computable here:
  - dimension: equal (k);
  - condition count on the orbit coordinate: m + 1 − F, against n + 1 − F;
  - Kodaira and genus: never better, strictly worse except at the n = 5 and n = 6 ties;
  - realness: no gain beyond the square lift.
- **Is multi-crossing ever strictly cheaper? No.** [Proof] for the orbit-size and realness statements;
  [Proof sketch] for κ monotonicity; [Heuristic] for point counts.

## 5. Open items

1. Write out the Orbit Lemma and the Dixon–Mortimer citation properly. Confirm the exact statement of Thm
   5.2B (n > 8) and the sporadic list for n ≤ 8, e.g. with GAP.
2. Arithmetic of different twists of the same cover. At n = 5, D = 6, the natural and PGL(2,5) twists of
   the genus-49 curve are a clean test: can one have points where the other has none? This bears only on
   the method, since n = 5 is done.
3. Non-linear families (W2's A/C two-graph engine): the Orbit Lemma and the realness dichotomy hold
   verbatim, but the weighted-adjunction table does not apply. κ monotonicity (Ord_m → Ord_n) still does.
4. Readout across a *tower*, where crossings of one engine become parameters of the next, is ordinary tower
   building and is covered by the existing framework.
   - For example, one pair-crossing z₁₂ with L(z₁₂) = K^(2) gives α₁ by a square root, since α₁ is quadratic
     over K^(2). That is legitimate under (RO).
   - But getting z₁₂ costs a C(n,2)-orbit, so it is dominated as above. No tower variant beating the factor
     case was found [Open whether one exists among non-linear engines].
