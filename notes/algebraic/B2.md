## (5) Lemma N over F; n = 11

- What the routes need: Engine C needs only Lemma N(i) (sigma has distinct conjugates, so chi_sigma has the simple root
  sigma(beta) and gcd recovers beta). Theorem O needs (i) and (ii) (tau_i != -tau_j).
- **Lemma N over F, repaired by a case split [Proof].** Let Hs = Gal(Omega/Omega cap F) = Gal(Omega F/F) (valid for
  any F, Omega/Q finite Galois), acting on the roots of q.
  - beta's Hs-orbit has size d <= 8: beta has degree <= 8 over F, so beta in P directly.
  - d = 10, Hs imprimitive: blocks 2 x 5 or 5 x 2 give F < M < F(beta) inside R with degrees (5,2) or (2,5); the real
    generator of M has degree <= 5 over F and beta degree <= 5 over M, so beta in P by the tower.
  - d = 10, Hs primitive: F(beta)/F has no intermediate field, so a non-constant sigma in F(beta) generates it:
    **Lemma N(i) holds**. (ii) also holds: R = {(i,j): tau_i = -tau_j} is Hs-stable; a diagonal entry forces tau = 0;
    an off-diagonal orbital of valency >= 2 contradicts (i); valency 1 is an Hs-invariant perfect matching, i.e. a
    block system, contradicting primitivity. (This covers the rank-3 primitive groups A5, S5 of degree 10, where
    the 2-transitivity used in X2 fails.)
  - d = 9 (q has one root in F): beta has degree 9 over F; this is the n = 9 problem over F, not covered here
    [Open, residual].
  So for n = 10 the A2 gap reduces to the d = 9 corner; in particular it never blocks Engine C or Theorem O when
  q has no root in F.
- n = 11: 11 is prime, so every transitive Hs is primitive; the solvable ones (C11, D11, F55, F110) give beta in P by
  radicals (F is closed under solvable extensions, Z8), the rest satisfy Lemma N (i), (ii) by the same argument.
  Intransitive: d <= 8 direct, d = 9, 10 reduce to the lower-degree problems over F [Open there].
- n = 11 route status: no conic gain (section 1b), so the problem is O_5 = CI(1,3,5,7,9) in P^10 plus one invariant
  (degree-12 equation in p2, p4, p6), dim 4, degree 945*12 = 11340, general type. Odd last step: an F-line in
  {p1=p3=p5=p7=0} cap {I = r} would leave the nonic-in-s p9 (odd, degree 9 > 8: real root exists but need not be
  in F) -- so even the line reduction fails at n = 11 unless the line also kills p9's degree; alternatively an
  F-line in O_4^{(11)} cap {p9 = 0} leaves the I-equation of degree 12 > 8. **No reduction to a single F-line with
  an admissible last step at n = 11** [Proof: degrees]. Best: F-line in {p1,p3,p5,p7} cap {I = 0} (I = 0: B2-type
  sextic) with p9 restricted of degree 9: not admissible. Open.
