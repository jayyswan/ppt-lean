# G2: drawable algebraic curves and their free P parameters

Stream G2 of `pptc/PLAN-algebraic.md`. Labels: [Proof], [Wolfram], [Heuristic], [Open].
No Lean was touched. All numbers are Wolfram unless marked. Scoping rule of the plan is kept:
every "cannot" below is scoped to a *route* (line crossings of the catalogue curves), never to
the target.

## 0. Findings summary

1. **What the constructors can draw algebraically [Proof sketch].** Every algebraic
   `PConstructibleCurve` is a piece of a curve in the family
   `Aff ∘ (offset ∘ Aff)^j (B)`, where `B` is one of: line, ellipse (= `Aff`-image of a circle),
   the closure of a cubic Bézier (one open `Aff`-orbit of the nodal cubic), a ℚ-polynomial graph
   of degree ≤ 6, or a rational power law `y = a x^(p/q)`. `restrict`, `arc_of_length` give pieces
   (same Zariski closure); `inter_*`, `box_*` give numbers only. `offset` is the only
   non-affine algebraic curve operation. There is no union constructor.
   **Control points, centres, widths and `Aff` entries are free P parameters, but they only ever
   move a curve inside an `Aff`-orbit. Cubic Béziers carry no invariant at all** (§2.3).

2. **Parameter count.** Let `M` be the dimension of the family of curves (Zariski closures) a
   construction reaches, inside the space of plane curves of its degree `D`.
   - `Aff`-images of a fixed curve: `M ≤ 6` (Béziers: exactly 6, ellipses 5, parabolas 4, ℚ-graphs
     of degree 5, 6: 6, rational power laws 5) [Wolfram].
   - Each further `offset ∘ Aff` layer adds **3** (2 shape of the linear part modulo similarity,
     1 for the distance): `M_j ≤ 6 + 3j` [Proof], equality measured for j ≤ 2 [Wolfram].
   - Crossing with a line: monic crossing polynomials form a family of dimension `≤ M − 2`
     [Proof]; crossing two curves: `≤ M₁ + M₂ − 4` (consistent with the measured rank 8 for two
     Béziers in NOTES-general).
   - **The line-crossing family is versal on monic degree-`D` polynomials iff `M − 2 = D`**
     (full rank).

3. **Graphs.** Arbitrary P-coefficient graphs: degree ≤ 4 (`M = D + 2 = 6`, 4 free monic
   parameters; Lemma Q). Degree 5, 6 with ℚ coefficients: the crossing family has 4 free
   monic parameters inside 5- resp. 6-dim spaces; the missing directions are the rational
   `Aff`-invariants (one, `A³/B²`, for quintics; two for sextics) [Proof + Wolfram]. **No other
   constructor changes a graph's invariants** (route-scoped statement, §4).

4. **Towers.** Parameters grow **linearly in depth** (`3j`), degree grows **exponentially**
   (`D_j = (j+2)·2^j` over the parabola, j ≤ 3 measured), genus `g_j = 1 + 2^j (g₀ − 1 + j m/2)`.
   So parameters ≈ `3·log₂ D`: **logarithmic in degree**. The best near-miss is the offset of a
   parabola (sextic crossing family of dimension 5 inside the 6-dim monic sextics: codimension 1).
   No line-crossing family from the catalogue is versal for `D ≥ 5` [Proof for depth ≤ 1,
   Wolfram for the measured cases, Heuristic beyond].

5. **No curve found whose line crossings are versal for D = 5, 6, 9.** For lines the necessary
   condition is `M ≥ D + 2`; the catalogue satisfies it only for `D ≤ 4`. Two-curve crossings
   obey `M₁ + M₂ − 4` against `D₁D₂` and are the business of G3 (suggestions in §6).

## 1. Methods (so every number can be reproduced)

- **Family dimension `M` by normal-velocity rank [Wolfram].** For a family `θ ↦ q(θ, t)` of
  parametrised curves, the infinitesimal motion of the *curve* (not the parametrisation) is the
  normal component `∂_θ q · N`. `M` is the numerical rank of the matrix
  `(∂_{θ_j} q(θ, t_i) · N(t_i))_{i,j}` at a random point, with 60–150-digit arithmetic. A clean gap
  (e.g. 10⁻⁷⁶ against 10⁰) was found every time. This works through arbitrarily nested offsets
  (the `√` is differentiated explicitly).
- **Crossing polynomial of an offset with a line [Wolfram].** For `q(t)` polynomial and
  `X = p + s v`, the offset condition is the pair
  `E1 = (X−q)·q' = 0`, `E2 = |X−q|² − d² = 0`. The resultant in `t` is the crossing polynomial
  in `s` (both sides ±d). Its degree is the degree of the offset curve; the rank of its monic
  coefficient vector with respect to *all* parameters (linear part `K`, `d`, line `p`, `v`) is
  computed one parameter at a time with a first-order symbolic `ε`.
- **Parallel-tangent lemma [Proof].** For `γ_d = γ + d·n`, `γ_d' = (1 − dκ)·γ'`. Hence the
  tangent *direction* of every offset in a tower equals the tangent direction of the base curve
  (before the linear maps that were applied in between). Used for the genus recursion in §3.

Notes on the table:
- Rows for ℚ-graphs of degree ≤ 6 and for P-quartic graphs: the group of graph-preserving affine
  maps (`x ↦ ax+b`, `y ↦ cy + ex + g`) has dimension 5. A monic degree-`D` polynomial family
  therefore has at most 4 free parameters from `Aff`, so degree 5 and 6 need 1 and 2 more, and
  these can only come from the coefficients of `f`, which are rational.
- Power-law offsets: `speed² = t^(2q−2)(q² + a²p² t^(2(p−q)))`. The factor `t^(2q−2)` is a square,
  so the branch locus is the `2(p−q)` roots of the second factor and `g = p − q − 1` [Proof].
  Neile (2,3) and `x^(1+1/n)` have genus 0, consistent with offset-inversion SUMMARY §2.

### 2.2 Why `M_j ≤ 6 + 3j` [Proof]

An offset commutes with similarities: `offset_d(S·C) = S·offset_{d/λ}(C)` for a similarity `S` of
ratio `λ`. Write each interior affine map as `A = S·K`, with `K` in a 2-dimensional transversal of
`Aff/Sim`; `S` is absorbed by the next map (or, for the innermost one, by `d`). Each layer thus
contributes `2` (shape of `K`) `+ 1` (the distance) and the outermost `Aff` contributes 6. Stabilisers
of the base curve only lower the count (parabola: the first shape parameter is absorbed because
every parabola is similar to `y = x²`; so its tower has `M_j = 4 + 3j`: 4, 7, 10 measured).
Two consecutive offsets with a *conformal* map between them add their distances, so they form
one layer, consistent with offset-inversion SUMMARY §2.

### 2.3 Cubic Béziers carry no invariants [Wolfram + Proof sketch]

The map from the 8 control-point coordinates to the curve has image dimension 6, and the
`Aff`-orbit of `(t²−1, t³−t)` has dimension 6 (also for `(t²+1, t³+t)`; the cusp orbit has
dimension 5). So the Zariski closure of every generic Bézier is an affine image of
`y² = x²(x+1)` or `y² = x²(x−1)` (or the cusp). The other two control-point dimensions only
choose *which arc* of that curve is drawn (the parameter window `[0,1]`), not the curve. This
explains the "rank 8 = 6 + 2" of the two-Bézier crossing in NOTES-general: `M₁ + M₂ − 4` with
the two reparametrisation directions counted once (`(6+2) + 6 − 6`).

## 3. Offset towers: genus, degree, parameters

### 3.1 Genus recursion [Proof, hypotheses stated; verified for j ≤ 3 on the parabola]

Let `X₀` be the normalisation of the base curve, `m` the number of points of `X₀` at which the
tangent has a given complex direction (class in a direction; `m = d − 1` for a degree-`d` graph,
`2` for a Bézier or ellipse, `1` for the parabola, `p − q` for `(t^q, t^p)`). The offset curve of
stage `j` is the image of a double cover `X_j → X_{j−1}` branched at the odd-order zeros of
`speed²` (zeros of `ω₊ω₋`). By the parallel-tangent lemma, `speed²` at stage `j` equals a
square times `Φ_j(c₀')`, where `Φ_j` is the binary quadratic form of the cumulative linear map
`A_j` and `c₀'` the tangent of the base. `Φ_j` splits into two linear forms `x' − ρ_j^± y'`, each
with `m` zeros on `X₀`. Assuming the `ρ_j^±` are distinct from earlier stages (generic `K_j`) and
that poles at infinity have even order, the branch locus at stage `j` consists of `b·2^{j−1}`
points with `b = 2m`. Riemann–Hurwitz gives

> `g_j = 2 g_{j−1} − 1 + 2^{j−2}·b`,  i.e.  `g_j = 1 + 2^j (g₀ − 1 + j m / 2)`.

Checks: graph of degree `d`: `g₁ = d − 2` (cubic: 1, quartic: 2) agrees with the SUMMARY.
Parabola (`g₀=0, m=1`): `g_j = 0, 1, 5, 17, 49, …`. Measured for j ≤ 3: `g = 0` (rational
parametrisation), `1` (squarefree part of `speed²` has degree 4), `5` (8 odd zeros: 4 roots of the
isotropic-direction factor times the 2 sheets; the degree-24 cusp factor has even order).
A conformal layer (`ρ = ±i` repeated) ramifies and adds no genus.

### 3.2 Degree

Measured over the parabola: `D_j = 2, 6, 16, 40` for `j = 0..3`, matching `(j + 2)·2^j`. A resultant
or norm computation for `j = 3` was done on the elliptic curve `z² = P₄(w)`: the norm of the
crossing function had numerator degree 88, of which `24²` is the cusp locus (even-order zeros)
and `40` is the curve degree. The closed form is a pattern on four data points: [Heuristic]
beyond `j = 3`. For any base the growth is `O(j 2^j)` at most in these examples.

### 3.3 Parameters against degree

| depth j (parabola) | 0 | 1 | 2 | 3 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| D | 2 | 6 | 16 | 40 |
| M | 4 | 7 | 10 | ≤13 |
| line-crossing dimension `M − 2` | 2 | 5 | 8 | ≤ 11 |
| versal would need | 2 | 6 | 16 | 40 |

The deficit `D − (M − 2)` is 0, 1, 8, 29, … so the parabola's offset is the *closest* miss and the
gap then opens exponentially. Parameters ≈ `3 log₂ D`.

## 4. Graphs: which P-coefficient polynomials are drawable

### 4.1 Degree ≤ 4 [Proof, Lemma Q]; degree 5, 6 only up to rational invariants

- Monic quartic graphs: the 5-dimensional graph group acts with an open orbit; rank 4 of 4 [Wolfram].
  `Aff` images of P-quartic graphs form a family of dimension exactly 6 [Wolfram], i.e. a single
  `Aff`-orbit per sign class.
- Quintic `x⁵ + c₄x⁴ + c₃x³ + c₂x² + c₁x + c₀`: translation of `x` kills `c₄`, `y`-shear and
  `y`-translation kill `c₁, c₀`. What is left is `x⁵ + A x³ + B x²` with
  `A = c₃ − 2c₄²/5`, `B = c₂ − 3c₃c₄/5 + 4c₄³/25` [Wolfram]. Scaling `x → λx`, `y → λ⁵y` acts by
  `A ↦ λ⁻²A`, `B ↦ λ⁻³B`. The absolute invariant is
  `J = A³/B²` [Wolfram]. For a ℚ-polynomial `f`, `J ∈ ℚ`. Every `Aff` image of the graph of `f`
  (any P parameters) crosses lines in polynomials with the *same* `J`.
- Sextic: normal form `x⁶ + A x⁴ + B x³ + C x²`, weights 2, 3, 4, invariants `A³/B²`, `A²/C`: two
  rational invariants.
- Measured: rank of (a, b, c, e, g) ↦ monic coefficients: 4 for degrees 4, 5, 6 and also 4 for a
  formal degree-9 graph (only 4 free parameters from `Aff` whatever the degree) [Wolfram].

So **an arbitrary P-quintic or P-sextic graph is not drawn by any `Aff`-image of a ℚ-graph**, but
the obstruction is in the invariants only: all graphs with the same rational invariants are drawable.
The set of reachable invariants is `ℚ` (quintic) or `ℚ²` (sextic), dense in ℝ or ℝ². This is
informative for G3: the drawable quintics/sextics are dense inside the whole space but each is a
*rational* point of the invariant space.

### 4.2 Can other constructors change the invariants? (route-scoped)

- *Béziers:* no, a Bézier is a degree-3 curve with no invariants (§2.3). A Bézier with `x(t)`
  linear is a cubic graph, covered by Lemma Q.
- *Offsets:* an offset of a graph is not a graph of a polynomial, and its line crossings have
  degree `4d − 2` with the measured parameter counts above. No offset gives a graph of degree ≥ 5
  with more P parameters; the offset crossing polynomials of degree 6, 14, 18, 22 have 5, 6, 7, 7
  free parameters respectively.
- *Towers:* `M − 2 = 4 + 3j` free parameters against degree `≥ (j+2)·2^j`.
- *Conclusion, scoped:* no catalogue curve crossed with a *line* produces an arbitrary
  P-coefficient polynomial of degree 5, 6 or 9. A statement about other engines (two-curve
  crossings, box/extremum readouts, inverse-arc-length) is not made.

### 4.3 Lines through special points [Heuristic]

A line through a known point of multiplicity `μ` of the curve has crossing polynomial of degree
`D − μ` with `M − 4` parameters; it gains over generic lines (`M − 2` against `D`) only for
`μ ≥ 3`. Power laws are the only catalogue curves with such points (the origin, multiplicity
`min(p,q)`), and there it gives trinomials. A line through one known (simple) point of a parabola
offset leaves a quintic with 4 free parameters inside the 5-dim monic quintics (codimension 1).

## 5. Parameter growth, summary of rates

| construction | free parameters | degree | growth |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Aff` of fixed curve | ≤ 6 | fixed | 0 |
| graphs, P-coefficients up to degree 4 | 6 (incl. coefficients) | 4 | capped by Lemma Q |
| ℚ-graph (degree ≤ 6) | 6 | ≤ 6 | capped |
| power laws | 5 (+ discrete `p/q`) | `max(p,q)` | discrete only |
| depth-`j` offset tower | `6 + 3j` | `~ (j+2)2^j` | **linear in depth, log in degree** |
| two-curve crossing | `M₁ + M₂ − 4` | `D₁D₂` | additive |

There is no family with parameters growing proportionally to the degree. The only unbounded
source is the depth of the tower, and it pays exponentially in degree and genus for each layer.

## 6. Consequences for G3 / the plan

- A versal line crossing exists for `D ≤ 4` only; the first obstruction (the sextic parabola
  offset) is codimension 1, and the next ones are codimension ≥ 8.
- For two-curve engines the budget is `M₁ + M₂ − 4` against `D₁D₂` (an engine need not be
  versal; it needs the *conditions* on the Tschirnhaus element to be few). Example: `power_law 9`
  × quartic graph has `M = 5 + 6 − 4 = 7` free parameters inside the monic nonics.
- **Offset curves of any degree pass through the circular points with high multiplicity**
  (offset degree `4n−2` for a polynomial curve of degree `n`; the circle meets it in fewer
  points than `2D`). Crossing an offset with a *circle* (an `ellipse` constructor with width =
  height) instead of a line adds three parameters (centre, radius) for lines' two. For the
  parabola offset, the elimination in the foot-point parameter has degree 10 in `t`
  (hand count, not Wolfram checked): a candidate engine for G3, **not evaluated here** [Open].
- The deficits above also say which family would be needed for a degree-`n` direct engine:
  a curve family with `M = n + 2`. All catalogue families have `M ≈ log D`.

  degree law `(j+2)2^j` is proved for no `j`.
- [Open] The depth-3 `M` (≤ 13) is not measured. The formula is an upper bound.
- [Open] Offsets of curves involving `sine`/`exp_two` are not algebraic and are out of scope.
- [Open] Crossings of offsets with circles / ellipses (see §6); the multiplicity of the circular
  points on a tower curve; and the monic rank of those crossing polynomials.
- [Open] Whether a *non-linear-crossing* readout (extremum of the offset curve, `box_*`) gives

  offset is an algebraic equation of degree ≈ the curve class, with `M_j − 2` parameters again;
  nothing new is expected, nothing was computed.

## Other things noticed

- The offset of a parabola is the only catalogue curve family that is *almost* versal: a
  codimension-1 hypersurface in the monic sextics (rank 5 of 6, Wolfram). Its sextic crossings
  satisfy exactly one polynomial relation, which was not extracted.
- The two-sided parametrisation of the parabola offset by `w`, `x = (w − 1/w)/4`, `σ = (w + 1/w)/2`,
  covers the `+d` and `−d` sides as one irreducible curve (`w ↦ −1/w` swaps them).
- In the depth-2 resultant for the parabola tower (28 = 12 + 16), the degree-12 factor was
  spurious (the squared cusp-locus sextic); the genuine degree is the 16. Spurious factors of
  this kind are the main risk when reading degrees off a resultant.
- The CLAUDE.md claim "rotate by whole degrees plus `scale_x/scale_y` gives every P-angle" is
  used here as given (all of `GL₂(P)` plus translations). If only a proper subgroup were
  available, the `M` values drop but the growth rates in §5 do not change.
- `power_law` with `q > p` (curve `x^(p/q)` with exponent < 1) is the same algebraic curve as
  the inverse exponent with `x ↔ y`, so the catalogue is symmetric.
