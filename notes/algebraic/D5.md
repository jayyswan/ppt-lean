## 1. `deg S ≥ 1466` [Computed, numeric with 45-digit refinement]

**Why this bounds the degree.**
- `deg S = σ₁²·S ≥ #(S ∩ σ₁₁(H))` for a general rational hyperplane H of the trace-zero ℙ⁷.
- This uses only isolated nonsingular points, each counted once.
- Those points are the good lines of `X ∩ H`.

**Method.**
- Chart on lines in `ℙ(W)`, with `W = H ∩ {Σy = 0}` (7-dimensional): `u = w₁ + Σaₖwₖ`,
  `v = w₂ + Σbₖwₖ`. That is 10 unknowns.
- Equations: the coefficients of `Σ(u + sv)³` (4) and `Σ(u + sv)⁵` (6).
- Damped Newton from random complex starts. H is `SeedRandom 11` integer coefficients on the basis
  `eᵢ − e₉`.
- **Filters.**
  - Discard singular Jacobians.
  - Discard pairing lines: `Σ(u + sv)⁷ ≡ 0`, since all odd power sums vanish on a pairing `ℙ³`.
    The excess components meet `σ₁₁(H)` in positive dimension, so their points are never
    isolated.
  - Deduplicate in the chart, which is unique per line.

**Run.**
- Seeds 1–5, 10,400 starts in total.
- Result: **1466 distinct lines, 952 of them real.** Per-seed counts were 56, 372, 384, 384, 371,
  with small overlaps. The count is far from saturating.
  - B3's 13 real lines came from 90 real-start Levenberg–Marquardt runs; this run finds many more.
- **Certification.** All 1466 were refined by mpmath Newton at 45 digits.
  - Each reached residual < 10⁻³⁸ within 12 steps and moved < 10⁻⁶ from the float solution.
  - Quadratic convergence confirms they are nonsingular isolated solutions, not points of a
    positive-dimensional component.
  - Caveat: the data H was converted to floats before the 45-digit run. It is the same H to
    10⁻¹⁶, and nonsingular solutions persist under such a perturbation.
- **Consequence.** Lemma A (degree < 9) is out for S and for every linear section of S. B3 already
  said this with the weaker bound.

## 2. Serrano reduction [Proof sketch]

Serrano (*Math. Ann.* 277, 1987): let C be a smooth ample curve on a smooth surface S with
`C² > (k + 1)²`. Then every degree-k morphism `C → ℙ¹` extends to `S → ℙ¹`.

- For `C ∈ |mH|`, `C² = m²·deg S ≥ 1466m² > 81`. So for k ≤ 8, any F-rational gonal map of C
  extends to a pencil `|D|` on S with `D·H = D·C/m ≤ 8/m`.
  - The extension is unique, so it is F-rational when the map is.
- Hence a curve for Theorem G of that kind exists only if S carries a pencil whose fibres have
  Plücker degree ≤ 8. Each such fibre is a ruled surface of degree ≤ 8 inside X swept by good
  lines.
- **Classes of the intrinsic curves [Proof].**
  - `Γ₇ = {p₇|_l has a multiple root}` is the discriminant of a binary septic, a section of
    `(det S*)^{42}`, so `Γ₇ ∈ |42H|`.
  - `Γ₂,₄ = {Res(p₂|_l, p₄|_l) = 0}`: the resultant has bidegree (4, 2) in the coefficients, which
    gives `|8H|`.
  - (`Γ_Q`, the discriminant of `p₂|_l`, is in `|2H|` and has no real points, B3.)
  - `Γ_pl`: class not determined.
  - For `Γ₇` and `Γ₂,₄`, Serrano applies once they are smooth. They are ample with
    `C² ≥ 1466·64`.
  - Smoothness was not checked. For a singular member, apply the argument to its normalisation
    only via variants of Serrano for nodal curves; not done.
- **Plücker sections `C_Π`** (B3 §3b) are smooth by Bertini for general F-rational Π, so this is
  now a proof sketch rather than B3's heuristic, given `deg S > 81`.
  - It assumes S is smooth along `C_Π`.
  - B3 found regular real points, but the surface's singular locus (contacts with the `G(2,4)`'s)
    was not located.

## 3. Irregularity [Heuristic]

- For Fano surfaces of lines, `H¹(S)` usually comes from the cylinder map
  `H³(X) → H¹(S)` (for the cubic threefold this gives q = 5).
- Here X is a complete-intersection 5-fold in ℙ⁷ (dropping the trace), so by Lefschetz
  `H³(X) = H³(ℙ⁷) = 0` and the natural source of `q(S)` vanishes.
- The zero scheme has excess `G(2,4)` components, so the Koszul computation of `h¹(O_S)` does not
  apply directly.
- So `q(S) = 0` is expected, not proved. If true, every fibration of S is over ℙ¹, and §2's pencil
  question is the whole fibration question.

## 4. The remaining question [Open]

> Does the split surface S carry a pencil whose general fibre D has Plücker degree `D·H ≤ 8`?
> That is a 1-parameter family of ruled surfaces of degree ≤ 8 in X, swept by good lines and
> covering S.

- **If no:** Theorem G fails for every smooth ample-class F-curve on S (Plücker sections, and
  `Γ₇`, `Γ₂,₄` if smooth). With Lemma A out, that closes D5 negatively, scoped to S.
- **If yes:** the pencil must also be F-rational for the twist, which is a further condition.
- **Constraints known for such a D.**
  - `D² = 0`.
  - `2p_a(D) − 2 = 13·D·H ≤ 104`, so `p_a(D) ≤ 53`.
  - For bounded `D·H`, Hodge index allows only finitely many such classes, permuted by S₉.
- No candidate is visible. The incidence curves `{l : l ∩ l₀ ≠ ∅}` form a 2-dimensional ample
  family, not a pencil.
- A test would compute NS(S) or find degree-≤ 8 ruled surfaces in X numerically. Neither was
  attempted.

## 8. Second pass: the pencil question and S ∩ (pairing components)

### 8.1 The intended argument [Proof, conditional]

Let `|D|` be a pencil on S with `D·H = d ≤ 8`, and let D be a general connected fibre (reduced,
`D² = 0`).
- Any reduced connected curve of degree d in projective space has
  `p_a(D) ≤ (d − 1)(d − 2)/2`. (Project it to a general plane; the image is birational, and
  `χ(O)` can only drop.)
- Suppose the zero scheme Z were locally 2-dimensional along D. Then Z would be lci there with
  `ω = (K_G ⊗ det E)| = O(13)`, so `2p_a(D) − 2 = 13d`.
  - Smoothness is not needed for this.
  - This would contradict the bound for every `d < 16`, so no such pencil would exist.

### 8.2 But S meets every pairing component along a curve [Computed]

**The jump locus.**
- On `Y_π = G(2, Λ_π)`, the first-order normal map `N_Y → E|_Y` (B3 §2a) has a corank-1 jump
  along a curve `J'_π` (script `scripts/D5_jump3.py`, local determinantal dimension 1).
- It also has 2-dimensional special loci:
  - **intersections `Y_π ∩ Y_π'`** (forms `α_i = ±α_j` or `α_i = 0`), with kernel 2. These are
    what make B3's excess formula non-clean (its −8460): the 945 excess components meet each
    other along 2-dimensional loci;
  - **lines with three proportional forms**, where the kernel is ≥ 1.

**Branch probe (`scripts/D5_probe.py`).**
- Method: solve for good lines (with `p₇|_l ≢ 0`) in a chart at l₀, with two linear slices at
  distance ε. If S contains l₀, good lines appear at distance ≈ c·ε for every ε.
- **Positive control** (a good line from §1): nearest good line at 1.09·ε for
  ε = 10⁻¹ … 10⁻³.
- **Negative control** (a generic pairing line): no good lines once ε ≤ 3·10⁻².
- **Results at jump points (`scripts/D5_probe_jump.py`):**
  - Generic `J'` points: ratio **1.0 and 1.4, constant** for ε = 3·10⁻² … 3·10⁻⁴. So
    **S ∋ l₀**.
  - All 2-dimensional special loci: the ratio grows as ε shrinks (e.g. 55 → ∞, 39 → 486), or no
    good lines are found. So S does not pass through their general points.

So `S ∩ Y_π ⊇ J'_π` for each of the 945 pairing components, and `R := Σ_π J'_π` is a curve on S.

**Size of R.** `deg J'_π ≥ 52`: there are 52 distinct isolated points of `J'_π` on one σ₁
hyperplane of `G(2, 4)` (`scripts/D5_Jdeg.py`, 6000 starts over seeds 1–4), still growing.

### 8.3 Corrected canonical class [Proof sketch]

Near a general point of R, `Z = S ∪ Y_π`.
- Blow up `Y_π` (codimension 8), with exceptional divisor Ẽ, so `K = π*K_G + 7Ẽ`.
- The section `s` of E vanishes on Ẽ. The residual section `s' ∈ H⁰(π*E(−Ẽ))` (rank 10)
  cuts out the strict transform `S̃ ≅ S` near R. Its restriction to Ẽ is the normal map, whose
  zeros over `J'` are exactly `S̃ ∩ Ẽ`.
- Hence `K_S = 13H + 7R − 10R = 13H − 3R` near R.
- Then `p_a(D) = 1 + (13d − 3R·D)/2`, and §8.1 only gives the necessary condition

  > **`R·D ≥ d(16 − d)/3`** (≥ 5 for d = 1, ≥ 22 for d = 8):
  > the scroll of every fibre must contain that many `J'`-type pairing lines.

**Upper bounds that do not reach a contradiction.**
- `p_a ≥ 0` gives `R·D ≤ (13d + 2)/3`.
- Pairing lines kill `p₇|_l`, which is a section of `Sym⁷S*|_D`, so `R·D ≤ μ_max(Sym⁷ S*|_D) ≤ 7d`.
- Both are compatible with the lower bound, so the pencil is **not excluded**.
- Other singular loci of S (none expected: the splitting-type stratum with `h¹(N) ≠ 0` has
  expected codimension 4) were not searched.

### 8.4 What finishing D5 needs

> Decide whether S has a pencil with `D·H = d ≤ 8` and `R·D ≥ d(16 − d)/3`.

Two ways to do it:
- **(a) Compute `NS(S)`, or at least `R²`, `R·H` and the components of R.** That needs an exact
  local or global computation (Macaulay2 or Singular on the zero scheme, or a Hodge-theoretic
  computation). None of these is installed here, and it is too large for the Wolfram MCP.
- **(b) Test the natural candidates.** The `R_π` and their W(B₄)-components are the obvious
  potential fibres or fibre components. Their Plücker degree is ≥ 52 in total. A component of
  degree ≤ 8 with `R_π² = 0` would be a fibre candidate; their number and degrees are not known.
- Until then D5 is **open**. Nothing here is a negative about the target.
