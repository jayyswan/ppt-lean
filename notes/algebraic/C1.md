# C1: the sine × sine engine (Chebyshev trinomials), and what it does to Theorem X

Stream C1 of `PLAN-algebraic.md` (2026-11-11). Research only: no `.lean` edits, no `Defs` changes.
Labels: [Proof], [Proof sketch], [Python] (python-flint, exact arithmetic; Wolfram was not available
in this session, so every computation is the plan's labelled fallback), [Cited], [Heuristic], [Open].
Every obstruction is scoped to the route it blocks. Nothing here is a negative about the target.

Notation as in SUMMARY: F = P ∩ ℝ_alg, K = ℚ(β) of degree n, K_L = K ⊗ L for a real field L ⊂ F.
T_k is the Chebyshev polynomial, T_k(cos θ) = cos kθ.

## 0. Verdict

**The target is not closed.** One new engine, one new unconditional theorem, one weaker conditional
hypothesis, and a reason why the remaining gap cannot be closed by geometry that is uniform in K.

1. **A gap in W3's scoping.** W3 §3.1 excludes crossings of `sine` with an *algebraic* curve
   (transcendental abscissa, Lindemann) and concludes that transcendental crossings give no algebraic
   output. A crossing of two **sine** curves with commensurable frequencies was never considered. Its
   abscissa θ is transcendental, but cos θ is algebraic, and `cos` of a P-number is a P-number.
2. **Engine S [Proof].** F is closed under roots x ∈ [−1, 1] of
   `T_p(x) + a·T_q(x) + c` (a, c ∈ F, any integers p > q ≥ 1), and more generally under cos θ, sin θ for
   every real solution θ of `A cos pθ + B sin pθ + C cos qθ + D sin qθ + E = 0` with coefficients in F.
3. **No Descartes barrier [Proof].** For |a| + |c| < 1 all p roots of T_p + a·T_q + c are real, simple and
   in (−1, 1). This is the first engine with unbounded degree, full symmetric group and all roots real.
   N4's gap theorem and "F coefficients serve totally real fields only for n ≤ 2F − 2" are statements
   about supports in the monomial basis; they do not apply in the Chebyshev basis.
4. **Theorem C1 [Proof; certified n = 5…30 in Python].** For every n, P contains totally real algebraic
   numbers of degree n with Galois group S_n. Example: x = cos θ with cos nθ = −cos θ/3 − 1/7.
   Before this the repo had totally real S_n only for n ≤ 12 (W2), and S_n for all n only with ≤ 3
   real roots (trinomials).
5. **Theorem X′ [Proof, conditional].** β ∈ P if the K-twisted Chebyshev variety has a suitable
   F-point. There is **no square lift**: the variety is twisted by K itself (degree n), not by
   K′ = ℚ(√(β − c)) (degree 2n). With sine terms the count improves from n − 2 conditions to n − 4.
   A positive answer to Taylor's question still implies it; the hypothesis of Theorem X is now a
   disjunction over two unrelated families of varieties.
6. **Why uniform geometry cannot finish it [Proof of the implication].** A construction that is
   uniform in K (valid for the generic polynomial) and uses only degree ≤ 8 steps, radicals and
   engines with ≤ m essential parameters proves RD(n) ≤ max(4, m) for that n. For the trinomial and
   Chebyshev engines m ≤ 3. So a K-uniform proof of (SR) for all n would bound the resolvent degree
   by 4 for every n. No such bound is known (best known: RD(n) ≤ n − α(n) with α growing very slowly
   [Cited, snippet]). The gap therefore has to be closed by arithmetic of the individual field (as
   Meyer did at n = 9), or by an engine whose parameter count grows (offset towers, Theorem R).
7. **Routes tried and closed, scoped (§6):** transcendental parameters for algebraic engines, period
   ratios from real multiplication, elliptic curves through the nine conjugates, complex "harmonic"
   engines for BR₅, more than two frequencies.

## 1. Engine S

### 1.1 The construction [Proof]

Curves, all from `Defs.lean`:
- C₁ = {(θ, cos pθ)}: `sine`, then `scale_x` by 1/p and `translate_x` by −π/(2p).
- C₂ = {(θ, −a·cos qθ − c)}: `sine`, `scale_x` 1/q, `translate_x`, `scale_y` by −a, `translate_y` by −c.

A real solution θ₀ of cos pθ + a cos qθ + c = 0 is an isolated point of C₁ ∩ C₂ (the difference is a
nonzero real-analytic function). `restrict` C₁ to a window with rational bounds around θ₀ that
contains no other crossing; then C₁′ ∩ C₂ = {(θ₀, cos pθ₀)} and `inter_x` gives θ₀ ∈ P. Then
cos θ₀, sin θ₀ ∈ P by `cos_sin_Pconstructible` (Basic.lean:2654). Since x₀ = cos θ₀ is a root of
T_p + a·T_q + c, it is algebraic when a, c are, so x₀ ∈ F.

Conversely every root x₀ ∈ [−1, 1] of T_p + a·T_q + c is cos θ₀ for such a θ₀. Tangential crossings
are allowed (the intersection is still one point).

The same argument with phases gives the **five-term family**

    A cos pθ + B sin pθ + C cos qθ + D sin qθ + E = 0,        A, B, C, D, E ∈ P.

The two curves must carry the same shear (otherwise θ itself enters and the equation is Kepler's), so
five terms is the limit: every affine image of `sine` that is a graph over the θ-axis has one
frequency. Rational frequencies reduce to integer ones by rescaling θ.

**Lean-feasible.** It needs `sine`, the affine closure lemmas, `restrict`, `inter_x`, an isolation
argument for one explicit crossing, and `cos_sin_Pconstructible`. Not in the repo.

### 1.2 What the crossing equation is

With u = e^{iθ} the five-term equation is the self-inversive polynomial

    α u^{2p} + β u^{p+q} + E u^p + β̄ u^{p−q} + ᾱ = 0,     α = (A − iB)/2,  β = (C − iD)/2,

and its roots on |u| = 1 are the crossings. With t = tan(θ/2) it is a real polynomial of degree 2p,

    G(t) = A·Re(1+it)^{2p} + B·Im(1+it)^{2p} + (C·Re(1+it)^{2q} + D·Im(1+it)^{2q})(1+t²)^{p−q} + E(1+t²)^p.

With B = D = 0 it is the polynomial T_p(x) + a·T_q(x) + c of degree p in x = cos θ.

This is the trigonometric member of W3's Ritt list. The power-law trinomial is the crossing of two
affine images of `exp_two` (X^λ = aX + b); Engine S is the crossing of two affine images of `sine`.
An `exp_two` × `sine` crossing is a complex power and gives nothing algebraic (Gelfond–Schneider).

### 1.3 All roots real [Proof]

Let |a| + |c| < 1. At x_k = cos(kπ/p), k = 0…p, T_p(x_k) = (−1)^k and |a·T_q(x_k) + c| < 1, so
T_p + a·T_q + c alternates in sign at p + 1 points of [−1, 1]. It has degree p, so it has exactly p
roots, all simple and in (−1, 1). The same argument gives 2p real roots of G when
|C| + |D| + |E| < √(A² + B²).

### 1.4 The Galois group is S_p [Proof for q = 1; Python for the rest]

- **T_p(x) + a·x is a Morse polynomial for all but finitely many a.** Its critical points solve
  T_p′(x) = −a; for small a ≠ 0 they are x_k(a) = cos(kπ/p) + O(a), k = 1…p−1, distinct, with critical
  values (−1)^k + a·cos(kπ/p) + O(a²), also distinct. Distinctness of critical points and of critical
  values is a Zariski-open condition on a, and it holds near 0, so it fails for finitely many a.
- A Morse polynomial f of degree p has Gal(f(x) − t / ℚ̄(t)) = S_p (Hilbert; Serre, *Topics in Galois
  Theory*, Thm 4.4.5) [Cited, from memory].
- By Hilbert irreducibility the rational c with Gal(T_p + a·x + c / ℚ) = S_p are dense in ℝ, so they
  exist with |a| + |c| < 1.

**Theorem C1.** For every n ≥ 2 there are rational a, c with |a| + |c| < 1 such that
T_n(x) + a·x + c has n real roots in (−1, 1) and Galois group S_n. All n roots are P-constructible.
So P contains totally real algebraic numbers of every degree n with group S_n.

**Certified examples [Python, `scripts/C1_chebyshev_trinomial.py`].** T_n(x) + x/3 + 1/7 for every
n = 5…30: n real roots in (−1, 1) by an exact Sturm count; S_n by Frobenius cycle types at three
primes (an n-cycle, an (n−1)-cycle, and a type with one 2-cycle and odd remaining lengths; a
2-transitive group with a transposition is S_n). For the five-term family
[`scripts/C1_trig_five_term.py`]: 21 cos pθ + 2 cos qθ + 5 sin qθ + 3 gives G of degree 2p with 2p real
roots and group S_{2p} for (p, q) = (3,1), (4,1), (5,1), (5,2), (6,1), (7,3), (8,3), (9,2), (10,3).

**Literature [snippet-level search only].** Nothing found on Galois groups of T_n + a·T_m + c or on
Chebyshev-basis trinomials. Nearest: Girstmair, arXiv:2007.14183 (De Moivre polynomials, mostly
metabelian groups).

## 2. The Tschirnhaus conditions for Engine S

### 2.1 Count [Proof]

- **Cosine form.** x ∈ K_L with T_p(x) ∈ L + L·T_q(x). That is n − 2 linear-dependence conditions on
  the n coordinates of x. The solution set is a surface 𝒞_{K,p,q}, the K-twist of the surface of
  ordered n-tuples of roots of a common T_p + a·T_q + c. Unlike the trinomial there is no scaling, so
  the surface is not a cone.
- **Five-term form.** Take m = tan(θ/2) ∈ K_L, so cos kθ and sin kθ are in K_L for all k. The
  condition is that 1, cos pθ, sin pθ, cos qθ, sin qθ are L-dependent: **n − 4 conditions**. Rotation
  θ ↦ θ + θ₁ (θ₁ with cos θ₁ ∈ L) acts on solutions, so the solution set is a 4-fold 𝒮_{K,p,q} with a
  free circle action. Overshoot is built in: 2p ≥ n.
- Bookkeeping law check: five coefficients, n + 1 − 5 = n − 4. Engine S obeys the law. Its new
  feature is realness, not parameter count.

### 2.2 Shape in the monomial basis [Proof]

T_n(x) + a·x + c is x·Q_n(x²) + a·x + c (n odd) with Q_n the Chebyshev coefficients. So in terms of
elementary symmetric functions the conditions are

    e₁ = e₃ = e₅ = … = 0   and   e_{2k} = κ_k·e₂^k  (2 ≤ k < n/2),

with κ_k fixed rationals. It is a **special case of the odd shape** t·Q(t²) − c with Q pinned to the
Chebyshev polynomial up to scaling.
- For n ≤ 9 the odd route with a free P-quartic Q is strictly better (4 conditions at n = 9 against
  7 here). **Engine S does not improve n = 9.**
- For n = 11, 13 Theorem O needs a rational `poly_graph` (rationality conditions).
- For n ≥ 15 the odd route has no drawable Q. Engine S is the only odd-shape engine that exists for
  every n.
- At n = 5 the conditions are e₁ = e₃ = 0, a cubic surface in ℙ³, so Lemma A applies: every quintic
  field has a "Chebyshev–Bring form" x⁵ + Ax³ + Bx + C reached by one cubic step. Not new for P.

### 2.3 Geometry [Proof]

𝒞_{K,p,q} is a twisted Galois cover of the (a, c)-plane with group S_p. Any F-curve on it is the full
K-twist of the cover over a plane curve (B3's component-orbit lemma applies verbatim: over a curve
where the monodromy drops, the components are permuted transitively and none is F-rational). So
Theorem G can only apply through a map transverse to the cover, exactly as for I_{K′}, V_τ, S, T.
Expect general type. No F-point is known for any K of degree ≥ 9.

## 3. Theorem X′

**Theorem X′ [Proof, conditional].** Let K = ℚ(β) ⊂ ℝ have degree n. Then β ∈ P if for some integers
p > q ≥ 1 there are a real field L ⊂ F and a real number θ with

1. cos θ, sin θ ∈ K_L, and β ∈ L(cos θ, sin θ);
2. A cos pθ + B sin pθ + C cos qθ + D sin qθ + E = 0 for some A, …, E ∈ L, not all zero.

*Proof.* By §1.1, θ ∈ P, so cos θ, sin θ ∈ F. Then β ∈ L(cos θ, sin θ) ⊂ F. ∎

Call hypothesis (SR′): 𝒮_{K,p,q} has an F-point, for some (p, q), whose point generates K_L.

Comparison with Theorem X.

| | Theorem X | Theorem X′ |
|---|---|---|
| engine | `power_law` × line | `sine` × `sine` |
| field twisted by | K′ = ℚ(√(β − c)), degree 2n | K, degree n |
| square lift | needed (≤ 3 real roots) | not needed |
| conditions / dimension | 2n − 2 on 2n (cone over a curve) | n − 4 on n (a 4-fold with a circle action) |
| discrete freedom | D | p and q |

- **Taylor's question implies (SR′) [Proof sketch].** The variety is geometrically integral
  (monodromy S_{2p} is n-transitive). It has a smooth real point with the conjugation pattern of K:
  for totally real K take the n of the 2p real roots from §1.3; for r real places and s complex pairs
  take coefficients for which G has at least r real roots and at least s nonreal pairs (the count of
  real roots of 21 cos pθ + E runs from 2p to 0 as |E| crosses 21; intermediate patterns by adding the
  q-terms) [Proof sketch]. A point over ℚ^{solv} ∩ ℝ close to it satisfies 1–2.
- **Sufficient, not necessary**, as for (SR). The headline hypothesis is now
  **(SR) or (SR′)**: an F-point on any one of the varieties I_{K′,D}, 𝒞_{K,p,q}, 𝒮_{K,p,q}.
- **Not a proof.** (SR′) is a point on a general-type variety over F, the same kind of problem as
  SUMMARY §10.

## 4. Two reformulations of the conditional hypotheses [Proof]

- **Trinomial = Belyi fibre.** If τ^D + aτ + b = 0 with a, b ∈ L, put u = −aτ/b; then
  1 − u = −τ^D/b and u^D(1 − u)^{−1} = −(−a/b)^D·b ∈ L. Conversely u^p(1 − u)^q = λ ∈ L (p, q integers)
  is the crossing of `power_law` x^p with an affine image of `power_law` x^{−q}, also drawable. So
  (SR), widened to the power × power engine, says: **u and 1 − u are multiplicatively dependent
  modulo L^×** for some u ∈ K_L ∖ L. Equivalently all conjugates of u lie on one fibre of a
  three-point map x ↦ x^p(1 − x)^q.
- **Chebyshev = Lissajous collinearity.** (SR′), cosine form: the n points (T_q(x_i), T_p(x_i)) of the
  Lissajous curve are collinear. For the power law the curve (x, x^D) is convex, which is the
  Descartes barrier; the Lissajous curve has no such restriction.
- **Transcendental parameters do not help a linear engine.** F is algebraically closed in P, so P and
  F(τ) are linearly disjoint over F: a P-linear relation among elements of F(τ) implies an F-linear
  one. For a nonlinear engine a P-point of the condition variety is the generic point of an
  F-subvariety W whose function field embeds in P, which is Theorem G's situation again.

## 5. Uniform routes and resolvent degree

**Proposition [Proof, from the definition of resolvent degree, Farb–Wolfson].** Fix n. Suppose the
generic degree-n extension K_gen/k (k = ℚ(a₁, …, a_n)) embeds in a tower over k whose steps are
(i) of degree ≤ 8, (ii) radical, or (iii) generated by a root of a member of an engine family with at
most m essential parameters, the parameters lying in the previous field. Then RD(n) ≤ max(RD(8), m)
≤ max(4, m).

*Proof.* RD of a tower is the maximum over its steps. A step of type (iii) is a pull-back of the
engine's universal cover over an m-dimensional base, so its essential dimension, hence its RD, is
≤ m. RD(8) ≤ 4 (Hilbert) and radical steps have RD 1. ∎

Consequences.
- Essential parameters: trinomial 1, Chebyshev trinomial 2, five-term 3, the odd route with a
  P-quartic 4, Engine C / the A, C families ≤ 13 (W2).
- A proof of (SR), (SR′), "one P-line on S", etc. by a construction **uniform in K** (a curve, line or
  fibration on the condition variety defined for the generic K: the B1 and B3 programme) would give
  RD(n) ≤ 4 … 13 for that n. At n = 9, 10 this is no obstruction (RD(9) ≤ 4 is Hilbert's theorem). For
  all n it would be a bound independent of n. No n with RD(n) > 1 is known, so this is not a proof
  that uniform routes fail; but every known bound has n − RD(n) growing extremely slowly
  [Cited, snippet: Wolfson; Sutherland; Heberle–Sutherland]. [Heuristic] Treat "uniform in K with
  bounded engines, for all n" as at least as hard as a major advance on Hilbert's 13th problem.
- Not constrained by this: (a) arithmetic arguments specific to each K (Meyer at n = 9, Theorem H,
  Taylor's question); (b) engines with growing parameter count (offset towers; Theorem R's squares).
- This is the scope statement behind B3's remark "the failure, and any success, is uniform in K".

## 6. Routes tried in C1 and closed (scoped)

1. **More than two frequencies.** Would be (BD) outright: a trigonometric polynomial with n free
   coefficients has any prescribed real roots. Not drawable: sheared or rotated sines put θ in the
   argument (sin of sin); offsets of `sine` against a horizontal line give an algebraic equation in
   sin θ but with the usual square roots (base relation y² + y′² = 1); text warps of a circle give
   harmonics of bounded order ≤ 4. [Proof sketch, same structure as Z4.]
2. **Period ratios from real multiplication.** An abelian variety with RM by K has β as an eigenvalue
   on differentials, so β is a ratio of periods of an eigendifferential. But End is defined over the
   field of definition (K has no automorphisms), so the eigendifferential is defined over L(β):
   circular. [Proof]
3. **Elliptic curves through the conjugates.** For n ≤ 9 every K_L-point of the plane lies on an
   L-rational cubic (the linear system of cubics has dimension 9), so curves E/L with new K_L-points
   exist rationally, and the minimal polynomial is f·b² − a² (deg a ≤ 4, deg b ≤ 3). But smooth cubics
   are not drawable, and the elliptic logarithm of the point is in P only if the point is. Theorem R's
   genus-1 curves (two layers) lie in 4-dimensional linear systems, too small for this. [Proof]
4. **Theorem R as 2-divisibility.** Q_j(τ) square for all j ⟺ the point (τ, ·) of the hyperelliptic
   curve y² = ∏Q_j has trivial image under the "x − T" descent map, i.e. is essentially in 2·Jac(K_L).
   Only the *product* ∏Q_j(τ) can be made a square for free (interpolation, when 2J ≥ n − 1). No
   rational curve parametrizes ≥ 2 forms at once (Riemann–Hurwitz: a pencil with four square members).
   Consistent with Z5. [Proof]
5. **Complex engines for BR₅.** The root z of a complex g of degree m is the meet of two real curves
   Re(A·g) = 0. Every real F-curve through z contains the m × m grid {(z_j, z̄_k)}, so it has degree
   ≥ m and lies in the system Re(A(z, z̄)·g(z)). For m = 3 the two nodal members have three real
   asymptotes; no drawable member found for m = 5. Engine S reaches only conjugation-stable fields
   (unimodular elements), so it does not touch the swap configuration. [Proof sketch; Open]
6. **Arc-length engines.** `arc_of_length` on y = a·x^{1+1/(2k)} has polynomial arc length: with
   u = √(1 + c·x^{1/k}), s is a multiple of Φ_k(u) = ∫u²(u²−1)^{k−1}du. So F is closed under real roots
   u > 1 of Φ_k(u) = C. One parameter, degree 2k + 1, a 3-cycle in the monodromy. A new engine but
   dominated by the trinomial. [Proof; Galois group not computed]

## Other things noticed

- W3 §3.1's sentence "the pattern cannot produce an algebraic output at all when the parameters are
  algebraic" should be scoped to crossings with at least one algebraic curve.
- SUMMARY §3 "Realness": the n ≤ 2F − 2 bound is for monomial supports.
- X1's c-chain lemma: Engine S adds totally real S_n fields of every degree to the list of fields
  known to lie in F, but each is a single field, not a closure property for a class.
