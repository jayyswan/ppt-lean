# D3: offset towers with a spare parameter (degree 9)

Stream D3 of `PLAN-degree9.md` (includes R4 and R5), 2026-11-13. Research only, no `.lean` edits.
Backend: Wolfram (wolfram MCP) for every number. Log: `HANDOFF-alg-D3.md`. Test field: `q₃` (the
S₉ totally real nonic from `D1E.md`).

**Verdict: open, with the exact remaining problem stated in §5.**
- The layer-2 square condition is genus 1, and no choice of real layer maps removes it (§2, a
  negative scoped to this route).
- R5 removes it arithmetically. Pick any K-point Q of the plane whose nine conjugates lie on a cubic
  with two real components. Then one shape parameter of layer 2 (one real root of a sextic) makes
  the layer-2 curve F-isomorphic to that cubic. The image of Q is then a K_F-point of the curve
  (§3). This was checked on `q₃`.
- What is left is the partner condition: 4 "conic-support" conditions on the remaining data (§4).
  No sequential structure for them was found.

## 1. Formulation over F [Proof]

- **Notation.** `K_F = F[β] = F[x]/(q)`. We assume `q` stays irreducible over F, since otherwise
  `β ∈ F` already.
- **The tower.** Base parabola `(t, t²)`. Layer k is an affine map `A_k`, then `offset` by `d_k`.
  Then an outer affine map, and the unit circle as partner. Every dial is in F, so this is an
  F-row.
- **The condition.** Some crossing has a coordinate that generates `K_F`. Equivalently, there is a
  `K_F`-point `P₂` of the tower curve `C₂` that lies on the partner conic. Then `inter_x` (after
  `restrict`) gives `τ = ℓ(P₂)`, and `β ∈ F[τ]`.
- **Count (W1).** Rank 10 against 9 needed, so for a fixed τ the dial fibre is a curve over ℚ. T9Q
  via D3 is therefore the statement "this fibre curve has an F-point for some τ". Theorem G would
  give that if the curve had gonality ≤ 8. Its degree and gonality were not computed (§6).

## 2. Forward chain and the square conditions [Proof]

Write `N₀ = (−2t, 1)` for the base normal, and `B' = A₁⁻¹A₁⁻ᵀ`, `M = A₁⁻¹B₂A₁⁻ᵀ` with
`B₂ = A₂⁻¹A₂⁻ᵀ`.

- **Layer 1 is free.** The unit normal needs `s₁² = h(t) := N₀ᵀB'N₀`, a quadratic in t. That is a
  conic with F-points (t = 0), so `(t, s₁)` is rational in one parameter `μ ∈ K_F`.
  - The parabola's affine stabiliser `t ↦ at + b` normalises `h` to `4t² + 1`.
  - The same holds for any base whose tangent direction is linear in the parameter. The other
    such base is the cuspidal cubic `(u², u³)`, i.e. `power_law` with b = 3/2 or a cuspidal cubic
    Bézier.
- **Layer 2 is genus 1.** The unit normal of layer 2 needs `|A₂⁻ᵀn₁|² = g(t)/h(t)` to be a square,
  where `g(t) = N₀ᵀMN₀`. So the K_F-data must be a K_F-point of
  `D : s₁² = h(t), σ² = g(t)`.
  - `D` is an unramified double cover of `w² = g·h`. That curve has branch points at the roots of
    two positive definite quadratics, so `D` has genus 1 (W1 §3 in this coordinate).
- **Step 3 of the plan (spend the spare parameter so the condition holds identically): no.**
  - `D` has genus 0 only if `g` and `h` share a root.
  - Two positive definite real quadratics share a root only if they are proportional. That means
    `B₂ ∝ I`: layer 2 is conformal and merges with layer 1.
  - Scope: depth-2 towers on a base whose tangent direction is linear in the parameter, with
    real `A_k`.
  - **R4** (choose generators so two square conditions hold structurally) fails for the same
    reason: only layer 1 can hold structurally.
- **Real structure of D.**
  - `D(ℝ) → ℙ¹(ℝ)` is an unramified 4-sheeted cover with monodromy in V₄. So it has 2 or 4
    components, and Harnack (at most 2 for genus 1) leaves exactly **2**.
  - Hence `Δ > 0` and `j(D) ≥ 1728`.
  - **[Computed, exact]** With `h = 4t² + 1` and `g = 4t² + b²` (a = 0), D's quartic model is
    `4v⁴ + (16b² − 8)v² + 4`. It has `j(D) = 256(b⁴ − b² + 1)³ / (b⁴(1 − b²)²)`, the Legendre
    j-invariant at `λ = b²`, and `J(D) = −4096(b² − 2)(b² + 1)(2b² − 1)`.
  - So for `b ∈ (0, 1)`, j covers `[1728, ∞)`, and the sign of J changes at `b² = ½`. Both real
    forms (twist classes) therefore occur.

## 3. R5: K_F-points of D from cubics through conjugates [Proof sketch; Computed on q₃]

**The mechanism.**
1. Take any `Q ∈ K²`. Its nine conjugates lie on a cubic `Γ_Q` over ℚ: one ℚ-linear relation
   among the 10 cubic monomials, i.e. a 9 × 10 nullspace.
2. Suppose `Γ_Q` is smooth with two real components (`Δ > 0`). Solve
   `j_Legendre(b²) = j(Γ_Q)` for `b²`. This is a sextic, so `b ∈ F`. Of its two roots in (0, 1),
   take the one whose J-sign matches `Γ_Q`'s.
3. Then `D_b ≅ Γ_Q` over F, because of two facts:
   - `F*/F*² = {±1} = ℝ*/ℝ*²`, so equal j and equal real form give an F-isomorphism.
   - Both curves have F-points: `Γ_Q` from a line meeting it, where a real root of a cubic lies in
     F; `D_b` at `t = 0`.
4. The isomorphism is built with F-operations: Nagell's reduction from an F-point, then a
   Weierstrass scaling `u` with `u²` a positive root, then a translation.
5. The image `ι(Q)` is a `K_F`-point `(t, s₁, σ)` of `D_b`. Translating by `D_b(F)` adds a
   1-parameter F-family.

So the layer-2 square condition costs one F-equation of degree 6 in `b²`. That is how the spare
parameter is spent, not on making the condition hold identically.

**Check on q₃ [Computed].**
- Take `Q = (β, 2β² + β⁴)`. Then
  `Γ_Q : 46695 + 7060390310X + 9740601572X² − 2832751209X³ − 1828682814Y + 102839784XY
  + 98189383X²Y − 1483340Y² − 14941XY² + 76Y³ = 0`, which is affine-smooth.
- `j(Γ_Q) = 318376.9606377513151543713…`.
  - It was computed from the quartic model (projection from the conjugate point `Q₁`, then the
    binary-quartic invariants I and J).
  - It agrees at all 9 conjugate base points.
  - It is rational: 103-digit numerator and 97-digit denominator, fitted to 600 digits with
    residual < 10⁻⁵⁸⁶.
  - `Δ > 0`, and `sign J = −1` at all 9 base points.
- The equation `j_D(b) = j(Γ_Q)` has degree 12 in b (a sextic in b²). Its positive roots are
  `0.16730…, 0.98590…, 1.01429…, 5.97716…`. The root `b = 0.1673033562…` has `sign J = −1`, so it
  matches.
- Survey of 18 small choices of Q: 11 have `Δ > 0`. The pairs `(β, β⁴)` and `(β, β⁴ + 2β²)` give
  `j < 1728` and cannot be matched.
- The isomorphism `ι` itself was not written out (standard, as above).

## 4. What remains: the partner condition

The `K_F`-point `e = (t, s₁, σ)` of `D_b` determines:
- `P₁ = A₁(t, t²) + d₁n₁`;
- `n₂ = A₂⁻ᵀn₁ / (σ/s₁)`;
- `P₂ = A₂P₁ + d₂n₂`, all in `K_F²`.

The partner condition says that `1, X, Y, X², XY, Y²` at `P₂` are F-linearly dependent (an ellipse
once the outer affine map is included). That is **4 conditions** (rank ≤ 5 among 6 vectors in F⁹).

- It is affine-invariant, so the conditions see only:
  - `d₁`, `d₂`;
  - the translation `T ∈ D_b(F)`;
  - the choice of Q (12 dimensions modulo `Aff(F)`, with b slaved to Q by the sextic).
- For fixed Q this leaves 3 free F-parameters against 4 conditions. So Q must move, and the
  conditions are then of very high degree in Q (through `Γ_Q`, j and `ι`).
- No sequential structure was found. Specifically: no ordering in which each new unknown solves an
  equation of degree ≤ 8 over the previous ones.
- [Heuristic] This is the same "linear support" shape that cost H1's families their conditions.
  R5 moves the genus-1 difficulty out of the way but does not touch it.

## 5. Exact remaining problem [Open]

> For `q₃` (or every totally real S₉/A₉ nonic), find `Q ∈ K_F²`, `T ∈ D_{b(Q)}(F)` and
> `d₁, d₂ ∈ F` such that `P₂(ι_Q(Q) + T, d₁, d₂)` lies on an F-conic.
> Equivalently: an F-point on the fibre curve of §1.

It would be enough to show that the fibre curve over some τ ∈ K has gonality ≤ 8 (Theorem G), or
to find any degree-≤ 8 sequential order for the 4 conic-support conditions.
