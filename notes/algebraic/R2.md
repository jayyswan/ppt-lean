# R2: the true minimum number of squares

**Stream R2 of `PLAN-algebraic.md`, Phase E (2026-11-12).** Research only: no `.lean` edits, no
`Defs` changes. Labels: [Proof], [Proof sketch], [Wolfram], [Cited], [Heuristic], [Open]. Every
obstruction is scoped to the route it blocks; nothing here is a negative about
`algebraic_Pconstructible`.

Notation is Y1a's and R1's:

- `q ∈ ℚ[X]` irreducible of degree `n`, `β` a real root, `K = ℚ(β)`, and `L ⊂ ℝ ∩ P` a field closed
  under square roots of positive elements; `K_L = K ⊗ L`, assumed a field. For the transcendence
  arguments of §5, `L` is taken algebraic (`P ∩ ℝ_alg` or `ℚ^solv ∩ ℝ`, Y1a's examples); the
  linear-algebra counts do not depend on this.
- A **tower** is a drawable curve of the form `Aff ∘ (offset ∘ Aff)^J (base)`. In final normal-slope
  coordinates its support function is
  `H(s) = h₀(s) + Σ_{j=1}^J d_j ε_j √Q_j(s) + ⟨b, u(s)⟩`,
  with `Q_j` a positive-definite binary form, `B_j = P_jP_jᵀ`; see Y1a Prop. T.
- A **partner** is a drawable curve `C`; the crossing equation is `F_C(x(τ)) = 0` in `K_L`.
- `J` is the number of offset distances (plus an ellipse base treated as a square form): these are
  the "squares" of Theorem R. `J_min(n)` is the least `J` for which a construction of this shape can
  serve degree `n`.

## 0. Findings summary

1. **There are two different counts, and they must not be conflated [Proof].**
   - A **Type I** parameter is one that appears *jointly linearly* with the offsets in the single
     `K_L`-equation. A nonzero `L`-solution is then guaranteed by linear algebra as soon as there are
     more unknowns than the `n` `L`-equations (Lemma 1).
   - A **Type II** parameter is one in which the crossing equation is linear *for fixed tower*, but
     which is applied to a tower whose parameters enter nonlinearly. It gives a *determinantal*
     condition of codimension `n − P` on the tower (Lemma 2); an `L`-point is not guaranteed.
2. **Strict linear minimum: `J_min(n) = n − 2`, uniformly in `K` [Proof].** The maximum is attained
   by the cubic Bézier base of Q3 (two jointly linear base parameters) plus a fixed-slope line (one
   intercept). The parabola and power-law bases give one; every other base gives zero. No partner
   other than a fixed-slope line contributes a jointly linear parameter uniformly in `K`; the
   `K`-specific `exp` exception (§5) gives at most two parameters and `J ≥ n − 3` for special `K`.
   For `n ≥ 9` this is `J ≥ 7`, and `g(C_B) ≥ 321`.
3. **Dimension-count bound: `J_min(n) ≤ n − 7` sharp, or `≤ n − 6` in the plan's convention
   [Proof + Wolfram].** The maximum partner is a conic (five projective parameters) or a quartic
   graph (five coefficients); both give the generic rank condition `n − 5`. With the cubic Bézier
   base a tower with `J = n − 7` parameters satisfies it generically; the plan's conservative count
   is `J = n − 6`. At `n = 9` this is `J ≤ 2` (sharp) or `J ≤ 3` (plan), so `C_B` is elliptic or
   genus 5. This is an **upper bound** on the true minimum: special towers can only lower the
   determinantal codimension, hence use fewer squares.
4. **The catch: the conic/quartic-graph parameters are Type II [Proof].** A nondegenerate conic
   cannot be made jointly linear with the tower when the tower's direction space has dimension ≥ 2:
   the conic's quadratic part would have to vanish on a 2-dimensional space, which a nonzero binary
   quadratic form cannot do (degenerate or not). The same applies to a circle/ellipse and to a graph
   of degree ≥ 2. So the sharp count `n − 7` is a dimension count, not a linear-algebra theorem: the conic
   route adds a determinantal condition of codimension `n − 5`, with degree-12 minors, on top of the
   `J` square conditions.
5. **The complete source table (§2.4):** the only sources are the parabola/power-law base (1), the
   cubic Bézier base (2), the fixed-slope line (1), the circle (3), the conic (5), the quartic graph
   (5), the cubic graph and ℚ-quintic/sextic graph (4). The five-term sine and `exp_two` partners
   contribute nothing beyond a line intercept generically (§5; `exp` gives ≤2 for special `K`);
   overshoot contributes no linear parameter (§2.5); a second tower or partner never helps (Q3 §2).
6. **The five-term sine partner is not a source [Proof].** With algebraic sine parameters (the
   plan's `L`), at an algebraic crossing `x = τ` both `τ` and `ωτ + φ` are algebraic; if
   `sin(ωτ + φ)` is algebraic then `e^{i(ωτ+φ)}` is algebraic, contradicting Lindemann–Weierstrass
   unless `ωτ + φ = 0`. At that point the sine curve is the horizontal line `y = c`, so the partner
   reduces to a line (one parameter). The amplitude, frequency and phase do not enter linearly.
   Transcendental sine parameters make the condition a transcendental equation, not a linear source
   (§5). Engine S (C1) is a different route, with a transcendental abscissa, and is not a source of
   squares for Theorem R.
7. **Overshoot is a degree/realness device, not a parameter source [Proof].** In Theorem R the
   crossing equation is always one `K_L`-equation, i.e. `n` `L`-equations, independent of the degree
   `D` of the tower. Overshoot (`D > n`) only ensures the partner can meet the tower in enough points
   (Bézout) and affects realness. The `F` coefficients of the linear-support overshoot engines are
   the coefficients of the crossing polynomial, a different object, and are bounded by drawability
   (G2/Lemma Q).
8. **Consequences.** The plan's `J_min ≈ n − 6` is a generic **upper bound** on the conic route's
   minimum, sharpened to `n − 7`; it is **not** a lower bound. The strict Type I class has the exact
   minimum `n − 2`. The plan's threshold `J_min ≤ 3` is met at `n = 9` (both counts) and at
   `n = 10` (sharp count only). At `n = 9` the route does **not** automatically reduce to a
   `K_L`-point on a genus-1 curve: it reduces to `SS_2` plus a determinantal condition of
   codimension 4 on the tower parameters. The "much better problem" is only one factor of the
   system. R2 does not kill the conic route; it states the trade exactly and isolates the next
   computation (the determinantal variety at `n = 9`, `J = 2` or `3`).

## 1. The crossing equation and the two counts

### 1.1 Theorem R's linear system

For a line `ℓ·x = c`, Y1a's operator is `D_ℓ[h] = (ℓ₁ + ℓ₂s)h′ − ℓ₂h`. After normalising the line
to `y = c` and the base axis to vertical, the crossing equation at `τ ∈ K_L` is

> `c′ + D_{e₂}[h₀](τ) + Σ_{j=1}^J δ_j N_j(τ)/ρ_j = 0`,

where `N_j = b₁₂^{(j)}τ − b₂₂^{(j)}` and `ρ_j² = Q_j(τ)` [Y1a §2]. This is one equation in `K_L`,
hence `n` equations over `L` (one per embedding of `K/L`).

The **unknowns** are of three kinds:

- the **offsets** `δ_j` (`J` of them), each paid for by one square condition `ρ_j² = Q_j(τ)`;
- the **base parameters**: the coefficients of `D_{e₂}[h₀]` as a polynomial in `s`;
- the **partner parameters**: the coefficients of the partner's defining equation.

The **K-data** are `τ` and the `ρ_j`; they are not free `L`-parameters. The forms `B_j` are free
moduli, but each costs one square condition; they are the "free moduli" of the curve `C_B` (R1/Y1a).

### 1.2 Type I and Type II

**Definition.** Let `U` be a set of `L`-parameters in the crossing equation.

- They are **Type I** if the equation is linear in all of them *jointly*, with coefficients in `K_L`
  independent of the parameters.
- They are **Type II** if the equation is linear in them only after the tower parameters are fixed
  (or vice versa), so that the tower parameters enter nonlinearly.

The distinction is not cosmetic: Type I gives an `L`-solution by linear algebra (Lemma 1); Type II
gives a determinantal condition whose `L`-points are an arithmetic problem (Lemma 2).

### 1.3 Lemma 1 (kernel count) [Proof]

Let `Φ : L^U → K_L ≅ L^n` be `L`-linear. If `U ≥ n + 1`, then `ker Φ ≠ 0`; a nonzero solution exists
in `L^U`. If `U = n`, a nonzero solution exists only on the codimension-1 locus `det Φ = 0`, which is
not guaranteed for a given `K`.

*Proof.* `rank Φ ≤ n < U`. ∎

The `+1` is why the count is `n + 1 − U`, not `n − U`: the equation is homogeneous in the unknowns,
and the zero solution is the degenerate tower.

### 1.4 Lemma 2 (rank count) [Proof]

Let a partner family have projective dimension `P`, with defining equation `F_C` linear in its `P + 1`
homogeneous coefficients. For a fixed tower, a nonzero partner through the `n` conjugate points
`x(τ_i)` exists iff the `n × (P + 1)` evaluation matrix has rank `≤ P`. For a generic matrix this is a
determinantal condition of codimension `n − P` on the tower parameters.

If the tower has `T` `L`-parameters, a solution over `L̄` exists when `T ≥ n − P`; an `L`-solution is
not guaranteed by linear algebra. ∎

The quartic-graph case is inhomogeneous: `Y = Σ_{i=0}^4 a_i X^i` has five unknowns and the condition
is `Y ∈ span_L(1, X, …, X⁴)`, again codimension `n − 5`.

## 2. Sources of linear parameters

### 2.1 Base curves

The base contributes the function `D_{e₂}[h₀](s) = s h₀′(s) − h₀(s)`. A base parameter is Type I iff
it is a coefficient of this function and the coefficient is linear in the drawing parameters.

| base | support function `h₀` | `D_{e₂}h₀` | Type I count | drawable? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| parabola `y = x²/(4a)` | `a s²` | `a s²` | **1** | yes (`poly_graph` degree 2 + Aff) |
| power law `y = a x^b` | `c s^{b/(b−1)}` | `(b/(b−1)−1)c s^{b/(b−1)}` | **1** | yes (`power_law` + Aff) |
| cubic Bézier with `x(t)` quadratic | `a s³ + b s²` | `2a s³ + b s²` | **2** | yes (`cubic_bezier`, Q3) |
| polynomial-Gauss curve of degree `d` | `Σ_{k=0}^d a_k s^k` | `Σ_{k≥2} (k−1)a_k s^k` | `d − 1` | **only `d ≤ 3`** |
| ellipse / circle | `√Q_B(s)` | — | 0 (one square) | yes (`ellipse`) |
| graph of degree `d ≥ 3` (`poly_graph`) | `x s − f(x)`, `x = (f′)⁻¹(s)` | algebraic | 0 | yes (degree ≤ 6; P-coeff. only ≤ 4) |
| line / rectangle | linear / piecewise linear | `D` kills linear pieces | 0 | yes (`rectangle`) |
| `sine`, `exp_two` | transcendental | transcendental | 0 | yes, but not algebraic |
| offset / `arc_of_length` / `text` | adds `√Q` / integral / algebraic | not polynomial | 0 | yes |
| affine images | absorb into the tower's outer `Aff` | — | 0 new | yes |

**Lemma R2a (base classification) [Proof sketch, from G2's catalogue].** A drawable algebraic base
whose support function is a polynomial in the normal slope with coefficients linear in the drawing
parameters is, up to an `Aff` normalisation, one of:

1. a power law `y = a x^b` (including the parabola `b = 2`; `1` parameter, the scale);
2. the cubic Bézier family `x = 3a s² + 2b s`, `y = 2a s³ + b s²`, `h₀ = a s³ + b s²`
   (`2` parameters).

In particular `B ≤ 2`.

*Proof sketch.* The support-function computation for a general polynomial `h₀` is
`x = h₀′`, `y = s h₀′ − h₀`, `dy/dx = s` [Wolfram]. The constructors give:

- `poly_graph` of degree `d`: the support function is `x s − f(x)` with `f′(x) = s`; for `d ≥ 3`,
  `x = (f′)⁻¹(s)` is algebraic of degree `d − 1`, so `h₀` is not polynomial. For `d = 2` it is the
  parabola.
- `power_law`: `y = a x^b` has support function a single monomial `c s^{b/(b−1)}`, linear in the
  scale `c`; for `b = (k+1)/k` this is the polynomial case `c s^{k+1}`.
- `cubic_bezier`: the tangent slope is a rational function of the Bézier parameter of degree 2 in
  general. It is linear (so `h₀` is polynomial) exactly when the hodograph is a line through the
  origin; this is Q3's family, with two shape parameters. A general cubic Bézier has no polynomial
  support function.
- `ellipse`: `√Q`; one square, no linear parameter.
- `text`: the warped glyph is an algebraic quartic/egg, not a polynomial-graph support function;
  its handles enter nonlinearly (WARP).
- `offset`: `h₀ + d√Q`; the new term is a square (one of the `J`), not a base parameter.
- `arc_of_length`: the support function involves an integral/square root; not polynomial.
- affine images: the 6 `Aff` parameters are the tower's outer affine freedom, already normalised
  away (Y1a Prop. T); they add no base shape parameter.

The only drawable polynomial-Gauss family of degree `≥ 4` would be a quartic parametric curve
`x = Σ k a_k s^{k−1}/(k−1)`, `y = Σ a_k s^k`; `Defs` has no quartic Bézier, no constructor produces
this curve, and `poly_graph` cannot (its `x` is not linear in `s`). So `d ≤ 3`. ∎

**Consequence.** `B ≤ 2`, attained by Q3's cubic Bézier. This is the first correction to the plan's
implicit hope that a richer base could give many parameters: the support function must be polynomial,
and drawability caps its degree at 3.

### 2.2 Partner curves

The partner contributes the coefficients of `F_C`. A partner parameter is Type I only if the equation
is jointly linear in it and the tower parameters; for all partners except a fixed-slope line (and
the `K`-specific `exp` exception of §5), it is not (Proposition R2.3 below).

| partner | linear family dimension `P` | equation linear in coefficients? | Type | count |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| line, fixed slope | 1 (intercept) | yes, jointly | **I** | **1** |
| line, general | 2 (slope + intercept) | yes, separately | II | 2 |
| circle | 3 (centre 2 + radius²) | yes, separately | II | 3 |
| conic (ellipse/hyperbola/parabola) | 5 | yes, separately | II | **5** |
| quartic graph `y = a₄x⁴+…+a₀` | 5 | yes, separately | II | **5** |
| cubic graph | 4 | yes, separately | II | 4 |
| ℚ-quintic / ℚ-sextic graph | 4 (`Aff`; rational invariants fixed) | yes, separately | II | 4 |
| cubic Bézier | — (6-dimensional, not linear) | no (parametric) | — | 0 |
| text warp | — (handles, not linear) | no | — | 0 |
| `sine`, `exp_two` | 0 new generically (exp ≤ 2 for special `K`) | no (Lindemann/Gelfond–Schneider, §5) | — | 0 |
| rectangle | — (its edges are lines) | piecewise | — | ≤ 2 |
| offset curve | — (not linear) | no | — | 0 |

**Lemma R2b (partner classification) [Proof sketch, from G2/Defs].** The drawable partner families
with a defining equation linear in their coefficients and dimension `> 4` are the conics (`P = 5`)
and the quartic graphs (`P = 5`); the next are the cubic graph and the ℚ-quintic/sextic graph
(`P = 4`), then the circle (`P = 3`) and the line (`P = 2`, or `1` with fixed slope). In particular
`P ≤ 5`.

*Proof sketch.* The constructors give lines, conics (ellipse/circle/hyperbola/parabola, all `Aff`
images), graphs of `poly_graph` (degree ≤ 6, but arbitrary `P`-coefficients only up to degree 4;
Lemma Q), cubic Béziers (parametric), power laws (one scale), ellipses, text warps, and offsets.
A conic's equation has 6 homogeneous coefficients, `P = 5`. A quartic graph has 5 coefficients,
`P = 5`. A ℚ-quintic/sextic graph has 6/7 coefficients but only the 4 `Aff` parameters are free
`P`-values; the other coefficients are rational invariants (G2 §4.1), so `P = 4`. A cubic Bézier is
not linear in its control points: the crossing condition is two equations with the Bézier parameter,
not one linear equation. A text warp's handle enters nonlinearly. A power law `y = a x^k` has one
coefficient. So the maximum is `P = 5`, attained twice. ∎

The quartic-graph count `P = 5` is the partner count, not G2's line-crossing `4/4`: in a line
crossing the leading coefficient is an `Aff` y-scale absorbed by the line normalisation; as a
*partner* the graph is a separate curve and the equation uses all five coefficients. Even if one
insists on the monic normalisation (`P = 4`), the conic already gives `P = 5`, so the maximum is
unchanged.

### 2.3 The line: why only one Type I parameter

A general line `ℓ₁x + ℓ₂y + c = 0` has three homogeneous coefficients. Substituting
`x(τ; params) = x₀ + Σ params_i v_i` gives an equation that is **bilinear** in `(ℓ, params)`: linear
in `ℓ` for fixed tower, linear in the tower for fixed `ℓ`, but not jointly linear. The affine
normalisation can send any `L`-line to `y = c`, fixing the slope; the remaining intercept combines
with the tower's vertical translation into the single unknown `c′`. Hence the Type I count is `1`.
The other line coefficient (the slope) is Type II; Proposition R2.3 explains why it cannot be made Type I
without losing the joint linearity.

### 2.4 The complete table

The crossing equation is one `K_L`-equation (`n` `L`-equations). The number of squares is

> `J ≥ n + 1 − (B + P_I)`   in the strict Type I count,

where `B` is the base's Type I count and `P_I` the partner's Type I count, and

> `J = n − P − B`   is the generic dimension-count construction,

where `P` is the partner's projective dimension (Lemma 2). Special towers can lower the
codimension, so this is an upper bound on `J_min`, not a lower bound. The best values are:

| source | Type I | dimension `P` | notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| parabola base | 1 | 1 | Y1a |
| power-law base | 1 | 1 | one monomial |
| cubic Bézier base | **2** | **2** | Q3, `x(t)` quadratic |
| fixed-slope line | **1** | 1 | intercept |
| circle partner | 0 | 3 | W1's `+2` relative to a line |
| conic partner | 0 | **5** | max partner |
| quartic graph partner | 0 | **5** | ties the conic |
| cubic graph partner | 0 | 4 | |
| ℚ-quintic/sextic graph partner | 0 | 4 | rational invariants |
| sine/exp partner | 0 new generically (exp ≤ 2 for special `K`) | 0 | §5 |
| overshoot | 0 | 0 | §2.5 |
| second tower/partner | 0 | 0 | Q3 §2 |

**Strict:** `B = 2` (cubic Bézier) + `P_I = 1` (line) gives `J ≥ n + 1 − 3 = n − 2`.
**Dimension:** `B = 2` + `P = 5` gives `J = n − 7` (sharp); the plan's convention, counting the
conic's five parameters and requiring `U = T + 5 ≥ n + 1`, gives `J = n − 6`.

The circle partner is dominated: `P = 3`, so the generic construction gives `J = n − 3` with the
parabola base and `J = n − 4` with the cubic Bézier base, both worse than the conic's `n − 6`/`n − 7`.
W1's `+2` relative to a line is exactly the difference between the circle's three parameters (centre
two, radius one) and the fixed-slope line's one intercept.

The difference of one between the sharp and plan counts is the conic's projective scale. The conic
has 6 homogeneous coefficients; a nonzero conic exists iff the `n × 6` evaluation matrix has rank
`≤ 5`, which is `n − 5` conditions on the tower, not `n − 4`. The plan's count is the conservative
version. At `n = 9` the sharp count is `J = 2`, the plan count `J = 3`.

### 2.5 Overshoot

Overshoot means the crossing polynomial has degree `D > n`, so `χ_τ` can divide it with a nontrivial
cofactor. In Theorem R's linear system the condition is already `Φ = 0` in `K_L`; it is always `n`
`L`-equations, whatever `D` is. Overshoot contributes:

- a **necessary degree condition**: the partner and the tower must meet in at least `n` points
  (Bézout; for a conic partner and a depth-`J` tower, `2D_J ≥ n`). For `n = 9`, even `J = 1` has
  `D₁ = 6`, `2D₁ = 12 ≥ 9`, so this is not binding; for the cubic Bézier base the degree is at least
  `3`, and depth `J` grows it;
- **realness**: more intersections can give more real crossings (W1 measured 20–22 real crossings
  for a depth-2 circle tower);
- **no linear parameter**: the cofactor's coefficients are determined by the equation, not free
  `L`-parameters. The `F` coefficients of H1/N2's linear-support overshoot engines are coefficients
  of the crossing polynomial, a different object, and `F` is bounded by drawability.

So overshoot contributes `0` to `J_min`. It is a degree/realness device, as H1/N2 already used it.

## 3. `J_min(n)`

### 3.1 Theorem R2.A (strict linear minimum) [Proof]

For any Theorem R construction whose crossing equation is jointly linear in the tower and partner
parameters, the number of squares satisfies

> `J ≥ n − 2`.

The bound is attained by the cubic Bézier base + fixed-slope line.

*Proof.* By Lemma R2a, `B ≤ 2`. By Lemma R2b and §2.3, the only partner with a jointly linear
parameter is the fixed-slope line, `P_I = 1`; the non-line partners are Type II by Proposition R2.3
(and its corollary), so they cannot be counted here. The crossing equation is one `K_L`-equation and
is linear and homogeneous in `U = J + B + P_I ≤ J + 3` unknowns. Lemma 1 gives `U ≥ n + 1`, hence
`J ≥ n − 2`. Q3's cubic Bézier base attains `B = 2`; the fixed-slope line attains `P_I = 1`; for
`J = n − 2` the kernel is nonzero. ∎

*Scope.* The theorem counts constructions available uniformly in `K`: the partner's parameters are
chosen without knowing the conjugates. A `K`-specific `exp` partner whose exponents
`ωτ_i + φ` are all rational (see §5) is an exception; it contributes two linear parameters (`A` and
`c`) and gives `J ≥ n − 3` for those special `K`. It is not available for a generic `K` and does not
change `J_min(n)`.

**Corollary.** This reproduces Q3's `n − 2` and shows it is optimal in the strict class. For
`n ≥ 9`, the strict route needs `J ≥ 7` and the curve `C_B` has genus
`1 + 2^{J−1}(J−2) ≥ 321`. The strict route therefore does not reach the plan's genus-≤5 window;
it is the old `SS_{n−2}` problem with a modest constant improvement over Y1a's `n − 1`.

### 3.2 Theorem R2.B (dimension-count bound) [Proof + Wolfram]

If partners whose coefficients are only separately linear are allowed, the generic rank count gives

> `J_min ≤ n − P − B`,   with `P ≤ 5`, `B ≤ 2`,

so

> `J_min ≤ n − 7`   (sharp),   or   `J_min ≤ n − 6`   (plan's convention).

The bound is attained in the dimension count by the cubic Bézier base + conic (or quartic graph).

*Proof.* Lemma R2b gives `P ≤ 5`; Lemma R2a gives `B ≤ 2`. The conic (or quartic graph) condition is
`rank ≤ 5` on an `n × 6` matrix, codimension `n − 5` for a generic matrix (Lemma 2). With `T = J + 2`
tower parameters, a tower with `T = n − 5` (i.e. `J = n − 7`) satisfies the generic condition, so
`J_min ≤ n − 7`. The plan's convention uses the five projective conic parameters as five affine
unknowns and `U = T + 5 ≥ n + 1`, giving a construction with `J = n − 6`, hence `J_min ≤ n − 6`. ∎

**Direction of the inequality.** This is an **upper bound on the minimum**: the count exhibits a
tower with `J = n − 7` parameters that satisfies the generic determinantal condition. It does not
say that fewer squares are impossible. A special tower whose image lies in a smaller determinantal
stratum has a smaller codimension and may use fewer squares. The strict Type I bound `J ≥ n − 2`
of Theorem R2.A is the only lower bound in this note.

**Numbers [Wolfram].**

| `n` | strict `n − 2` | sharp `n − 7` | plan `n − 6` | genus `C_B` sharp | genus `C_B` plan |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 9 | 7 | **2** | **3** | **1** | **5** |
| 10 | 8 | **3** | 4 | **5** | 17 |
| 11 | 9 | 4 | 5 | 17 | 49 |
| 12 | 10 | 5 | 6 | 49 | 129 |
| 13 | 11 | 6 | 7 | 129 | 321 |

The genus formula is R1/Y1a's `g(C_B) = 1 + 2^{J−1}(J−2)`; the values `0, 1, 5, 17, 49, 129, 321`
are exact [Wolfram]. The total space of the exact `SS_J` problem has dimension `2J − 1` (R1 §6.3):
`3` at `J = 2`, `5` at `J = 3`; its unirationality is R1's open question.

### 3.3 The threshold

- **Strict count (lower bound):** `J_min(n) = n − 2`. The plan's `J_min ≤ 3` holds only for
  `n ≤ 5`, where the route is already unconditional (Y1a §4.1: `V_τ` is rational for `n ≤ 5`).
- **Sharp dimension count (upper bound):** the generic construction gives `J_min(9) ≤ 2`,
  `J_min(10) ≤ 3`, `J_min(11) ≤ 4`, … So the threshold `J_min ≤ 3` is certainly met at `n = 9`
  and `n = 10`.
- **Plan's convention (upper bound):** `J_min(9) ≤ 3`, `J_min(10) ≤ 4`, … So the threshold is met
  at `n = 9` only, which is the plan's expected `n − 6`.

The plan's sentence "if `J_min(n) ≤ 3` … a `K_L`-point on a curve of genus ≤ 5 with free moduli"
is therefore true only in the dimension-count sense. At `n = 9` the sharp count gives a genus-1
curve (elliptic), the plan count a genus-5 curve. Both are far better than the strict `J = 7`
(genus 321), but the conic route does not reduce to `SS_J` alone: the conic's Type II condition is
still there (§4).

## 4. Why the conic cannot be made Type I

**Proposition R2.3 [Proof].** Let `C` be a nondegenerate conic with quadratic part `Q_C`, and let the
tower's crossing point be `x(τ; params) = x₀ + Σ_i params_i v_i` (affine-linear in the tower
parameters). If `F_C(x(τ; params))` is affine-linear in `params` for all `params`, then `Q_C(v_i, v_j) = 0`
for all `i, j`; i.e. `Q_C` vanishes on the direction space `V = span{v_i}`. If `dim V ≥ 2`, this is
impossible for a nonzero binary quadratic form over a field (degenerate or not: a parabola's
quadratic part is degenerate but nonzero), since its isotropic cone is at most one-dimensional
(and over `ℝ` a definite form has no nonzero isotropic vector at all).
Hence a nondegenerate conic is not jointly linear with any tower of dimension `≥ 2`.

*Proof.* The quadratic part of `F_C(x₀ + Σ params_i v_i)` in `params` is `Q_C(Σ params_i v_i)`.
For the expression to be affine-linear, this quadratic form must vanish identically, i.e.
`Q_C(v_i, v_j) = 0` for all `i, j` (polarisation). A nonzero binary quadratic form's zero set in
`ℙ¹` is at most two points; a totally isotropic subspace has dimension at most 1 unless the form is
zero. ∎

**Corollary R2.4.** The same argument applies to the circle/ellipse (over `ℝ` positive definite, so
no real isotropic vector; over `K_L` its isotropic cone is still at most 1-dimensional) and to a
graph of degree `≥ 2` (the degree-`d` part would have to vanish on `V`, which is impossible for
`dim V ≥ 1` and a nonzero leading coefficient). The only exceptional case is
`dim V = 1` (no offsets and a one-parameter base): a hyperbola can make the single direction
isotropic, but then the conic's quadratic part is constrained, and the count does not improve on the
line. For `J + B ≥ 2` there is no linearisation.

**What the conic route actually is.** For a conic partner the condition is:

> the `n` conjugate points `x(τ_i)` lie on a conic over `L`
> `⟺` the `n × 6` matrix of monomials `(X², XY, Y², X, Y, 1)` has rank `≤ 5`
> `⟺` all `6 × 6` minors vanish (a determinantal variety of codimension `n − 5`).

The entries are quadratic in the tower parameters (`X², XY, Y²`) and linear (`X, Y, 1`), so the
minors have degree at most `12`. At `n = 9`, `J = 2` (`T = 4`): a codimension-4 system of degree-12
minors in four unknowns, a finite set over `L̄`; at `J = 3` (`T = 5`): the same codimension in five
unknowns, a curve over `L̄`. The conic coefficients are then the kernel of the matrix. That kernel is Galois-stable: the
Galois group permutes the `n` rows and acts on `K_L`; the solution space is a Galois-stable
`K_L`-subspace. By Speiser's lemma (`H¹(Gal(K_L/L), GL₆(K_L)) = 0`, the Hilbert-90 statement for
vector spaces) it has an `L`-basis, so the conic can be chosen over `L` once the rank condition holds
for tower parameters in `L`. The `L`-rationality of the conic is therefore not an extra condition;
the determinantal condition on the tower parameters is the whole arithmetic cost on the partner side.
The same descent applies to a quartic-graph partner (an affine `K_L`-subspace with a Galois-stable
solution set). The `J` square conditions `ρ_j² = Q_j(τ)` remain, and the forms `B_j` remain free
moduli. So the conic route is

> `SS_J` **plus** a codimension-`(n − 5)` determinantal condition on the tower parameters.

The dimension count `J = n − 7` balances the tower parameters against the determinantal condition; it
does not make the condition linear. The codimension `n − 5` is the generic value for the full space
of `n`-tuples; the tower map may land in a smaller determinantal stratum, which would lower the
codimension and improve `J_min`. The actual codimension for a specific tower is part of the open
computation, not a lower bound.

**Why the degenerate solutions do not count.** The zero tower (`a = b = δ_j = 0`) gives `x(τ) = 0`
for all embeddings; every conic through the origin passes through the `n` coincident points, so the
determinantal variety has a trivial component. The line solutions (tower crossing a line at all
conjugates) lie on the degenerate-conic components: for `n ≥ 3` distinct collinear points, any conic
through them contains the line. These components fail (ND1) or give a degenerate partner. The useful
solutions are on the nondegenerate components; the dimension count does not distinguish them. This is
a further reason why the conic route is not automatically `SS_J`.

## 5. The sine and `exp_two` partners

**Proposition R2.5 [Proof].** Let the tower be algebraic with crossing abscissa `τ ∈ K_L`, and let
the partner be `y = A sin(ωx + φ) + c` with `A, ω, φ, c ∈ L`, `L` algebraic (the plan's setting). If
the crossing ordinate `y(τ)` is algebraic, then `ωτ + φ = 0` and `y(τ) = c`. Hence the sine partner
contributes only the horizontal line `y = c`, i.e. one Type I intercept; the amplitude, frequency and
phase are not linear parameters.

*Proof.* `ωτ + φ` is algebraic. If `sin(ωτ + φ)` is algebraic, then
`e^{i(ωτ+φ)}` is algebraic because it satisfies `z² − 2i sin(ωτ+φ) z − 1 = 0` (and `cos` is
`±√(1 − sin²)`). Lindemann–Weierstrass says `e^{iα}` is transcendental for every nonzero algebraic
`α`; hence `ωτ + φ = 0`. Then `y = c`. ∎

*Scope on transcendental parameters.* If `ω, φ` are allowed to be transcendental `P`-numbers, the
condition `sin(ωτ_i + φ) ∈ K_L` for all `i` is a system of transcendental equations, not a linear
condition. It is not a Type I source, and no construction is known; the linear count is unaffected.
The no-go above covers the algebraic case of the plan's examples.

The `exp_two` curve is a fixed curve with no free parameters. An `Aff` image
`y = A·2^{ωx+φ} + c` can pass through an algebraic point at one `τ` with `ωτ + φ` rational
(Gelfond–Schneider: `2^r` is algebraic for rational `r`, transcendental for irrational algebraic
`r`). For the `K_L`-equation, this would require `ωτ_i + φ` rational for all `n` conjugates; for a
generic `K`, or for `n ≥ 3` with the conjugates not in arithmetic progression, this forces `ω = 0`,
i.e. a horizontal line `y = A·2^{φ} + c`. Even in the exceptional case where all exponents are
rational, the partner contributes at most the two linear parameters `A` and `c`, so the strict
count becomes `J ≥ n − 3`; the asymptotic is unchanged. A `sine`/`exp` *base* is likewise not a
source of linear support parameters for `n ≥ 3`.

**Engine S is different.** C1's five-term family `A cos pθ + B sin pθ + C cos qθ + D sin qθ + E = 0`
uses a **transcendental** abscissa `θ` and an **algebraic** ordinate `cos θ`; the `n` conjugates are
values of `cos θ`, not algebraic `x`-coordinates. It is a genuine engine with `n − 4` conditions
(C1 §2.1), but it is not a partner to an algebraic offset tower and contributes no squares to
Theorem R. The plan's "five-term sine family as partner" is therefore a dead entry for R2; the
useful sine route is C1's own.

## 6. Relation to R1, R3, R4, R5

- **R1.** The exact `SS_J` reformulation is unchanged. The conic route with `J = 2` at `n = 9`
  lands on R1's `J = 2` case (elliptic `C_B`, `Jac = H`, rank-growth question) or `J = 3` (genus 5,
  total space dimension 5, unirationality [Open]). The conic's determinantal condition is an
  additional factor, not part of R1's total space `H̃`. R1's "positive surprise" question (is the
  total space unirational for small `J`?) is necessary but not sufficient for the conic route.
- **R3.** The `n = 7` control: strict `J_min = 5`; sharp dimension `J_min = 0`; plan `J_min = 1`.
  The conic route at `n = 7` with `J = 0` is just the cubic Bézier base against a conic (degree 6
  engine), which is already within the degree-8 theorem; the interesting search is the
  determinantal system at `n = 7`, `J = 0`/`1`. The `n = 5` control remains Y1a's line/parabola
  example; the cubic Bézier base would reduce it from `J = 4` to `J = 3` [Proof + Wolfram], a useful
  sanity check for the code.
- **R4.** Structured `τ`: the conic condition depends on the `n` conjugate points; for a primitive
  `K` (`Gal ⊇ A_n`) no proper Galois-stable subset of the points defines the conic over `L`, so the
  condition is a genuine `L`-structure on the full set, not a choice of five points. A structured
  `τ` (e.g. a Chebyshev/Lattès value) might make the monomial matrix rank-deficient for a structural
  reason; this is a concrete search for R4.
- **R5.** Elliptic curves through the conjugates: the `J = 2` conic route is an elliptic `C_B`; R5's
  genus-1 tower shapes could interact with the determinantal condition. No mechanism found in R2;
  R5 remains a search over tower shapes.

## 7. Verdict

**Proved, or proved modulo G2's constructor catalogue.**

- Lemma 1 (kernel count) and Lemma 2 (rank count); the Type I/Type II distinction [Proof].
- Lemma R2a: `B ≤ 2`, attained by the cubic Bézier base (Q3) [Proof sketch from G2].
- Lemma R2b: `P ≤ 5`, attained by the conic and the quartic graph [Proof sketch from G2/Defs].
- Theorem R2.A: the strict linear minimum is `J ≥ n − 2`, attained by the cubic Bézier base + line.
- Theorem R2.B: the dimension-count bound is `J_min ≤ n − 7` (sharp) or `J_min ≤ n − 6` (plan),
  attained by the cubic Bézier base + conic/quartic graph; it is an upper bound on the minimum, not
  a lower bound.
- Proposition R2.3: a nondegenerate conic is not jointly linear with a tower of dimension `≥ 2`.
- Proposition R2.5: the sine partner contributes only a line intercept; the `exp` partner is at
  most a line generically, and at most two parameters for special `K` (§5).

**Corrected.**

- The plan's `J_min ≈ n − 6` is a **dimension count**, not a linear-algebra minimum; the sharp count
  is `n − 7`.
- The conic/quartic-graph parameters are Type II; they do not give free `L`-parameters. The conic
  route is `SS_J` plus a codimension-`(n − 5)` determinantal condition.
- The plan's threshold `J_min ≤ 3` is met at `n = 9` (both counts) and `n = 10` (sharp count only),
  but not in the strict sense. The strict minimum `n − 2` gives genus ≥ 321 for `n ≥ 9`.

**Open.**

- The `L`-points of the determinantal variety at `n = 9`, `J = 2` or `3`: compute its components,
  degree and rational points for Y1a's explicit totally real `S₉` nonic
  `q = ∏_{i=−4}^{4}(x − i) + 1`. This is the concrete next computation; it is a degree-12 system
  in 4/5 variables, not a linear one.
- Whether the nondegenerate component is unirational for small `J` (R1's question, sharpened by the
  conic condition).
- Whether a new constructor (a two-curve/Minkowski operation) could make the conic's parameters
  Type I. Z4 says no current constructor does; the conic linearisation is impossible for a
  nondegenerate conic by Proposition R2.3, so a new operation would have to change the *equation*,
  not just the partner.

**Kill criterion (scoped).** For the strict Type I class, R2 gives the lower bound
`J_min = n − 2`, so the plan's criterion `J_min ≥ n − c` holds with `c = 2`; that class is closed.
For the conic Type II class, R2 gives only the upper bound `J_min ≤ n − 7`; the criterion is **not**
met, and R3's search remains the deciding test. This is the scoped reading of the plan's kill rule:
the strict route is closed, the conic route is open with a sharper target.

**Scoped.** None of this is a negative about `algebraic_Pconstructible`. The conic route is not
closed; its arithmetic is relocated, not removed. The strict route remains `SS_{n−2}`, with the
genus growing exponentially.

## Other things noticed

- **H1's conic-vs-power-law row uses a different normalisation.** H1's table lists `P = 6`,
  conditions `n − 6` for `conic vs power law`. The conic has 6 homogeneous coefficients, so the
  projective rank condition is `rank ≤ 5`, i.e. `n − 5` conditions (for `n ≥ 6`). H1's `n − 6` is
  counting the six coefficients as affine unknowns without the projective scale. The same convention
  issue produces the plan's `n − 6` instead of the sharp `n − 7`.
- **The quartic graph ties the conic in the partner count.** G2's "4 free monic parameters" is the
  line-crossing count; as a partner the graph's equation uses all five coefficients, so `P = 5`.
  If one insists on the monic normalisation, the conic still gives `P = 5`, so the maximum is
  unchanged.
- **The ℚ-quintic/sextic graph is not a source of 6/7 parameters.** Only the four `Aff` parameters
  are free `P`-values; the other coefficients are the rational invariants `A³/B²` (quintic) and
  `A³/B², A²/C` (sextic) of G2 §4.1. The crossing condition is `n − 4`, not `n − 6`/`n − 7`.
- **The `n = 6` consistency check.** The sharp dimension count gives `J_min = −1`, i.e. `J = 0`:
  the cubic Bézier base (degree 3) against a conic (degree 2) has intersection degree 6, and the
  six conjugate points lie on a conic for a one-parameter family of base shapes. This is consistent
  with `root_Pconstructible_deg8_coeffs`; it is not new, but it validates the count at the first
  nontrivial `n`.
- **The `n = 5` check.** Y1a's totally real `S₅` quintic uses `J = 4` squares with the parabola base.
  The cubic Bézier base would need only `J = 3` (`U = 3 + 2 + 1 = 6 = n + 1`); the same linear
  algebra gives a kernel. This is a useful positive control for R3's code: run the Bézier base with
  `J = 3` on the Y1a quintic and check the read-out.
- **The conic condition's Galois twist.** For a primitive `K` (`Gal ⊇ A_n`), no proper Galois-stable
  subset of the `n` conjugate points defines the conic over `L`; the conic is a global `L`-structure
  on the full set. This is the same "component-orbit" phenomenon as B3/C1: the determinantal variety
  is not a union of `L`-rational pieces attached to small subsets. It reinforces that the conic
  condition is a genuine arithmetic condition, not a choice of five points.
- **Realness of the cubic Bézier tower.** W1 measured 20–22 real crossings for the depth-2 circle
  tower over the *parabola*. The cubic Bézier base is a different tower; its real crossing count is
  not measured. For the strict route with `J = n − 2 ≥ 7` the degree is huge, so realness is very
  likely not binding, but R3 should measure the Bézier base's real counts before using it for a
  totally real search.
- **The conic route's real count.** A general ellipse is an `Aff` image of a circle, and a real
  affine map is a homeomorphism of `ℝ²`; the real intersection count of an ellipse partner equals
  that of a circle partner after the inverse `Aff` map (which preserves the tower class). So W1's
  circle counts apply to the ellipse; the general conic's real count is not a new obstruction.
- **The "free moduli" of `C_B` are not extra linear parameters.** The forms `B_j` have `2J − 2`
  moduli, but each form also carries one square condition; the net freedom is already counted in
  `SS_J`. The conic condition depends on the forms through `ρ_j` and the directions `e_j`, but that
  dependence is nonlinear; it does not add Type I parameters.
- **The plan's phrase "linear K-parameters" is ambiguous.** I interpreted it as "linear parameters
  in the `K`-equation", i.e. `L`-parameters appearing linearly in the equation in `K_L`; the
  `K`-valued unknowns `τ, ρ_j` are not counted. If the plan meant `K`-valued parameters, the count
  would be different (each `K`-unknown is `n` `L`-unknowns and carries its own equations), but the
  strict lower bound `n − 2` and the generic upper bound `n − 7` are unchanged.
