# W1: affine-offset towers as overshoot engines for general n

Stream W1 of `pptc/PLAN-algebraic.md` (wave 3). No `.lean` edits. Labels: [Proof], [Wolfram], [Heuristic], [Open].
Backend: Wolfram for every number (no Python). Every "cannot" is scoped to a route; nothing here is a negative
about the target.

Notation. q in Q[X] of degree n, K = Q[X]/(q), F = F_{<n}. A tower is `Aff o (offset o Aff)^j (parabola)`
(base: the parabola `(t, t^2)`; G2 shows the other bases cost the same 3 parameters per layer). `r` = rank of the map
(all tower parameters, including the outer `Aff`) -> (x-coordinates of the crossings with the partner), i.e. the
dimension of the cone of good tau (shift and scale of tau included). Conditions on tau = n - r.

## 0. Findings summary

1. **Ranks (measured) [Wolfram].** Numerical Jacobian of the crossing x-coordinates against all parameters
   (5j + 6 of them: per layer a 2x2 matrix and a distance, plus the outer `Aff`), at a random rational point, 40-60
   digits, finite differences at h = 1e-15..1e-25 after Newton continuation of every crossing.

   | partner | j = 0 | j = 1 | j = 2 | j = 3 | formula |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | line | 2 (monic quadratic) | **5** (6 of 6 crossings found) | **8** (12 of 16) | **11** (15 of 40) | 3j + 2 |
   | circle / ellipse / conic (through the circular points) | **4** | **7** | **10** | not run | 3j + 4 |

   Clean gaps every time (e.g. line j = 2: 0.33 against 5e-26; circle j = 2: 0.55 against 7e-15; line j = 3:
   2.5e-4 against 3.5e-16, finite-difference noise level 1e-15). The line values reproduce G2's bounds M - 2 (equality, not
   just an upper bound, at j = 1, 2, 3); the circle values are new. **Nothing collapses: r keeps pace at exactly 3 per
   layer.** A conic partner adds nothing beyond a circle plus the outer `Aff` (an ellipse is an `Aff` image of a circle);
   parabolic and hyperbolic partners and power-law partners were not measured [Open].

2. **Pace with n.** r >= n needs j(n) = ceil((n-2)/3) with a line, ceil((n-4)/3) with a circle:

   | n | 9 | 10 | 11 | 12 | 13 | 16 | 22 |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | j, line | 3 | 3 | 3 | 4 | 4 | 5 | 7 |
   | j, circle | 2 | 2 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 4 | 6 |
   | conditions on tau at that j | 0 | 0-1 | 0 | 0-2 | 0-2 | | |

   (with the previous depth: n - r = 1, 2, ... ). The price: curve degree `D_j = (j+2) 2^j` (line, G2), genus
   `g_j = 1 + 2^j(j/2 - 1)` (0, 0, 1, 5, 17, ...), so **degree and genus are exponential in n/3**. At n = 10 with the
   circle: j = 2, M = 10 = n (zero conditions), crossing degree >= 19 [Wolfram: 19 distinct complex crossings found].

3. **Realness: no Descartes obstruction [Wolfram, grid count; Heuristic as a maximum].** Counting sign changes along
   all 2^j sheets of the curve, best of ~150 random + hill-climbed parameter vectors:
   line j = 1: 6 (D = 6), j = 2: 15 (D = 16); circle j = 1: 8, j = 2: 20-22 real crossing points (the j = 3 line count of
   42 > D = 40 is finite-difference noise at cusps, so read it as "dozens"). So a depth-2 circle tower has 20 real crossings
   against the 10 needed for a totally real decic.
   **Trivial real existence [Proof].** For any real tower with >= n real crossings, interpolation gives tau in K tensor R with
   tau(beta_i) = those x-coordinates. Real solvability is therefore never the issue; the issue is tau in K and parameters in F.

4. **Structure [Proof, elementary; Wolfram checks].** *Overshoot with a line/conic partner is a K-point problem.*
   chi_tau divides the crossing polynomial iff there is a point P of (tower curve) cap (partner) with coordinates in a small
   extension of K whose x-coordinate is tau. For a **rational** tower curve (genus 0) this is a K-point `P(omega)`, `omega in K`
   free, and the partner condition is **F-linear dependence of the functions 1, a, b (line) or 1, a, b, a^2, ab, b^2 (conic)
   at omega**: exactly H1's linear-support family with F = 3 or 6 coefficients and n - 2, n - 5 conditions on omega, with the
   tower's one modulus d free. That is the only regime with a free n-dimensional parameter omega.
   **Genus 0 holds only for j <= 1** (G2's recursion `g_j = 1 + 2^j(g_0 - 1 + jm/2)`, parabola: 0, 0, 1, 5), and a genus-0
   tower curve has r = 5 (line) / 7 (circle), so it serves n <= 5 / 7 for free and pays n - 5 / n - 7 conditions beyond that.
   For j >= 2 the tower curve has genus >= 1 and `omega` is not free: one needs a K-point of a curve of genus g_j, i.e. an
   F-point of a Weil restriction, which is not known to be unirational. The incidence variety I_K = {(p, tau)} has dimension
   M = r (+ the 2 for a line) in all cases (H1), so the dimension count never fails. The cost is arithmetic.

5. **Cost, scoped.** I found **no sequential construction** (layer parameters one at a time, each by an equation of degree < n).
   Route-scoped reasons, none a statement about the target:
   - *Few conditions, huge degree.* For j = 1, line, n = 6 (one condition), the hypersurface of good tau in the centred
     coefficients `e_2..e_6` (weights 2..6) has **no weighted-homogeneous equation of weight <= 41** [Wolfram, mod p linear
     algebra, 420 samples, up to 412 monomials]. So its degree in tau is >= 42: one condition, degree already > 6.
   - *No conditions, but a zero-dimensional fibre.* For r >= n and fixed tau the parameters solve n equations in M >= n
     unknowns, each of high degree. Already at j = 1 a single point condition has degree 8 in d and 4 in the line parameters
     s, g (resultant exponents [Wolfram]; 1575 monomials). The degree of the fibre over tau at r = n (n = 5, j = 1) was
     attempted by Groebner basis mod p and timed out, so it is [Open]; it is certainly > 1 (the sign d -> -d alone gives 2).
   - *Genus.* Past j = 1 the K-points of the tower curve are not rationally parametrised (point 4).
   Where linear structure survives: see section 3. It is real, but it cannot carry more than the first non-conformal layer.

6. **Concrete test n = 10 (section 5).** q = prod(x - a_i) + 7x + 3, a = triangular numbers 0..45: irreducible, 10 real
   roots, Galois group S_10 [Wolfram + Jordan]. A depth-2 circle tower has 20 real crossings and M = 10 = n. A real
   continuation of the 10 chosen crossings to prescribed tau-values stalled at lambda = 0.07-0.09 for two target sets
   (this only measures the real image of the map, which is a proper subset). No tau in K and no parameters in F were found.
   [Open]; no negative about the target.

## 1. Measurement method

- Tower point by recursion: `P_0 = (t, t^2)`, `P_k = A_k P_{k-1} + d_k J(A_k P'_{k-1}) / s_k`, `s_k^2 = |A_k P'_{k-1}|^2`.
  Derivatives with respect to `t` are taken through the `s_i` (`s_i' = Q_i'/(2 s_i)`). Translations between layers are
  absorbed into the last one (offset commutes with translation). The final map `Z = E P + f` is intersected with the partner:
  `Z_y = 0` (line) or `|Z|^2 = 1` (unit circle).
- Unknowns `(t, s_1, .., s_j)`; equations `s_k^2 - Q_k = 0` and the partner equation. All crossings found by Newton
  from random complex starts that satisfy the sign pattern (start `s_k = +-sqrt(Q_k)`), 50-300 starts.
  This avoids resultants (hence **no spurious factors of the kind G2 warns about**: every counted crossing satisfies the
  original equations to 1e-30).
- Jacobian column k: re-run Newton at `theta + h e_k` from the base root, difference of `Z_x`. `rank` = number of singular
  values above 1e-6 of the largest (gaps were >= 8 orders).
- Caveat: only 12 of 16 (j = 2 line), 15 of 40 (j = 3 line), 19 of ? (j = 2 circle) crossings were found, so each rank is
  a lower bound; it equals the G2 upper bound M - 2 (line) and the M = 3j + 4 count (circle), so I take it as the value.

## 2. How r compares with the bookkeeping law

H1/H2: an engine with f effective parameters leaves n - f conditions, and only ~log n / log log n cheap ones. Towers are
the one family with f = 3 log_2 D growing; at the circle partner f = r_j = 3j + 4 = n for j = (n-4)/3. So the **count**
is met at every n with j ~ n/3 (the plan's expectation), and **the whole question is the cost of the F-point**, as the
brief says. What this note adds is the identification of that cost with K-points on the tower curve (point 4), which
explains why depth 1 is special.

## 3. The one linear structure that does exist (backward foot chain) [Proof, elementary]

Build the tower from the crossing point backwards. Take a K-point P on the partner (a circle has the rational parametrisation
by m_0 in K, no square roots). Layer j: the foot is `Phi = P - d_j u`, `u` a unit K-vector (rational in m_j in K, again
no square roots), and `Phi = A_j P'` with the tangent of `A_j C_{j-1}` at `Phi` equal to `J u`. Pulling back by `A_j^{-1}`
gives a K-point and a K-tangent vector `v = A_j^{-1} J u` of C_{j-1}. All of this is free (n dimensions per layer) and
the layer parameters enter **linearly**: `d_j` linearly in `Phi`; the shape of `A_j` through the three entries of
`B = A_j^{-T} A_j^{-1}`, which appear linearly in `|v|^2 = u^T B u`. The next layer needs the unit normal `J v / |v|`, i.e.
**`|v|^2` must be a square in K**: `s^2 = u(m)^T B u(m)`, a quartic in m, a curve of genus 1 unless B is a multiple of I
(the conformal case, where the layer merges with its neighbour and gives no parameter). For a fixed m, s in K the condition
is F-linear in the three entries of B: "four K-elements `s^2 (1+m^2)^2, (1-m^2)^2, 4m(1-m^2), 4m^2` are F-dependent",
n - 3 conditions on (m, s) in K^2 (2n dimensions). At the base the curve (a conic) is interpolated through the K-point and the
K-tangent: linear in the six conic coefficients.

This reproduces r = 3j + 4 as a count (per layer: 2n K-dimensions, n equations) and shows which parameters are cheap
(linear in F given K-data: d, B) and which are not (the K-data themselves, constrained by one genus-1 square condition per
non-conformal layer). **A sequential construction would need the K-points of those genus-1 curves**; they are not
parametrised unless the curve has extra structure, e.g. a rational 2-torsion, but a positive definite B (real A) has
four complex branch points, so no rational root. Scoped: this is the obstruction of the backward-chain route, and it would
be relaxed by any family of A_j for which `u^T B u` is a square times a conic.

## 4. Fibres: what is and is not low-degree

- *Over tau, parameters:* n equations (K-valued) on the M = r parameters, degrees 8 in d at j = 1 and growing with j
  (offset degree 4n - 2 for a degree-n parametrisation). No fibration into degree-< n pieces seen. [Heuristic]
- *Over p, roots:* the n-subsets of the D crossing roots; generic p has Galois group S_D (monodromy of the tower, expected
  from the genus-0 case, `Gal` of the sextic crossing of the parabola offset is S_6 at random points [not computed here]).
  By H1 (index argument, [Proof]) rational p must lie on a reducibility locus. For **F-valued** p the same argument
  needs p on the locus where C_p has a degree-n factor with the right field, which is I_K itself.
- *Reducibility strata that are cheap:* symmetric towers (axis-symmetric A_k, axis-symmetric partner) give crossing
  polynomials G(x^2): D halves, r drops. Partners through known points of the tower (N4-2 trick) lower D by 1 and r by 1.
  [Heuristic, not measured.]
- *Lemma A.* It applies to W of degree < n with a smooth real point. I_K has degree >= 42-ish already at n = 6, j = 1
  (point 5 of the summary), so Lemma A does not apply to I_K directly. Whether I_K contains low-degree subvarieties of
  dimension >= 1 (rational curves, the linear strata of section 3) is the open question; section 3 gives rational
  families only for j <= 1.

## 5. The n = 10 test in detail

- q = prod_{a in {0,1,3,6,10,15,21,28,36,45}} (x - a) + 7x + 3. [Wolfram] irreducible over Q, 10 real roots, discriminant
  squarefree factor structure checked at primes 37, 97, 107 (type (10)) and 73 (type (1,2,7)). The 10-cycle is odd, the square
  of the (1,2,7) element is a 7-cycle fixing three points, and a transitive group containing a prime cycle of length
  p <= n - 3 is primitive (Jordan), so Gal(q) = S_10. [Proof from the Wolfram cycle types.] (The first candidate
  `prod(x - i) - 1` was rejected: it is even in x - 5.5, hence imprimitive; noted so nobody reuses it.)
- Depth-2 circle tower (M = 10 effective parameters, 16 formal): random search found parameters with 20 real crossing points,
  so a totally real decic can be matched crossing-by-crossing over R (point 3).
- Real continuation: fixed the 10 spread crossings, moved their x-values linearly to (a) a degree-3 integer polynomial in beta,
  rescaled, (b) 10 equally spaced values (an interpolating tau); Gauss-Newton with pseudo-inverse. Both stalled at
  lambda = 0.07-0.09. Interpretation [Heuristic]: the real image of (parameters) -> (x-tuple) is a proper semialgebraic subset,
  with a boundary where two crossings merge, and sorted targets that are too spread out leave it; not tested further.
- Nothing here searches F or K arithmetically; there is no numerical way to detect "p in F_{<10}" without the structure of
  section 3. The test therefore shows: real data exist, the count is exactly met (M = n), the cost is the K-point of a genus-1
  curve with a conic condition and one more genus-1 square condition at the second layer.

## 6. What would make the tower route work (route-scoped wish list)

1. A family of non-conformal layer maps A_j (2 free shape parameters each) for which `|v|^2` is a square on a rational
   curve; this would give back rational K-point parametrisations at every depth. [Open] (conformal maps are the only ones
   found, and they merge layers).
2. Genus-1/5/17 curves with explicit K-point supply (e.g. a rational isogeny structure) so that Weil-restricted families are
   unirational. [Open]
3. A partner that cancels a layer's square-root condition, e.g. crossing with the **previous layer's curve** or with a
   circle through the *circular points of the offset*: the circular points are already singular on every offset (G2), so
   one extra layer might be carried by the partner. Not measured. [Open]
4. A measurement of the number N of parameter vectors per generic tau at r = n (j = 1, n = 5; j = 2, n = 10): the Groebner
   attempt timed out; a numerical homotopy count would do. [Open]

## Other things noticed

- The circle rank 3j + 4 exceeds the line rank 3j + 2 by exactly 2 at every depth, which is the group fixing a line
  pointwise (2 dimensions) that the line partner wastes: a partner with trivial pointwise stabiliser is strictly better.
- At j = 1 the circle partner has 8 real crossings but the crossing degree is about 10-12, again no Descartes limit.
- The line crossing polynomial of the parabola offset has leading coefficient `4194304 d^2 (1 + s^2)` and is genuinely
  degree 6 with no spurious factor for random rational (d, s, g) [Wolfram]; the spurious factor G2 saw at j = 2 appears in the
  resultant route, not in the root-by-root route used here.
- The x-values of crossings often come in coincident triples in the grid search (e.g. x = -0.80467 three times at the same
  parameters): sheets of the curve meet at self-intersections; counting such points once, the circle j = 2 tower has ~15-17
  distinct real crossing points, still > 10.
- A first q for the test (`prod(x - i) - 1`) is even about its centre and so has Galois group inside S_2 wr S_5. Anyone
  testing "S_n" claims with product-minus-constant polynomials should centre-check them.
