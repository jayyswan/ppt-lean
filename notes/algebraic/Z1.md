# Z1: additive or sweeping PowerPoint operations for (BD)

Stream Z1 of `pptc/PLAN-algebraic.md`. Research only: no `.lean` edits, no commits, no pushes.

- **Labels:** [Measured], [Wolfram], [Proof], [Heuristic], [Open].
- **Backends:** Python (win32com, SVG/XML parsing, numpy) for driving and read-out. Wolfram
  (`WolframLanguageEvaluator`) for the symbolic checks. Scripts are in the session scratchpad `Z1\`
  (c1.py, c2.py, m1b.py, m2.py, mg2.py, cn.py, o7.py).
- **Scope:** every negative below is scoped to the operation tested. None is a statement about the
  target.
- **Construction rule:** COM was only used to observe (data edits and exports). Nothing here is a
  construction. The one thing I tried to drive by keyboard (the equation editor) failed (see §3).

## 0. Findings summary

**No (BD) family found.** Each tested operation falls into one of three classes.

1. **Local support.** Parameters are many, but each crossing sees a bounded number of them.
   - Smoothed scatter or line charts are uniform Catmull-Rom splines. Each Bézier segment depends on
     exactly 4 data points.
   - Radar (filled or not) and area charts are polylines.
   - Curved connectors and Merge Shapes are also local or boolean (§4).
2. **Globally coupled but of fixed degree.** Polynomial trendlines are least-squares fits. Their
   coefficients are linear in the data, but the order is capped at 6. So there are 7 parameters, not
   M → ∞.
3. **Not vector, or not exposed.**
   - Surface and contour charts: raster in the SVG export.
   - Morph: no intermediate frame in any object-model or export channel I could reach.
   - 3-D models and the equation editor: not measured (§3).

**Smoothed line: (BD)-shaped locally, not unbounded [Measured + Wolfram].**
- The family is a cubic with coefficients linear in 4 data values, so M = 4 (8 if x is also a
  datum). That is just `cubic_bezier` with P control points, already a constructor.
- Perturbing one datum changed exactly 4 Bézier segments, k−2..k+1, and left all the others
  unchanged to 10⁻³ data units (§1.1).

**Chart data cannot carry P-numbers [Heuristic, from P3b].**
- Even a globally coupled chart family would only see parameters from the fill-handle closure:
  products of rational powers of the default cells, not arbitrary P-numbers.
- I found no UI channel that moves a value from drawn geometry into a chart cell (P3b's rule: typed
  numbers do not count).
- The (BD) hypothesis needs P-valued parameters. So this is a second, independent reason the chart
  routes do not give (BD) as they stand.

**Possible small by-product [Open; not proposed].** A polynomial trendline of order 6 through 7 chart
points is the interpolating sextic. Its coefficients are `m·y` with `m` a rational matrix
(denominators dividing 720 [Wolfram]). That is a sextic graph with algebraic, data-determined
coefficients, but the data are fill-handle-only (above), so it is not the arbitrary-coefficient
sextic. I did not check whether this is already derivable from `poly_graph` plus the closure ops.

## 1. Charts

### 1.1 Smoothed scatter or line: local Catmull-Rom [Measured, SVG export; Wolfram]

Setup: scatter-with-smooth-lines chart (type 73), 12 points x = 0..11, axes fixed. Export SVG of the
chart and parse the series path.

- **Formula.** Segment i has control points P_i + (P_{i+1} − P_{i−1})/6 and
  P_{i+1} − (P_{i+2} − P_i)/6. Fit residual over all 33 path points: 0.0103 data units = 0.7 px
  (same as P3).
- **Locality.** Change y_6 from 2 to 4.5 and re-export. Control points move only in segments 4, 5, 6
  and 7 (largest shifts 0.42 and 2.50 units). Every other control point moves by ≤ 0.001.
- **Wolfram.** The segment is the cubic

  B(t) = b(1 − 5t²/2 + 3t³/2) + c(t/2 + 2t² − 3t³/2) + a(−t/2 + t² − t³/2) + d(−t²/2 + t³/2),

  with a, b, c, d four consecutive data points. The dependence is linear in 4 inputs.
- **Crossing parametrisation (if one wanted it).** A crossing in segment i with a graph curve
  y = g(x) is the condition g(B_x(t)) = B_y(t), cubic in t and linear in (a, b, c, d) when g is a
  line. That is bounded M = 4 and degree 3, no gain over `cubic_bezier`.
- **Number of points allowed.** The sheet takes any N; the constraint is locality, not N.
- **Idealised?** Yes (a Catmull-Rom Bézier chain), but it adds nothing at a fixed segment width.
- **Global functionals** of the chain (total arc length, area) sum over the N segments. They are
  integrals of square roots, not algebraic crossing conditions, so they are not (BD) [Heuristic].

### 1.2 Polynomial trendline: global but capped [Measured]

- `Trendlines.Add(3).Order` = 7, 8 or 10 is rejected; 6 is accepted. Moving-average periods ≤ n−1
  are accepted (n = 10: 9 ok, 10 rejected) and give a local polyline.
- So a polynomial trendline is a degree-≤6 graph whose coefficients are a linear function of the data
  (least squares `(XᵀX)⁻¹Xᵀy`, a rational matrix when the x are integers). That is the model case of
  (BD) with linear entry, but M = 7, not M → ∞. The degree cap is the obstruction.
- Several trendlines on one series give several separate curves, not a sum.
- A log-x axis gives p(2^u), still 7 parameters (P3).

### 1.3 Radar, area, surface, contour, wireframe [Measured, SVG export]

| Type | Output geometry | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Radar, radar with markers | polylines (`L` segments only) | vertices r_k(cos θ_k, sin θ_k) with θ_k = 2πk/N: linear in data but local (2 data per edge). No smoothing option. |
| Filled radar, area | filled polylines | same |
| Surface, wireframe surface, contour, wireframe contour | axes plus **one raster image** | not vector |

Stacked line or area charts add series values pointwise before smoothing (P3). That is sampling then
a local spline, not a sum of curves.

### 1.4 Other chart parts (not measured)

Error bars, up/down bars and high-low lines are straight segments whose ends are data, so local
[Heuristic]. The newer statistical chart types (box, histogram, Pareto, funnel) compute statistics:
quartiles are linear in order statistics and histogram auto-binning uses data-dependent widths. They
add algebraic numbers, not curve families [Heuristic]. P3 already catalogued SVG exports of those
types.

## 2. Morph [Measured]

- COM: `SlideShowTransition.EntryEffect` = 3954 / 3955 / 3956 writes
  `<p159:morph option="byObject|byWord|byChar"/>` to the slide XML. Morph is a transition between two
  stored slides.
- I ran the slide show windowed with a 6 s Morph (a 4-node Bézier to a different 4-node Bézier, same
  shape name) and polled the running slide's object model every 0.8 s. The nodes, `Left`, `Top`,
  `Width` and `Height` were the **final** slide's values at every poll. `Slide.Export` also gives
  slide state.
- So no vector intermediate frame is exposed by the object model or by export. Observing one would
  need a raster capture or video, which does not count.
- **What Morph interpolates.** Not observed [Open]. If it interpolates matched nodes linearly, the
  frame would be (1−t)A + tB, exactly what (BD) wants. But a frame cannot be frozen as vector, and t
  cannot be set to a chosen value (the transition runs on a clock).
- **Node-count mismatch.** Not tested [Open].
- **Idealisation.** An idealised "Morph at fraction t" would be an affine map on matched Bézier
  control points. That would be a new constructor, not a UI one.

## 4. Connectors and Merge Shapes [Measured]

- **Curved connectors.** Between two shapes (BeginConnect/EndConnect), COM gives `curvedConnector3`,
  `curvedConnector4` and `curvedConnector5` with 1, 2 and 3 adjustment values. The preset is a fixed
  Bézier chain from the endpoints and those ≤ 3 numbers, and each connector attaches to only two
  shapes (local).
- **Merge Shapes.** Union of two ellipses, read from the saved XML: 6 cubic Béziers and 4 short
  `lnTo` stitches. Every anchor is within 0.07 pt of one of the two original ellipses (most are
  0.000; the stitch ends at the intersections are 0.02–0.07). So the merged boundary lies on the
  constituents' boundaries, and a crossing with it is a crossing with an original curve, up to the
  stitching. A union of offsets is only a union (consistent with Y1c). Measured on one pair of
  ellipses; the general case is a Boolean-operation fact [Proof sketch].

## 5. What would give (BD) here

Nothing found. The shape it would take, in terms of the above:

- A **global** coupling is needed (a fit, or an additive sum over all N parameters) with **degree not
  capped**. Polynomial trendlines have the coupling but are capped at 6; splines and polygon charts
  have unbounded N but are local.
- The **parameters must also be P-valued**, which requires a channel from drawn geometry into the
  data. The only reachable parameter values are the fill-handle closure (P3b).
- If a P-valued channel existed, the order-6 trendline would give an arbitrary-P-coefficient sextic
  graph (7 parameters, linear). A crossing with a line at x = τ would be the single linear equation
  Σ c_j τ^j = c_0, as in the plan's model case. That reaches degree n ≤ 6 only.

## 6. Other things noticed

- **Chart export flakiness.** `Chart.Export("SVG")` fails on a *second* new chart on a fresh slide of
  the same presentation (no gridlines found). Editing the first chart's sheet in place and
  re-exporting works, but the new values must be read back before the workbook is closed or the
  refresh does not show.
- **Text warps** (warp notes) map a stroke to (1−y)·T(x) + y·B(x), a linear combination of two guide
  curves. The weight y is set by glyph geometry (one parameter per point), so it is not a
  many-parameter family either.
- **Radar angles** are 2πk/N for any N the sheet allows. cos(2π/N) is already constructible, so no
  new points.
- **Leftover presentations.** `Presentation10/14/17/21/23/41` exist in the session. They are not
  mine and I left them alone.
