## 0. Findings summary

1. **Arc length on an offset tower is a closed formula, and it has no hyperelliptic content beyond the base
   curve [Proof + Wolfram].** For a base `gamma0(t)`, with `Q_k(t) = |M_k gamma0'(t)|^2` (`M_k = A_k...A_1`
   the cumulative linear map, offsets `d_i`, `e_i = d_i det M_i`, `W = x'y'' - y'x''` of the base), the speed
   of stage k (after its offset) is
   `sigma_k = sqrt(Q_k) * (1 - sum_{i<k} e_i W / Q_i^(3/2)) - e_k W / Q_k` (absolute value).
   Verified against direct symbolic differentiation of the nested offset curves to 40 digits for depth 2 and
   depth 3 (parabola base, non-conformal integer matrices, distinct `d_i`). So every tower arc length is
   - the arc length of the **linear image `M_k gamma0`** of the base (a base-curve integral, H4 section 1.2),
   - plus `sum_i e_i * Int W sqrt(Q_k Q_i)/Q_i^2 dt`, an integral on the curve `z^2 = Q_k Q_i` (two forms only),
   - plus the elementary term `e_k * (angle)`.
   The genus `~2^j` of the tower curve (G2) is a property of the curve, not of its length differential:
   the Jacobian of the (Z/2)^j-cover splits and the length sees only the pieces `z^2 = Q_k Q_i`, `i < k`.
   For the parabola (G2: genus 0,1,5,17) these are elliptic, and the genus-2 factor `z^2 = Q_0 Q_1 Q_2` with six
   free branch points never appears. **Answer to 1(a): arc lengths on offset towers never give a period of a
   curve with more than 5 free branch points.** Scoped to `arc_length`/`arc_of_length` on tower curves.
2. **Inverse periods (1(b)) are blocked for a structural reason stronger than H4's [Proof, mod Kovacic].**
   `arc_of_length` + `box` invert `t -> L(t)` in the *position*. Moduli (the branch points, `c` in `K'(c)/K(c)`)
   are *parameters*. To invert `r = K'/K` the position along some drawn curve would have to be `c` and the
   speed `pi/(4c(1-c)K(c)^2)`; every drawn curve is algebraic or elementary, and `1/K^2` is not Liouvillian
   (monodromy of the Legendre equation is Zariski dense in SL2). Equivalent form: the inverse of an abelian
   integral of an algebraic differential satisfies a first-order algebraic ODE `t'^2 = P(t)`, whereas
   `lambda(tau)` does not satisfy any first-order ODE over elementary functions (`lambda' = i pi lambda(1-lambda) theta_3^4`
   with `theta_3^4 = (2K/pi)^2` non-elementary in `lambda`).
3. **Siegel theta constants (1(c)):** five separate missing capabilities, listed in section 1.4. The
   Thomae-Umemura formulas are *forward* (algebraic) once the period matrix is known; the missing pieces are
   (i) a drawable differential on a genus >= 2 curve with free branch set (excluded by finding 1), (ii) a genus-g
   period determinant (`theta^4 = +-(det A)^2 prod(e_i - e_j)/pi^{2g}`-type), (iii) periods computed from
   *coefficients* rather than from known roots.
4. **Ritt/Lattes (2):** the equations that become linear under an available transcendental change of variable are
   exactly the power, Chebyshev and Lattes maps (Ritt 1923, Eremenko). Class of algebraic numbers reached and
   its Galois groups: section 2.3. The new fact is that **complex radicals are free in P** (`cos_Pconstructible`,
   `sin_Pconstructible` are in `Basic.lean`), so every real algebraic number with a solvable Galois closure is in
   P and `P cap R_alg` strictly contains the bounded-degree tower `F_0` (`cos(2 pi/23)` has prime degree 11).
   Non-solvable sections from this mechanism are only `A_5` and `PSL(2,p)`: no `A_n`, `n >= 6`.
5. **Transcendental crossings (3):** with algebraic parameters, a crossing of `sine` with an algebraic curve has
   transcendental abscissa (Lindemann-Weierstrass) and a crossing of `exp_two` has abscissa rational or
   transcendental (Gelfond-Schneider) [Proof]. So the pattern "x = f(root of a transcendental equation whose
   parameters come from q)" cannot produce an algebraic output at all when the parameters are algebraic. The
   (infinite) monodromy of the root function contains every finitary permutation group and therefore every `A_n`,
   but that monodromy is never *realised* at an algebraic specialisation. For transcendental parameters the
   argument needs Schanuel's conjecture (open), and works unconditionally only in transcendence degree 1
   (section 3.3).
6. **Framing correction.** The task says an `A_n`-bearing number must pass through a transcendental step. More
   precisely it must pass through a transcendental step *or an unbounded-degree algebraic engine*: `power_law n`
   against a line gives the trinomial `x^n + ax + b` with monodromy `S_n` (G1 R7). The filter of H4 section 5 is about
   towers of steps of degree <= 8. What a transcendental step would add is not `A_n` (engines already carry it) but
   **many free parameters**, which is the bookkeeping law's missing resource. Section 3.5 argues (Pila-Wilkie)
   that the transcendental part of a definable family cannot supply them.
7. **Numerical tests (section 4).** Control: `x^5 + 20x + 16` has square discriminant `32000^2` and Frobenius
   statistics of `A_5` [Wolfram]; the Lattes equation `x(5Q) = x(P)` on `y^2 = x^3 - x + 1`, `P = (1,1)`, is
   irreducible of degree 25 [Wolfram] (a primitive group, not decomposable into bounded steps, but not P-reached
   by any current theorem). A depth-2 tower inversion `L(t*) = 1` gives `t* = 0.23404733...` with no integer
   relation of degree <= 8 and height ~10^6 [Wolfram, Heuristic].

## 1. Inverse periods via offset towers

### 1.1 The tower length formula [Proof, Wolfram]

Notation. `gamma0` base (any algebraic drawable curve, parametrised by t), `v_k = M_k gamma0'`, `Q_k = |v_k|^2`,
`psi_k` the direction angle of `v_k`, `psi_k' = det(M_k) W / Q_k`.

- Parallel tangent lemma (G2): the offset of a curve with tangent `v` has tangent `f v`, `f = 1 - d psi'/|v|`.
  Offsetting preserves the direction angle; a linear map `A` sends the direction to that of `A v`, and scales the
  speed by `|A e|`, `e` the unit tangent.
- Hence, with `sigma_k` the speed after the k-th offset, `sigma_k / sqrt(Q_k) = sigma_{k-1}/sqrt(Q_{k-1}) - d_{k-1}psi'_{k-1}/sqrt(Q_{k-1})`, and
  unrolling gives the formula of finding 1. Sign flips of `f` (cusps) only change absolute values.
- Wolfram check: direct `sqrt(g2'.g2')` of the nested symbolic offset curve vs the formula at `t = 37/100`:
  depth 2 (`A1 = [[1,0],[1,2]]`, `A2 = [[3,1],[0,1]]`, `d = 1/10, 1/7`): both 5.841831095027116682862339063068701925365...,
  difference 0 to 38 digits; depth 3 (extra `A3 = [[2,-1],[1,1]]`, `d3 = 1/20`): both 11.28241218779188678680300498970573502527...
  (one run had the product order of the `M_k` reversed and mismatched, the corrected order matches).

### 1.2 What kinds of integral occur

- Term `Int sqrt(Q_k) dt` is the arc length of the affine image `M_k gamma0`. For a drawn base this is H4 section 1.2:
  graph of degree d: `z^2 = C(f'-e)^2 + D`, a curve of bidegree `(d-1, 2)` in `(t, s)` (the fibre product of the
  rational map `f'` with a conic), genus <= 4, branch set = the roots of `f'(t) = e +- i w`, i.e. **two fibres of a fixed
  quintic map**. Free P parameters `(e, w)`; the branch points are *not* free. Power laws: abelian/CM. Bezier: genus 1.
- Terms with `W dt`: `W dt = du` with `u = y'/x'` the tangent slope (Gauss map), so
  `Int W sqrt(Q_k Q_i)/Q_i^2 dt = Int sqrt(q_k(u) q_i(u))/q_i(u)^2 du`, with `q` the binary forms in `u`.
  This is an **elliptic integral in u, for every base curve**: four branch points `rho_k, rho_k-bar, rho_i, rho_i-bar`,
  free (two P-parameters per layer), with *real* cross-ratio `|rho_i - rho_k|^2 / |rho_i - rho_k-bar|^2`, which is the
  Legendre parameter `c` already in the repo.
- Elementary part: `Delta psi` (arctan).
- Conclusion [Proof]: the Jacobians seen by tower lengths are products of elliptic curves with a family of dimension
  <= 4 whose Weierstrass set sits in two fibres of a degree <= 5 map. In the 2-torsion field the group is in
  `S_5 wr C_2` (H4). Compositum over several `(e_i, w_i)`: the splitting fields of `f'(t) = z_i` for independent
  `z_i` give `S_5^m`: still only `A_5` sections.
- Dimension remark [Heuristic]: genus 4 would be enough to host `S_10 > A_9, A_10` in `Sp(8, F_2)`; the obstruction is
  not the genus but that the branch family has 2 moduli, not `2g - 1 = 7`.

### 1.3 Inversion

The tower inversion solves `L(t*) = x` with `L` the above sum on a fixed curve X (parameters fixed).

- **Torsion mechanism.** If `X` is itself elliptic and `x` is a rational multiple of a period, `t*` is a torsion point,
  algebraic with group in `GL(2, Z/N)` (H4). For a tower with >= 2 distinct elliptic pieces `E_ki` the length is a linear
  functional `phi` on `A = prod E_ki` evaluated on the image of X in A. `{phi = x}` is a codimension-1 analytic coset; it
  meets the curve X in isolated points that are torsion in A only finitely often (Manin-Mumford/Raynaud when X has
  genus >= 2), generically never. So torsion no longer pins `t*`. [Heuristic, Open to make precise.]
- **Algebraic parameters, algebraic x.** `L(t*)` for algebraic `t*` and algebraic parameters is a nonzero combination of
  elliptic logarithms, quasi-periods and logarithms (first, second, third kind). By Wuestholz's analytic subgroup theorem
  [Cited: G. Wuestholz, Ann. of Math. 129 (1989), 501-517; generalises Baker; confirmed bibliographically by search]
  such a combination is transcendental unless the algebraic-group data forces a relation. Taking it as the 'logarithm of a
  point of a commutative algebraic group over Q-bar', nonvanishing implies transcendence. So **algebraic data cannot
  give `L(t*) = x` with both `t*` and `x` algebraic** except for degenerate cases [Cited theorem, application Heuristic:
  the group (extension of the product of elliptic curves by G_m^k) was not written out]. Transcendental `x` is therefore
  needed, and then `t*` is algebraic only at the special points (torsion).
- **Numerics.** Depth 2 over the parabola with the matrices above, `x = 1`: `t* = 0.23404733038795790558791914164621970633...`
  (90-digit solve). `LatticeReduce` on `(1, t*, ..., t*^d)` at scale 10^60 for `d = 2..8` returns shortest vectors with
  height 5e19, 5e14, 5e11, 5e9, 2e8, 2e7, 2e6 and residuals not small (e.g. d = 8: height 2.4e6, residual 2.6e5): no
  algebraic relation of degree <= 8 and height <~10^6 [Wolfram, Heuristic].

### 1.4 What a period-inversion-to-moduli (Siegel/Umemura) route would need

For the general equation `f` of degree n (Umemura, appendix to Mumford, *Tata Lectures on Theta II*, Birkhaeuser 1984,
pp. 261-272, via Thomae 1870; as cited in G1/H4, not re-read here):

| needed | status in P |
|---|---|
| (a) a drawable differential `x^j dx / y` on `y^2 = F(x)`, genus >= 2, free branch set | none: finding 1 (towers give elliptic pieces; base term has 2 fibre-values of a quintic) |
| (b) integrals between *simple real roots* | none: `speed^2` is a sum of two squares, real zeros have even order (H4) |
| (c) period matrix `Omega` computed from coefficients, not roots (equivalently the inverse of the Igusa-Clebsch/Rosenhain map `Omega -> invariants`) | none; needs Picard-Fuchs continuation, an ODE solver |
| (d) `theta[m](0, Omega) = sum exp(pi i n^T Omega n)` | genus 1 only, via `theta^2 = 2K{...}/pi`; genus g is `(det A)`-type period determinant |
| (e) root from theta by Thomae | algebraic, fine once (d) |

(a)-(c) fail structurally for towers; (d), (e) are not constructors. Note that for g = 1 the forward direction (roots known,
get `tau`) is available; Umemura's value comes from running it with unknown roots, which is (c).

## 2. Ritt / Lattes scope

### 2.1 Classification [Cited]

- J. F. Ritt, *Permutable rational functions*, Trans. AMS 25 (1923), 399-448 (one source gives 398-448; DOI
  10.1090/s0002-9947-1923-1501252-3): rational maps of degree >= 2 that commute and share no iterate are, up to
  Mobius conjugacy, power maps, Chebyshev polynomials, or Lattes maps (finite quotients of affine maps of a group variety).
  Eremenko (1989, Algebra i Analiz 1; Leningrad Math. J. 1 (1990) 905-919; recalled, not re-opened) gives the orbifold
  proof: covers of the orbifolds with Euler characteristic 0 (`C^*`, the interval, the torus mod automorphisms).
  Secondary sources confirm: Pakovich, arXiv:1108.1900 ("On semiconjugate rational functions") and arXiv:1604.04771.
- J. F. Ritt, *Prime and composite polynomials*, Trans. AMS 23 (1922), 51-66 (recalled): decomposition theory; the only
  non-trivial solutions of `A o B = C o D` with coprime degrees are `z^n o z^s h(z^n)` and `T_m o T_n`.
- Milnor, *On Lattes maps* (arXiv math/0402147; recalled): Lattes maps are exactly maps semiconjugate to an affine map of a
  torus.

### 2.2 Conjugacies available in P

| change of variable | in P | maps linearised | equation `phi(y) = a` |
|---|---|---|---|
| `y = e^z` (`exp_two`, `exp_one_Pconstructible`) | yes | `z^n` ~ `n z` | `y^n = a`: radicals (already `power_law`) |
| `y = cos z` (`cos_Pconstructible`, `arccos_Pconstructible_of_mem_Icc`) | yes, arbitrary P angle | `T_n` ~ `n z` | `T_n(y) = a`: `y = cos((arccos a + 2 pi k)/n)` |
| `y = wp(z)` / `sn(z)` | only `z = F(phi)` and dyadic multiples (`JacobiHalving`, `JacobiAddition`); odd division of a generic point is a degree n^2 algebraic step | Lattes `R_n` ~ `n z` on `E` | `R_n(y) = a`, degree `n^2` |
| Poincare function of a generic polynomial | no (a limit, not an elementary or abelian function) | any polynomial | not available |

Reading: a polynomial or rational equation becomes linear under a P-available transcendental change of variable iff it is
conjugate to `z^n`, `+-T_n` or a Lattes map. [Cited Ritt/Eremenko for the 'only if' direction among maps with a nontrivial
commuting partner; the general statement "semiconjugate to a group-variety endomorphism" is the Lattes/Medvedev-Scanlon
class, not verified here.]

### 2.3 The class of algebraic numbers and its Galois groups

Let `F_1` be the smallest real field containing Q, closed under real roots of degree <= 8 polynomials (current `F_0`),
under `cos`, `sin` of its elements' real-closure angles, and under `n`-th roots of complex elements. Then:

- **Complex radicals are free [Proof, Lean-supported]**: `cos_Pconstructible`, `sin_Pconstructible` (`Basic.lean:2652,2657`) and
  `arccos_Pconstructible_of_mem_Icc` make `P(i)` closed under `z -> z^{1/n}` (`|z|^{1/n}(cos(theta/n) + i sin(theta/n))`). So every
  real algebraic number in a radical tower over Q is P-constructible: **all real algebraic numbers with solvable Galois closure
  are in P**. This is a proposed Lean corollary (nothing in the repo states it; `rg` for "solvable/radical" found only the Hasse-Minkowski
  file). It also settles the casus irreducibilis and degree-9 primitive solvable groups that no real degree-<=8 chain reaches.
- `P cap R_alg` is strictly larger than `F_0` [Proof]: `cos(2 pi/23)` has degree 11, and every number in `F_0` lies in a field of
  degree a product of integers <= 8, hence prime to 11.
- **Power, Chebyshev**: Galois groups of `y^n = a`, `T_n(y) = a` over `k(zeta_n)` are cyclic; over `k`, subgroups of
  `C_n x| (Z/n)^x`, `D_n`: solvable.
- **Lattes** on `E/k`: splitting field of `R_n(y) - a` is `k(E[n], (1/n)P)`, group `<= (Z/n)^2 x| GL(2, Z/n)/{+-1}`
  [Proof, standard]. Generic (non-CM, Serre) it is the full group, whose composition factors are cyclic, `A_5` (p = 5), and
  `PSL(2,p)` for each prime `p | n`, `p >= 5` [Dickson, as used in H4 section 2.2]. CM curves: metabelian.
  Isogenies and Lattes maps from other endomorphisms add nothing new.
- **Exact class**: groups with composition factors in `{C_p} U {A_5} U {PSL(2,p) : p >= 7 prime} U (composition factors of F_0)`,
  and `PSL(2,p)` only for those p whose division is reachable (p <= 7 now; p = 11 is H4's open level). **No `A_n`, n >= 6, no `PSL(2,8)`, `PSL(2,9)`
  from this mechanism, whatever n** [Proof modulo Dickson; same ceiling as H4 finding 4].
- Iterated compositions: wreath products of the above; factors unchanged.

Scoped statement: the Ritt/Lattes mechanism enlarges P's algebraic part (all of the solvable ones, plus `PSL(2,p)` division values
as they become reachable) but cannot supply `A_n` for n >= 6.

## 3. Monodromy of transcendental crossings

### 3.1 Algebraic parameters: exact transcendence [Proof]

- `sin x = g(x)` with `g` algebraic over Q-bar, `x` algebraic nonzero: `sin x` algebraic, so `w = e^{ix}` satisfies
  `w^2 - 2i(sin x) w - 1 = 0`, hence is algebraic, contradicting Lindemann-Weierstrass (Hermite-Lindemann). So
  **every nonzero crossing abscissa of `sine` with an algebraic curve over Q-bar is transcendental.** Same for the
  bifurcation curve `{(a,b) : exists x, sin x = ax + b, cos x = a}`: `(a,b)` algebraic forces `x = (sin x - b)/a` algebraic,
  so it contains no algebraic point except the trivial one. Hence the 'monodromy at the tangent crossings' is never realised
  at algebraic parameters.
- `2^x = p(x)`, `p` algebraic: `x` algebraic irrational impossible by Gelfond-Schneider (`2^x` transcendental), so
  solutions are rational or transcendental.
- Crossings of `sine`/`exp_two` with Beziers and power laws: the same, because `y = B(x)` is algebraic.

### 3.2 Monodromy as functions of P-parameters [Proof sketch]

The roots of `sin x = a x + b` (and `2^x = p(x)`, `sin x = a x^b`) as functions of the parameters have infinitely many branch points
(critical values `b = sin x0 - x0 cos x0, a = cos x0`, one per critical point `x0`), each a simple transposition of two roots.
The monodromy group acts on the infinite root set transitively and generically contains the group of finitary permutations,
hence has every `A_n` as a section [Heuristic for 'contains all finitary permutations'; the transposition at each simple branch point
is [Proof]]. So 'monodromy contains `A_n`' is cheap. It is not a resource: by 3.1 it cannot be realised at algebraic parameters.

### 3.3 A specialization argument: where it works and where it fails

Setting: a derivation produces numbers `n_1, ..., n_k` by P-operations; the final output `x` is algebraic.

- **Works [Proof]:** algebraic parameters, any exp-polynomial/sin equation in one variable (3.1).
- **Works [Proof]: transcendence degree 1.** If all transcendental inputs lie in `Q-bar(t)^alg` (one transcendental `t`) and the output is
  an algebraic function `f(t)` of `t` with algebraic coefficients, then `f(t)` algebraic and `t` transcendental forces `f` constant. So
  the transcendental seed 'cancels' and the Galois group is that of the algebraic skeleton, not of the transcendental step.
- **Works as a statement about functions:** Khovanskii's topological Galois theory [Cited: A. Khovanskii, *Topological Galois Theory*,
  Springer 2014; the 2019 arXiv summary states: the monodromy group of a function representable by quadratures is solvable, and functions
  representable by k-quadratures have 'k-solvable' monodromy]. P's steps are exactly: arithmetic, exp/log/trig (quadratures),
  algebraic equations of degree <= 8 (k-quadratures with k = 8) plus *inversions of abelian integrals* (`arc_of_length` + `box`). The
  first three fall inside Khovanskii's class: an algebraic *function* of the parameters built from them has monodromy with composition
  factors cyclic or in `S_8`. Inversions are outside it (they are 'k-quadratures plus inverse Pfaffian functions').
- **Fails (a): constants.** Function-level monodromy bounds the geometric group. The arithmetic group has an extra factor `Gal(k'/Q)`
  for the constant field `k'` of the Galois closure; this is the part the filter tracks, so the argument needs an induction on the
  derivation, which is what the plan's `F_{<n}` framing does.
- **Fails (b): special points.** Values of transcendental functions at special arguments are algebraic with *arithmetic* groups
  (`cos(2 pi/p)`, `sn(K/5)`, singular moduli). The Zariski closure of monodromy (differential Galois group) does not see them;
  what sees them is the discrete monodromy mod N. For `sn(K(c)/5, c)` the function of c has finite monodromy `GL(2,5)/{+-1}` although the
  Picard-Fuchs group is Zariski dense in SL2.
- **Fails (c): transcendental parameters.** Characterising the relations among values `(t, e^t, ...)` is Schanuel's conjecture (open);
  Ax's theorem [Cited: J. Ax, Ann. of Math. 93 (1971), 252-268; recalled] is the functional version. Zilber-Pink-type statements
  would say that the algebraic outputs correspond to special subvarieties of the relevant (mixed) Shimura structure: tori for exp/sin
  (abelian groups), modular curves for elliptic (GL2/PSL(2,p)), and for tower integrals only the elliptic pieces and the quintic-fibre
  genus-4 family of 1.2. This predicts the Galois groups of section 2.3 and no `A_n`, but it is a prediction, not a theorem.
- **Fails (d): inversion.** For `arc_of_length` the unknown is a position on a curve, with parameters fixed; the output is a transcendental
  function of the length, whose algebraic values are again special points.

### 3.4 o-minimal and Pfaffian framework

`sine` (bounded window) and `exp_two` are Pfaffian; all P curves, lengths and inverses of lengths are definable in the Pfaffian/`R_an,exp`
structures (Wilkie: Pfaffian closure is o-minimal; recalled). Pila-Wilkie [Cited: J. Pila, A. J. Wilkie, Duke Math. J. 133 (2006), 591-616;
bounded-degree algebraic points: J. Pila, *On the algebraic points of a definable set*, Selecta Math. 15 (2009), 151-170, per a survey that
dates it; confirmed by search]: the number of algebraic points of degree <= k and height <= H lying on a definable set but not on a
positive-dimensional semialgebraic subset is `O(H^eps)`.

- **Application [Heuristic].** A definable engine with parameters of bounded degree k' cannot produce more than `O(H^eps)` algebraic outputs of degree
  <= 9 and height <= H on its transcendental part, while the number of degree-9 targets is ~`H^10`. So **versality has to come from the algebraic
  parts** (power law, Beziers, offsets), exactly the engines of the bookkeeping law; a transcendental step can only deliver a thin set of special
  outputs. Caveat: parameters of unbounded degree are not covered, and the count is for fixed k'.
- The algebraic part of the Lindemann example (`sin x = ax + b`) is the family of lines `b = sin x0 - a x0`, `x0` fixed; for algebraic `x0 != 0`
  the intercept is transcendental, so the lines contain no algebraic points either.

### 3.5 Is there a theorem limiting Galois groups of algebraic numbers expressible this way?

- Unconditional [Proof]: for derivations whose transcendental data are exp/sin/log of **algebraic** arguments (Lindemann-Weierstrass, Gelfond-Schneider,
  Baker), algebraic outputs from the transcendental part lie in cyclotomic/Kummer-type fields: abelian over the base. For algebraic data in one elliptic
  integral (Schneider, Wuestholz): groups in `GL(2, Z/N)`-type (H4 finding 4 plus the Dickson ceiling).
- Conditional [Heuristic]: for arbitrary P-constructible transcendental inputs, the groups are those of the special subvarieties; with the tower
  decomposition of 1.1 this is products of `GL(2,Z/N)`, the genus-4 quintic-fibre family, and tori.
- [Open]: whether the genus-4 quintic-fibre family (finding 1) has large `ell`-torsion monodromy giving *new* simple sections `PSp(8, F_ell)`; this
  would be new simple groups but not `A_n`; torsion of J(C) is also not a point of the drawn curve, so it is not a P-number without more argument.

## 4. Concrete tests

1. **Control, `A_5` quintic.** `f = x^5 + 20x + 16` [Wolfram]: disc 1024000000 = 32000^2 (square), cycle-type counts over 151 primes (11..160th prime range):
   `(1,1,3)`: 46, `(5)`: 60, `(1,2,2)`: 44, `(1^5)`: 1; this matches `A_5`'s class proportions (20/60, 24+24/60, 15/60, 1/60). So `f` is an `A_5` quintic;
   P already solves it (`root_Pconstructible_le_six_coeffs`), consistent with the A_5 ceiling of the elliptic route (the `Phi_5` sextic of H4).
2. **Lattes at a generic point.** `E: y^2 = x^3 - x + 1`, `P = (1,1)`. `x(5Q) = x - psi_4 psi_6 / psi_5^2` (division polynomials). `numer - 1*denom` is irreducible
   of degree 25 over Q [Wolfram]. By section 2.3 its group is in `(Z/5)^2 x| GL(2,5)`, the point stabiliser is maximal (it acts irreducibly on
   `(Z/5)^2`), so no bounded-degree chain reaches it: this is a degree-25 number with an `A_5` section that P does not yet have (odd division of a generic
   point) and is a concrete [Open] target, scoped to current theorems. (Contrast: the torsion point `sn(K/5)` has degree 12, H4.)
3. **Tower inversion.** 1.3 above: no low-degree algebraic `t*`.
4. **Degree 9 or 10 via a transcendental step:** no candidate survived. The routes in this note either reduce to the algebraic engines (finding 6) or to
   groups without `A_n` (finding 4). I did not find a numerical case worth testing beyond the three above.

## 5. Route scoping (what is, and is not, closed)

- Closed (routes): tower arc lengths as a source of genus >= 2 periods with free branch points; single-curve position inversion as period inversion;
  transcendental crossings with algebraic parameters as a source of algebraic numbers; Ritt/Lattes/Chebyshev as a source of `A_n`, n >= 6.
- Open: the `ell`-torsion monodromy of the genus-4 quintic-fibre family; a P-reachable odd division of a generic elliptic point (would give the
  degree-25 `A_5`-bearing numbers and the level-11 problem); extending topological Galois theory (Khovanskii) to inversion of abelian integrals; any curve
  family not generated by the constructors; whether two *different* constructions can share a hidden parameter (an arc of a tower fed into a second tower
  as the offset distance gives only 'triangular' composition, offset-inversion R4, but not fully analysed for these integrals).
