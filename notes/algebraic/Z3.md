# Z3: the cusp/extremum engine (cusps of the offset of a cubic Bezier)

Stream Z3 of `pptc/PLAN-algebraic.md`. Labels: [Proof], [Wolfram], [Heuristic], [Open]. Backend: Wolfram
for every number (no Python). Every obstruction is scoped to the route it blocks; nothing here concludes a
negative about the target.

Notation. gamma(t) = a0 + a1 t + a2 t^2 + a3 t^3 (a_i in P^2), S = |gamma'|^2 (quartic), K = x'y'' - y'x''
(**quadratic**: the t^3 terms cancel), kappa = K / S^(3/2), d in P. Cusp polynomial
**C(t) = S^3 - d^2 K^2**, degree 12. Its real roots are the cusps of Off_d (where d K > 0) and of Off_{-d}
(where d K < 0).

## Findings summary

1. **Readout exists and is legal [Proof + Wolfram].** Let O = Off_d(gamma) (whole curve, base speed S > 0
   on the arc, arc injective). Then O' = (1 - d kappa) gamma'. At a simple zero t* of 1 - d kappa with
   x'(t*) != 0 the coordinate x(O(t)) turns around, so the cusp E(t*) is a local extremum of x. After
   `restrict` to a small rational box around it (box bounds exist as rationals because the cusp is isolated
   unless O self-meets there), `box_xmax` reads it (`box_xmin` by reflection, which is free via
   `scale_x` by -1). Hypotheses: A = restrict(O, W) is a `PConstructibleCurve`, compact (closed box ∩
   compact), nonempty. Check [Wolfram]: base with 4 real roots, both cusps of the right sign have
   |O'| ~ 1e-14 at t*, x(O) has its window min/max exactly at E_x(t*) (-0.347896 and 0.612918).
   - The cusp point is the **centre of curvature**: O(t*) = E(t*) = gamma + (S/K)(-y', x') [Proof,
     since sqrt(S) = dK/S at a root]. So E_x, E_y in Q_P(t*), rational in t*.
   - Bad direction: the box direction must not be perpendicular to gamma'(t*) (there the coordinate is
     monotone through the cusp). A generic rational window direction avoids it.
   - Reparametrise t affinely so all real roots lie in (0,1); the arc must also be a loop-free cubic.
     Both are open conditions; the injectivity on the whole interval is a side condition [Open: not
     verified for the 6-root cases].
2. **Other readouts.**
   - *Intersection with a known circle* [Proof sketch]: once E = (X, Y) is read, the circle of radius d
     about E is the osculating circle of gamma at gamma(t*) (3-point contact). `restrict` to a small window
     gives S ∩ T = {gamma(t*)}, so `inter_x`/`inter_y` read the base point. This is an extra, not needed:
     t* = h(X*) with h in F[X] whenever X* generates F(t*), and P is a field [Proof].
   - *Evolute / two offsets*: the evolute is an envelope, not a drawable curve; Off_d and Off_{d'} meet
     at points unrelated to the cusp locus. No route [Heuristic].
   - *Arc length* [Proof, R0]: arc_of_length crosses a cusp legally (no nonvanishing speed needed) and
     the length is DeltaR - d Delta psi, but the endpoint readout fails across the cusp. It yields the
     cusp length as a function of t*, not t*. No new readout.
   - Verdict: the single legal readout is **box on a restricted window**; E_x (or E_y or any P-linear
     combination ell . E) is the readable number.
3. **Equation family [Proof + Wolfram].**
   - Degree 12 verified. Jacobian rank of (a0..a3, d) -> monic C(t): **5** [Wolfram, exact at a random
     integer point; also 5 without a0]. Count: 9 parameters minus translation 2, rotation 1,
     similarity scale (traded with d) 1. The family is closed under t -> alpha t + beta, so **modulo the
     affine group in t it is 3-dimensional**.
   - Readout variable X = E_x(t*) (a rotated direction ell is a similarity of the curve, so 'ell' adds
     nothing): rank **6** [Wolfram, 120-digit central differences, SVD gap > 1e57; also 6 with the angle as
     a 10th parameter]. Mod affine in X: 4.
   - Structure of C [Wolfram, mod-p weighted-degree search, 400 random points]: the top 8 coefficients
     are those of S^3, S a sum of two squares: relations of weights **5, 6, 7** among centred e2..e7; further
     generators at weights **11, 12, 13** (the Hilbert-function test deviates there); 7 relations are needed
     (codim 7) and the seventh lies above weight 22. So a direct degree-12 engine has conditions of degree
     >= 5.6.7.11.12.13 = 360360 if complete intersection [Heuristic], versus 105 for the odd route.
   - **Conditions on tau** (conditions = n - r):

     | n | direct/overshoot, t-variable (r=5) | X-readout presentation (r=6) |
     |---|---|---|
     | 12 | 7 | 6 |
     | 11 | 6 | 5 |
     | 10 | 5 | 4 |
     | 9 | 4 | 3 |

     [Heuristic on the second column]: the invariant object is the 5-dimensional family of algebras
     A_p = F[t]/C_p; existence of an F-point with a degree-n factor algebra isomorphic to K ⊗ F does not
     depend on the readout variable. The X presentation just enlarges the target locus by a rational
     fibre (H1's twisted fibre product has dimension 5 or 6). I take n - 5 as the honest count.
   - **Galois group [Proof for the instances + Wolfram].** Two random integer instances
     (-3,1,5,-3,-4,-4,1,-1 ; d=4) and (-5,-5,-3,-5,-4,4,1,5 ; d=2): irreducible over Q, and over ~100 primes
     the cycle types include 12, 11+1, 5+7, 4+8, 6+6, ... A 12-cycle is odd; a 5+7 type has a 7-cycle power
     with 7 <= 12 - 3, and an imprimitive group of degree 12 contains no element of order 7, so Jordan gives
     A12 ⊆ G. Hence **G = S12** for these instances, and the generic group of the family contains it, so
     it is **S12** (N4's 'apparently ⊇ A12' is now settled).
4. **Realness [Proof sketch + Wolfram].**
   - Real roots of C are solutions of kappa = ± 1/d. kappa' has numerator K'S - (3/2)KS' of degree <= 5,
     so kappa has <= 5 critical points and <= 6 monotone pieces, tending to 0 at ±infinity. A piece
     meets kappa = c at most once, and meets both ±c only if it contains a zero of K (<= 2 of them). So C has
     **at most 8 real roots** [Proof sketch].
   - Observed maximum **6** in ~4,000 random draws and ~40 annealed climbs [Wolfram, exact Sturm counts].
     Sign split: typically 2 + 2 or 2 + 4. 8 not found [Open whether attained].
   - Consequence, scoped to this route: a q with r real roots needs r <= 8 (proved) and in practice r <= 6.
     **Totally real q of degree >= 9 cannot be served**; n = 9 needs s >= 1 (proved), s >= 2 in practice;
     n = 10 needs s >= 1 (s >= 2 in practice); n = 12 needs s >= 2 (s >= 3). A cusp of the wrong sign is a cusp
     of Off_{-d}, also legal.
5. **Factor forcing (N4 open item) [Wolfram].** A cubic factor over F is exactly the n = 9 overshoot
   (the cofactor of an F-factor is automatically over F), and I tested explicit constructions:
   - one prescribed root t=0: d = radius of curvature at 0, in P by a square root. Always available.
   - two prescribed roots: the condition R(t1) = R(t2) is **linear** in the hodograph derivative w1, so a
     rational parametrisation exists (P-valued after sqrt).
   - three prescribed roots 0, 1, s: solve rho_i^2 = Σ_j c_ij rho_j sin(theta_j - theta_i), three quadrics;
     one positive real solution at my test point (Bezout 8, degree <= 7 over F, so in F_{<9}). The remainder
     of C divided by t(t-1)(t-s) was zero, and the degree-9 cofactor has **rank 5** with shift and scale
     (3 without, = 2 angle differences + s) [Wolfram, 70 digits]. So **forcing a cubic factor does not lower
     the count**: still 9 - 5 = 4 conditions; it only gives an explicit parametrisation of the cofactor
     family, whose dimension is the same as that of W (finite cover).
   - Special families: Pythagorean-hodograph cubics give C = sigma^2 (sigma^2 - 2cd)(sigma^2 + 2cd)
     (K = 2c sigma), i.e. a degree-4 factor repeated, but at most 2 real cusps [Proof]. A mirror-symmetric
     cubic makes C even in t and collapses it to degree 6.
6. **Comparison for n = 9 (conditions, degree of conditions, realness).**

   | route | conditions | degrees | totally real? |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | odd route | 3 | 3, 5, 7 (CI 105) | yes |
   | N2 overshoot, sextic graph | 2 | main component 210 | yes |
   | N4-1 / N4-2, Z2 Bezier × x^-2 | 2 | >= 15 to >= 28 | yes |
   | **cusp engine (this note)** | **4 (3 in X presentation)** | >= 5,6,7,11,.. [Heuristic] | **no (<= 8 real roots)** |
   | Theorem R (offset towers) | none, needs simultaneous squares | genus >= 5 | yes |

   The cusp engine is **dominated** at n = 9 on conditions, degree and realness. For n = 10, 11, 12 the
   count grows linearly (n - 5), like every measured family with bounded rank [H1]. No numerical end-to-end
   test was run on the totally real S9 nonic: the route has no totally real point at all (<= 8 real
   roots), and the count is not below the other routes, so the plan's trigger for the test was not met.
7. **Verdict, scoped.** The cusp of the offset of a cubic Bezier is a legal readout, and its polynomial has
   Galois group S12, but as an engine the family has rank 5 (6 in X presentation) against degree 12 with
   <= 8 real roots. It adds an independent, non-totally-real S_n carrier for n = 9...12 at 4...7 conditions
   of very high degree. It does not change the bookkeeping law.

## Other things noticed

- The top 8 coefficients of C are those of S^3; d and K enter only the bottom five. So the family
  'looks like' S^3 + small perturbation, and the Galois group is still S12 nonetheless.
- The squared polynomial mixes the cusps of Off_d and Off_{-d}. Using E(t) for all roots is consistent
  because sqrt(S) = dK/S is rational on the roots.
- At d = radius of curvature at any t0 the offset has a cusp at t0 for free: prescribing one cusp costs
  nothing, so the 'one rational root' overshoot (degree 11 cofactor) is trivial and has the same rank 5.
- The exact kappa-critical-point bound (5) is the degree of the 'vertex' polynomial of a cubic; a cubic
  has at most 5 vertices, which is what limits real cusps.
