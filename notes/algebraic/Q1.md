# Q1 — Lemma LS + Theorem H (solvable points on high-dimensional varieties)

Status: done (2026-11-06)

## (1) Lemma LS rigorously

**Lemma LS.** Let k be a number field, S a finite set of finite places of k, and for v ∈ S let
K_v/k_v be a finite extension. Then there is a finite extension M/k such that
- (i) every real place of k splits completely in M (so if k is totally real, M is totally real);
- (ii) for every v ∈ S and every place u | v of M, M_u contains a k_v-copy of K_v;
- (iii) M/k is a tower of cyclic extensions of prime degree (so if k ⊂ ℚ^solv then M ⊂ ℚ^solv).

**Proof [Proof].**
1. (Local tower.) Replace K_v by its Galois closure K''_v over k_v. G_v = Gal(K''_v/k_v) is solvable
   (ramification filtration [Cited: Serre, *Local Fields*, Ch. IV §2, Cor. 5 of Prop. 7]). A
   composition series of G_v gives k_v = K_{v,0} ⊂ … ⊂ K_{v,r_v} = K''_v, each step Galois cyclic of
   prime degree.
2. (Serialise.) Order the pairs (v, j) as steps t = 1..T; at step t only one place v(t) and one prime
   ℓ_t are involved; all other v get a trivial step. (This avoids having to use different primes at
   one step.)
3. (Hypothesis H_t.) M_t ⊃ k is a tower of cyclic prime-degree steps; every real place of k splits
   completely in M_t; for each v ∈ S and u | v of M_t there is a k_v-isomorphism
   ι_u : M_{t,u} ≅ K_{v,j(v,t)}. H_0: M_0 = k.
4. (Step.) v = v(t), ℓ = ℓ_t. For each u | v of M_t transport K_{v,j+1}/K_{v,j} along ι_u; by local
   CFT it is cut out by a character χ_u of G_{M_{t,u}} of exact order ℓ. At every other place w of the
   finite set T_t = {places of M_t above S} ∪ {real places of M_t} put χ_w = 1. **Grunwald–Wang**
   gives a global χ : G_{M_t} → ℤ/ℓ with χ|_{G_{M_{t,w}}} = χ_w for all w ∈ T_t. The special case
   needs 2^s ∥ n with M_t(μ_{2^s})/M_t non-cyclic, so s ≥ 3, i.e. 8 | n; here n = ℓ prime, so it
   never occurs. χ ≠ 1, so M_{t+1} := fixed field of ker χ is cyclic of degree ℓ over M_t; real
   places stay real (χ_w = 1 there), completions above v' ≠ v are unchanged, and above each u | v
   there is exactly one place, with completion ≅ K_{v,j+1}. So H_{t+1} holds.
   [Cited: Artin–Tate, *Class Field Theory*, Ch. X, Thm 5 + §2; NSW 2nd ed. Thm 9.2.8 — see (1b)]
5. After T steps, M_u ≅ K''_v ⊇ K_v for every u | v ∈ S. ∎

**Remarks.**
- Wang's counterexample (16 is an 8th power in ℚ_p for every odd p but not in ℚ_2 or ℚ; no cyclic
  degree-8 extension of ℚ is unramified of degree 8 at 2) is irrelevant: all steps have prime degree.
  [Proof]
- Totally real + solvable [Proof]: if k is totally real, (i) makes M totally real, and the Galois
  closure of a totally real field is totally real (compositum of conjugates). The Galois closure of M/ℚ
  is solvable when k ⊂ ℚ^solv: solvable extensions are closed under towers and conjugation. So for
  k = ℚ, M ⊂ ℚ^{tr,solv}.
- [Inferred] If every K''_v/k_v has 2-power degree, M is a tower of quadratic steps, all real under
  every embedding (real ruler-and-compass numbers).

### (1a) Local solubility at ALL places (adversarial check of Theorem H step 2)

Setting: X/k smooth, projective, geometrically integral, σ_0 : k ⊂ ℝ, X^{σ_0}(ℝ) ≠ ∅.
- **Good places [Proof sketch, standard].**
  - Spread X out to a smooth projective 𝒳 over O_{k,S_0} with geometrically integral fibres, S_0
    finite [Cited: EGA IV_3 Thm 9.7.7 (geometric integrality of fibres is constructible); EGA IV_4
    17.7.11 (smooth locus open)].
  - For v ∉ S_0 with residue field F_q, Lang–Weil [Cited: Lang–Weil, Amer. J. Math. 76 (1954),
    Thm 1] gives |𝒳_v(F_q)| ≥ q^n − C q^{n−1/2} > 0 once q > q_0(n, D, N).
  - Every F_q-point is smooth, and Hensel lifts it to O_{k_v}.
  - So X(k_v) ≠ ∅ outside the finite set S_1 = S_0 ∪ {v : q_v ≤ q_0}. For every finite L_w/k_v
    (v ∉ S_1), X(L_w) ⊇ X(k_v) ≠ ∅.
- **Bad places [Proof].** For v ∈ S_1, a k̄_v-point exists and is defined over some finite K_v/k_v.
  Lemma LS over the base field makes every completion above v contain K_v.
- **Archimedean [Proof].** Twist L_1 = k(√a), with σ_0(a) > 0 and σ(a) < 0 at the other real σ (a
  exists by the approximation theorem). The real places of L_1 restrict to σ_0. LS over L_1 keeps
  all real places real.
- **No circularity [Proof].** S_1 is computed once over k. For any L ⊇ k and w | v ∉ S_1,
  X(L_w) ⊇ X(k_v) ≠ ∅; so choosing M does not create new bad places.
- **Conclusion [Proof].** With L = L_1·M (M from LS over L_1, applied to the places above S_1):
  - X(L_w) ≠ ∅ for EVERY place w of L, with smooth local points (X smooth);
  - every real place of L lies over σ_0;
  - L ⊂ ℚ^solv, and L ⊂ ℝ via an extension of σ_0. For k = ℚ: L = M ⊂ ℚ^{tr,solv}.
  - (Bonus: LS can make the completions above S_1 contain ANY prescribed finite extensions, which is
    useful if HP is only known for local points of a special shape.)

**Verdict on (1):** Lemma LS and the local half of Theorem H are correct. [Proof]

### (1b) Grunwald–Wang citation check
- NSW, *Cohomology of Number Fields*, 2nd ed., **Thm 9.2.8** is the Grunwald–Wang theorem: it holds
  unless one is in the special case, i.e. Gal(K(μ_{2^r})/K) is not cyclic, 2^r ∥ n. [Cited, as quoted
  in arXiv:2603.04201 (2026), Introduction; the book itself was not opened]
- Special case ⇒ r ≥ 3 ⇒ 8 | n; so for n = ℓ prime GW holds. [Proof]
- Wang's counterexample: n = 8, K = ℚ, 2 ∈ S [Cited: Wikipedia "Grunwald–Wang theorem"; Wang, Ann.
  Math. 51 (1950)].

## (2) Circle method over number fields: HP + WA

Source opened: Frei–Madritsch, *Forms of differing degrees over number fields*, Mathematika 63 (2017)
92–123, arXiv:1412.6419v2 (full text read, §1).

- **[Cited: FM Thm 1.1]** For K any number field of degree n and polynomials G_{d,i} over O_K, the
  counting function in expanding boxes P·B ⊂ V^s (V = K ⊗ ℝ) is 𝔖𝔍 P^{n(s−𝒟)} + O(P^{n(s−𝒟)−δ})
  under hypothesis (1.2). FM: "our hypotheses (1.2) are exactly the ones used by Browning and
  Heath-Brown over Q. In particular, they are independent of the degree n of K." So the bounds are
  **uniform in L** (not that Theorem H needs uniformity: it applies the theorem to one L at a time).
- **[Cited: FM, (1.3)]** All degrees equal to D: s − B_D > t_D(t_D+1)(D−1)2^{D−1}, Skinner's condition
  [Skinner, *Forms over number fields and weak approximation*, Compositio Math. 106 (1997) 11–29],
  identical to Birch's. FM also correct an error in Skinner's singular-integral treatment (§1.5).
- **[Cited: FM Thm 1.3]** Leading-form system non-singular (rank J(x) = T at every nonzero common
  zero) and s > (𝒟 − 1)2^𝒟, 𝒟 = Σ degrees ⇒ asymptotic; 𝔍 > 0 iff the leading forms have a nonzero
  zero in the interior of B ⊂ V^s (i.e. at ALL archimedean places simultaneously); 𝔖 > 0 iff
  nonzero zeros in O_{K,p}^s for every prime p.
- **[Cited: FM after Thm 1.3]** "It is known that an asymptotic formula as in Theorem 1.3 implies the
  Hasse principle and weak approximation for X, see [Ski97]." Consequence stated: every smooth
  non-degenerate CI X ⊂ ℙ^{s−1}_K with s > (deg X − 1)2^{deg X} satisfies HP, WA and Manin–Peyre.
- **[Cited: FM Thm 1.4]** "Let X ⊂ ℙ^m_K be a smooth and geometrically integral variety satisfying
  dim(X) ≥ (deg(X) − 1)2^{deg(X)} − 1. Then X satisfies the Hasse principle, weak approximation, and
  the Manin–Peyre conjecture." Proof: BHB show such X is a complete intersection (over ℚ; FM: "their
  arguments hold as well over arbitrary number fields" — proof omitted in FM) [Cited: Browning–
  Heath-Brown, *Forms in many variables and differing degrees*, JEMS 19 (2017) 357–394,
  arXiv:1403.5937, Thm 1.1].

**Answers to the precise questions.**
- *Smooth varieties or only CIs?* Theorem 1.4 is for ANY smooth geometrically integral X ⊂ ℙ^m, with
  dim and deg taken in that embedding; in this range X is automatically a CI. Degeneracy is harmless
  (replace ℙ^m by the linear span, defined over K; dim and deg unchanged). [Cited + Proof]
- *Birch rank / singular locus.* Thm 1.1/Cor 1.2 use B_d = dim of the Birch singular locus of the
  degree-d leading forms; Thm 1.3/1.4 replace it by nonsingularity (B_max ≤ T−1 for an optimal
  system). [Cited]
- *Uniform in L?* Yes: hypotheses independent of [K:ℚ]; only the error term depends on K. [Cited]
- *WA at real places?* WA is claimed at all places (the box B is any small box in V = ∏_{v|∞} K_v, so
  one can centre it at a nonsingular real/complex zero; standard Skinner argument). [Cited: FM §1.2,
  Skinner 1997] **Requirement:** 𝔍 > 0 needs a nonsingular zero at EVERY archimedean place of L,
  which is exactly what the "all real places of L lie over σ_0" step guarantees. [Proof]
- *Smooth local points:* X smooth ⇒ all local zeros nonsingular ⇒ local densities positive. [Proof]

**Hypersurface and type-specific ranges (number-field versions).**
- Hypersurface degree d in ℙ^{s−1}: s > (d−1)2^d, i.e. N ≥ (d−1)2^d. [Cited: Skinner 1997 / FM (1.3)
  with t_D = 1, B_D = 0]
- Nonsingular system, 𝒟 = Σd_i: s > (𝒟−1)2^𝒟. [Cited: FM Thm 1.3]
- Equal degrees, R forms of degree d, smooth CI: s > R + R(R+1)(d−1)2^{d−1} (B_d ≤ R for a smooth CI's
  affine Birch locus, as in P1). [Cited + Proof]
- Two forms of differing degrees and quadrics + one form: FM say "[BHB15, Cor 1.4, 1.5] ... are still
  valid in our number field version." [Cited: FM §1.2] So P1's H(e) is confirmed.

**Verdict on (2):** P1's citations for H(a)–(e) are correct; HP + WA over every number field L with
L-independent hypotheses. [Cited]

## (3) Literature: is (H*) known?

### (3a) Wooley, *Solvable points on smooth projective varieties*, Monatsh. Math. 180 (2016) 391–403, arXiv:1501.01789 (full text read)
- **Thm 1.1 [Cited]:** X ⊂ ℙ^n smooth, geometrically integral over K algebraic over ℚ; X has a point
  over a solvable extension of K if dim X ≥ 2^{2^{deg X}}. Thm 4.1: m-planes if dim X ≥
  (2(m+1))^{2^{deg X}}.
- **Method [Cited]:** NOT the circle method. BHB/Bertram–Ein–Lazarsfeld ⇒ X is a CI over K
  (Lemma 2.3, needs dim X > 2 deg X(2 deg X − 3)); then Brauer diagonalisation with φ_d(K^sol) = 1
  (a_0x_0^d + a_1x_1^d = 0 solvable by a radical). The points are in towers of steps of degree
  ≤ deg X, and are in general NOT real (radicals of negative numbers, complex roots of unity).
- **Totally real [Cited, quoted]:** "In view of [Çiperiani–Wiles, Remark 2.8.2], it may be of interest
  to restrict attention to totally real solvable extensions of Q." His only totally real result is
  **Cor 4.6**: X of ODD degree over a totally real K has a K-point if dim X ≥ exp(2^{D+2}(log D)²)
  (via Thm 4.4 = local solubility over any algebraic extension of ℚ_p by Brink–Godinho–Rodrigues, +
  FM Thm 1.4 HP; odd degree gives real points by Borsuk–Ulam / Lang).
- **Cor 4.5 [Cited]:** for L algebraic over ℚ, X has an L-point if X has points at all infinite
  completions and dim X ≥ exp(2^{D+2}(log D)²). (This is the RW of X2.)
- **Consequence [Inferred, important]:** Wooley in 2015–16 explicitly wanted totally real solvable
  points and did NOT use the Grunwald–Wang trick; with it his Cor 4.5 range drops to FM's
  (D−1)2^D − 1 for totally real solvable points, for every D (not only odd). So (H*) is a strict
  improvement on the published state of the art in Wooley's paper, both in range and in allowing even
  degree. Novelty beyond that: see (3b)–(3d).

### (3b) Other searched literature (search snippets unless stated)
- **Pál**, *Solvable points on projective algebraic curves*, Canad. J. Math. 56 (2004) 612–637
  [Cited via Wooley's intro]: genus 0, 2, 3, 4 curves have solvable points (Thm 1.6); geometrically
  rational surfaces have solvable points (Thm 1.7); for g ≥ 40 there are LOCAL fields F (with residue
  fields having non-solvable quotients, per Rawson's summary) with genus-g curves lacking F^sol-points
  (Thm 1.5). No realness, no circle method. Pál's local counterexamples do not apply to p-adic fields
  (finite residue fields ⇒ G_{ℚ_p} prosolvable). [Inferred]

### (3c) Novelty assessment [Inferred]
- Both ingredients are well known (LS: Grunwald–Wang folklore; HP+WA: FM 2017). The combination is a
  short observation. Wooley (2016), who explicitly asked about totally real solvable points, did not
  make it; his best totally real result needs odd degree and dim ≥ exp(2^{D+2}(log D)²). So (H*) is
  "new in print as far as found, but an easy corollary of known results"; publishable at most as a
  remark/note. Risk that it appears in a paper not found by search: moderate.

## (4) Verdict

### (4a) Corrected statement of (H*)
**Theorem H* [Proof sketch, all ingredients Cited].** Let k be a number field, σ_0 : k ↪ ℝ, and
X ⊂ ℙ^m_k smooth, geometrically integral, with X^{σ_0}(ℝ) ≠ ∅, and either dim X ≥ (deg X − 1)2^{deg X}
− 1 (FM Thm 1.4), or X a hypersurface / nonsingular system / two-form system in the ranges of FM
(1.3), Thm 1.3, Cor 1.2 (+ BHB Cor 1.4–1.5). Then:
- (a) there is a finite extension L/k, solvable over k (tower of prime-degree cyclic steps after one
  quadratic step), with an embedding σ : L ↪ ℝ extending σ_0, such that X(L) is dense in X^{σ_0}(ℝ);
- (b) if every real embedding of k gives a real point (e.g. k = ℚ), the quadratic step is skipped and L
  is totally real; if moreover k ⊂ ℚ^{tr,solv}, then L ⊂ ℚ^{tr,solv}, and X(ℚ^{tr,solv}) is dense in
  ∏_{σ real} X^σ(ℝ) (WA over L at all real places);
- (c) in general L ⊂ k^{solv} ∩ ℝ; if k ⊂ ℚ^{solv} then L ⊂ ℚ^{solv} ∩ ℝ (not nec. totally real).

**Flaw in (H*) as phrased [Proof].** "over a number field k ⊂ ℝ ... has a point over ℚ^{tr,solv}" is
false unless k ⊂ ℚ^{tr,solv} (or one reads points over k·ℚ^{tr,solv}): e.g. k = ℚ(α), α the real root of
x^5 − x − 1 (Galois group S_5), X = the point (1 : α) ⊂ ℙ^1 (smooth, deg 1, dim 0 ≥ (1−1)2^1 − 1 = −1).
X has no point with coordinates in ℚ^{solv}. Also, over a non-totally-real k the field obtained is
only real-at-σ_0, not totally real. For k = ℚ (the case relevant to Taylor) (H*) is correct.
- Other checks passed: Lemma LS (prime-degree steps avoid Wang); local solubility at ALL places
  (Lang–Weil + Hensel at good places, S_1 fixed once, LS at bad ones); HP + WA over arbitrary L with
  [L:ℚ]-independent hypotheses (FM Thm 1.1–1.4, read in full); 𝔍 > 0 needs real points at ALL real
  places of L — guaranteed since every real place of L lies over σ_0 (or over a real place of k with
  a real point).

### (4b) What it gives for Taylor's question [Inferred]
- Taylor's Question 5.5: is ℚ^{tr,solv} PSC (every variety over it with smooth real points at all
  orderings has a point)? A variety over ℚ^{tr,solv} is defined over some finite k ⊂ ℚ^{tr,solv}, and
  the orderings of ℚ^{tr,solv} restrict to all real embeddings of k. So H*(b) proves the PSC property
  for the class: smooth projective (and their dense opens) X with dim ≥ (D−1)2^D − 1, or hypersurfaces
  with N ≥ (d−1)2^d, etc. — "ℚ^{tr,solv} is PSC for varieties in the circle-method range", with
  density. This is a genuine but modest partial answer; it is NOT what Taylor needed (his
  applications concern twisted moduli spaces — Hilbert–Blumenthal / Shimura-type, typically general
  type or low dimension — far outside the range).
- It also improves Wooley 2016 Cor 4.6 (odd degree, exp range, no extension) to: all degrees, range
  (D−1)2^D − 1, at the cost of a totally real solvable extension.
