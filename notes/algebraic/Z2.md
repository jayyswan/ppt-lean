# Z2: engine shape C = {9,7,5,4,2,0} (is it drawable, and at what cost?)

Stream Z2 of `pptc/PLAN-algebraic.md`. Labels: [Proof], [Wolfram], [Heuristic], [Open]. Nothing here concludes a
negative about the target; every obstruction is scoped to the family it was tested on.
Backend: Wolfram for every number (no Python). Some long Wolfram loops hit the 60 s gateway; those were rerun
with shorter budgets, and every number below comes from a call that returned.

## Findings summary

1. **The conditions for support C [Proof + Wolfram].** `χ_τ = t⁹ + a t⁷ + b t⁵ + c t⁴ + d t² + e` means the
   coefficients of `t⁸, t⁶, t³, t¹` vanish. Since the coefficient of `t^{9-k}` is `±e_k`, these are
   **e₁ = e₃ = e₆ = e₈ = 0** (confirmed by index count, and in power sums below).
   - Degrees 1, 3, 6, 8. They are algebraically independent symmetric functions, so the locus in `K ⊗ ℂ = ℂ⁹` is
     a complete intersection of degree **1·3·6·8 = 144** (dimension 5 cone; after the free shift `e₁ = 0`, a CI of
     degrees (3, 6, 8) in ℙ⁷, dimension 4, degree **144**).
   - Odd route (A): `p₁ = p₃ = p₅ = p₇ = 0`, CI (3, 5, 7) in ℙ⁷, degree **105**. So **C is not cheaper: 144 > 105.**
   - Expected Fano dimension of lines: A: 2·6 − (4+6+8) = −6; C: 12 − (4+7+9) = −8. Neither has lines
     (expected). The circle-method exponent `9 − Σ deg` is −7 for A and −9 for C.
   - In power sums, with `p₁ = 0`: `e₃ = 0 ⇔ p₃ = 0`, and then `e₆ = 0 ⇔ p₆ = (6p₂p₄ − p₂³)/8`,
     `e₈ = 0 ⇔ p₈ = (−p₂⁴ + 4p₂²p₄ + 4p₄²)/16` [Wolfram]. So the C conditions are not linear in the odd power
     sums, unlike A (which is four *linear* conditions on `p₁, p₃, p₅, p₇`). This is a structural loss.
   - The variety is stable under `τ ↦ 1/τ` (it swaps `e₁ ↔ e₈`, `e₃ ↔ e₆`), as C is self-reversed. Fixed points are
     `τ = ±1`, useless.
   - **Irreducible [Wolfram + Proof].** The generic C polynomial has Galois group S₉: the specialisation
     `t⁹ − 3t⁷ + 5t⁵ + 7t⁴ − 2t² + 11` is irreducible with Frobenius types (4,5) (a 5-cycle in a transitive group,
     so by Jordan the group contains A₉) and (1,8) (odd). Hence the labelled variety is irreducible of degree
     144; degenerate (repeated-root) loci are not extra components.
2. **Realness of the C variety [Proof + Wolfram].**
   - Descartes capacity of C is 2+2+1+2+2 = 9, so a totally real C polynomial needs every even gap to be a sign
     change: signs `(+, −, +, σ, −σ, σ)` on `(t⁹, t⁷, t⁵, t⁴, t², 1)`. That is an open condition on `(a, b, c, d, e)`.
   - **Smooth totally real points exist [Wolfram].** FindRoot (5 roots fixed, 4 solved) found 5 distinct
     real solutions of `e₁ = e₃ = e₆ = e₈ = 0` with all nine roots real and separated by ≥ 10⁻². One of them:
     `−1.63708, −1.13752, −0.09954, −0.05809, 0.05519, 0.20578, 0.27884, 0.61715, 1.77527`. At distinct roots the
     Jacobian of `(e_k)` in the roots is a Vandermonde-type matrix of full rank, so the points are smooth
     [Proof].
   - **All signatures occur [Wolfram].** Random C polynomials (6000 samples) had 1, 3, 5, 7 real roots; 9 was
     found by the Newton search. So `V_C(ℝ)` has smooth points for `s = 0, 1, 2, 3, 4`.
   - Totally real points are narrow: random sampling never hit 9 real roots in 6000 draws (max 7), so the 9-root
     region is thin. [Heuristic]
3. **Which drawable crossings give support C [Wolfram brute force + Proof sketch].** One, and only one shape
   has support exactly C: the **aligned power law `y = A x^{±5/2}` crossed with the shifted negative-exponent
   power law `(y − y₀)(x − x₀)² = a`** (that is `y − y₀ = a (x − x₀)^{−2}`), root variable `t` with `x = B t²`,
   `y = A t⁵`:
   `χ(t) = (A t⁵ − y₀)(B t² − x₀)² − a`. All parameters are P-valued (`power_law` with rational `b = 5/2`, `b = −2`,
   then `scale_x`, `scale_y`, translations). But:
   - **Rank 3 without shift, 4 with shift [Wolfram]** (5 conditions on τ), not the 5 / 4 that a versal realisation
     of C would have. Extra relations `c₅ = c₇²/4` and `c₂ = c₇ c₄`.
   - Conditions in weights (3, 4, 6, 7, 8): `e₃ = 0`, `e₆ = 0`, `e₈ = 0`, `e₄ = e₂²/4`, `e₇ ∝ e₂ e₅`; degree
     4032 if a complete intersection [Heuristic].
   - **Not totally real [Proof].** `f = (t⁵ − Y₀)(t² − X₀)²` has `f' = t(t² − X₀)(9t⁵ − 5X₀t³ − 4Y₀)`. The
     quintic factor has at most 3 real roots, so `f' ` has at most 6 real roots and `f − a` at most 7. So s ≥ 1 only.
     Random sampling (5000): at most 5.
4. **Sheared variant: support C ∪ {6} [Wolfram].** Shearing the partner, `(y − y₀ − σx)(x − x₀)² = a`, gives
   `{9,7,6,5,4,2,0}` (7 terms, all gaps ≤ 2, capacity 9).
   - Rank **4 without shift, 5 with shift**, so **4 conditions**, weights (4, 6, 7, 8) (relations `c₃ = c₁ = 0`,
     `c₇² = 4c₅`, `c₂ + 3c₅c₆ − c₄c₇ = 0`, plus the centring `c₈ = 0`). Degree 1344 if CI.
   - Real roots: at most 7 observed (20000 random samples, plus 29 hill-climbs minimising `Σ Im²` that reached
     0.04 at best). A totally real point was **not found** [Open]; the structure (extrema at `±√x₀` of value 0, `0`
     a maximum between them) allows alternation, so no obstruction was proven.
5. **Everything else tried does not give C** (details in §2): graphs `y = P(x)` or `x = P(y)` with P of degree ≤ 4
   (P-valued) or ≤ 6 (ℚ), aligned or general conics, lines, cubic nodal Béziers, shifted `y^s = a(x−x₀)^r`,
   L-shapes and boxes, all signs and `t ↦ 1/t` and `t ↦ t^k`. The best are 5-term sub-supports of C with a gap 4
   (not totally real capable). Béziers crossed with power laws give sub-loci of codimension ≥ 2 inside C.
6. **Why C is not reached [Proof sketch, scoped to the catalogue of G2].** In the lattice picture, a crossing of
   `(t^q, t^p)` with a curve `G(x,y)` has support `{iq + jp : x^i y^j ∈ G}`. C = {0,2,4} + {0,5} is exactly the image of
   the rectangle `{0,1,2}×{0,1}` for `(q,p) = (2,5)`, i.e. the Newton polygon of **`y·Q₁(x) + Q₂(x) = 0`, `deg Q₁ = deg Q₂ = 2`**:
   the graph of a general degree-(2,2) rational function. That is a nodal cubic with its node at the line at infinity
   (tangents the two vertical asymptotes). Drawable cubics (G2) are Aff-images of the Bézier nodal cubic (finite
   node, flex at infinity), `y = x³`, `y² = x³`, `y = x^{-2}`; none has a node at infinity. The cuspidal member
   `Y X² + d X + e = 0` is also not an aligned-Aff image of `y x² = 1` (it has a generic, not a cusp–flex, line at
   infinity). Only the 3-parameter slice `Q₁ = (x − x₀)²`, `Q₂ = −y₀ Q₁ − a` is drawable, which is item 3.
   **Cost to realise C with 5 free P-parameters:** a constructor drawing `y = k + l/(x − r₁) + m/(x − r₂)`
   (a sum of two hyperbolas / a projective image of a conic or Bézier), or equivalently an affine-inequivalent
   nodal cubic. Not available with the current constructors.
7. **Overshoot (N2) does not force C [Proof for the natural family].** N2's variant (c) with `m = 5`,
   `N = A t⁵ − P₆(t²)` (degree 12, cofactor ρ cubic): writing `χ = Q₂(u) + t u² Q₁(u)`, `ρ = ρ_e(u) + tρ_o(u)`, the odd
   part of `χρ` is `Q₂ρ_o + u²Q₁ρ_e`, which must equal `A u²`. Mod `u²`: `Q₂(0)ρ_o ≡ 0` forces `ρ_o = 0`, then
   `Q₁ρ_e = A` forces `Q₁` constant. So **no C polynomial lies in that overshoot locus**; the overshoot family
   (2 conditions) neither contains nor is forced into C.
8. **Totally real gap-≤2 classes at n = 10…13 [Wolfram enumeration + Proof].**
   - n = 10, 12: besides the odd-condition engine (`k = 1`, Theorem O even), **even + one odd monomial**
     `{0,2,…,n} ∪ {k}` for `k` odd is drawable (`x = t²`, power law `y = a x^{k/2}` against a rational degree
     `n/2` graph, so `n/2 ≤ 6`). Totally real exists for every `k` [Proof: `T_n + ε t^k` has `n` simple real roots for
     small ε]. Their conditions are CI of weights (3,5,7), (3,5,9), (3,7,9), (5,7,9) at n = 10 (degrees 105, 135, 189, 315
     for k = 1, 3, 5, 7) and (3,5,7,9), (3,5,7,11), (3,5,9,11), (3,7,9,11), (5,7,9,11) at n = 12 (degrees 945, 1155,
     1485, 2079, 3465 for k = 1,…,9). **None beats the k = 1 case.**
   - n = 11, 13: only the odd engine class `{n, n−2, …, 1, 0}` and its reversal (degrees 945, 10395); plus the
     sheared-box class `{11,9,7,6,4,2,0}` (and `{0,2,4,5,6,7,9,11}` from the unsheared (2,5) box with r = 3), which
     has ≤ 5 parameters and ≥ 6 conditions [Heuristic, rank not measured].
   - Conics, lines, nodal Béziers give no ≥ 6-term gap-≤ 2 support at any n in 9…13.

## 1. Exact conditions (task 1)

Newton with `p₁ = 0` (Wolfram):

    e₃ = p₃/3
    e₆|_{p₁=p₃=0} = −p₂³/48 + p₂p₄/8 − p₆/6
    e₈|_{p₁=p₃=0} = p₂⁴/384 − p₂²p₄/32 + p₄²/32 + p₂p₆/12 − p₈/8
    after e₆ = 0:   e₈ ∝ −p₂⁴ + 4p₂²p₄ + 4p₄² − 16p₈

With `p₃ = 0` the power-mean inequality `p₄² ≤ p₂p₆` becomes `p₂²/4 ≤ p₄ ≤ p₂²/2` for totally real τ; the region is
nonempty, consistent with the numerical solutions.

Comparison table (n = 9, conditions after the free shift, shift-invariant variety in ℙ⁷):

| support | conditions | degree of CI | linear in odd power sums | totally real (Descartes) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A `{9,7,5,3,1,0}` | 3 (weights 3,5,7) | 105 | yes | capacity 9, smooth real points |
| B `{9,8,6,4,2,0}` | A under `t ↦ 1/t` | 105 | (after inversion) | capacity 9 |
| **C `{9,7,5,4,2,0}`** | 3 (weights 3,6,8) | **144** | no (p₆, p₈ in p₂, p₄) | capacity 9, smooth real points for s = 0…4 |
| even `{9,…}` with e₂ = e₄ = e₆ = e₈ = 0 | 4 | 384 | no | needs `p₂ = 0`, impossible for s = 0 |

So C is **dearer** in degree (144 vs 105), has no linear structure, and has no lines. Its only advantage is the
`τ ↦ 1/τ` symmetry, which has no fixed point of use. [Heuristic: no advantage in finding points.]

## 2. Enumeration of crossings (task 2)

**Method.** A power law `y = a x^{p/q}` is parametrised `(t^q, t^p)`. If the partner has monomial set `M ⊂ ℤ²`, the
crossing polynomial has exponent set `{iq + jp : (i,j) ∈ M}` (shifted by any integer: curve data `x₀, y₀` may be nonzero
and a factor `t^m` is harmless). Partner types: `y = P(x)` (degree ≤ 4 with P coefficients, ≤ 6 over ℚ), `x = P(y)`,
aligned and general conics, lines, nodal Bézier (aligned form `Q(u,v) + (λu + μv)³`), shifted
`(y−y₀)^s = a(x−x₀)^r` (L-shapes), `(y−y₀)(x−x₀)^r = a` (boxes) with and without shear, and full triangles
`{i + j ≤ s}`. All `|q|,|p| ≤ 14`, `gcd = 1`, signs included, subsets of monomials allowed for droppable coefficients.
Shifting or shearing the **power law** is absorbed into the partner (it only changes P's low coefficients), so aligned
frames suffice; an unaligned partner is dense and fails by AP counting (it would need a 5-term arithmetic
progression inside C+m, but C has none). [Wolfram + Proof]

Results (support ⊆ C+m containing both ends):

| family | best support inside C | note |
|---|---|---|
| `y = P(x)`, `x = P(y)`, deg ≤ 6 | `{0,2,4,5,9}`, `{0,4,5,7,9}` | 5 terms, gap 4; P of degree 5 (ℚ) |
| conic (aligned, general), line | none with ≥ 5 terms | |
| nodal Bézier aligned | ≤ 4 terms | |
| L-shape `(y−y₀)^s = a(x−x₀)^r` | `{0,2,4,9}` etc., 4 terms | |
| **box `(y−y₀)(x−x₀)²=a`, `(q,p)=(±2,±5)`** | **C, 6 terms, 3 params** | item 3 |
| box with shear | C ∪ {6}, C ∪ {3}; 7 terms | item 4; rank 4/5 |
| `y Q₁(x) + Q₂(x) = 0` general | C, 6 terms, 5 params | **not drawable** (item 6) |

**N4 item 4 correction [Wolfram].** N4 says no aligned power law × shifted power law has a gap-≤2 support. That is
false for negative second exponent: `b = −2` gives C (and C∪{6}). The 69 configurations of N4 evidently used positive
exponents. This does not change N4's ranking (rank 3–5, so ≥ 4 conditions).

**Béziers × power laws (root variable s) [Wolfram].** Free cubics `X(s), Y(s)` against `y^q x^{p'} = a`:
- positive exponents, degree 9 needs `max(p,q) = 3`: `Y − aX³`, `Y³ − aX`, `Y³ − aX²` (N4-1), `Y² − aX³`: restricted to
  C-support, `Y³ − aX` forces `c₄ = 6vw = 0` (c₆ = 3w = 0 needs w = 0), so C is **not** reached; `Y³ − aX²` leaves a 3-dim
  slice (6 unknowns, 3 equations). Bézier × cubic graph `P(X) − Y`: `c₄ = 2v c₆` on the top coefficients, so
  `c₆ = 0 ⇒ c₄ = 0`; again not C.
- negative exponent `Y X² = a` (**new engine, rank 7 [Wolfram]**, so 2 conditions, same as N4-1): `χ + a` has three
  double roots, i.e. the nonic `χ` has three equal critical values. 9 real roots occurs (2 of 6000 random draws),
  so it serves s = 0. **No polynomial relation among the centred `e₂…e₉` of weight ≤ 27** (mod p, up to 296 monomials),
  so the two conditions have degree ≥ 28, worse than N4-1 (≥ 18) and N4-2 (≥ 15). It does not reach C
  (C-support slice has 3 parameters, 7 − 4).
- Any family whose image has `r ≥ 6` meets the 6-dim (with shift) C-locus in codim ≥ 1 unless it contains it. Such a
  family is a cheaper engine than C anyway (fewer conditions), so it does not "realise C" in a useful sense. A
  full test "is a random C polynomial in the image of Bézier × Bézier" was not completed (a symbolic expansion
  timed out) [Open, low value].

**Two-term structure.** `C = t⁵Q₁(t²) + Q₂(t²)` with `Q₁` monic quadratic, `Q₂` quadratic. Equivalently
`t⁴R + S`, `R = t⁵+at³+bt+c`, `S = dt²+e`. The only way to make this a crossing is `(x, y) = (t², t⁵)` and
`yQ₁(x) + Q₂(x)`; this is item 6. The norm `χ(t)χ(−t) = Q₂(u)² − u⁵Q₁(u)²` is the char. polynomial of τ², dense in u.

## 4. Cost comparison with the odd route at n = 9, s = 0 (task 4)

C is **not realised with enough parameters**, and neither realisation serves s = 0 (R1 provably not; R2 not found).
Costs (conditions, degree) on τ: odd route (3; 105), C itself (3; 144, no drawable crossing), R2 (4; 1344),
R1 (5; 4032), Bézier × `YX² = a` (2; ≥ 28 each). No numerical end-to-end example was run (no engine for
totally real nonics came out of this stream).

## Other things noticed

- **N4 item 4 is wrong for negative exponents** (see §2). The N4 table of 69 power-law × power-law configurations
  should be rerun with signed `p'`, `q'`. The box `(y−y₀)(x−x₀)^r = a` for r = 2 is the one that matters.
- **New engine Bézier × `y = a x^{-2}`** (rank 7, 9 real roots occur, three equal critical values) has conditions of
  degree ≥ 28; unlike the `M³ + v` family it is not weighted-low-degree. Same cost class as N4-1.
- At n = 10 the drawable family "even + one odd monomial `t^k`" has five supports; only `k = 1` has the
  (3, 5, 7) conditions that Theorem O uses. The others (`k = 3,5,7`) are costlier but give the same engine step with
  different power-law exponent `k/2`. At n = 12 six supports.
- N2's "overshoot variant (c)" with m odd reads as `x = t²`, `y = t^m`: a power law `y = a x^{m/2}` against a
  degree-6 rational graph, i.e. the same box/graph lattice picture; the monomial brute force reproduces N2's list.
- Several Wolfram calls hit the 60 s gateway (3 of ~20). The monomial and elimination calculations are robust; the
  global random searches (`NSolve` loops) are the slow ones. A smarter search for totally real points of the sheared
  engine (solve for the 8 critical points directly) would be faster than random sampling.
