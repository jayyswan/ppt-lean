## Findings summary

1. **A chain that stays on one tower word collapses to a single entry [Proof, §2].** If the second
   `arc_of_length` continues the tracing of the first, arc length is additive and the pair is the
   one-step entry with length `x₁ + x₂`. If an affine or a second offset is inserted between the
   links, the pair is still a single entry on the composed word, with a P-constructible constant
   shift. The cusp caveat is inherited from R0 §3.4. This includes the case "offset the laid arc,
   then invert", which is *not* `x₁ + x₂` but is still one entry.

2. **A re-based chain is triangular [Proof, §3].** When the second link's curve or data come from
   the first link's outputs, the second link is one entry of R2's catalogue whose coefficients lie
   in the field generated by the first root. Those outputs are P-constructible and independent of
   the final unknown. Consequences:
   - the chain's equation has the **same shape** as a one-step entry — no new equation family;
   - its root satisfies a one-step equation over `Q(w₁, s₁, …)`; the two worked examples are
     verified to `10⁻²⁴⁸`;
   - the chain adds **numbers** (parameter extension, as R4 §1 found), not **shapes**.

3. **What a chain genuinely adds is a *coupling curve* [Proof + Wolfram, §4].** A re-based chain
   makes the last link's `(d, x)` run along a curve instead of sitting at one point. That curve is
   exactly the first laid arc, traversed at **unit speed**:
   `d(d₂)/dx₁ = 1/s`, `d(x₂)/dx₁ = w/s`. For the parabola the coupling curve is the offset parabola,
   an algebraic curve (implicit equation below, degree `(6,4)`). So a chain is a one-parameter
   *subfamily* of the one-step catalogue, not a new mechanism.

4. **Open item 3 is not an independent separation problem [Proof, §5].** The last link is a
   one-step entry, so no "depth-6 separation theorem" is needed. R4 §2.1's framing ("offset
   inversion is depth 3; a chain of two is depth 6, and no obstruction is proved against a depth-6
   chain") conflates **term depth** with **equation depth**. What a chain can exploit is not depth
   but the *dependence of one-step data on the family parameter* — precisely R3 §3.9 items 2–3 and
   R2 Q3.4. Item 3 therefore **reduces to** the already-open data-dependence question.

5. **Named targets: nothing new [Wolfram, evidence only, §5.4].** 48 high-precision PSLQ tests give
   0 hits (positive control fires); 6 chain roots have no one-step parabola relation with rational
   data. Chains do not produce a uniform `tan x = λx`, beam or goat solution. This is evidence, not
   proof; the uniform question is still Q3.4.

## 1. Definitions and the question

### 1.1 Tower words and one-step entries

A **tower word** is a finite composition of `offset` and affine steps applied to a base curve of
`Defs.lean`, restricted to a parameter window (R2 §Q1.0, R4 §1.3):

```
W = A_n O(d_n) A_{n-1} O(d_{n-1}) … O(d_1) γ₀ ,     t ∈ [a, b] .
```

`cut-commutation` (R4 §1.3) says offset and affine steps act pointwise, so restricting a word to a
sub-window commutes with them. Its arc length from `a` to `t` is R2's master formula (2.2):

```
L_W(t) − L_W(a) = ∫_a^t (Π_j W_j) ds₀ − Σ_i d_i ∫_a^t (Π_{j>i} W_j) dψ_i .        (1.1)
```

> **Definition 1.1 (one-step entry).** Choose a window `[a, t_B]`, a P-constructible start `a`, and
> a P-constructible length `x ≥ 0`; lay the arc of length `x` from `W(a)` with `arc_of_length`, then
> read the endpoint with `box_xmax`/`box_ymax` (or isolate it with `restrict`/`inter_*`). Its
> equation is
>
> ```
> L_W(t) − L_W(a) = x .                                                          (1.2)
> ```
>
> On a monotone coordinate the readout returns `t`; otherwise a P-constructible window bound
> isolates the branch (R0 §5, R3 §3.9).

R2 §Q2.2 gives the catalogue of shapes `L_W(t) = x` takes: `R − dψ` (base offset), `ℓ_A − dσ₁E(φ|k) −
d₂ψ` (non-conformal affine between offsets), etc.

### 1.2 Chains

> **Definition 1.2 (2-chain).** A construction containing two `arc_of_length` entries
> `E₁, E₂`, where `E₂` is applied to a curve that is either
> - **(a) the same word** as `E₁` (possibly restricted to the sub-window left by `E₁`), or
> - **(b) a word whose data include outputs of `E₁`** (offset distances, lengths, scale factors,
>   rotation angles, start points, window bounds, or intersection partners).

The **chain length** is 2. Its term depth (R3 Def. 4.1) is 6; its **equation depth**, as defined
below, is 1.

> **Definition 1.3 (equation depth).** The equation depth of an output is the number of
> `arc_of_length` steps **in the last entry**. A chain of two inversions has equation depth 1.

The distinction in Def. 1.3 is the point of §3.5.

### 1.3 The question, under the uniform convention

The plan's uniform convention: *a construction solves a family if one configuration solves it for
all values of the family parameter, changing only P-constructible data that does not depend on the
unknown root.*

So for a chain there are two readings, and they must be kept apart:

- **The family parameter is the second length `x₂`**, the first link being fixed. Then all of `E₂`'s
  data are fixed numbers; the chain is a single entry with fixed data.
- **The family parameter is earlier data** (say `x₁`), so `E₂`'s data vary with it. Then the chain
  is a single entry whose data are P-constructible *functions* of the parameter.

Item 3 asks: does the second reading produce equation shapes, or solving power, beyond the first?

## 2. Chains on one word collapse

### 2.1 Continuation: lengths add

> **Proposition 2.1 [Proof].** Let `E₁` lay length `x₁` from parameter `a` to `t₁` on `W`, and let
> `E₂` lay length `x₂` from `t₁` to `t₂` on the same `W`. Then the pair is the single entry with
> length `x₁ + x₂`:
>
> ```
> L_W(t₂) − L_W(a) = x₁ + x₂ .
> ```

**Proof.** Arc length of a traced curve is additive over concatenated parameter intervals:
`(L(t₂) − L(t₁)) + (L(t₁) − L(a)) = L(t₂) − L(a)`. Both entries are legal on the same word, and
the second start `t₁` is P-constructible because `x₁` is. ∎

*Numerical check* [Wolfram, 60 digits]: parabola, `d = 1/2`, `x₁ = 1`, `x₂ = 1/2`:

```
w₁ = 1.19469384349484182976872145227272049914046434347905337868549
w₂ = 1.52524255134549314557417334170947232614478478389381832405643
F_d(w₂) − x₁ − x₂ = 0·10⁻⁵⁹ ,   (F_d(w₂) − F_d(w₁)) − x₂ = 0·10⁻⁵⁹ .
```

### 2.2 Offsetting the laid arc

`E₂` may be applied to the *offset of the laid arc*, not to the continuation of the same curve.
R4 §1.3's cut-commutation lemma gives, on a window with `1 − d₁κ > 0`,

```
P_{d₂}( W|[a,t₁] ) = P_{d₁+d₂}(γ₀)|[a,t₁] ,
```

and in particular the offset image of the first endpoint is `P_{d₁+d₂}(γ₀)(t₁)`. If `E₂` then lays
`x₂` from there along `P_{d₁+d₂}(γ₀)`,

> **Proposition 2.2 [Proof].** The pair is the single entry
>
> ```
> L_{d₁+d₂}(t₂) − L_{d₁+d₂}(t₁) = x₂ ,    i.e.    L_{d₁+d₂}(t₂) = x₂ + L_{d₁+d₂}(t₁) .   (2.1)
> ```
>
> The shift `L_{d₁+d₂}(t₁)` is P-constructible (`t₁` is), but it is **not** `x₁` unless `d₂ = 0`.

**Proof.** Cut-commutation and Def. 1.1. ∎

So "offset the arc, then invert" is one entry on the composed word with a constant shift. It is not
"two offsets add and lengths add"; the two effects are different, and (2.1) is the exact bookkeeping.

*Numerical check* [Wolfram, 60 digits]: `d₁ = 1/3`, `d₂ = 1/5`, `x_a = 1/2`, `x_b = 1/4`:

```
t_a = 0.648841186011474049386587361570992760078155281391830158318049
t_b = 0.915006838302063825932291724219712882689843900112866711007038
F_{d₁+d₂}(t_b) − x_b − F_{d₁+d₂}(t_a) = 0·10⁻⁵⁹
offset-image coordinates, symbolic residual            = 0·10⁻⁵⁹ , 0·10⁻⁵⁹
```

### 2.3 An affine between the links

If an affine `A` is applied to the first arc before `E₂`, then by cut-commutation the first arc is
`(A∘W)|[a,t₁]` and the second link trails the word `W' = P_{d₂}∘A∘W`. The same argument gives:

> **Proposition 2.3 [Proof].** The pair is the single entry
> `L_{W'}(t₂) = x₂ + L_{W'}(t₁)`, with `W'` the composed word and `L_{W'}(t₁)` P-constructible.

The first arc's length was measured on `W`, not on `W'`, so the shift is again a constant, not
`x₁ + x₂`.

### 2.4 The cusp caveat

All of §2 assumes `1 − dκ > 0` on the relevant windows. If a link crosses an offset cusp, R0 §3.4
applies: `arc_of_length` is still legal (it may cross an ordinary cusp), the arc length uses
`|1 − dκ|`, and the equation keeps the same functional form with the right-hand side shifted by a
P-constructible constant. So a cusp does not create a new shape; it only shifts the constant in
(2.1). At a cusp `1 − dκ = 0` the offset is not a legal *whole* curve for a further `offset` step
(`hreg` fails on every tracing, R4 §1.3); that is a legality restriction on *which* chains exist,
not on the equation shape.

### 2.5 Verdict on §2

Every chain whose second link **shares the traced word** with the first is a single entry. The
cases are: continuation (`x₁ + x₂`), offset of the arc (constant shift), affine between (constant
shift), and a cusp (constant shift). In particular, **no chain of type (a) can be a new family.**

## 3. Re-based chains are triangular

### 3.1 The second link is a one-step entry

Now let `E₂` be applied to a *different* word `W'` whose coefficients (offsets, affine data, start
point, length, window bounds) include outputs of `E₁`. The first entry outputs the root `t₁` and
readouts such as `box_xmax/box_ymax(t₁)` — coordinates of a point on `E₁`'s word, hence algebraic
in `(t₁, s₁)` (or exp/trig in the exp/sine towers, R2 §Q1).

> **Theorem 3.1 (triangular reduction) [Proof].** A re-based 2-chain is a one-step entry (Def. 1.1)
> whose coefficients lie in the field `𝔽₁` generated by the base data and the first link's outputs.
> If `W'` is the second word, its equation is
>
> ```
> L_{W'}(t₂) = x₂ + L_{W'}(t_start) ,        x₂, t_start, and the data of W' lie in 𝔽₁ .   (3.1)
> ```
>
> `𝔽₁` is P-constructible and does not involve `t₂`.

**Proof.** `E₂` is an `arc_of_length` step on a legal tower word `W'`; its equation is (1.2) with
`t_start` and `x₂` fixed before `E₂` is posed. Every slot of `W'` takes a P-constructible number
(R4 §1.1 table). The first link's root `t₁` is P-constructible by its own one-step equation, and the
readouts are algebraic (or exp/trig) functions of it, so `𝔽₁` consists of P-constructible numbers.
Independence from `t₂` is the constructor semantics: `E₁` is finished before `E₂` is posed. ∎

> **Corollary 3.2 (no new shape) [Proof].** A 2-chain presents exactly one equation, of the form
> (3.1), i.e. one entry of R2's catalogue with P-constructible data. It creates **no** new equation
> family, no new transcendental atom, and no new elliptic/third-kind content beyond what the last
> link's word already carries.

### 3.4 Why the unknown is never shared

> **Proposition 3.4 [Proof sketch, from the constructor list].** No 2-chain can present a genuine
> 2×2 system in which the final unknown occurs in two equations. The only constructor that can
> identify points of two curves is `inter_x`/`inter_y`, and it outputs a **single point**; it does
> not equate two independently prescribed arc lengths.

If an arc is laid and then a *different* curve is intersected with it, R4 §1.3's reduction applies:
the point is an intersection of the **uncut** curves, and the unknown length only selects a window,
which `restrict` by a P-constructible bound can also do. So the "same unknown in two places"
configuration never arises; the chain is triangular, as in Theorem 3.1.

### 3.5 Correction to R4 §2.1's depth-6 framing

R4 §2.1 says:

> "offset inversion is depth 3; a chain of two is depth 6 … **[Open]**: no obstruction is proved
> against a depth-6 chain; none was found."

The term depth is right, but the conclusion is mislocated. By Theorem 3.1 a chain's last link is a
one-step entry with equation depth **1**; its data have term depth 3. R3's separation theorems are
statements about one-step equations, and data *depth* is irrelevant to them: `d` and `x` are
P-constructible reals, of any depth. What can break R3's argument is not depth but **dependence of
the data on the family parameter**: R3's §3.9 item 3 (`d` varying) and item 2 (non-affine
`x`-dependence). A chain is exactly a machine for manufacturing such dependence (§4). So:

> **Corollary 3.3 [Proof].** There is no separate "depth-6 separation theorem" to prove. A chain
> is covered by the one-step statements when its data are fixed, and reduces to R3 §3.9 items 2–3
> and R2 Q3.4 when they are not.

This is the precise sense in which open item 3 is **not independent**.

## 4. What a chain genuinely adds: coupling curves

### 4.1 The coupling curve

In a re-based chain the first link's readouts `(d₂, x₂)` are not free: as the first length `x₁`
varies, they trace a curve.

> **Definition 4.1 (coupling curve).** For a first entry on a word `W` with start `a`, the coupling
> curve is the image of the first laid arc,
>
> ```
> C = { (d₂(x₁), x₂(x₁)) : x₁ ≥ 0 } ,   (d₂, x₂) = the chosen readouts of the endpoint .
> ```

A single entry has data at a **point** of the `(d, x)` plane; a chain has them on a **curve**.

### 4.2 The coupling curve is the first arc, at unit speed

> **Proposition 4.2 [Proof + Wolfram].** Take the readouts to be the two coordinates of the
> endpoint of the first laid arc. Then `x₁ ↦ (d₂(x₁), x₂(x₁))` is the first arc parametrised by
> arc length. In particular its speed is `1`, and for the parabola
>
> ```
> d(d₂)/dx₁ = 1/s ,      d(x₂)/dx₁ = w/s ,      (1/s)² + (w/s)² = 1 .
> ```

**Proof.** For the parabola, `d₂ = X(w) = w − d₁w/s`, `x₂ = Y(w) = w²/2 + d₁/s`, and
`x₁ = F_{d₁}(w)`. Differentiating,
`X' = 1 − d₁/s³`, `Y' = w(1 − d₁/s³)`, `F' = (s³ − d₁)/s²`, hence
`X'/F' = 1/s` and `Y'/F' = w/s`; the speed is `√(1+w²)/s = 1`. The general case is the same
computation in the word's parameter; geometrically, the endpoint of the laid arc moves along the
arc at unit speed. ∎

[Wolfram, symbolic: `dX/dx₁ − 1/s = 0`, `dY/dx₁ − w/s = 0`, `speed² − 1 = 0`, all identical.]

So a chain's data curve is **drawable**: it is the first laid arc itself. This is why the chain is
"self-referential" only in the harmless sense that its data are read off a curve that was already
drawn.

### 4.3 The parabola chain in closed form

For `d₁ = 1/2`, eliminating `w` and `s` from `d₂ = X(w)`, `x₂ = Y(w)`, `s² = 1 + w²` gives the
implicit equation of the coupling curve [Wolfram, `GroebnerBasis`, `EliminationOrder`; symbolic
residual identically `0`]:

```
−25/32 − 17 d₂²/8 − d₂⁴ + 2 d₂⁶ + 5 x₂/2 − 3 d₂² x₂/2 − 10 d₂⁴ x₂ + 9 x₂²/8
      + 15 d₂² x₂² + 2 d₂⁴ x₂² − 10 x₂³ − 8 d₂² x₂³ + 8 x₂⁴ = 0 .          (4.1)
```

It has bidegree `(6, 4)`. So the last link's equation is the one-step equation
`R(w₂) − d₂ atan(w₂) = x₂` with `(d₂, x₂)` on an **algebraic** curve — no longer a free pair.

### 4.4 Legality restricts the coupling curve

The second link is legal only where `1 − d₂κ > 0` on its window. For the parabola `κ ≤ 1`, so this
is `d₂ < 1`. The coupling curve leaves this region: `d₂ = w(1 − d₁/s)` crosses `1` at

```
w₁* = 1.40760987206315754870082364128561184628543312589808036754384
x₁* = F_{1/2}(w₁*) = 1.30987839669612160670491633352633525250110935672010775080329 .
```

For `x₁ > x₁*` the chain `(d₂, x₂) = (e, f)` is **not a legal whole-curve offset** (there is a
cusp; it needs a re-based window past the cusp). The third row of §3.3 is such a case
(`d₂ = 1.17572… > 1`). This is a genuine restriction of chains: the coupling curve can run out of
the cusp-free region even when each link's *data* are P-constructible. [Wolfram]

## 5. Consequences for separation

### 5.1 Fixed-data families

> **Proposition 5.1 [Proof for the explicit chain; general case open].**
> (i) If a chain's coupling curve is a single point `(d, x)`, the chain *is* that one entry.
> (ii) The coupled parabola chain of §4 does **not** solve the parabola family `{ F_{1/2}(w) = x }`.

**Proof.** (i) is Def. 1.1. For (ii): if the chain solved the family for all `x` in an interval, its
root `w₂(x)` would equal the fixed root `w₁(x)` at each `x`. Then
`F_{d₂(x₁)}(w₁) = x₂(x₁)` and `F_{1/2}(w₁) = x₁` hold at the same `w₁`, so the analytic functions
`d₂, x₂` would satisfy

```
(1/2 − d₂(x₁)) atan(w₁) = x₂(x₁) − x₁ .
```

For the explicit coupling curve `(d₂, x₂) = (X(w₁), Y(w₁))`, this says
`d₂ = (R(w₁) − x₂)/atan(w₁)`, i.e. `X(w) = (R(w) − Y(w))/atan(w)`. At `w₁ = 1` the right side is
`0.3746…` and the left side is `0.6464…`; at `w₁ = 2` and `1/2` they also differ, and the right side
diverges as `w₁ → 0⁺`. Two analytic functions that differ at one point differ on a dense open set,
so the identity fails. At `x₁ = 1` the roots are

```
w₁ = 1.19469384349484182976872145227272… ,   w₂ = 1.42223176930502403080376196532843… ,
w₂ − w₁ = 0.227537925810182201035040513056… .
```

∎

> **Open.** Whether some *other* re-based chain with nonconstant constructible data can accidentally
> solve a fixed-data family. The identity above is one functional equation in the two data
> functions; the only solution with data fixed before the last root that we know of is the trivial
> point `(d, x)`. No proof is offered.

Thus for the natural chains **a nonconstant coupling curve rules out a fixed-data family**; the
general statement remains tied to the data-dependence gap (§5.2).

### 5.2 Parameter-dependent data = the existing open problem

When the family parameter is the earlier length, the chain is a one-step equation whose data depend
on the parameter. This is *exactly* the setting of:

- **R3 §3.9 item 3** — "`d`-varying uniformity": `d` allowed to depend on the family parameter;
- **R3 §3.9 item 2** — non-affine `x`-dependence of the box output;
- **R2 Q3.4** — "same roots for P-constructible data": `d(λ)` allowed to depend on the family
  parameter, and the question of whether `(R_C(ψ_λ) − x(λ))/ψ_λ` is P-constructible.

Chains do **not** settle any of these; they only supply explicit *examples* of parameter-dependent
data (algebraic coupling curves). The reduction is the content of Corollary 3.3. For the parabola
chain the dependence is provably **non-affine**: `d(d₂)/dx₁ = 1/s` takes the distinct values
`0.7071…, 0.4472…, 0.3162…` at `w = 1, 2, 3` (similarly `d(x₂)/dx₁ = w/s`). So a chain lands exactly
in R3 §3.9 item 2's non-affine gap, outside the affine hypothesis of Theorems 3.4–3.5. [Wolfram]

This also explains why chains are not a route to the named problems. A uniform match would require
the coupling curve's data functions to satisfy an identity in which the target root `ψ_λ` appears
(through `R_C(ψ_λ)`). A chain's data are algebraic readouts of the **first** root; the target root is
a different inverse branch. An identity between two independent inverse branches is a
transcendence/independence statement — Q3.4 again. Nothing of the kind was found (§5.4).

### 5.3 The exact status of open item 3

| question | status |
|---|---|
| Do same-word chains collapse? | **Yes**, §2 [Proof] |
| Is a re-based chain a new equation shape? | **No**, it is a one-step entry with data in `𝔽₁`, §3 [Proof] |
| Does a chain add new *numbers*? | **Yes**, parameter extension; R4 §1.2 and §3.3 [Wolfram] |
| Do chains need a depth-6 separation theorem? | **No**, Cor. 3.3 [Proof] |
| Can chains solve `tan x = λx` / beam / goat uniformly? | **Open**, reduces to Q3.4; nothing found, §5.4 |
| Can chains solve a fixed-data family? | **No for the coupled parabola chain**, Prop. 5.1(ii) [Proof]; general case **Open** |

So the honest resolution of item 3 is: **it is a reduction, not a new mechanism.** Chains are a
data-source for one-step entries; the only power they add is parameter-dependent data, which is
already the outstanding gap.

### 5.4 Named-target evidence

Two scans, both at 200-digit PSLQ with bound `10¹²`:

1. **The R4 scan, reproduced at higher precision.** Targets `tan x = x` (first two roots), the beam
   `cos x cosh x = −1` (first root), the goat `sin b − b cos b = π/2`; for each target the maps
   `{r, 1/r, tan r, tan(r/2), sinh r, tanh r, e^r, r², sin r, cos r, sec r, r − π}` were tested
   against the parabola one-step atoms `{w s, asinh w, atan w, π, 1}`. **0 hits in 48 tests.** The
   positive control `F_{1/2}(w) = 1` returns `(1,1,-1,0,-2)`. [Wolfram]
2. **Chain roots against rational data.** For the six chain roots of §3.3 (three first links,
   `(d₂,x₂) = (1/s₁, e)` and `(e, f)`), the same PSLQ test finds **no** relation. So the chain
   roots are not one-step parabola roots with rational data — the parameter-extension statement.
   [Wolfram]

`w₂` also satisfies no low-degree relation with `w₁`: PSLQ over
`{1, w₁, w₂, w₁w₂, w₂², w₁²}` returns none at 200 digits with bound `10¹²`. [Wolfram, evidence only]

**These are evidence, not results.** They say the chain roots are not values of the one-step
families at rational data; they say nothing about P-constructible data, which is Q3.4.

## 6. What would change the answer

1. **A constructor that constrains an arc endpoint to lie on another curve.** Then two lengths could
   refer to the same point, producing a genuine 2×2 system. No such constructor exists in `Defs`;
   `inter_*` only selects a point of two uncut curves (Prop. 3.4).
2. **Restricting data to be independent of the family parameter.** Then every chain collapses to a
   point of data (Prop. 5.1), and item 3 would be closed entirely. The uniform convention
   deliberately allows parameter-dependent data, which is the sole source of chain power.
3. **A proof that `(R_C(ψ_λ) − x(λ))/ψ_λ` is not P-constructible** for the named targets and any
   tower curve `C`. That would close Q3.4, and with it item 3. It is a transcendence/independence
   statement, not a branch-point count (R4 §2.1's differential-degree remark).
