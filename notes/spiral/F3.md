# F3: the spiral against graphs, power laws and Beziers in general affine position

Stream F3 of `pptc/PLAN-spiral.md` §4. Pure mathematics, no Lean. Every identity and every number
below was computed with the Wolfram MCP (50-60 digit working precision, residuals below 1e-48;
no Python). Labels: [Proof] argument written here, [Wolfram] computed, [Heuristic], [Open].
Classes: E elementary, R already reachable (construction named), N new and named, ? new and

## Findings summary

1. **Pulled-back partner shapes [Proof + Wolfram].** An affine image `M S + c` of the plain spiral
   `S: z = theta e^{i theta}` meets a curve G exactly where S meets `M^-1 (G - c)`. Rank-1 `M`
   gives a segment (E). Otherwise the partner is
   - **graph, deg <= 6**: a graph `Y' = Phat(X')` in a *Euclidean-rotated* frame, `Phat` a polynomial
     of degree <= 6. The frame angle `alpha` is the direction of the first row of `M`; the shift of the
     spiral centre, the aspect and the shear along the vertical direction are all absorbed into the
     coefficients of `Phat`. (The shear along the *other* axis is not a separate parameter: an
     oblique frame `(u, v)` is again a graph over the orthogonal coordinate, see §1.)
     The intersection equation is **`theta sin(theta - alpha) = Phat(theta cos(theta - alpha))`** (G).
   - **power law**: a *sheared, shifted* power law `Y' = a (X' - x0)^b + k X' + y0` (the shear `k` is
     not absorbed here), rotated by `alpha`.
   - **cubic Bezier**: a general cubic Bezier (the class is affine-closed), `z(t)` a complex cubic.
     Moduli: 8 real control-point coordinates minus the 2 of reparametrisation = 6.
2. **Rotation and pitch offset [Proof].** Rotating the partner by `alpha` is the same as the spiral
   `r = theta + alpha` (W's "A adds nothing" is true for *arcs*, but for an intersection with a
   fixed partner it is exactly this parameter). In every collapse below `alpha` enters only as the
   phase `t = theta - alpha` of the trig function; it never changes the class. A shift of the spiral
   centre changes the class only through the *symmetry loci* in finding 3.
3. **General collapse conditions [Proof unless marked].** Substituting `x = theta c`, `y = theta s`
   gives `sum_k theta^k F_k(c, s)` (F_k the degree-k part of the pulled-back equation, `F_k(c,s)` a
   trig polynomial of harmonics `<= k`, parity `k`). The only mechanisms that reduce this to
   something reachable are:
   - **C0, degree <= 1 (lines):** hyperbola meets a phase-shifted sine. **R** (and **E** through the
     centre).
   - **C1, mirror symmetry** of the partner in a line through the spiral centre (angle `alpha`):
     the equation becomes `Phi(theta, cos(theta - alpha)) = 0`, a *single trig function* (sine
     meets an algebraic curve `Phi`). **R iff `Phi` is itself drawable**, otherwise **?**.
   - **C2, central symmetry** about the centre: `Psi(theta, cos 2(theta - alpha))`. Same verdict.
   - **C3, focal factorisation (new, not in I1/I2):** if the distance `r` is a rational function on
     the partner curve, `F = a^2 - b^2 (x^2 + y^2)` and the equation splits into two
     equations of the form `theta a(c, s) = +-b(c, s) theta + ...`, each *one harmonic*.
     For a polynomial graph this happens **iff the graph is a parabola with its focus at the spiral
     centre** (or a line through the centre) [Proof, §2]. The same holds for the hyperbola
     (`power_law` `b = -1`) and for the ellipse. The equation is `theta (1 - e cos(theta - phi0)) = p`,
     a shifted hyperbola meeting a cosine: **R**.
   - **C4, the Bezier node.** Node at the centre plus mirror symmetry gives
     `a theta c^3 + (1 + a m) c^2 = 1`, `c = cos(theta - alpha)`. It is **R iff `1 + a m = 0`**
     (solitary node; `c = (a theta)^(-1/3)`, sine meets `power_law b = -1/3`), else **?** (loop, cusp).
   - **C5, centred conics and centred power laws `b in {0, -1}`:** R (I1/I2, unchanged by `alpha`).
   - A harmonic-bookkeeping obstruction [Proof]: for graph partners of degree `d >= 2` the
     monomials `theta e^{+-i theta}` (from the `y`-term) and `theta^d e^{+-i d theta}` (from `c_d`)
     both occur and cannot cancel. So `theta^p x (one harmonic)` never occurs for graphs of degree
     >= 2; the only escape is C1/C2/C3.
4. **Everything else is `?`** [Heuristic]. In particular the generic graph, the generic power law
   and the generic Bezier have two trig functions of `theta` (harmonics 1 and >= 2) and no drawable
   partner. Non-reducibility is not proved (F4).
5. **New R strata (relative to I1/I2):** parabola, hyperbola and ellipse with a focus at the spiral
   centre (conic condition `B^2 = 8 f A` in I1's master equation, 2 real conditions); the
   solitary-node Bezier with any mirror angle and aspect. New `?` stratum: the **focal cubic Bezier**
   `z(t) = kappa (t - zeta)^2 (t - r)`: its equation is "linear in `psi` equals cubic in `cot psi`".
6. **(i) Sign-flipped sine [Proof].** `theta sin theta = -sin(theta cos theta)` has no non-trivial root
   for any `theta > 0`; the "+" case has exactly one (`0.75986...`), and it lies in the first
   quarter-turn. Mechanism and proof in §4.
7. **(ii) T1/T2/T6 [Proof + Wolfram].** No general-position configuration of a graph, power law or
   Bezier contains infinitely many roots of T1 (`lambda != 0`), T2 or T6. Stronger than I1: *any*
   algebraic partner would have to contain a specific irreducible quartic with a double point at
   the spiral centre, and none of the three families (nor lines/conics) can. §5.

## 1. Pullback and the normal equation (G) [Proof + Wolfram]

Let `T(z) = M z + c`, rows `r1, r2` of `M`, `c = (c1, c2)`. T(S) meets the graph `y = P(x)` iff
`(Mz + c)_2 = P((Mz + c)_1)`, i.e. `l2(z) = P(l1(z))` with independent affine functionals `l1, l2`.
Take the unit vector `n = r1/|r1| = (cos alpha, sin alpha)` and `m = (-sin alpha, cos alpha)`,
`X' = n.z`, `Y' = m.z`. Then `l1 = |r1| X' + c1` and
`l2 = (r2.n) X' + (r2.m) Y' + c2`, so
`Y' = Phat(X') := [P(|r1| X' + c1) - c2 - (r2.n) X'] / (r2.m)`
(`r2.m != 0` is the independence of `l1, l2`). On the spiral `X' = theta cos(theta - alpha)`,
`Y' = theta sin(theta - alpha)`. This is why an *oblique* or *non-uniform* image still gives a graph:
the direction `ker l1` is the single-valued direction, and the tilt of the other axis is a linear
term of `Phat`.

[Wolfram check] `M = [[3/2, 1], [-1/2, 2]]`, `c = (1/2, -1/3)`, `P(x) = x^3/10 - x/2 + 1/3`:
`alpha = 0.58800260354756755124561108062508542760`; the four roots of the *geometric* condition in
(0, 4 pi] are 0.48581545641569971750625936453326618763, 4.5847282573918678561788859244868621772,
8.0742658344389313405094615062023878702, 11.232298987024016040489960923569652627, and each satisfies
(G) with the derived `Phat` to residual below 1e-61.

Power law `v = a u^b` (`u > 0`): the same steps give `Y' = [a (|r1| X' + c1)^b - c2 - (r2.n) X'] / (r2.m)`,
a shifted power law **plus a linear term**; the linear term is the shear `k`, a genuine extra parameter
(it changes the harmonic content: `theta (sin t - k cos t)` has phase `delta != 0`). For the
parabola (`b = 2`, `1/2`) the shear is absorbed in `Phat` again. Bezier: pulled back, it is a
Bezier. Degenerate `M` (rank 1) maps the spiral onto a segment: the intersection with the partner is
a polynomial equation, class **E**.

Parameter counts [Heuristic bookkeeping]: graph of degree d: `d + 2` (coefficients + `alpha`);
the real `Phat` coefficients are not fully free, since `Phat` comes from rational `P` by 5 real
affine parameters (`A`, scale, two shifts, shear), so for `d = 6` two of the seven coefficients
are tied to rational data. This does not change any structural statement below.

## 2. Collapse analysis

### 2.1 Mirror and central symmetry [Proof]

If the partner is invariant under the Euclidean reflection in the line through the spiral centre at
angle `alpha`, then in the rotated frame `F(X', Y') = H(X', Y'^2)` and the equation is
`H(theta c, theta^2 (1 - c^2)) = 0`, `c = cos(theta - alpha)`: a polynomial in `(theta, c)`.
For a graph this is `Phat` even: `theta s = sum c_{2i} theta^{2i} (1 - s^2)^i`, `s = sin(theta - alpha)`.
For `Phat` odd (central symmetry) one gets, after dividing by `cos t`,
`tan t = E(theta^2 cos^2 t)`, equivalently `cos 2t = (1 - E^2)/(1 + E^2)` with
`E = E(theta^2 (1 + cos 2t)/2)`, a polynomial in `(theta, cos 2t)` (sign-spurious roots removed by
`restrict`). [Wolfram: the cos 2t identity holds at the root 0.4141914568774373... to 50 digits.]
The curve `Phi = 0` carrying the trig function is, for a quadratic: `x y = c2 x^2 (1 - y^2) + c0`
(quartic; cubic `C_k` when `c0 = 0`). It is not a poly graph, power law, ellipse or Bezier
(asymptote/points at infinity argument of I2/`sheared-sine` §3d). So C1/C2 give **?**, with `alpha`
only the phase of the sine.

### 2.2 The focal stratum [Proof + Wolfram]

Write the intersection as a quadratic in `theta` with trig coefficients (the shape of I1's master
equation). If the partner has `r = sqrt(x^2 + y^2)` rational *on the curve*, i.e. `r = Q(point)`
with `Q` rational, then `F = (r - Q)(r + Q)` in the ring `R[x, y, r]/(r^2 - x^2 - y^2)` and on the
spiral `r = theta` the equation is `theta = +-Q(theta c, theta s)`.

- **Polynomial graphs [Proof].** `r` is rational on `y = Phat(x)` iff `x^2 + Phat(x)^2 = Q(x)^2` with
  `Q` a polynomial, i.e. `(Q - Phat)(Q + Phat) = x^2`; unique factorisation gives exactly
  `Phat = (x^2/lambda - lambda)/2` (parabola with focus at the origin: `c1 = 0`, `4 c0 c2 = -1`) or
  `Phat = (1/lambda - lambda) x/2` (a line through the centre). Nothing of degree 3..6.
- **Parabola with focus at the centre, in the rotated frame [Wolfram, exact]:**
  `Phat = c x^2 - 1/(4c)`  gives  `(2 c theta s + 1)^2 - (2 c theta)^2 = 0`, so
  `sin(theta - alpha) = +-1 - 1/(2 c theta)`: the **sine meets the shifted hyperbola
  `y = +-1 - 1/(2 c x)`** (both are base curves, `power_law b = -1` translated). This is
  `theta (1 - sin t) = 1/(2c)`, i.e. "distance to the focus = distance to the directrix".
- **Hyperbola and ellipse [Wolfram].** `x y = a` with the centre at the focus `(sqrt(2a), sqrt(2a))`
  (`(x+1)(y+1) = 1/2`): `(theta c + 1)(theta s + 1) - 1/2 = (1/2)(theta (c + s) + 1 - theta)(theta (c + s) + 1 + theta)`
  exactly. Ellipse with a focus at the centre: `theta (1 - e cos(theta - phi0)) = p`.
  Cosine meets `y = (1 - p/x)/e`: **R**.
- **In I1's master equation `Re(A th^2 e^{2i th} + B th e^{i th}) + m th^2 + f = 0` the focal
  stratum is `B^2 = 8 f A` (with the sign normalisation `m + |A| > 0`, `f < 0`).** Two real
  conditions, codim 2, same codimension as I1's R strata `A = 0` and `B = 0`. I1 classed the generic
  conic as `?` and listed no focal case. [Wolfram: with `A = -|A| e^{-2 i phi0}`,
  `B = -2 kappa p e e^{-i phi0}`, `kappa = m + |A|`, `p^2 = -f/kappa`, `e^2 = 2|A|/kappa`, the master form
  equals `kappa (theta^2 - (p + e theta cos(theta - phi0))^2)` identically (three test points, residual 0
  at 78 digits) and `B^2 - 8 f A = 0`.]
  Why no other factorisation: asking `M theta^2 + N theta + f` (`M = m + Re A e^{2it}`, `N = Re B e^{it}`)
  to split into factors `(u_i + v_i cos(t - delta_i)) theta + b_i` forces either `u_i = 0` (a
  degenerate pair of lines) or `delta_1 = delta_2`, `u_1 = u_2`, `v_1 = -v_2` (focal) [Proof sketch].
- Higher-degree partners: `r` rational on the curve is the same as `x(t)^2 + y(t)^2 = Q(t)^2` for
  the parametrisation; with `w = x + i y` a complex polynomial this holds iff every nonreal pair
  `(zeta, conj zeta)` of roots of `w` has even *total* multiplicity (real roots are free). For a
  degree-3 Bezier this leaves, beyond lines, only `w = kappa (t - zeta)^2 (t - r)` ("focal cubic",
  `zeta` nonreal, `r` real) and, at degree 2, `w = kappa (t - zeta)^2` (focal parabola). For power
  laws: never (`x^2 + y^2` has simple nonreal roots), except the `b = 2`, `1/2` and `-1` conic cases
  above (these are the parabola/hyperbola).

### 2.3 The focal cubic Bezier: a new `?` stratum [Wolfram]

`z(t) = e^{i gamma} (t - i)^2 (t - r)`, `r` real. Put `psi = arccot t`. Then
`z/|z| = +- e^{i gamma} (t - i)/(t + i) = +- e^{i (gamma - 2 psi)}` and `|z| = (1 + t^2) |t - r|`, so
`theta = |z|` and `theta = gamma - 2 psi + pi eps + 2 pi k` give

**`(gamma + 2 pi k + pi eps - 2 psi) sin^3 psi = sign(t - r) (cos psi - r sin psi)`**

(`eps = [t < r]`), equivalently `gamma' - 2 psi = (1 + u^2)(u - r)` with `u = cot psi`:
**an angle that is linear in `psi` equals a cubic polynomial in `cot psi`**. Harmonics 1 and 3 on the
left, 1 on the right: two harmonics, and one trig function `cot`, not a base curve. Verified:
`gamma = 3/10`, `r = 1/2`, the residual of the reduced equation at the roots of the geometric condition
is below 1e-49 over ~90 roots. Roots in (0, 4 pi]: 0.43457526058086319367611824868531954,
4.7612298915709385997928871538065680, 5.5721482699175577628108789040730898,
10.705329620847630937874260854457389, 12.061627697404765873726837391216359. Class **?** [Heuristic].
It is *not* T1/T2 (cubic in `cot`, not linear), consistent with §5.

### 2.4 The cubic Bezier node [Proof + Wolfram]

Up to affine maps the nodal-type Beziers are `y^2 = x^2 (x + m)` (loop `m > 0`, cusp `m = 0`,
solitary `m < 0`; `m` normalised so the node is the origin). With the node at the spiral centre and
general linear maps `l1, l2`: `F_2 = l2^2 - m l1^2`, `F_3 = -l1^3`, equation
`l2(c,s)^2 - m l1(c,s)^2 = theta l1(c,s)^3`.

- It is **one trig function** iff `l2(c,s)^2 = q^2 sin^2(theta - alpha)` where `l1 = p cos(theta - alpha)`:
  the two forms are orthogonal, i.e. the Bezier is *Euclidean mirror symmetric* about a line through the
  node (C1). Then, with `a = q^2/p^2`: **`a theta c^3 + (1 + a m) c^2 = 1`**, `c = cos(theta - alpha)`,
  `c` of the sign of `x' - m`; no condition on `alpha`.
- `1 + a m = 0`: `c = (a theta)^(-1/3)`, **R** (cosine meets `power_law b = -1/3`, phase-shifted).
  `1 + a m != 0`: `theta = (1 - (1 + a m) c^2)/(a c^3)`, cosine meets a Laurent graph that is not a base
  curve: **?**.
- No mirror symmetry (`l1` not orthogonal to `l2`): two phases `phi1 != phi2`; the equation is
  `theta = Psi(tan theta)` with `Psi` algebraic of degree 2 over the field of `tan`: **?** (not T1:
  the weights differ by parity, F_2 harmonics {0,2} against F_3 harmonics {1,3}).
- [Wolfram, `a = 2`, `alpha = 1/3`] solitary `m = -1/2`:
  0.53009750171063552459644995458568147763, 0.97706137327555626215873061647285172668,
  5.5117320044494359374302930146763760509, 7.7750993361044427318727397104667626896,
  11.686218401184870405969405561385368687 (all satisfy `y'^2 = 2 x'^2 (x' - 1/2)` to 1e-60).
  Loop `m = 1`: 1.4038808738018716369353167194912708589, 5.4309590624836728920132743542960805496,
  7.8351532770619116593281896694000503520, 11.646270835310393585435404653548349077.

### 2.5 Power laws in general position

- `b = 1`, `0`: lines, E/R. `b = -1`: a hyperbola; *centred* (centre at its centre, any `alpha`, any

- `b = 2` and `1/2`: parabolas (the `1/2` curve is the half of a sideways parabola). Classified with
  graphs: focal R, centre-on-axis (even) ?, otherwise ?.
- `b` other: with centre at the origin of the power law and no shear: `tan t = a (theta cos t)^{b-1}`;
  single harmonic times a power of `theta` only for `b in {1, 0, -1}` (I2). With shear `k != 0` the
  left side `theta (sin t - k cos t) = R theta sin(t - delta)` and the right side `(theta cos t)^b` have
  different phases: two functions, **?**. Centre off the origin or `x0 != 0`: **?**. Never focal.
- Windows: `X' > x0` confines `cos t` and so the roots to one quarter-turn per period.

## 4. (i) Why `theta sin theta = -sin(theta cos theta)` has no non-trivial root [Proof]

`g_s(theta) = theta sin theta - s sin(theta cos theta)`, `s = +1` (I3's "+") or `-1`.

*Step 1, trivial roots.* `g_s(m pi) = 0` for all integers `m`.

*Step 2, the region `|tan theta| > 1`.* There `theta |sin theta| > theta |cos theta| >= |sin(theta cos theta)|`
(because `|sin u| <= |u|`), so `g_s != 0` for both signs. No roots away from `m pi`.

*Step 3, `theta = m pi + eps`, `|eps| <= pi/4`, `m >= 1`.* Put `w = eps - (m pi + eps)(1 - cos eps)`.
Then, exactly, `sin(theta cos theta) = sin w` and
**`g_s = (-1)^m theta sin eps - s sin w`** [Wolfram: identity checked for `m = 0..5`, `s = +-1`, four
values of `eps`, difference 0 at 78 digits]. A root needs `|sin w| = (m pi + eps)|sin eps|`, so it is
excluded as soon as `|w| < (m pi + eps)|sin eps|` (since `|sin w| <= |w|`):
- `eps > 0`: `w <= eps` and `w >= -(m pi + eps)(1 - cos eps)`. `eps < (m pi + eps) sin eps` because
  `m pi + eps >= pi` and `sin eps >= (2 sqrt2/pi) eps`; `(m pi + eps)(1 - cos eps) - eps < (m pi + eps) sin eps`
  because `1 - cos eps < sin eps` on `(0, pi/4]`. So `|w| < (m pi + eps) sin eps`.
- `eps = -delta < 0`: `|w| = delta + (m pi - delta)(1 - cos delta)`, and we need this `< (m pi - delta) sin delta`,
  i.e. `delta < (m pi - delta)(sin delta + cos delta - 1)`. Since `m pi - delta >= pi - delta`, it suffices that
  `k(delta) = (pi - delta)(sin delta + cos delta - 1)/delta > 1` on `(0, pi/4]`. [Wolfram: `k` decreases from
  `pi` to `1.24264...` at `pi/4`, so `k > 1`.]
So for `m >= 1` there is **no non-trivial root for either sign**.

*Step 4, `m = 0`.* `theta = eps in (0, pi/4]`: `g_s = eps sin eps - s sin(eps cos eps)`. For `s = -1` both
terms are positive: no root. For `s = +1` the sign changes: near 0, `eps sin eps ~ eps^2 < sin(eps cos eps) ~ eps`
and at `eps = pi/4` the order reverses, giving exactly the I3 root.

**The "+" root is `theta = 0.75986237989815059970609525812795898651` [Wolfram, residual 2.5e-61]**, point
`(0.550847653067879502397568258793604425, 0.523409686091201799439600832531792978)`.

**Geometry.** At the origin the sine has slope +1 and the spiral is tangent to the `x`-axis
(`y ~ x^2`); the spiral first lies *below* `y = sin x` and then rises above it, so there is one more
crossing in the first quarter-turn. The "-" sine `y = -sin x` runs below the spiral's initial
arc (the spiral's `y > 0` for `0 < theta < pi`), and by step 2 no crossing can happen later. At every
`theta = m pi` the spiral has steep slope `~ m pi >= pi > 1 = |sin'|`, the crossing is transversal
and isolated, and by step 3 nothing else survives. The reflection argument of I3 ("only two inequivalent
equations") is consistent: the half-spiral `theta < 0` is the mirror image `x -> -x`, and
`g_+(-theta) = g_-(theta)`, so the *two-sided* spiral meets `y = sin x` in the non-trivial root
`0.75986...` and nowhere else. The "asymmetry" is entirely the sign of the sine's slope at the origin
against the first quarter-turn of the spiral, not a property of large `theta`. I3's local expansion
`eps = 2(1 - s m pi)/(m pi)` (order 1, outside the local regime) is simply the spurious solution of a
linearisation; step 3 uses the exact bound instead [Heuristic reading of I3's remark].
Scan to `10 pi` [Wolfram, step 0.0005]: `g_+` has zeros `0.242 pi` and `m pi`; `g_-` only `m pi`.

[Heuristic] Same mechanism for other amplitudes/frequencies with matched pitch (`omega b = 1`): the
bound `|sin w| <= |w|` kills every `m >= 1` root when `A omega b <= ~1`; the near-origin crossing exists iff
the sine's initial slope has the sign making it start above the spiral. Not proved for general `(A, omega, phi, D)`.

## 5. (ii) T1/T2/T6 in general position [Proof + Wolfram]

**Claim.** Fix a spiral image and a partner from the families above (any affine position). They cannot
share infinitely many points whose `theta` solves T1 (`tan theta = lambda theta + mu`, `lambda != 0`), T2
(`(lambda theta + mu) tan theta = 1`) or T6 (`theta tan(theta - beta) = b`, `b != 0`), *including the
versions with a shifted argument `tan(theta - beta)`* (that is a rotation: the curves below are rotated).

*Proof.* At a T1 root, the spiral point `(theta cos theta, theta sin theta)` satisfies
`y/x = tan theta = lambda r + mu` with `r = theta = sqrt(x^2 + y^2)`. So it lies on
`Gamma_1: (y - mu x)^2 = lambda^2 x^2 (x^2 + y^2)`. Likewise T2 gives
`Gamma_2: lambda^2 (x^2 + y^2) y^2 = (x - mu y)^2` and T6 (in the frame rotated by `beta`)
`Gamma_6: Y^2 (X^2 + Y^2) = b^2 X^2`. Each is a quartic with
- **multiplicity-2 point at the origin** (lowest form `(y - mu x)^2`, `(x - mu y)^2`, `-b^2 X^2`) [Wolfram: series];
- **irreducible over C**: as a quadratic in `y` (resp. `x`, `X`), the discriminant is
  `4 lambda^2 x^4 (1 + mu^2 - lambda^2 x^2)` (resp. `4 lambda^2 y^4 (1 + mu^2 - lambda^2 y^2)`,
  `-4 Y^4 (Y^2 - b^2)`), which is never a square in the coefficient field (distinct roots). [Wolfram: `Factor`
  over `Q(lambda, mu)` and over `Q(i)` gives the quartic unchanged.]

If the partner `G'` shared infinitely many of these points, Bezout forces a common component, and
`Gamma` irreducible means `Gamma subset G'`. Now every candidate is excluded:
- **polynomial graph** (any degree, any affine position): `l2 - P(l1) = 0` is smooth in the affine plane
  (gradient has a nonzero `l2` component), while `Gamma` is singular at the spiral centre. Contradiction.
- **power law**, Zariski closure `y^q = a^q x^p` (`b = p/q` lowest terms, `b > 0`): singular point of
  multiplicity `min(p, q)` at the origin only, degree `max(p, q)`. Double point and degree 4 would need
  `{p, q} = {2, 4}`, not coprime. For `b < 0` the affine part is smooth. Contradiction.
- **cubic Bezier**: the image is a cubic (or lower), degree 3 < 4. Contradiction.
- (Lines and conics: degree < 4, so I1's result is the special case.)

This is stronger than I1: it replaces its coefficient-comparison (`Solve` giving the zero solution)
by a degree/singularity argument and covers **every algebraic partner of degree < 4 or smooth at the centre**.
The only way in would be a partner that contains `Gamma_1/2/6` as a component; none of
`poly_graph`, `power_law`, `cubic_bezier`, `ellipse`, `rectangle` has such a component, and neither do their
closure operations (affine maps preserve smoothness and degree).

**What T-like equations do appear?** The two new tangent-flavoured equations in this note are not T1/T2/T6:
`theta = Psi(tan theta)` for the nodal cubic without mirror symmetry (§2.4), and
"linear = cubic in `cot psi`" for the focal cubic (§2.3). A circular cubic with `F = l'(z) + |z|^2 l''(z)`
(central symmetry, `F_1` and `F_3 = |z|^2 l''`) would give `theta^2 = -l'(c,s)/l''(c,s)`, a quadratic Mobius
in `tan` and so a T-flavoured family, but no base curve is a circular cubic [Heuristic]. T4/T5
(`sin x = +-tanh x`, goat) were not tested here [Open].

## 6. Negatives and what the failing paths give

- No general-position graph, power law or Bezier gives T1, T2, T6 (§5).
- Non-focal, non-mirror, non-central positions: two trig functions, no reduction.
- Even/odd symmetric graphs: one trig function, but the partner `Phi = 0` is a non-drawable algebraic curve
  (quartic or cubic for `d = 2`, degree `<= 13` for `d = 6`). The failing path still gives an **exact
  single-function equation**, so the only open question is drawability, which is F4's.
- The pitch offset `alpha` (rotation) is a free phase and never turns `?` into R.
- Windows: all listed roots are `<= 4 pi`; the focal strata are R, so their roots are reachable without
  the spiral regardless of window.

## Other things noticed

- **I1 amendment.** The conic table lists the generic conic as `?`. The focal stratum (`B^2 = 8 f A`) is R;
  it is the polar form `r (1 - e cos(phi - phi0)) = p` of a Kepler orbit with the focus at the spiral centre,
  hence "spiral meets a conic at its focus". Dottie/Kepler (I1 §5) are *not* focal cases; they are the
  `A = 0` stratum.
- **W amendment.** "`A` in `r = A + B theta` adds nothing" holds for arcs only. For intersections with a fixed
  partner `A` is the rotation of the partner (finding 2) and is a free phase.
- **Cheap R generator.** For any partner for which `r` is a rational function of the point, the spiral
  equation is `theta = Q(point)`; with `Q` linear in `x, y` this is always a hyperbola meeting a sine.
  Polar curves `r = a + b cos phi` (limacons) would also give `theta = a + b cos t` (a line meets a cosine) but
  are quartics, outside the base set.
- **Exact trivial root.** Any partner through the centre gives the root `theta = 0` (the shared point).
  `restrict` with a rational window removes it, same as I3's `theta = m pi`.
- **Count asymmetry.** With a focus-centred hyperbola the root list interleaves two branches (both factors
  contribute); only the factor with `X > 0, Y > 0` belongs to the `power_law` branch. Restrict handles it.
- **Counts of the family.** The parabola alone has three distinct R/non-R positions (focus, vertex/axis,
  generic), the hyperbola two R positions (centre, focus), the nodal cubic one R position (solitary node on a
  mirror axis); the coincidence of codimension 2 for lines, centres and foci in the master equation suggests
  the same structure in higher degree partners, but I did not find a focal-type R stratum beyond conics and
  the focal cubic is `?`.
- **Rational-coefficient caveat.** `poly_graph` has rational coefficients; the pulled-back `Phat` has the
  P-constructible data of the affine map. Focality is a metric condition (`c0 c2 = -1/4`), reachable because
  the affine map is arbitrary, but not for a fixed rational `P` without stretching.
- **Possible follow-up.** The focal cubic Bezier equation looks like a one-trig-function class (`cot`) with a
  clean parametrisation; worth giving to L/F4. The class `theta = Psi(tan theta)` for the unsymmetric nodal
  cubic is the closest the catalogue gets to T-type without being T1/T2/T6.
