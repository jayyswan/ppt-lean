## Framing

Two scopes. (i) Rational nonics: enough for real algebraic numbers of degree 9 over Q; rational-shape families are allowed. (ii) Over P: needed because a real algebraic number can have high degree over Q but degree 9 over P. **Session 49: the goal is the statement that P is real closed; each allowed step says that P has no extension of some degree d with a given number r of real places, and the open cases are exactly the (d, r) pairs not yet excluded. After session 51: excluded are d ≤ 8 (all r) and d = 9 with r ≤ 3; nothing else.**

Existence is enough: y is P-constructible as soon as some target variety has a P-point. Uniform obstructions say nothing about existence for a given K. Versality is not detected on Sylow subgroups (arXiv 2607.25118). **Over P, versality of a family is not a decidable criterion at all short of the goal (S41.1): it is vacuous if the goal holds.** The repo arrives at the same frontier and calls it (PRC₈): every smooth variety over Q (twisted by K) with a smooth real point has a P-point. No literature on it; the nearest results are Q^tr PRC (Moret-Bailly, Pop), the open ampleness of Q^ab, Q^solv (S41.2), and the solvable-points / resolvent-degree literature, all without reality control (S42.4). **Session 43 sharpened the frontier to one surface per field: the goal ⇔ every twisted Chebyshev moment surface M_{n,K} (fact 7, q = 1) has a P-point (Theorem 43.1).** Sessions 37–40 sharpened this for lines: both line components of L_135 are Tschirnhaus varieties of explicit rational 2-parameter families (Engine S (9,5) and Engine O), so a P-line exists iff K is a P-specialisation of one of them. Session 42 closed the linear-slice routes from X into W: every such route ends with a 0-dimensional scheme of degree ≥ 20 with full monodromy, unless it passes through a curve in L_135 or Y4. **Session 44: for r ≤ 3 the classical reduction to x⁹ = quartic goes through with steps ≤ 8 (S44.2); the real place blocks it for r = 5 (on lines) and r ≥ 7 (Rolle).** **Session 45: the real obstructions on linear spaces all come from the trace form's negative index s (S45.1).** **Session 46: the same index count reaches half of the conics (Lemma C); the conics through two P-points form a degree-12 scheme with monodromy S_12.** **Session 47: its codimension-2 degenerations are real-feasible but only move the problem.** **Session 48: where a chain exists with open real conditions, it can be made rigorous and uniform in K by one interval certificate per signature.** **Sessions 49–50 extended the chain to other degrees and to padded algebras, but (session 51) the endpoint there is V_4, which feeds x^n = (degree n − 5), not a toolkit engine. The engine x^n = quartic has a 5-dimensional Tschirnhaus variety in every degree, so higher degree or padding only makes it harder. What decides the r ≤ 7 nonics most directly is a toolkit question: whether quintic polynomial graphs are constructible (S51.3).**

Over C the varieties Q4, Q5, D, F(Q4), L_135, W and the conic family C do not depend on K. On trace-zero z in C^9: Q4 = 6 p_4 − p_2^2 and Q5 = 18 p_5 − 5 p_2 p_3. P-points of a family over K are points of its twist by the Galois action of f; for a totally real f the twist is trivial over R, so real points of the twist are the real points found numerically. More generally, for any signature r the real points of every trace-defined variety are those of the model ℝ^r × ℂ^s (S44.2, S48.1), and for an étale algebra K × B those of the model of K × B (S50.2).

Sources of P-points: degree ≤ 8 varieties with a real point; 0-dimensional schemes of degree 9 with ≤ 3 real points (S48.1); conics and quadrics; fibres of the fact families and repo engines, and of Engine O (S37.2, S38.2); Hasse-principle varieties; genus-one curves with period 2^a 3^b or CM (session 24); curves with a chain of degree ≤ 8 maps to P^1 (repo Theorem G); P-lines on X and dense P-points on L_135(R) from the odd-Tschirnhaus chain (S25.1); curves on a quartic del Pezzo over P whose pencil has a real (2,2) singular member (S44.2, and the conic curve of S50.1), and in degree n, C_Q in a (2,2) complete intersection whose pencil has a real member of index ≥ 2 (S49.2); P-points of V_3 from P-planes through two P-points of V_3 (a quartic step, S47.1), and V_3(P) dense at smooth real points (S48.1), for étale algebras too (S50.2); P-points of V_4 in degrees 10–12 (S49.3, S50.1), useful only with degree-(n − 5) graphs.
