## 0r. Session 41 (Nov 18): results

Topic: priority item 1 (the dimension question: can the curve-in-L_135 program succeed at all?), with the literature on L and P that the plan asked for, plus first counts for item 2. No computation; no scripts.

**S41.1 (versality over P is not a usable criterion: it is the goal itself).** *unchecked* (elementary, complete).
- Let 𝓕 be any family of nonic algebras over a P-variety T (any dimension, even a single point). For a signature r ≥ 1 compare **(V_r)** every field of degree 9 over P with r real places is a P-specialisation of 𝓕, and **(N_r)** no field of degree 9 over P has r real places (the degree-9, signature-r case of the goal).
- (N_r ⇒ V_r) is vacuous, for every 𝓕. (V_r ⇒ N_r) holds whenever every P-specialisation of 𝓕 with r real roots has P-constructible roots (for Engine O: T2 when r = 9, S37.4; the D_K route when r = 5 and Q4|line has a real root, S39.4): a real root of a specialisation lies in P, which is impossible in a field of degree 9.
- So **any proof that some family is not versal over P would exhibit a nonic field over P with a real place, i.e. disprove the goal.** The heuristic "a 2-parameter family cannot be versal, since ed(A_9), ed(S_9) ≥ 4" is not just unproven over P: if the goal holds, it is false for every family. The curve-in-L_135 program can be neither retired nor confirmed by dimension, thin-set or counting arguments.
- Rational nonics (scope (i)), same logic: if the goal holds, P is real closed, so K_0 ⊗ P is determined by its signature r, and it is a P-specialisation of 𝓕 iff 𝓕 has a separable real member with r real roots (real chambers are open, and P ⊇ ℚ is dense). **Corollary: the only versality obstructions consistent with the goal are real-signature obstructions** (the image of 𝓕(ℝ) in {1, 3, 5, 7, 9}). For Engine O that image is {1, 3, 5, 9} (S39.3), so it can never serve S_9, r = 7; for r ∈ {5, 9} no obstruction can be proved.
- What does distinguish programs is **mechanism**: a construction of P-points from the primitives (facts 1–7, T0–T7) that works for a given K. Engine O versality has none (S41.3).

**S41.2 (what is known about L and P; literature).** Elementary facts *unchecked* (one line each); literature as cited.
- **L and P are not Hilbertian**: x³ − t is reducible for every t (real cube roots lie in L), and x² − t for every t > 0. So Hilbert-irreducibility and thin-set arguments (the usual source of "a d-parameter family misses most fields") do not exist over L or P. Q^solv is not Hilbertian either (it is closed under radicals).
- Whether Q^ab or Q^solv is **ample (large)** is open (Bary-Soroker–Fehm, "Open problems in the theory of ample fields", arXiv 1106.1310, Question I; restated as open in later papers on large fields). Nothing is known about L or P.
- The closest known theorem of PRC type: **Q^tr (the totally real numbers) is PRC** (Moret-Bailly 1989; Pop 1996; elementary proof by Green–Pop–Roquette 1995). Its proofs produce points over totally real fields with no control of the Galois group, so they don't give P-points.
- **Resolvent degree:** no nontrivial lower bound for RD(n) is known; it is open whether RD(n) = 1 for all n, even over ℂ (Farb–Wolfson; Wolfson, "Tschirnhaus transformations after Hilbert", arXiv 2001.06515, which also makes rigorous Hilbert's RD(9) ≤ 4 via the 27 lines). P is closed under accessory irrationalities of degree ≤ 8 with a real root and under 1-parameter functions (trinomials), so the relevant measure for "can a few-parameter family reach every nonic over P" is a real, bounded-accessory analogue of resolvent degree, not essential dimension. Essential dimension at a prime gives nothing either: the primes dividing |S_9| are 2, 3, 5 and 7, and P has accessory extensions of each of these degrees.
- PRC with a unique ordering does not by itself imply real closed (real projective groups such as ℤ/2 ∗ F̂_ω occur as absolute Galois groups of PRC fields; *unchecked* recollection of Haran–Jarden), so (PRC₈) gives the goal only together with P's closure properties, as in the repo.

**S41.3 (the twisted surface of Engine O is of general type).** *unchecked* (orbifold computation).
- The P-points of Y_K := Isom(K, generic Engine O member), a variety over P(1,2,3), are exactly the (U : a : b) with G ≅ K. Over P̄, Y_K is the Galois closure X̃ → P(1,2,3) (degree 9!, connected since the monodromy is S_9, S39.2).
- Branch data (S40.1): Δ_h (weight 6, inertia of order 2), A (weight 6, order 14), B (weight 5, order 2); K_{P(1,2,3)} = O(−6). Orbifold canonical class: −6 + 6·½ + 6·13/14 + 5·½ ≈ +5.07 > 0 (weight units), so X̃ is of general type (modulo the singularities over A ∩ B ∩ Δ_h and at the orbifold points).
- This is not an obstruction: the Galois-closure curve of the trinomial family is hyperbolic too (χ_orb = 2 − (8/9 + 7/8 + ½) < 0 for k = 1), yet fact 2 gives its P-points by fiat. It says only that Y_K is not unirational, so no geometric mechanism gives P-points on it; Theorem G could apply only to special K-dependent curves in Y_K.

**S41.4 (item 2: first counts; no single-curve route to W of degree ≤ 8 outside the curve program).** *unchecked* (expected dimensions, dim = −K·C + (n − 3)(1 − g)).
- L_135 is a Calabi–Yau 5-fold: rational curves of every degree d form 2-dimensional families, none through a general point; curves of genus ≥ 1 have expected dimension ≤ 0. Q4 restricted to a rational curve of degree d has degree 4d, which is ≤ 8 only for lines and conics. **Inside L_135 the curve program (lines → Engine S, Engine O; conics → C_b^gen) is the only rational-curve route to W with steps of degree ≤ 8.**
- X = {p1 = p3 = 0} is Fano of index 5 (lines through a general point form a 3-dimensional family; this is what T3 uses). A P-rational curve Γ ⊂ X meets W (codimension 2 in X) only under one condition on Γ, unless Γ lies in L_135 or in Y4 := X ∩ {Q4 = 0}. In Y4 (Fano of index 1) P-lines are excluded for r = 9 (Theorem F), and p5 has degree 5d ≥ 10 on rational curves of degree d ≥ 2. So every route has the shape: a P-rational family of curves in X whose incidence with W (a resultant condition) gets a P-point by further steps of degree ≤ 8.
- For lines through a fixed P-point P ∈ X (directions v in B_P, a (1,2,3) complete intersection 3-fold of degree 6), the incidence is **R_20(v) = Res_κ(Q4(P + κv), p5(P + κv)), of degree 20 in v whether P is general, in L_135 or in Y4** (a vanishing constant term only splits off a constant factor). This is S25.4 again, now as the main line of item 2. (Degree confirmed numerically in S42.1.)
- Dead end checked: for H = ℓ·Q4 + μ·p5 (ℓ linear), X ∩ H ∩ {p5 = 0} = W ∪ (L_135 ∩ {ℓ = 0}), but on a line in X ∩ H one has p5 = −ℓ·Q4/μ, so its roots in W are the roots of Q4|line: the trick needs lines 4-secant to W, which is harder.

**S41.5 (decision and consequences for the plan).**
- **Item 1 is closed.** The curve-in-L_135 program cannot be retired by an obstruction (S41.1), but it has no mechanism for producing P-points on the general-type surfaces Y_K (S41.3), so it is **parked**: Engine O versality, the C_b^gen engine test and all family comparisons wait until such a mechanism appears. Recorded criterion: a family is usable for signature r only if its real members reach r.
- **Item 2 becomes the main line**, in the sharpened form of S41.4: a T3-style chain into W through the degree-20 incidence on B_P, looking for P-rational curves in B_P (or P-rational ways to move P) along which R_20 has a factor of degree ≤ 8 with a real root.
- Item 3 ((PRC₈) head-on) remains; the literature (S41.2) offers PRC results of Q^tr type only, without Galois control.

## 0s. Session 42 (Nov 18): results

Topic: priority item 1 after session 41 (the R_20 chain into W), steps (a)–(c), plus the literature check of priority item 2 ("points over solvable extensions").

**S42.1 (R_20 has degree 20).** [numerical] `r20deg.py` (60-digit mpmath).
- Along a line v = v0 + t·v1 in the N_P-directions, R_20(v) = Res_κ(Q4(P + κv), p5(P + κv)) (9 × 9 Sylvester determinant) interpolated at 31 points has coefficients of relative size 10⁻⁴ … 10⁻² up to t²⁰ and ≤ 10⁻⁵⁴ above, for P generic in X, P ∈ L_135 and P ∈ Y4. So R_20 is a form of degree exactly 20 in v in all three cases (S41.4 confirmed).

**S42.2 (the T3 cone slices: 60 points of W, monodromy S_60).** [numerical] `w60.py`, `mono60.py`.
- Setting. For P ∈ X let N_P/P be the 6-dimensional direction space (Σv = 0, Σ P_i² v_i = 0, modulo P) and Q_P(v) = Σ P_i v_i² on it (well defined mod P). For an isotropic plane Π of Q_P (a maximal isotropic subspace: over ℂ always; over P when Q_P has signature (3,3), which happened for 3 of 5 random real P), the 3-space Λ = ⟨P, Π⟩ satisfies p3(sP + w) = p3(w), so **X ∩ Λ is the cone with vertex P over the plane cubic {p3 = 0} ⊂ Π**: every direction of that cubic is a T3 direction (a line through P in X). Then **W ∩ Λ = {p3(w) = 0, Q4(sP + w) = 0, p5(sP + w) = 0} has exactly 60 points** (Bezout 3·4·5; a total-degree homotopy finds 60 distinct solutions every time).
- Monodromy as Π moves in the spinor chart Π_A = graph of a skew 3 × 3 matrix A ∈ ℂ³ (40 random triangle loops each, all tracked): **transitive, primitive, with a transposition (e.g. cycle type (43, 2)), so S_60**, for P generic in X, P ∈ L_135 and P ∈ Y4.
- So the natural T3 slices give a 0-dimensional scheme of degree 60 with full symmetric monodromy, and the special choices P ∈ L_135, P ∈ Y4 do not split it. Over P (not Hilbertian) a specialisation can still split, but nothing in this family forces it.

**S42.3 (degree floor for slice routes).** *unchecked* (Bezout and expected dimensions).
- Every route through X ends with a P-point on a 0-dimensional scheme Z = W ∩ Σ cut out by P-rational data, and a P-point exists via fact 3 only if Z has a P-rational subscheme of degree ≤ 8 with a real point. For a P-rational surface Σ ⊂ X meeting W properly, deg Z = Σ·Q4·p5 = 20·deg Σ ≥ 20, with equality only for **P-planes in X** (W ∩ Π = plane quartic ∩ plane quintic). Threefolds in the ambient (as in S42.2) give 60·deg.
- **Factor-pair lemma (planes).** For a P-plane Π ⊂ X, Z splits only along P-components A of Q4|Π (degree a) and B of p5|Π (degree b), with |A ∩ B| = ab. ab ≤ 8 needs a = 1 (a P-line in Y4: excluded for r = 9 by Theorem F), b ≤ 2 (a line or conic in L_135: the parked curve program), or both restrictions reducible with conic factors. Planes in X form a 5-dimensional family; a conic factor of Q4|Π is codimension 4, a conic factor of p5|Π codimension 6, so the last case is not expected.
- Curves: a P-curve in Y4 of degree d ≥ 2 meets W in 5d ≥ 10 points; in L_135, 4d (lines and conics only, S41.4). So **every single-step route either is the curve program or needs a split of a scheme of degree ≥ 10 with no structural reason**, and S42.2 shows the natural T3 family does not split.
- Side remark (*unchecked* count, a lead only; **count corrected in S43.2**): conics in Y4 form a 4-dimensional family (finitely many through a general point), and p5|C is a binary decic; decics of fact-4 shape x¹⁰ = cubic (after a Möbius change) have codimension 3, so conics in Y4 with p5|C of fact-4 shape form an expected **curve**. Its P-points would give P-points of W by fact 4 (D = 10). No mechanism for P-points on that curve is known; parked. (S43.2: the cubic shape has codimension 4; the curve Γ needs the quartic right-hand side.)
- For r ≤ 7, a P-line in Y4 would give W by a quintic step (always a real root). This is the findings log's "line on Q4 for r < 9" route (session 4, S17.1–S17.2: a rational parametrisation needs a step of degree > 2000); nothing new here.

**S42.4 (literature: solvable points and polar cones).** Literature as cited; comparisons *unchecked*.
- Curves: every smooth curve of genus 0, 2, 3, 4 over any field has a solvable point, and there are curves of genus ≥ 40 over suitable (local) fields without one (Pál, Canad. J. Math. 56 (2004)). Genus one over ℚ: Çiperiani–Wiles (Duke 2008) for curves with points everywhere locally and semistable Jacobian; Pál (2012) over local fields. Genus ≥ 5 over ℚ is open and tied to Bombieri–Lang (Rawson, JTNB 36 (2024)).
- Higher dimension: smooth geometrically integral X over a field algebraic over ℚ has a solvable point once dim X ≥ 2^{2^{deg X}}, through a tower of steps of degree ≤ deg X (Wooley, arXiv 1501.01789); odd-degree X has a real point (Borsuk–Ulam type), so over ℚ a rational point under the same kind of bound (his Cor. 4.3, 4.6).
- Resolvent degree: iterated polar cones and Sylvester's obliteration (Sutherland; Heberle–Sutherland, NYJM 29 (2023)) and the Fano-variety version with "level ℓ" points (Gómez-Gonzáles–Wolfson 2025; Gómez-Gonzáles, arXiv 2510.10272) give dense points over extensions of bounded resolvent degree once the ambient dimension N ≥ a polynomial in the numbers of hypersurfaces of each degree. Small computed case: one quadric, cubic and quartic needs N ≤ 8 bound f₀(1,1,1) ≤ 8; the (3,4,5) type is not computed there.
- **None of these controls reality**, which is the whole difficulty over P (Theorem F is a real-place obstruction), and all need large N. In our setting the polar-cone route to W is "a line in Y4 through a point of Y4, then the quintic": its expected dimension is N − 8 = −1 in P⁷ = {p1 = 0} (3 conditions from p3, 4 from Q4 on P⁶ of directions), so it **misses by exactly one dimension** (it would start to work for n = 10 coordinates), and for r = 9 Theorem F forbids P-lines on Q4 anyway. The other order (a line in L_135, then Q4) is the curve program.
- So the literature gives no tool for W; the "points over solvable extensions" question is answered for curves of genus ≤ 4 (Pál), which Theorem G and Pál's attribution in the findings log already use.

**S42.5 (decision and consequences for the plan).**

Record it as a clean negative, not an obstruction (S41.1 still applies).
