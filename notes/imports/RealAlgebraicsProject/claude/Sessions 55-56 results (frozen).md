## 0af. Session 55 (Nov 19): results

Topic: priority 1 of the session 54 order (A_9 r = 5), with a new structural mechanism on the first of the three families (conics in V_3 through a P-point). **Mechanism tested: residual conics of quadrilaterals of lines.** Real lines in V_3 exist for r = 5 (S44.3; they only fail to meet V_4 themselves), and a quadric-surface section of V_3 that contains a skew quadrilateral of lines has a plane conic as residual. So: P-point Q of V_3 → two P-lines ℓ1, ℓ3 of V_3 through Q → a P-point Q′ (fourth vertex) of the curve Σ of quadrilaterals → residual P-conic C ⊂ V_3 → p4|C (degree 8, fact 3) → a P-point of V_4 → x⁹ = quartic (T1). **Charpoly shape of the endpoint: x⁹ + quartic (n = 9), matches T1.** **Tests that could fail:** (a) the real test (real quadrilaterals for r = 5 whose residual conic carries a sign change of p4); (b) the mechanism (degree of the natural map Σ → ℓ1 ≤ 8, or a split of its fibre). **Result:** (a) passes (rarely: 3 of 41); (b) fails (degree 65, transitive monodromy).

**S55.1 (residual curves of line configurations; the quadrilateral construction).** *unchecked* (elementary, short).
- Let ℓ ⊂ V_3 = {p1 = p2 = p3 = 0} be lines and Λ ≅ P³ ⊂ {Tr = 0} a 3-space containing them, S = Λ ∩ {p2 = 0} (a smooth quadric surface for general Λ; it is P¹ × P¹ over P as soon as it contains a P-line, since then both rulings are P-rational) and T = Λ ∩ {p3 = 0} a cubic surface. Then Λ ∩ V_3 = S ∩ T has class (3,3) on S, and the residual of the given lines is a P-curve in V_3: one line (1,0) → a quintic of class (2,3), genus 2; two meeting lines (1,0) + (0,1) → an elliptic quartic (2,2) with the P-rational degree-2 divisor E ∩ ℓ1; two skew lines → a rational quartic (1,3); a chain ℓ3–ℓ1–ℓ4 (ℓ3, ℓ4 skew, both meeting ℓ1) → a twisted cubic (1,2) meeting ℓ1 once, so ≅ P¹ over P (**cheap P-rational twisted cubics in V_3**, but p4 has degree 12 on them); **a skew quadrilateral ℓ1, ℓ3, ℓ2, ℓ4 (classes 2(1,0) + 2(0,1)) → a plane conic C (1,1) ⊂ V_3**, meeting each side once, on which p4 has degree 8.
- Counts: a general conic C ⊂ V_3 (8-dimensional family) is a quadrilateral residual in finitely many ways (Λ ⊃ Π_C is a P⁴, and a (2,2) curve on S splitting into a 4-cycle of lines is codimension 4), so the construction loses no conics. With adjacent sides ℓ1, ℓ3 through Q fixed, the fourth vertex Q′ (Q′ = ℓ2 ∩ ℓ4, ℓ4 through x1 ∈ ℓ1, ℓ2 through x3 ∈ ℓ3) runs over a curve Σ = M1 ∩ M3 ⊂ V_3 (M_i = union of the lines of V_3 meeting ℓ_i, a 3-fold; the intersection also contains the cone over C_Q, discarded). Given Q′, the sides ℓ2, ℓ4 are generically unique, so a P-point of Σ gives a P-quadrilateral and a P-conic.
- Equations for the fibre of Σ → ℓ1 over x1 (`quad55.py`): v on the curve of lines of V_3 through x1 (3 linear, 2 quadratic, 1 cubic condition, gauge 2), Q′ = x1 + tv, x3 = Q + σv3, and the line x3Q′ ⊂ V_3: Tr(x3Q′) = Tr(x3²Q′) = Tr(x3Q′²) = 0 (11 × 11). Spurious solutions: σ = 0 (ℓ2 through Q) and v ∈ ⟨v1, x1⟩ (Q′ on ℓ1), filtered.

**S55.2 (the fibre of Σ → ℓ1 has degree 65 and does not split).** [numerical] (`quad55.py`, `quadchk.py`, `quadres.py`, `quadmono.py`, `quadmono2.py`; generic complex data).
- Distinct genuine fourth vertices over a generic x1: **65** (seed 1: 59 after 200 starts, 65 from 2400 to 3000; seed 2: 65 at 2000–6000; seed 3: 64 at 2000–6000, one presumably missed). Checks: all four sides lie in V_3 (residual < 10⁻⁸ at sample points), the four vertices span a P³, the residual conic exists (least-squares residual of p3 ≡ h13·h24·h_C mod p2 below 2·10⁻¹⁰) and is nondegenerate, all 65 values of σ are distinct (no factorisation through x3 ∈ ℓ3). Real-data runs give 58–68 by random starts (unsaturated, or duplicates at the 10⁻⁶ tolerance).
- Monodromy as x1 runs around loops in ℓ1 (seed 1, 4000 steps per loop; many loops are rejected for path collisions): seed 1: 12 saved loops (of about 30 run, the rest rejected for path collisions) give one orbit of 63 of the 65 sheets; the two remaining sheets (11, 58) were moved into that orbit by larger loops (|c − a0| ≈ 2–5, `quadmono2.py 1 6 6000 3.0 1 11,58`). **So the geometric monodromy of the fibre is transitive on all 65: no split.**
- **Mechanism ruled out:** the fibration Σ → ℓ1 (and, by the symmetry of the construction, Σ → ℓ3, and the variant with ℓ1, ℓ4 adjacent at x1 fixed, which has the same combinatorial type).

## 0ag. Session 56 (Nov 20): results

Topic: priority 1 of the session 55 order (A_9 r = 5), first candidate: the curve Σ of fourth vertices of quadrilaterals (S55.1). **Mechanism named: Theorem G on Σ.** Σ is defined over P as soon as Q, ℓ1, ℓ3 are P-rational, and a P-point of Σ near one of the real quadrilaterals of S55.3 gives a P-conic in V_3 → p4|C, an octic (fact 3) → a P-point of V_4 → x⁹ = quartic (T1). **Charpoly shape of the endpoint: x⁹ + quartic (n = 9), matches T1.** Theorem G (repo A1) gives dense P-points on a curve with a chain of P-maps of degree ≤ 8 to P¹ (unconditionally for genus 0, 2–5, hyperelliptic, trigonal). **Tests that could fail:** (a) a natural P-map of Σ of degree ≤ 8; (b) the genus of Σ (g ≤ 5 keeps Theorem G unconditional; g ≤ 13 at least allows complex gonality ≤ 8). This is also the first use of exact counts mod p (Singular, msolve; Section 1a, G1a.8) for this track's own curves. **Result:** (a) fails: every natural map has degree ≥ 65; (b) (b) not settled: the ramification runs did not finish in this session (S56.2).

**S56.1 (exact degrees of the natural maps of Σ).** [exact mod p] (`sigma56.py`, `irr56.py`, `red56.py`, `lam56.py`, `wdeg.py`; p = 32003; Q a random F_p-point of V_3, ℓ1, ℓ3 lines of V_3 through Q found as F_p-points of C_Q).
- Model: unknowns Q′ (chart λ(Q′) = 1), σ (x3 = Q + σv3) and a (x1 = Q + a v1); equations: Q′ ∈ V_3, the line x1Q′ ⊂ V_3, the line x3Q′ ⊂ V_3 (10 equations, of degrees 1, 1, 2, 2, 1, 2, 3, 2, 3, 3); exclusions σ ≠ 0, Q′ ≠ Q, a ≠ 0 by Rabinowitsch variables. The exclusion a ≠ 0 is needed: over x1 = Q the model contains the spurious surface {Q′ ∈ ℓ3, σ free}.
- **Σ → ℓ1 has degree exactly 65** (seeds 1 and 2, 18 fibres: vdim 65, squarefree eliminant in a random linear coordinate). The F_p-factor degrees of the 18 fibres have common subset sums {0, 65} (e.g. [1, 13, 51], [4, 61], [2, 2, 61], [8, 57]), so the cover is irreducible and its monodromy is transitive. **S55.2 (65, transitive, from random starts and loop tracking) is confirmed exactly.** The reduced model of `red56.py` (Q′ in the 4-dimensional kernel of the five linear conditions, 4 remaining equations) gives the same 65.
- Σ → ℓ3 (coordinate σ): 65 (the symmetry check). Σ → the P⁴ of 3-spaces Λ ⊃ ⟨ℓ1, ℓ3⟩, followed by a generic projection to P¹: **166**. A generic linear form of Q′ (the degree of Σ ⊂ P⁷): **230**.
- So no natural P-map of Σ has degree ≤ 8 (the smallest is 65): **test (a) fails.**

**S56.2 (genus of Σ).** **Not settled.** Method set up: Riemann–Hurwitz for a generic linear form w of Q′ (degree 230 on Σ, no special fibres): 2g − 2 = −460 + R_w, where R_w counts the critical points of w on Σ, computed as the solutions of the curve equations plus a kernel vector k of the Jacobian with dw(k) = 0. The method was validated on C_Q (degree 12, genus 13: R = 48 exactly, `valid56.py`). The map Σ → ℓ1 is less convenient: over x1 = Q its fibre degenerates (the spurious surface of S56.1), so Riemann–Hurwitz for it needs a separate local count there. Runs: the 25-variable kernel system for w in msolve (`export56.py 1 w`) ran for more than 25 minutes at 5 GB without finishing; the reduced 10-variable model with a symbolic 6 × 6 determinant (`red56.py 1 ramw`) did not finish in about 15 minutes. *Unchecked (track):* Castelnuovo's bound for a curve of degree 230 spanning P⁷ gives g ≤ 4256; no lower bound is known here, and the gonality is at most 65.

**S56.3 (numerical hygiene: Singular integer overflow).** The first runs of this session gave a fibre degree of 82, squarefree, with common subset sums {0, 82}: an exact-looking but wrong number. Cause: in a polynomial string such as `((928+22101*(11889)))*((928+22101*(11889)))*y1`, Singular evaluates the constant subexpressions in machine `int` arithmetic, which overflows silently, so the equations were not the intended ones. Keep parameters as ring variables and add `a - a0` as an equation (as `irr56.py` now does), or reduce every constant mod p in Python before writing it. A count that disagrees with an earlier independent count is a bug until shown otherwise.
