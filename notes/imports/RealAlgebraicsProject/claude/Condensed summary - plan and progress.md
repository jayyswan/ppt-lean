- **Method lesson (S41):** (1) **Before asking whether a family is versal over P, check that the question can be decided at all.** Over P every non-versality statement is a counterexample to the goal (S41.1), so dimension, essential-dimension and thin-set heuristics say nothing; the only legitimate obstructions are those that survive when P is real closed (real signatures). Rank programs by mechanism (a construction of P-points from the primitives), not by parameter counts. (2) Before designing a curve route to a codimension-2 target, do the expected-dimension count (dim = −K·C + (n − 3)(1 − g)): a Calabi–Yau ambient has only 2-dimensional families of rational curves and none through a general point, and a curve not inside one of the two hypersurfaces meets their intersection only under a resultant condition.

- **Method lesson (S45):** (1) **Apply the trace-form isotropy test before any search for real lines or planes with power-sum conditions**: if p_{2m} must vanish identically on a real subspace Λ, then Λ^m is isotropic for T (signature (r + s, s)), so dim Λ^m ≤ s, and nondegenerate lines need s ≥ m + 1.
