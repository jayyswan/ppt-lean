## 0b. Session 25 (Nov 17, later): results

**S25.1 (the n = 9 chain works for r = 9; item 1 closed).** *unchecked* argument plus [numerical] check (`t3_n9.py`, `l135_sig.py`).
- The T3 chain needs no polynomial-specific centre for totally real nonics. Since b ↔ y is a real linear isomorphism and P-rational choices are dense among real ones, all that is needed is one real configuration with simple roots at each step. The steps are open conditions.
- Argument. Take Z ∈ X(R) = {p1 = p3 = 0}. The space of directions of lines in X through Z is {v : Σv = 0, ΣZ²v = 0} modulo Z. On it, Q_Z(v) = ΣZ_i v_i² is well defined, and p3(v) is well defined on {Q_Z = 0} (p3(v + cZ) = p3(v) + 3c·Q_Z(v)). If Q_Z has signature (p, q) with p, q ≥ 2 on that 6-dimensional quotient, the real cone {Q_Z = 0} \ 0 is connected. p3 is odd on it, so it vanishes somewhere: there is a real line of X through Z, and generically it is transversal. So every such Z ∈ L_135(R) is a limit of chain outputs. The map from choices to outputs is open there, so P-points of L_135 are dense near Z.
- [numerical] Of 1643 random real points of L_135 with distinct, unpaired coordinates, 1563 had signature (3,3), 34 had (4,2) and 46 had (2,4). p, q ≥ 2 held in every sample. Random chain runs: 367 of 373 reached the sextic and had a simple real root there. The 27 runs with no real point in step 2 came from a crude point search, not from a true failure. Every quintic gave points of L_135 with distinct coordinates and full-rank Jacobian. Q4 < 0 at 12.7% of the outputs.
- Consequence: **P-points of L_135 are dense in the part of L_135(R) with distinct, unpaired coordinates**, for any totally real nonic over P (all three r = 9 groups). This does not give a point of W or D_K. A curve is still needed.

So h^0(N_{m1}) = 4: the four first-order deformations of m1 are exactly the lines of one P³ Λ ⊃ m1, and Λ is tangent to L_135 along m1. Λ is not contained in L_135. m1 does not meet Sing(L_135).

The ordinary lines (kernel 3) form the other component, now called **S_ord**.

**S27.2 (what E is).** [numerical] `conicQ.py`, `eline.py`; the algebra is *unchecked* but short.
- For a line Z + s·a, take the 9 points Q_i = (Z_i, a_i) ∈ C². The line lies in L_135 iff Σ_i h(Q_i) = 0 for every odd polynomial h of degree ≤ 5 (12 conditions).
- **E-lines are exactly the lines whose 9 points Q_i lie on a central conic q(x, y) = 1.** Numerically, the rank of [1, Z², Za, a²] is 3 on E-lines and 4 on ordinary lines. Parametrise the conic: then z_i = s·λ_i + t·λ_i⁻¹, and the line conditions become p_m(λ) = 0 for m = ±1, ±3, ±5. Equivalently, λ ∈ L_135 and λ⁻¹ ∈ L_135, which is the same as **∏(X − λ_i) = X⁹ + A X⁷ + B X² + C** (e1 = e3 = e4 = e5 = e6 = e8 = 0). So E ≅ T/ι, where T = {e1 = e3 = e4 = e5 = e6 = e8 = 0} ⊂ P⁸ (a (1,3,4,5,6,8) complete intersection surface, degree 2880, formally K_T = O(18), so of general type; *unchecked* at e9 = 0) and ι is λ ↦ λ⁻¹.
- Reality. A real E-line is of one of two types. **Hyperbola type** (λ real) can't happen in the totally real case: by Descartes, X⁹ + AX⁷ + BX² + C has at most 6 real roots. **Ellipse type**: λ_i = e^{iθ_i}. All 15 real E-lines sampled are of this type, with a self-inversive quadrinomial (B = C·Ā, |C| = 1). So a real E-line is 9 angles θ_i with Σ e^{imθ_i} = 0 for m = 1, 3, 5, modulo rotation, and the line is {(r cos(θ_i − φ))_i}. The Chebyshev line (θ_i = θ0 + 2πi/9; the points of the line are the root sets of T_9(x/r) = c) is one example, but it is stable only under D_9.
- Over P: a P-rational real E-line ⇔ a norm-one Tschirnhaus λ = φ(α) + iψ(α) ∈ K(i) (φ² + ψ² = 1 at the roots) whose minimal polynomial over P(i) has the shape X⁹ + AX⁷ + BX² + C. This is a P-point on a twist of T, a general-type surface. **S28.1 identifies this with Engine S.** (For r < 9, hyperbola type: S31.3.)
- Q4 restricted to the sampled real E-lines has 2 real roots in every case (15 of 15). On the ordinary partner lines it had none (0 of 15). (Session 29: on 20 independently sampled real ordinary lines, 2 of 20 have real roots; S29.4.)

**S27.3 (ordinary lines ↔ odd plane cubics; a canonical j-map).** [numerical] `ordcubic.py`, `conicQ.py`; the derivation is *unchecked*. **Superseded in practice by the Weierstrass form S29.1.**
- For an ordinary line, the even polynomials of degree ≤ 4 that vanish at the ±Q_i form a pencil {l·g}, where l is any linear form and **g = l0 + c3 is a fixed odd cubic**. So the 9 points lie on a unique odd plane cubic C. C is an elliptic curve whose centre O is a flex, Q ↦ −Q is the group negation, and the 3 points at infinity are the nontrivial 2-torsion points. Checked: the rank of the 9 × 6 odd-cubic matrix is 5 on every ordinary sample.
- Normal form. Change coordinates so that l0 = y, and write C: w² = t·c(t), with t = y/x, w = x·c(t), c a cubic. The odd functions of degree ≤ 5 restrict to w·N(t)/c(t)³ with deg N ≤ 7. The 8 trace conditions are then Σ ρ_i t_i^k = 0 for k ≤ 7, which forces ρ_i ∝ 1/F'(t_i). The **line condition** becomes
  **w_i F'(t_i) = κ c(t_i)³, equivalently F(t) | t F'(t)² − κ² c(t)⁵**, where F = ∏(t − t_i).
  Numerically the ratio w_i F'(t_i)/c(t_i)³ is constant to 10⁻¹³. Parameter count: 9 + 4 + 1 − 9 − 3 (Möbius maps fixing 0, and scaling) = 2 = dim S_ord.
- Over P: a P-rational ordinary line ⇔ a Tschirnhaus element t ∈ K with **t·F'(t)² ∈ P*²·c(t)⁵** in K, for some cubic c ∈ P[T].
- The **j-invariant of C is a canonical map j: S_ord → P¹, defined over the base field and non-constant** (it varies along the walk, for example −2.5·10⁵ and −5.1·10⁵). Caveat: the fibres in y-space are twists of curves of ordered 9-tuples, roughly a 9!-fold cover of the curve of polynomials F. So the fibration does not by itself give a low-degree map for Theorem G.
- E is the degenerate case of this picture, where the cubic splits as (line through O) × (central conic). Cuspidal cubics give only paired lines (vanishing odd power sums up to p15). The nodal fibre j = ∞ is a curve in S_ord, defined over the base field; **computed in S29.2 (dead for r ≥ 5).**
- Residue-theorem reading: the 9 points Q_i are zeros of φ = w·F'(t) − κ·c(t)³ on C, and the trace conditions are Σ N(t_i)/F'(t_i) = 0 for deg N ≤ 7.

**S27.4 (consequences).**
- **The repo's K_S = O(13) is the class of the whole zero scheme of lines**, which contains E with multiplicity ≥ 2. So it is not yet a statement about S_ord or E separately. The general-type claims for the separate components have to be redone (E: K_T = O(18) on its double cover T, *unchecked*). The same caution applies to the C_b computation (S25.2, S26.1). ## 0e. Session 28 (Nov 17, later): results

**S28.1 (E is the Engine S (9,5) Tschirnhaus variety).** Algebra *unchecked* but short; [numerical] `scripts/engS_E.py`.
- Statement: a real E-line in L_135 for a totally real nonic K over P is the same thing as a Tschirnhaus element u ∈ K \ P whose minimal polynomial is, up to a constant, H(v) = (1+v²)^{9/2}·[a cos 9θ + b sin 9θ + c cos 5θ + d sin 5θ] in v = tan θ, with a, b, c, d ∈ P. This is the four-term sine shape of Engine S with p = 9, q = 5 and zero constant term.
- Proof sketch. By S27.2, a real E-line ⇔ unit-circle λ = e^{2iθ} ∈ K(i) with minimal polynomial over P(i) the self-inversive quadrinomial λ⁹ + Aλ⁷ + Bλ² + C. Multiplying by λ^{-9/2} gives e^{9iθ} + Ae^{5iθ} + Be^{-5iθ} + Ce^{-9iθ}, a real multiple of the sine expression. Under the Cayley substitution λ = (1+iu)/(1−iu), u = tan θ, norm-one λ ∈ K(i) ⇔ u ∈ K. The line is {r cos(2θ_j − φ)}.
- Why q = 5: log(λ⁹ + Aλ⁷ + Bλ² + C) at ∞ has no odd powers before λ^{-7}, so p1 = p3 = p5 = 0 and p7 ≠ 0. For the other (9, q): q = 1 gives lines only in X = {p1 = p3 = 0} (T3 already supplies those), and q = 3, 7 don't even give p1 = p3 = 0.
- [numerical] 152 random (a, b, c, d) with all 9 roots real: |f(θ_j)| ≤ 1.3·10⁻¹⁴; p1, p3, p5 on the line ≤ 3·10⁻¹⁵; |p7| ≥ 4·10⁻³; conic-rank test s4/s1 ≤ 10⁻¹⁶ (all E-lines). Over ℚ, (a, b, c, d) = (3, 1, 1, 2) gives H = 3v⁹ + 32v⁸ − 52v⁷ − 252v⁶ + 98v⁵ + 364v⁴ − 84v³ − 116v² + 19v + 4, irreducible with 9 real roots; its Frobenius cycle types include 9, (2,7), (1,8) and (1,3,5), consistent with S_9.

**S28.2 (consequences).**
- **A P-point on E for K ⇔ K is Tschirnhaus-equivalent over P to a fibre of Engine S (9,5) with zero constant term.** Engine S then solves K directly (no Q4 step needed). So E is not a new route: it is the repo's Engine S route (T7: "does not improve n = 9") in other coordinates. **E is dropped as an independent target.**
- Plan item 2(b) is answered: a fact family (Engine S) produces exactly the E-lines, and the route is circular.
- Heuristic, not a proof: E is a 2-parameter family, and ed(S_9) ≥ 4 over generic fields, so over a generic base it can't be versal. This says nothing about P, which is not generic. The same count applies to S_ord and C̃_b.
- Quick negatives for 2(a): fact-4 fibres (F = x⁹ − c(x)) don't fit tF'² ≡ κ²c⁵ mod F; trinomials F = t⁹ + at^k + b make the condition overdetermined (9 equations, 5 unknowns).
- **The frontier is now one P-point on S_ord (lines) or on C̃_b (conics).**

## 0f. Session 29 (Nov 17, later): results

**S29.1 (S_ord in Weierstrass form: an explicit line formula and a short proof).** Proof *unchecked* but complete and short; [numerical] three independent checks.
- Put the flex O of the odd cubic at infinity: C is y² = h(x) with h a cubic, the 9 points are Q_i = (x_i, y_i), and F = ∏(X − x_i). **Criterion: the line is in L_135 iff F'(x_i)² = c²·h(x_i)⁵ for all i (one constant c), i.e. F | F'² − c²h⁵, with y_i = c·h(x_i)³/F'(x_i).** This is the S27.3 criterion tF'² ≡ κ²c⁵ moved by the Möbius map that sends the image of O to ∞.
- **Explicit line:** z_i(s) = h(x_i)²·(1 + s·x_i)/F'(x_i). In the odd-cubic plane the two coordinates are the odd functions y/h and x·y/h, so (Z_i, a_i) = (1/y_i, x_i/y_i) up to GL2. (Session 30 note: since h(x_i)²/F'(x_i) = 1/(c·y_i), the line is simply **z_i(s) = (1 + s·x_i)/y_i** up to a constant; over P it is the span of 1/y and x/y in K.)
- Proof (both directions). (⇐) Using F'² = c²h⁵, z_i^m = c^{1−m}·h(x_i)^{(5−m)/2}(1 + s x_i)^m / F'(x_i) for m = 1, 3, 5. The numerators have degree 7, 6, 5 ≤ 7, so Σ_i z_i^m = 0 by partial fractions (Σ P(x_i)/F'(x_i) = 0 when deg P ≤ n − 2 = 7). (⇒) The odd functions on C with poles of order ≤ 5 at the three 2-torsion points are exactly y·P(x)/h(x)³ with deg P ≤ 7 (an 8-dimensional space, matching S27.3). So the trace conditions say that the weights w_i = y_i/h(x_i)³ annihilate all polynomials of degree ≤ 7, which forces w_i ∝ 1/F'(x_i); with y_i² = h(x_i) this is the criterion. (Equivalently: η = h³dx/(F·y) is the unique differential with residues ±½ at ±Q_i and zeros of order 5 at the 2-torsion points.)

**S33.4 (consequences; one *unchecked* reformulation).**
- **Why S29.1 has no direct analogue on E_C.** In S29.1 the scale of each point is a function on the curve (y_i² = h(x_i)), which turns the weights for m = 1, 3, 5 into one weight times polynomials of degree ≤ 7. On E_C the scale λ_i (Q_i = λ_i q̂_i) would need λ_i^e = R(q_i) for a rational function R on E_C. For e = 2 and deg R ≤ 2 this is exactly S30.1's "even ≤ 2" test, which is negative. Higher degrees are unconstrained by a count (always solvable), so they give no criterion. So E_C alone does not carry the weights in low degree.
- **A better curve, *unchecked* (a reformulation only).** Write Q_i = λ_i(t − α_i)(t − β_i). The pairs {α_i, β_i} lie on the curve Γ = {(α, β) : [(t − α)(t − β)] ∈ E_C} ⊂ P¹ × P¹. Γ is a symmetric (3,3)-curve, the double cover of E_C branched at the 6 points E_C ∩ Δ, of genus 4. The conic conditions read Σ_i λ_i^m (t − α_i)^m (t − β_i)^m = 0 for m = 1, 3, 5. This is a partial-fraction or residue identity on P¹ with 18 marked points, the same shape as the S29.2 nodal computation. That is where an S29.1-style criterion is most likely to come from (item 4(a), revised). **S34.2: counting shows a plain 18-point residue representation is not forced for conics.**

## 0k. Session 34 (Nov 17, later): results

Topic: a coordinate-free "residue invariant" for curves in L_135, first validated on lines, then tried on C_b^gen (item 4(a)).

**S34.1 (the quintic weight form g of a line; S_ord ⇔ g is a square).** Algebra *unchecked* but short; [numerical] `conics135b.py 21 1 400` → `lines_filter.py` → `line_T.py`.
- Setting: a line in L_135 is 9 binary linear forms L_i (z_i = L_i(u, s)) with Σ L_i^m = 0 for m = 1, 3, 5. Put T_i = ∏_{j≠i} det(L_i, L_j) (well defined up to one global constant under GL2).
- **Lemma (*unchecked*, short).** If the 9 points [L_i] ∈ P¹ are distinct, then Σ L_i⁵ = 0 iff there is a binary quadratic form g with **T_i = g(L_i) for all i**; g is unique up to scale. Proof: write L_i = μ_i L̂_i with L̂_i = u + x_i s. The quintic condition says the weights μ_i⁵ annihilate polynomials of degree ≤ 5 in x_i, so μ_i⁵ = c(x_i)/F'(x_i) with deg c ≤ 2 (partial fractions, n − 2 − 5 = 2). Then T_i = ∏_{j≠i} μ_iμ_j(x_j − x_i) = ±(∏μ)·μ_i⁷F'(x_i) = ±(∏μ)·μ_i²c(x_i) = ±(∏μ)·g(L_i), with g the homogenisation of c. Conversely, reverse the steps.
- **Dichotomy** [numerical]. 400 line starts (seed 21) gave 32 genuine real lines (no zero or paired coordinate). T_i = g(L_i) holds on all 32 (relative error ≤ 4·10⁻⁷, at LM accuracy). **On all 18 S_ord lines disc(g)/|g|² ≤ 10⁻¹³ (g = ℓ², a perfect square); on all 14 E-lines it is between 1.5·10⁻³ and 1.5 (g nondegenerate).** So: **S_ord ⇔ g has a double root; E ⇔ g has two distinct roots.** This matches S29.1 (*unchecked* identification): putting the double root of g at ∞ gives μ_i⁵ = c/F'(x_i), which is the S29.1 normalisation (y = 1/μ); for E the two roots of g are the asymptote directions of the central conic of S27.2.
- **Consequences.**
  - A sharp, intrinsic test for the two line components (gap 10⁻¹³ vs 10⁻³), usable instead of the conic-rank test.
  - **A P-rational S_ord line carries a canonical P-rational point P_O**: the root of g = ℓ² gives the point with z_i(P_O) = ℓ(L_i), and z_i(P_O)² ∝ T_i. In the S29.1 coordinates P_O = (1/y_i)_i, the point s = 0. On the 18 real samples T_i has one sign (so all z_i(P_O)² ∝ T_i is consistent), Q4(P_O) > 0 (0.23 to 0.40 for |z| = 1, larger than at a random point of the line), and p7(P_O) is not special. So P_O does not by itself help the D_K route; recorded as structure.
  - Reformulation of the S_ord P-point problem: a P-rational line in L_135 whose quintic weight form is a square. Equivalently (*unchecked*): a P-point z ∈ L_135 (namely P_O) and a direction a such that z + s·a ⊂ L_135 and ∏_{j≠i}(z_i a_j − z_j a_i) ∝ z_i² for all i.

**S34.2 (no analogous invariant relation on C_b^gen conics).** [numerical] `inv_fit.py`; sample `conics135b.py` seeds 11, 12 × 300 → `cb_struct.py`, `cb_split.py`: 139 genuine conics, 16 C_a, 123 C_b, **104 C_b^gen** (97 with Jacobian rank 21) and 19 C_b^pen.
- Invariants per index: Δ_i = disc Q_i, T_i = ∏_{j≠i} Res(Q_i, Q_j) (resultant of binary quadratics), U_i = ∏_{j≠i} B(Q_i, Q_j) (polarised discriminant). Test: v_i = T_i^p Δ_i^q U_i^r (p, r ∈ [−2, 2], q ∈ [−4, 4]) equal to Φ(Q_i)/Ψ(Q_i) with (deg Φ, deg Ψ) ∈ {(0,0), (1,0), (2,0), (0,1), (1,1), (0,2), (2,1)} (all overdetermined on 9 points). - Validation: the same script on the 18 S_ord lines finds (p, Φ, Ψ) = (1, deg 2, 1) at 10⁻¹⁰ (S34.1), and (1, deg 3, deg 1), (−1, 1, deg 2) as its trivial variants.
- On C_b^gen the best median deviation is **2.3 (a factor e^2.3 ≈ 10)**: no relation of this kind. **Negative.**
- Count explanation (*unchecked*). For lines, writing μ_i⁵L̂_i⁵ as a residue c(x)(τ − x)⁵/F'(x) is forced: the quintic relation leaves a 3-dimensional weight space, and the residue form has 3 parameters. For conics, a residue representation over the 18 roots, λ_i⁵Q̂_i(τ)⁵ = g(τ, α_i)/G'(α_i) + g(τ, β_i)/G'(β_i) with G = ∏Q̂_i and deg_x g ≤ 16, has 187 unknowns against 99 equations, so it always exists and carries no information. A conic criterion must use the harmonic constraint (only 11 of the 27 sections of O(5,5)|Γ are tested) and is not a plain residue statement on P¹ or on Γ.
- Method lesson: when a per-index invariant spans many orders of magnitude (T_i here spans up to 10¹⁰), a singular-value test for "v_i = Φ(Q_i)/Ψ(Q_i)" is fooled (a few large v_i dominate and the kernel is spurious, even with row scaling). **S35.1 (balance lemma: near the node the cluster is exactly 6 points at scale β; *unchecked*, short).**
- Suppose that as β → 0 the points split into levels: m_0 points at distance ~β from b1 (the core), n_ℓ points at distance ~β^{α_ℓ} (1 > α_1 > … > α_r > 0), and outer points at distance ~1. For a point at level ℓ, the β-power of its potential is (n_ℓ − 1)α_ℓ + (N_{<ℓ} − 5)α_ℓ + S_ℓ, where N_{<ℓ} counts the points below level ℓ (the node contributes −5, from h ≈ (x − b1)² there) and S_ℓ = Σ_{ℓ'>ℓ} n_ℓ' α_ℓ'. The outer points fix L at order β⁰, so every level needs total power 0. The core needs m_0 − 6 + S_0 = 0. Level ℓ needs (N_{≤ℓ} − 6)α_ℓ + S_ℓ = 0, so N_{≤ℓ} ≤ 6 with equality exactly at the top level r. Then level r − 1 gives n_r(α_r − α_{r−1}) = 0, a contradiction. **So there are no intermediate levels: the core has exactly m_0 = 6 points and there are 3 outer points.**
- Other degenerations are excluded the same way: a sub-cluster of k points at scale ε around a generic point has potential power k − 1, so k = 1; a sub-cluster at a simple 2-torsion point (t = ±1 or ±i in the bubble, or at e) needs k − 1 − 5/2 = 0, which has no integer solution.
- The core problem is then the **bubble problem**: 6 points t_i with F'(t_i)² = K·(t_i² ± 1)⁵, equivalently **F | F'² − K(t² ± 1)⁵**, deg F = 6, and K determined by the outer points (for m_0 = 6 the β-powers cancel: log K = 2(L − Σ_outer log|x_k − b1| − log A(b1 − e)) up to the normalisation of A). The 3 outer points solve the same equal-potential problem with h ≈ A(x − e)(x − b1)² and a charge +6 at b1, i.e. a net charge +1 at the node. This outer problem does not see the sign ±.
- Caveat: this assumes the degeneration as β → 0 has the power-law level structure above (no logarithmic scales, no points escaping to O faster than a power of β). It is consistent with all S32.2 samples (6–8 points within 30β only because β/|b1 − e| is as large as 3·10⁻²).
