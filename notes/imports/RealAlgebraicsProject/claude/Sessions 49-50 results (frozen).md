**S49.1 (what the steps mean; decic groups over P).** *unchecked* (elementary Galois theory; group facts standard).
- **Steps as non-existence statements.** Fact 3 says exactly that an irreducible polynomial over P of degree 2 … 8 has no real root, i.e. P has no extension of degree d ≤ 8 with a real place. The S48.1 corollary says the same for d = 9 and r ≤ 3: every irreducible nonic over P has at least 5 real roots. So a step "a real point of a 0-dimensional P-scheme Z of degree 9 with ≤ 3 real points" works because the P-component of Z through the real point has degree 1. In general: complex conjugation c acts on Z(ℂ), and the P-component through a real point is a P-point unless its degree d and its number of real points form a pair (d, r) not yet excluded.
- **Consequence for priority 2(a).** For totally real K the Galois closure N is real, so c = 1 on N, and every scheme built from K alone is totally real: the excluded pairs (9, r ≤ 3) never occur there. An auxiliary family is needed, as S48.3 said; no candidate was found in the earlier routes (their schemes have degrees 12, 20, 24, 60, 105, 120, with full monodromy). The audit is closed with this statement.
- **Decics over P.** Let K/P have degree 10 and a real place, with Galois closure N and c ∈ G = Gal(N/P). An intermediate field F (degree 2 or 5) would be a real extension of degree ≤ 5, so K is primitive. Of the nine primitive groups of degree 10: A_5, S_5 have an index-5 subgroup and c fixes a point of 5 letters (a real quintic subfield); A_6 = PSL(2,9) has index-6 subgroups and its involutions (type 2²) fix 2 of 6 letters; S_6 = PΣL(2,9) has two classes of index-6 subgroups, exchanged by the outer automorphism, which swaps the classes 2 ↔ 2³ and fixes 2², so c fixes a letter in one of the two actions; M_10: c ∉ A_6 (otherwise N^{A_6} is a real quadratic extension, impossible since P is Euclidean), and M_10 ∖ A_6 has no involutions; PΓL(2,9): G/A_6 ≅ V_4, so c lies in one of the three index-2 subgroups, a real quadratic subfield; PGL(2,9): c ∉ PSL(2,9) and, since 9 ≡ 1 mod 4, the involutions outside PSL(2,9) have no fixed points on P¹(F_9), so r = 0. A_10: c even, r ∈ {2, 6, 10}; S_10: c odd (else N^{A_10} is real quadratic), r ∈ {4, 8} (or 0).
- **So the decic cases over P with a real root are A_10 with r ∈ {2, 6, 10} and S_10 with r ∈ {4, 8}.** The same argument shows that a field of degree 12 over P with a real place is primitive (intermediate degrees 2, 3, 4, 6), and degree 11 is prime.

## Section 5 method lessons, sessions 29–40 (moved from the summary in session 50)

- **Method lessons (S29):** (1) For a family of 9 points on a curve with "odd trace" conditions, the residue theorem gives the criterion directly: find the differential with residues ±½ at ±Q_i and the required vanishing at the poles of the test functions. On a genus-0 normalization the criterion becomes a lacunary/divisibility statement (E, nodal fibre); on genus 1 it becomes F | F'² − c²h⁵. (2) For real S_ord samples, use line-space LM (`rlines.py`), not Newton in Weierstrass coordinates. Also, a NaN-producing Newton run passes a `norm > tol` test, so check `isfinite`.
- **Method lesson (S30):** to find hidden components of a family of curves in L_135, compute the rank of the "evaluation matrix" of the 9 coefficient vectors on natural polynomial spaces (all monomials of each degree, and the harmonic ones for conics), and sort the smallest singular values: a component shows up as a clean gap (10⁻¹⁵ vs ≥ 10⁻⁶). Then confirm with perturb-and-resolve. For conics, use harmonic forms (coefficients of q^m), not plain monomials: plain monomial tests are generic on both components.
- **Method lesson (S31):** once a special component is found, (1) compute the singular points of the special curve (a reducible curve shows up as nodes), and (2) run a plain incidence survey (collinear triples, 6 on a conic). Then apply the **canonical-subset filter** (S31.4): an incidence that singles out a proper subset of the indices kills P-points for transitive G. Do this before the engine test; it is cheaper.
- **Method lesson (S32):** u = 1/j is the right real coordinate through the nodal fibre of S_ord. Line-space continuation toward u = 0 works down to |u| ≈ 10⁻¹², but crossing needs a blown-up chart at the node (a weight 1/|u| makes LM ill-conditioned). Random-start solvers for S_ord in x or m = 1/x coordinates fall into the "one point at O + 4 colliding pairs" valley; always start from `rlines.py` output. Recovering R = G/F by polynomial division from LM-accuracy samples is hopeless (one x_i ≈ 10⁴–10⁷); use the sign lemma S32.1 instead.
- **Method lesson (S33):** for group-law questions on a plane cubic, use the exact chord formula third(a, b) = a − (∇F(a)·b / ∇F(b)·a)·b and the tangent version (`grp.py`). Do not test linear equivalence by contact conditions on forms of degree 3k (the "kD ~ 3kH" rank test is badly conditioned even for random points). For real components of a plane cubic, project from a point *on the pseudoline* (from an oval point every residual pair is real, so the projection shows one interval).
- **Method lesson (S34):** (1) Before searching for a criterion on a new family, check whether its residue representation is *forced* by a count (unknowns of the residue form vs equations); if it is not, no plain residue criterion will appear. (2) Per-index invariants like ∏_{j≠i} Res(Q_i, Q_j) span many orders of magnitude; test relations with the maximum log-deviation of the fit, not with singular values. (3) `conics135b.py <seed> 1 <starts>` + `lines_filter.py` is a quick real line sampler (seed 21, 400 starts: 18 S_ord + 14 E lines in a few minutes), an alternative to `rlines.py`.
- **Method lesson (S35):** (1) For an equal-potential ("charge") system near a degeneration, first do the **β-power balance count** over the possible scale levels; it can fix the cluster sizes outright (here: exactly 6 at the node). (2) Then solve the leading-order bubble problem completely over C (complex Newton with the log residual taken mod 2πi; count distinct solutions up to permutation) and read off the real forms by substitution (t ↦ it swaps the two real sides). Real-only searches miss the structure and are slow. (3) Glue the leading-order solutions and Newton-refine: this gives exact samples near the degeneration and checks the asymptotics. (4) LAPACK prints "DLASCL" warnings to stdout on singular batches; filter them out.
- **Method lesson (S36):** (1) To map a real 2-parameter locus, fix one parameter (here L) and continue in the other by pseudo-arclength from known samples; the slices showed the region's shape (arcs node → fold → node) at once. Then trace the boundary itself with a bordered fold system (G = 0, J_x v = 0, c·v = 1). (2) In step-size tests, measure relative moves against |x_i| + β, not |x_i|: a point crossing x = 0 otherwise stalls the continuation. (3) A random outer-problem scan can miss solutions (small K); don't conclude "no glued lines at this L" from it.
- **Method lesson (S37):** (1) Random-start counts of a fibre miss clustered solutions (here the 3 + 3 + 3 sheet); always confirm with monodromy loops seeded by every known solution. (2) Random monodromy loops can leave a sheet untouched for 100+ loops; don't conclude reducibility from that. Instead move the whole fibre to rational parameters, polish to 60 digits and compute a symmetric-function resolvent: integer coefficients and its Galois group settle irreducibility exactly. (3) Interpolating the resolvent in the remaining parameter gives the curve's equation; then try substitutions such as T = c + u that make it linear in one variable (that is how rationality showed up).
- **Method lesson (S38):** (1) **Use weights before interpolating.** Normalise to a weighted-homogeneous form (here depressed h, weights 1, 2, 3) and count monomials: A and B had only 7 and 4 unknowns, so two curves already determined them with checks. (2) **Pole-order bound for the denominators:** e_k has only simple poles where c² → ∞ (one point runs to O) and is bounded at U = ∞ (the 3 + 3 + 3 sheet), so B·e_k is a polynomial of degree ≤ 5 in U. That bound made the fit linear and small. (3) Once a family is fitted, **prove it by a symbolic pseudo-remainder** (G'² + A·B·h⁵ mod G over ℚ(a, b, U), seconds in PARI) and run a negative control. (4) For exact samples, continue in the parameters near a moderate hub (U = −22 + 5i worked) and walk U along the integers with complex bumps around the roots of B; a hub at large |U| is bad (the 3 + 3 + 3 clusters make the Jacobian ill-conditioned), and long continuation in all parameters at once is slow. (5) In PARI strings, parenthesise every substituted value: `{G}%{F}` and `x-{c}` with negative c silently parse wrong.
- **Method lesson (S39):** (1) **For an explicit family, factor its discriminant first.** It gives the branch locus, the expected local monodromy (the valuation is Σ(e − 1) when the cover is tame and the order is maximal), and the real chamber structure. (2) **Chamber census by slices:** in each 1-parameter slice, take the real roots of the branch polynomials and run one exact Sturm count per interval; this is exhaustive per slice and much cheaper than a 2-dimensional grid. Refine the slice grid near the parameter values where the branch curves meet (here Δ_h = 0). (3) In numerical monodromy, check the distance between neighbouring branch points before choosing the loop radius (a root of A and a root of B were 1.7·10⁻⁴ apart; radius 10⁻³ merged them and gave a wrong cycle type).
- **Method lesson (S40):** (1) **Compare families by inertia, not by discriminants.** A pullback's inertia along a divisor is a power of the target's inertia at the image (a branch point of a curve base, a branch or orbifold curve, or the local group at a point of a surface base), and a divisor that dominates a curve base is unramified. Compute the target's inertia at its orbifold points as well: for weighted families they carry the interesting types (a 9-cycle and a (k, 9 − k) element for trinomials). (2) A large discriminant valuation can hide small inertia (Δ_h: valuation 15, inertia (2³,1³)); factor the member over the branch curve exactly before guessing the type. (3) Trivariate discriminants of weighted families can time out in PARI; work in an affine chart.
