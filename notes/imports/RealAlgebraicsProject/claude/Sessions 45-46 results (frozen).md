## 0v. Session 45 (Nov 18): results

Topic: priority item 1 after session 44 (A_9, r = 5 over P): a structural reason for S44.3, then conics in V_3.

Notation: A = K ⊗ ℝ ≅ ℝ^r × ℂ^s (r + 2s = 9), T(x, y) = Tr(xy), a nondegenerate form of signature (r + s, s). For a real subspace Λ ⊂ A, Λ^m is the real span of all products of m elements of Λ. A line Q + κv (Q, v ∈ A independent) is **m-degenerate** if the 9 points [Q_i : v_i] ∈ P¹ (conjugate coordinates included) take at most m distinct values.

**S45.1 (trace-form isotropy lemma and sign lemma; p4 > 0 on real lines in V_3 for r = 5).** *unchecked* (elementary, complete) + [numerical] (`iso45.py`).
- **Lemma A (isotropy).** If Tr(λ^{2m}) = 0 for all λ in a real subspace Λ, then Λ^m is T-isotropic (polarise: the symmetric 2m-linear form Tr(a_1⋯a_{2m}) vanishes on Λ, so T(Λ^m, Λ^m) = 0), hence dim Λ^m ≤ s. For a line Λ = ⟨Q, v⟩, dim Λ^m = m + 1 unless the line is m-degenerate (a dependency Σ c_a Q^a v^{m−a} = 0 is a binary form of degree m vanishing at every [Q_i : v_i]). For a P-line in a nonic field K without intermediate fields, m-degeneracy with m ≤ 8 would make Q/v a root of a P-polynomial of degree ≤ m, so Q/v ∈ P and Q, v are dependent. **So a P-line along which p_{2m} vanishes identically needs s ≥ m + 1** (likewise every real line that is not m-degenerate). m = 1: no real line in V_2 for r ≥ 7 (recovers S44.1); m = 2: p4 ≡ 0 on a P-line needs r ≤ 3; m = 3: p6 ≡ 0 needs r = 1; p8 ≡ 0 never.
- **Lemma B (sign lemma).** Let Λ ⊂ A be a real subspace with Tr(Λ) = 0, T(Λ, Λ) = 0 and T(Λ, Λ²) = 0 (that is, Λ lies in the cone V_3), and dim Λ = s. Then T is positive semidefinite on W = ℝ·1 + Λ + Λ², with radical exactly Λ. *Proof.* Λ lies in the radical R of T|W (its pairings with 1, Λ and Λ² are Tr Λ, T(Λ, Λ) and T(Λ, Λ²)). For a nondegenerate form of negative index s, (negative index of T|W) + dim R ≤ s; R is isotropic, so dim R ≤ s, hence R = Λ and T|W ≥ 0. ∎ So **Tr(λ⁴) = T(λ², λ²) ≥ 0 on Λ**, with equality only if λ² ∈ R = Λ. In that case Tr(λ^k) = 0 for k = 1, …, 6 (λ⁴ = T-pairing of λ², λ² ∈ Λ; λ⁵ = λ·λ²·λ² and λ⁶ = λ²·λ²·λ² are triple products of elements of Λ), so charpoly(λ) = x⁹ + αx² + βx + γ. Such a polynomial has **at most 3 real roots counted with multiplicity** (f'' = 72x⁷ + 2α has one simple real root, so Rolle gives ≤ 2 for f′ and ≤ 3 for f; for α = 0 use f′ = 9x⁸ + β; λ = 0 is excluded), while λ ∈ ℝ^r × ℂ^s has at least r real roots. **So Tr(λ⁴) > 0 on Λ ∖ 0 when r ≥ 4.**
- **Corollary (r = 5, s = 2).** Real lines in V_3 are exactly 2-dimensional Λ as in Lemma B, so **p4 > 0 at every point of every real line in V_3**, and no real line in V_3 meets V_4. By compactness the normalised minimum is positive, matching min p4/|y|⁴ = 0.1201 of S44.3; S44.3 is now a theorem (*unchecked*). It is the trace-form analogue of Lemma F.2. For r ≤ 3 (s ≥ 3) lines have dim Λ = 2 < s and nothing follows, consistent with S44.2; Lemma B would apply to real planes in V_3 when r = 3, but its strictness needs r ≥ 4.
- **Correction to S44.3 (V′ lines).** By Lemma A with m = 2, the real lines in V′ = {p1 = p2 = p4 = 0} for r = 5 are 2-degenerate (the coordinates split into two blocks, each with its own power sums vanishing), so none is P-rational. [numerical] `iso45.py lines 5 1,2,4 12 1`: all 6 solutions have ≤ 2 ratio classes and σ3([Q², Qv, v²]) ≈ 10⁻⁸ (the 39 lines of `vprime.py` were not rechecked individually but must be of this kind). Control, `iso45.py lines 3 1,2,4 8 5` (r = 3, s = 3, allowed by Lemma A): all 7 real lines found are nondegenerate (6 distinct classes, σ3 ≥ 0.013). `iso45.py lines 5 1,2,6 8 6`: only degenerate solutions. `iso45.py lines 5 1,2,3 12 2`: 8 nondegenerate real lines in V_3, min p4/|y|⁴ between 0.167 and 0.237, no real roots of p4.

**S45.2 (conics in V_3 for r = 5 reach V_4; conics through a point are lines on a quintic).** [numerical] (`iso45.py conics`, `sig45.py`) + *unchecked* (projection identity, short).
- **Real conics reach V_4.** Real conics q(κ) = A + Bκ + Cκ² in V_3 (15 conditions; LM with 6 random gauge rows): of 42 nondegenerate samples (rank [A, B, C] = 3, all coordinate forms distinct), **10 have p4|C (a binary octic) with 2 or 4 real roots** (`iso45.py conics 5 1,2,3 10 3` and `… 10 4`: 2 of 17; `sig45.py 5 14 11` and `… 14 12`: 8 of 25); min p4/|y|⁴ goes down to −0.33. So the conic step of the V_4 route has no real obstruction: a P-conic in V_3 with p4|C real-rooted gives a P-point of V_4 by an octic step.
- **Trace form on the conic plane.** Tr(q(κ)²) ≡ 0 forces the Gram matrix of T on (A, B, C) to be the Hankel matrix [[0, 0, x], [0, −2x, 0], [x, 0, 0]], of signature (2, 1) or (1, 2) by the sign of x (a reparametrisation-invariant). Samples: (2, 1) on 24 of 25, (1, 2) once (with p4 > 0). Whether (1, 2) (the plane uses both negative directions, as in Lemma B) forces p4 > 0 is open. (S46.2: yes, Lemma C.)
- **Projection from Q.** For Q ∈ V_3, the projection of V_2 from Q is birational over P: a direction w ∈ {p1 = 0}/Q gives the second point R(w) = p2(w)Q − 2B(Q, w)w (B the polar form of p2). With α = p2(w), β′ = B(Q, w), t1 = Tr(Q²w), t2 = Tr(Qw²): p3(R(w)) = −2β′·F5(w), **F5 = 3α²t1 − 6αβ′t2 + 4β′²p3(w)**, a quintic on P⁶ (invariant under w ↦ w + cQ, checked by expansion), singular along Z = {α = β′ = 0}. So **V_3 is P-birational to the quintic 5-fold {F5 = 0} ⊂ P⁶, and conics through Q in V_3 ↔ lines on F5** (a 4-dimensional family; lines in {β′ = 0} are spurious, and lines through points of Z give line pairs whose second component is a real line in V_3, useless for r = 5 by S45.1).
- **Conics through two points.** For a second point Q2 ∈ V_3, the planes ⟨Q, Q2, u⟩ (u ∈ P⁵) carrying a conic of V_3 are cut out by the coefficients of τ¹, …, τ⁵ of F5(Q2 + τu), of degrees 1, …, 5 in u (Bezout 120). The solution set contains a spurious curve (the planes ⟨Q, ℓ1⟩ for lines ℓ1 ⊂ V_3 through Q2), so the number N of genuine conics through two general points is < 120; not computed. If N ≤ 8 with a real member, or if the conics split, this is a direct P-mechanism; otherwise it is another 0-dimensional scheme to rule out. (S46.1: N = 12, monodromy S_12.)
- No mechanism for P-conics in V_3 is known yet.

**S45.3 (decision and consequences for the plan).**
- A_9 r = 5 over P: the V_4 route now has a proved line obstruction (S45.1) and a numerically live conic step (S45.2). The open problem is a P-rational conic in V_3, i.e. a P-line on the quintic F5 (projection from a P-point Q of V_3).
- Lemma A is a general filter: on a P-line, an even power sum can vanish identically only when s ≥ m + 1. For S_9 r = 7 (s = 1) only odd power sums (and mixed conditions) can hold identically along P-lines, which is how T3 works; any route for r = 7 through lines must avoid even power sums.

## 0w. Session 46 (Nov 18): results

Topic: priority item 1 after session 45 (A_9, r = 5 over P via conics in V_3): the number and monodromy of conics through two general points of V_3, and the signature question of S45.2.

Notation as in Section 0v. For Q1, Q2 ∈ V_3 put m = Tr(Q1Q2), a = Tr(Q1²Q2), b = Tr(Q1Q2²).

**S46.1 (conics through two points: N = 12 on a genus-one curve; monodromy S_12).** *unchecked* (derivation, elementary) + [numerical] (`conic12.py`, `mono12.py`, `enat.py`).
- **Parametrisation.** For Q1, Q2 ∈ V_3 with m ≠ 0, every smooth conic through both is q(κ) = Q1 + κu + κ²Q2 for a vector u, unique up to u ↦ −u (κ ↦ −κ; the scalings of Q1, Q2 and κ are used up). The coefficients of κ¹, …, κ^{2k−1} in p_k(q(κ)) (k = 1, 2, 3; the end coefficients vanish since Q1, Q2 ∈ V_3) are 9 equations. Five are linear, Tr(u) = Tr(Q1u) = Tr(Q2u) = Tr(Q1²u) = Tr(Q2²u) = 0, and put u in a 4-dimensional space L (defined over P when Q1, Q2 are P-points); the others are
  **Tr(u²) = −2m, Tr(Q1u²) = −a, Tr(Q2u²) = −b, Tr(u³) + 6Tr(Q1Q2u) = 0.**
  Bezout gives at most 2·2·2·3 = 24 vectors u, so at most 12 conics.
- **The genus-one curve.** Put u = U/t and s = t². The first equation gives s = −Tr(U²)/(2m), and eliminating s, the conics are the points [U] ∈ P(L) ≅ P³ of **E ∩ S3**, where E = {aA1 − 2mA2 = 0, bA1 − 2mA4 = 0} with A1 = Tr(U²), A2 = Tr(Q1U²), A4 = Tr(Q2U²) (a (2,2) complete intersection, a genus-one quartic curve), and S3 = {m·Tr(U³) − 3Tr(Q1Q2U)·Tr(U²) = 0} (a cubic surface). The 24 vectors u are the 12 points of E ∩ S3 with the two square roots of s. The pencil of E is {Tr(ρU²)|_L : ρ = α + βQ1 + γQ2, 2mα + aβ + bγ = 0}. For P-points Q1, Q2, E and S3 are defined over P, and a real conic is a real point of E ∩ S3 with s > 0.
- [numerical] `conic12.py c 4 1`: 12 distinct genuine conics (p1, p2, p3 residuals ≤ 3·10⁻¹⁴ along each) on 4 of 4 complex pairs; `conic12.py 5 60 3`: 12 of 12 on all 60 real r = 5 pairs (total-degree homotopy with 12 paths, retried in a new chart when a path is lost). So **N = 12**: the Bezout count is exact and nothing lies at infinity. (S45.2's degree-120 system for the same count therefore carries 108 spurious solutions, *unchecked*.)
- [numerical] `mono12.py`: Q1 fixed, Q2 moved around loops in V_3 (Q2 = Z(θ) + D·c(θ) with Z on a circle and c ∈ ℂ³ solved by Newton, a holomorphic family; loops on which Q2 does not return to the base point are discarded). Seeds 1, 3 and 4 (4, 9 and 4 returning loops): **the group on the 12 conics is S_12** each time (transitive, primitive, order 12!; seed 3 has a 3-cycle, so it contains A_12 by Jordan, and a 12-cycle), and the group on the 24 vectors u is the full wreath product C_2 ≀ S_12 (order 2¹²·12!). Seed 2 had one returning loop only.
- So for Q1, Q2 ∈ V_3(P) the conics through both form a degree-12 scheme over P with generic monodromy S_12: **two-point slices give no split**, and 12 > 8. E has no obvious natural point: the T-orthogonal projections to L of 11 low-degree products of Q1, Q2 all miss E (`enat.py`, residuals ≥ 5·10⁻³).
- Conics through Q1 with a prescribed tangent vector (q = Q1 + κv + κ²w): the same expansion gives an affine (2,2,2,3) system in a 4-dimensional affine space of w, Bezout 24 (*unchecked*, not run). The two-point slice is the smaller one.

**S46.2 (Lemma C: signature (1, 2) conics have p4 > 0; the Hermite filter).** *unchecked* (elementary; the Riccati casework step is marked) + [numerical] (`conic12.py`).
- **Lemma C.** Let s = 2 and let C ⊂ V_3 be a real conic with real parametrisation q(κ) and plane Λ. If T|Λ has signature (1, 2), then p4 > 0 at every real point of C, unless C is degenerate (at most two distinct nonzero coordinate functions q_i(κ)), which is impossible for a P-rational conic in a primitive K.
  *Proof.* At a real point q with tangent q′, the pairings T(1, q), T(1, q′), T(1, q²), T(q, q), T(q, q′), T(q, q²), T(q′, q²) all vanish (p1, p2, p3 ≡ 0 along C and their derivatives), and T(q′, q′) = −c with c > 0, since the tangent line of the isotropic conic of a (1, 2) form is negative semidefinite with radical q. So on W = ⟨1, q, q′, q²⟩ the Gram matrix is diag(9, 0, −c, p4(q)). If the four vectors are independent and p4(q) ≤ 0, then (negative index) + (radical) ≥ 3 > s, impossible (a T-negative-semidefinite subspace has dimension ≤ s). So p4(q) ≤ 0 forces q² = βq + γq′ (the coefficient of 1 is 0 by the trace), hence p4(q) = −cγ²; p4(q) = 0 would give q² = βq, so all coordinates in {0, β} with trace 0, so q = 0. Hence p4 ≠ 0 on the real conic (a circle), and its sign is constant. If it were negative, q² ∈ ⟨q, q′⟩ for all κ, so every coordinate solves the same Riccati equation y′ = (y² − βy)/γ, and the 1/q_i lie on one affine line of functions (w = 1/y solves a linear equation); with deg q_i ≤ 2 this leaves at most two distinct nonzero coordinate functions (casework on the root pairs of a pencil of binary quadrics, *unchecked*). For a P-rational conic in a primitive K a single coincidence q_i ≡ q_j already puts every q(κ), κ ∈ P, in P. ∎
- For signature (2, 1) the Gram matrix is diag(9, 0, +c, p4) and p4 < 0 is allowed (index count 2 = s), as the numerics show.
- **Consequence.** For a real conic through two real points the Hankel entry of S45.2 is x = T(Q1, Q2) = m, so its plane has signature (1, 2) iff Tr(Q1Q2) > 0. **Only pairs with Tr(Q1Q2) < 0 can carry conics that reach V_4.** [numerical] `conic12.py 5 30 2` and `conic12.py 5 60 3`: of 90 random real pairs, the 36 with m > 0 have no real conic at all; of the 54 with m < 0, 15 have 2, 4 or 5 real conics, and 5 have a real conic on which p4 has 2 or 4 real roots. The real condition is open, and it holds at any two points of a real conic of S45.2 with real p4 roots.
- **The Hermite filter (pointwise).** For y ∈ V_{2k−1}(ℝ) with 1, y, …, y^k independent, the Gram matrix of ⟨1, y, …, y^k⟩ is diag(9, 0, …, 0, p_{2k}(y)), so p_{2k}(y) ≤ 0 needs s ≥ k. Hence p4 > 0 on V_3(ℝ) for r ≥ 7 (so V_4(ℝ) = ∅, as Rolle gave in S44.1), p6 > 0 on V_5(ℝ) for r ≥ 5 (so V_6(ℝ) = ∅ for r ≥ 5), p8 > 0 on V_7(ℝ) for r ≥ 3. Lemmas A, B (S45.1) and C are the same count on linear spaces and on conics; all of them are shadows of Hermite's theorem (the Hankel form of the power sums has negative index equal to the number of nonreal pairs).
