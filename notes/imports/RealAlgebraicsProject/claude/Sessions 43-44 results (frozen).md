## 0t. Session 43 (Nov 18): results

Topic: priority item 1 after session 42, (PRC₈) head-on: (a) the cleanest conditional theorem, (b) the smallest test variety, including the fact-4 conic curve in Y4 of S42.3, (c) the comparison with the literature.

**S43.1 (the goal is equivalent to PRC for the twisted Chebyshev moment surfaces).** *unchecked* (elementary, complete) + [exact] for n = 9 (`f7_census.py`).
- **Reduction.** P is Euclidean (fact 1 plus square roots), so the goal ⇔ P is real closed ⇔ every odd-degree polynomial over P has a root in P ⇔ P has no finite extension of odd degree > 1 (ℝ_alg is algebraic over P, and a real closed field has no proper ordered algebraic extension). If the goal fails, take an odd-degree extension K/P of minimal degree n > 1: every intermediate field has odd degree, so K/P is primitive (Galois group G primitive of degree n); n ≥ 9 by fact 3; K has an odd number r ≥ 1 of real places, and G ≤ A_n iff r ≡ n mod 4.
- **Engine varieties.** For a family 𝓕 of monic degree-n polynomials over a P-variety T such that every real root of every separable P-member lies in P (an "engine"), let Ts_K(𝓕) = {(y, t) : y ∈ K, charpoly(y) = 𝓕_t} (a variety over P). A P-point with separable charpoly contradicts minimality (the real conjugate of y is in P, but y has n distinct conjugates). Over P̄ the separable part of Ts_K is the ordered-roots cover of T, smooth where T is, and irreducible iff the geometric monodromy is S_n. Ts_K has a smooth real point iff 𝓕 has a separable real member with exactly r real roots at a smooth point of T (K ⊗ ℝ = ℝ^r × ℂ^s; a nonreal ℂ-component gives a conjugate pair).
- **The choice: fact 7 with q = 1.** For T_n(x) + ax + c (fact 7, p = n, q = 1) the coefficients of x^{n−1} … x² are fixed, so Ts_K is **M_{n,K} := {y ∈ K : Tr(y^k) = π_k, k = 1, …, n − 2}**, where π_k is the k-th power sum of the Chebyshev nodes cos((2j − 1)π/2n) (rational: π_1 = 0, π_2 = n/2, …). A complete intersection surface of degrees 1, …, n − 2 in A^n (twisted). **Every P-point is separable**: a non-separable y in the primitive field K has charpoly (x − y)^n with y ∈ P, so π_1 = 0 forces y = 0, against π_2 = n/2. Projective closure: the (1, 2, …, n − 2) complete intersection {p_k(y) = π_k λ^k} in P^n; for n = 9, K = O(28 − 10) = O(18), general type; deg 7! = 5040. Its curve at infinity {λ = 0} is the twisted Bring–Jerrard curve {p1 = … = p_{n−2} = 0} (the Tschirnhaus curve of fact 2), which has no real points when r = n (p2 > 0) and is a smooth complete intersection (Jacobian rank = number of distinct coordinates; *unchecked*, short).
- **Signature lemma.** T_n + ax + c reaches every odd r ≤ n: for small a > 0 the (n − 1)/2 interior maxima of T_n + ax have distinct values 1 + aξ_j, increasing with the abscissa ξ_j, and the level −c just below the top m of them is crossed 1 + 2m times. [exact, n = 9] Grid census (a ∈ [−4, 4], c ∈ [−2, 2], step 1/10 and 1/20, Sturm counts): **every q ∈ {1, 2, 4, 5, 7, 8} reaches r ∈ {1, 3, 5, 7, 9}.** (q = 3, 6 are imprimitive compositions with T_3.)
- **Integrality for n = 9.** [exact] disc_x(T_9 + aT_q + c) is irreducible in ℚ[a, c] with exponent 1 for each q ∈ {1, 2, 4, 5, 7, 8} (bidegrees (9, 8), (10, 8), (12, 8), (13, 8), (15, 8), (16, 8)), so the inertia along it is a transposition, and since π_1(A² − D) is generated by meridians the geometric monodromy is generated by transpositions and transitive: **S_9**. The member at (a, c) = (3/7, 2/11) is irreducible with Frobenius types including (9), (8,1), (7,1,1) (S_9 over ℚ). Hence M_{9,K} is geometrically integral (each component has dimension 2 and the map y ↦ charpoly is finite, so each component dominates the (a, c)-plane and meets the separable locus) and smooth along its separable locus.
- **Theorem 43.1.** *The goal is equivalent to* **(PRC_Ch)**: for every odd n ≥ 9 and every primitive extension K/P of degree n, the twisted Chebyshev moment surface M_{n,K} has a P-point. M_{n,K} always has smooth real points (signature lemma), so (PRC_Ch) is an instance of "P is PRC", restricted to one explicit surface per field. (⇒: if the goal holds, P is real closed and no such K exists. ⇐: a minimal counterexample K gives a P-point of M_{n,K}, contradiction.) For n = 9 the surface is geometrically integral and of general type; for n > 9 integrality needs the monodromy of T_n + ax + c to be S_n (*unchecked*).
- **What it uses:** facts 1, 3 and 7 only (not fact 2, T0–T7 or Engine O). The rational version (scope (i)): a real algebraic number of degree 9 over ℚ with field K_0 is P-constructible if M_{9,K_0 ⊗ P} has a P-point; M_{9,K_0} is defined over ℚ (π_k ∈ ℚ).
- **Consequences.** (i) The repo's (PRC₈) (every smooth twisted variety with a smooth real point has a P-point) is far more than needed: one surface per field suffices, of dimension 2 (W and L_135 ∩ {p7 = 0} are 4-folds, Engine O's Y_K is also a surface but misses r = 7). (ii) Real-signature coverage (the only legitimate obstruction, S41.1) is complete for fact 7: it serves every r, including **S_9, r = 7 over P**, which Engine O never reaches. Fact 2 trinomials reach r ≤ 3 (Descartes) and fact 4 (x^9 = cubic) r ≤ 5 (Rolle on the 4th derivative). (iii) As for every engine, a P-point of M_{9,K} is a versality statement (S41.1) and cannot be refuted by dimension arguments. **Smallest test object: M_{9,K} for the PSL(2,8) r = 9 test polynomial** (a twist of a general-type surface defined over ℚ). A ℚ-point would mean K_0 ≅ ℚ[x]/(T_9 + ax + c); not expected (a thin set), and not what is needed.
- **Curve version.** For β ∈ K, γ ∈ P the curves M_K ∩ {Tr(βy) = γ} are P-curves on M_K, with smooth real points for open sets of (β, γ); a P-point on any of them suffices. They are (1, 1, 2, …, 7) complete intersections in P^9 of degree 5040 and genus 47881 (adjunction, n = 9), so Pál- or Theorem-G-type results (genus ≤ 4, gonality ≤ 8) have nothing to say about them.

**S43.2 (the fact-4 conic curve in Y4: corrected count, real points).** *unchecked* (count) + [numerical] (`cy4.py`, `gam_an.py`).
- **Count.** Decics of shape x¹⁰ = cubic: a P⁴ of shapes, PGL_2 orbits of dimension 3, stabiliser x ↦ αx of dimension 1, so a 6-dimensional locus, **codimension 4** in P¹⁰ (S42.3 missed the stabiliser). So conics C ⊂ Y4 with p5|C of fact-4 cubic shape are expected to be **finitely many**, a 0-dimensional scheme, not a curve. With a **quartic** right-hand side (x¹⁰ = quartic: the power law y = x¹⁰ against the quartic graph of T1 / Lemma Q, monic and sign normalised by x ↦ αx with α⁶ > 0 and a reflection; *unchecked* that this is an allowed construction) the shape locus has codimension 3 and the conics form an expected **curve Γ**. Equivalently: a point P∞ on C such that, in an affine coordinate with P∞ at ∞, the 10 roots of p5|C have vanishing central moments of orders 2 to 5.
- **Real conics in Y4 exist** (r = 9 real structure). `cy4.py 1 15 0`: 7 of 15 Levenberg–Marquardt starts give smooth conics (A, B, C independent, no zero or paired coordinates), each with Jacobian rank 16 (the expected 4-dimensional family plus the 4-dimensional GL_2 gauge). So there is no Lemma F.2-type real obstruction for conics in Y4 (unlike lines and planes on Q4). On the 7 samples p5|C has 8, 8, 6, 6, 2, 4, 2 real roots; every real root is a real point of W (checked to 10⁻¹²).
- **Γ has smooth real points.** `cy4.py <seed> 40 1` (special point at s = ∞ and coefficients s⁵ … s⁹ of p5|C set to 0; gauge: two random linear normalisations): 2 of 80 starts give genuine conics, each with Jacobian rank 21 (full), so Γ is a smooth curve there; the other starts fall to degenerate (rank-2) conics. On the two samples the decic x¹⁰ = quartic has 2 and 0 real roots, so the step to W works on part of Γ(ℝ) only.
- **Status.** Γ is a curve over P (after the K-twist) with real points; P-points need a map of degree ≤ 8 to P¹ defined over P (so built from S_9-invariant data); none is known, and by S43.1 Γ is no better placed than the P-curves on M_K. **Parked**, with the corrected count.

**S43.3 (literature comparison).** *unchecked* (comparison).
- (PRC_Ch) is a real-field analogue of the open ampleness question for Q^solv (S41.2): an ample field puts points on every smooth curve that has one; here one needs points on one explicit family of twisted surfaces that have real points. Q^tr is PRC (Moret-Bailly, Pop) but its points have no Galois control; Pál's genus ≤ 4 theorem and its relatives (S42.4) are reality-free and concern low genus, while the P-curves on M_K have genus 47881. What would be needed is a **real, Galois-controlled** PRC theorem for one family; no such result is known to us.

## 0u. Session 44 (Nov 18): results

Topic: housekeeping (Sections 0r and 0s moved to `claude/Sessions 41-42 results (frozen).md`), then the main line, the genuine gaps over P with nonreal roots: **the V_4 route**, a Bring–Jerrard-type reduction (kill p1, …, p4) to the engine x⁹ = quartic that T1 makes available.

**S44.1 (the V_4 target and the engine x⁹ = quartic).** *unchecked* (elementary).
- V_k := {y ∈ K : Tr(y^j) = 0, j = 1, …, k} (cones; projectively in P(K) ≅ P⁸). A P-point y ≠ 0 of V_4 generates K (K primitive; y ∈ P would force y = 0), and its charpoly is x⁹ + c5x⁴ + … + c9 (Newton: p1 = … = p4 = 0 ⇔ e1 = … = e4 = 0). Its real roots are P-constructible: after x ↦ αx with α⁵ = −c5 (a real fifth root, in L) the equation is x⁹ = monic quartic, i.e. the power law y = x⁹ against the T1 quartic graph (the use of T1 that Section 1 lists as "fact 4 with a quartic right-hand side"; negative roots by the reflection y ↦ −y; c5 = 0 is fact 4 itself). *unchecked* that this is an allowed construction, as in S43.2. So V_4 is the Tschirnhaus variety of the engine "x⁹ = quartic" (S43.1 terminology): a (1, 2, 3, 4) complete intersection cone, projectively a 4-fold of degree 24 with K = O(1).
- **Signature limit (Rolle).** f = x⁹ + quartic has f⁽⁴⁾ = 3024x⁵ + 24c5 with exactly one real root, so f has ≤ 5 distinct real roots. Hence **V_4(ℝ) = ∅ for r ∈ {7, 9}**, and the engine can serve only r ∈ {1, 3, 5}. (Numerics agree: Newton finds no real point of V_4 for r = 7.)
- **V_2 and its lines.** On the trace-zero hyperplane, Tr(y²) has signature (r + s − 1, s) (r + 2s = 9). For s ≥ 1 it is indefinite, so isotropic over the Euclidean field P, and V_2 is a P-rational quadric. The residual form on Q^⊥/Q has signature (r + s − 2, s − 1), so **for r = 7 (s = 1) V_2 contains no real lines**: no line-based step inside {p2 = 0} exists for r = 7.

**S44.2 (the V_4 chain for r ∈ {1, 3}).** *unchecked* (chain) + [numerical] (`v4chain.py`, `v4cert.py`).
- The chain. Every step has degree ≤ 8 and a real root (fact 3); all varieties are defined over P by trace forms.
  1. **Q ∈ V_3(P), dense in V_3(ℝ).** A P-plane section of V_2 through a real conic is a conic over P with a real point, hence ≅ P¹ over P (P is Euclidean); p3 on it is a binary sextic over P with a simple real root near any chosen real point of V_3 where the conic is transversal. (Sextic step.)
  2. **Lines through Q in V_3** ↔ points of C_Q = {A1 = A2 = c = 0} ⊂ P⁴, after the linear conditions p1(v) = Σ Q v = Σ Q² v = 0, with A1 = p2(v), A2 = Σ Q v², c = p3(v): a (2, 2, 3) curve of **degree 12 and genus 13** on the quartic del Pezzo surface S_Q = {A1 = A2 = 0}.
  3. **A P-rational conic bundle on S_Q.** The pencil λA1 + A2 has 5 singular members (a quintic in λ; its real roots are in P by fact 3). If λ0A1 + A2 has rank 4 and **signature (2, 2)**, it is a cone over a split quadric surface (⟨1, 1, −1, −1⟩ is hyperbolic over P), so its two rulings are defined over P, and the planes of one ruling cut S_Q in a P¹-family of conics C_t over P. (Quintic step.)
  4. **A P-point of C_Q.** For t ∈ P¹(P) near the ruling plane through a real point v0 of C_Q, C_t is a real conic, hence ≅ P¹ over P, and C_Q ∩ C_t is the zero set of a binary sextic over P with a simple real root near v0 (transversality). So C_Q → P¹ has degree 6 over P, and its P-points are dense near v0. (Square root and sextic step.)
  5. **p4 on the P-line Q + κv** is a quartic in κ; a simple real root κ0 gives y = Q + κ0v ∈ V_4(P). (Quartic step.) Then S44.1 applies.
- The real conditions (V_3 smooth at Q; v0 a smooth real point of C_Q; p4|line with a simple real root; a real (2,2) singular member; C_t transversal to C_Q at v0) are open in (Q, v0) and **independent of K** (K ⊗ ℝ ≅ ℝ^r × ℂ^s with the standard traces), so one real sample per signature gives them for every K, with P-points dense nearby.
- [numerical] `v4chain.py <r> 4 1` for r = 3 and r = 1: C_Q(ℝ) ≠ ∅, lines with 2 or 4 real roots of p4, and a (2,2) singular member, on 4/4 random Q each (also 4/4 for r = 3 when Q starts at a real point of V_4, `v4chain.py 3 4 2 v4`). `v4cert.py 3 5` and `v4cert.py 1 5` give one sample each with margins (r = 3 / r = 1): smallest singular value 2.6 / 2.9 for V_3 at Q and 0.43 / 1.0 for C_Q at v0; |dp4/dκ| = 1.16 / 4.75 at the root; a (2,2) member with relative eigenvalue gap 0.23 / 0.32; the ruling conic lies on S_Q to 10⁻¹⁴ and meets C_Q transversally (normalised 0.12 / 0.30); y = Q + κ0v0 has p1 = … = p4 = 0 to 10⁻¹² and exactly r real roots.
- **Consequence.** For every nonic K over P with r ∈ {1, 3} (any Galois group), the real roots are P-constructible through steps of degree ≤ 8 plus the x⁹ = quartic engine, modulo the *unchecked* chain and the engine's status. This is a **second route for PSL(2,8) r = 1, A_9 r = 1 and S_9 r = 3 over P**, independent of THREE-PAIRS (T4), and it comes with the explicit real open region that T4 lacked. Not a full proof: no interval arithmetic, and the density argument of step 1 is short but *unchecked*.

**S44.3 (r = 5: real lines in V_3 never meet V_4).** [numerical] (`r5min.py`, `r5probe.py`, `v4chain.py 5 4 2 v4`, `vprime.py`). **Proved in S45.1; the V′ part is corrected there.**
