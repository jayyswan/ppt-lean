# Research plan: trinomial generators over the real solvable closure (SR)

Nov 15, 2026 · @Arya

## Problem and context

The goal is to prove that every real algebraic number is P-constructible, by reducing that claim to a statement about trinomials over the real solvable closure of Q.

**Background.** P-constructibility is a fixed notion of geometric constructibility for real numbers. Its definition is not needed here and should be treated as a black box: this plan uses only three facts about it, all already proved, and nothing else about the construction model should be assumed or changed.

1. The P-constructible reals form a field containing L (defined below).
2. Any real root of x^D + ax + b with P-constructible a, b is P-constructible.
3. Any real root of a polynomial of degree at most 8 with P-constructible coefficients is P-constructible.

**Further proved families.** Real roots of the following are also P-constructible whenever every listed parameter is P-constructible. Use them as black-box facts 4 to 7. The list is closed: do not look for further families or reason about the construction model.

| Fact | Family | Free parameters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 4 | x^D = c\_3 x^3 + c\_2 x^2 + c\_1 x + c\_0, any D > 3, any real root | 4 |
| 5 | U(t)^3 + lambda U(t)^2 + V(t)^2 = 0, with U a monic cubic and V a cubic (degree 9) | 8 |
| 6 | (A c + B e - C)^2 (c'^2 + e'^2) = d^2 (B c' - A e')^2, with c, e cubics in t (degree 10). Holds only under an extra sign condition and a regularity condition, not reproduced here | 12, with some redundancy |
| 7 | T\_p(x) + a T\_q(x) + c = 0 with roots in \[-1, 1\], T the Chebyshev polynomials | 2 |

Larger families exist whose shape parameters must be rational. Points over L do not help with those, so they are out of scope.

**Base field.** Every statement below posed over L may be posed instead over P, the field of real algebraic P-constructible numbers. P contains L and is Euclidean, since x^2 - m is a trinomial. A point over P suffices in the one-step form as well as the tower form, so a negative answer over L does not block the route.

**Notation.** L is the field of real numbers whose minimal polynomial over Q has solvable Galois group, that is, the real numbers in the solvable closure Q^solv of Q. A trinomial over L is a polynomial x^D + ax + b with D at least 2 and a, b in L.

**Open statement (SR), as originally posed.** For every real algebraic number y there is a real number tau such that tau is a root of some trinomial over L and L(tau) = L(y). Equivalently, every finite extension of L generated by a real algebraic number is generated by a real root of a trinomial over L.

**Why it would suffice.** By fact 2, tau is P-constructible. Since L(tau) = L(y), y is a polynomial in tau with coefficients in L, so by fact 1 it is P-constructible too.

**Weaker variants that also suffice.**

- *Tower form.* Let M be the smallest subfield of R that contains L and is closed under adjoining real roots of trinomials with coefficients in M. Claim: M is the field of all real algebraic numbers. This allows several trinomial steps instead of one.
- *Larger base.* In the tower form, M may also be closed under adjoining real roots of any polynomial of degree at most 8 over M, by fact 3.
- *Reduced targets.* It is enough to treat y = sqrt(beta - c) for beta real algebraic and c rational, so that y has at most two real conjugates.

**Geometric form.** Fix K = Q(y) of degree D. Let I\_K be the Q-variety of triples (tau, a, b) where tau is an element of K with distinct conjugates whose characteristic polynomial is exactly x^D + ax + b. It is a smooth, geometrically integral surface. (SR) for y follows if I\_K has a point with coordinates in L. Modulo scaling, I\_K is a cone over a curve of genus at least 2, so by Faltings each fixed number field contributes only finitely many points; the points must come from infinitely many solvable fields.

**The three research questions.**

1. Is (SR), or the tower form, known, or does it follow from known results on points of varieties over Q^solv? The difficulty is that the points must be real.
2. Is there an obstruction: an invariant of L(y)/L that every trinomial extension satisfies and some extension violates?
3. What is the smallest test case, and can tau be exhibited explicitly or over a small solvable field?

**Terms used below.**

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Real conjugates of y over L | Real roots of the minimal polynomial of y over L; equivalently, L-embeddings of L(y) into R |
| r, s | For a number field K of degree D: the number of real embeddings and of complex-conjugate pairs, so D = r + 2s |
| Euclidean field | An ordered field in which every positive element is a square; it has exactly one ordering |
| Real closed field | An ordered field with no proper ordered algebraic extension; the real algebraic numbers are the example here |
| PAC (pseudo algebraically closed) | A field over which every geometrically integral variety has a rational point |
| PRC (pseudo real closed) | A field over which every geometrically integral variety with a smooth point in each real closure has a rational point |
| Power sums p\_k | p\_k(tau) is the trace of tau^k from K to Q. The characteristic polynomial of tau is x^D + ax + b exactly when p\_1 = ... = p\_(D-2) = 0 |
| Tschirnhaus transformation | Replacing a root y by another generator tau of the same field, chosen so its polynomial has fewer terms |
| Bring–Jerrard form | x^5 + ax + b, or more generally a polynomial with its three subleading coefficients removed |

## Summary

(SR) as stated is false, and the corrected one-step version (SR-3) is implied by a conjecture at least as strong as the open question whether Q^solv is PAC. Degrees up to 8 are settled by fact 3, so the first cases that matter are nonics; degrees 5 and 6 are arithmetic test beds only. The richer families (facts 4 to 7) cut the conditions from D - 2 to D - 4 and, for fact 7, remove the real-root cap, but none keeps pace with D.

The order of work:

1. Fix the statement (Step 0, which states the corrected version (SR-3)). A trinomial has at most 3 real roots, which rules out any y whose minimal polynomial over L has 4 or more real roots.
2. Confirm the literature position: nothing known gives real solvable points on curves of this genus, and nothing known forbids them.
3. Push the classical Tschirnhaus method over L as far as it goes. It proves the degree-5, one-real-root case and removes three coefficients in general.
4. Hunt for an obstruction at the first open cases (degree 5 with three real roots, degree 6 with two).
5. Reformulate the tower form as "M is real closed" and attack it by odd-degree root existence.

Workstream E then reruns steps 3 and 4 with facts 4 to 7 in place of trinomials. That is where the nonic cases live.

Everything marked *to verify* below is my own argument and has not been checked against a source or by computation.

## Step 0: correct the statement

(SR) fails for a real root y of any totally real quintic with Galois group S\_5. The argument is short and should be written up first.

**Root count.** For g = x^D + ax + b with real a, b, the derivative Dx^(D-1) + a has at most 2 real roots. So g has at most 3 real roots (D odd) or at most 2 (D even).

**Counterexample.** Let f be a totally real S\_5 quintic over Q with real root y.

1. The splitting field of f meets Q^solv in Q(sqrt(disc)), because the largest solvable quotient of S\_5 is C\_2. So f has group A\_5 over Q^solv and stays irreducible over L.
2. L(y) therefore has 5 distinct L-embeddings into R.
3. If L(tau) = L(y), each embedding sends tau to a distinct real root of its minimal polynomial h over L. So h has 5 real roots.
4. If tau is a root of a trinomial g over L, then h divides g, and g has at most 3 real roots. Contradiction.

**Corrected statement (SR-3).** For every real algebraic y whose minimal polynomial over L has at most 3 real roots, there is a real trinomial root tau over L with L(tau) = L(y).

**Consequences for the rest of the plan.**

- The "reduced targets" variant is not optional. Taking y = sqrt(beta - c) with c rational between the two largest real conjugates of beta gives exactly 2 real conjugates, which is inside (SR-3).
- In the geometric form, I\_K has a real point only for the right signatures: D odd with 1 or 3 real places, D even with 2. "Has a real point" is a hypothesis on K, not a general fact.
- L is Euclidean (a positive real solvable number has a real solvable square root), so L has a unique ordering and its real closure is the field of real algebraic numbers. The real place is the only place where a local condition can live; see Reformulations.

**Tasks.**

- [ ] Write the counterexample and (SR-3).
- [ ] Re-check the "reduced targets" reduction against (SR-3), including the case where the minimal polynomial of y drops degree over L.
- [ ] Decide whether to allow tau whose minimal polynomial is a proper factor of the trinomial. The geometric form excludes this; allowing it gives a family of auxiliary curves (Workstream D).

## Reformulations

Three equivalent or stronger forms organise the work; each is *to verify* before relying on it.

**Specialization form.** An L-point of I\_K is the same as a pair (a, b) in L with L\[x\]/(x^D + ax + b) isomorphic to K ⊗ L as L-algebras. With t = a^D / b^(D-1), the trinomial family is an S\_D cover of the t-line branched at three points, with monodromy a D-cycle, a (D-1)-cycle and a transposition. The curve under I\_K is the twist of this cover by K.

Riemann–Hurwitz gives its genus:

```latex
2g - 2 = D!\left(\tfrac12 - \tfrac1D - \tfrac1{D-1}\right)
```

| D | Genus |
| --- | --- |
| 5 | 4 |
| 6 | 49 |
| 9 | 47,881 |
| 10 | 524,161 |

**Conjecture P: L is pseudo real closed.** That is, every geometrically integral L-variety with a smooth real algebraic point has an L-point. Since L has one ordering, Conjecture P implies (SR-3) for every K with I\_K(R) nonempty. It also implies Q^solv is PAC, so it is not available as a tool. Its value is as a guide: it predicts the real place is the only obstruction.

Supporting heuristic: every finite place of L should have algebraically closed henselization. G\_Qp is prosolvable and Grunwald–Wang lets global solvable extensions approximate local ones, so Q^solv is p-adically as large as possible; an index-2 subfield of an algebraically closed field is real closed, which a p-adic field cannot be.

**Tower form as real closedness.** M contains square roots of its positive elements (x^2 - m is a trinomial), so M is Euclidean. By Artin–Schreier, M is all of the real algebraic numbers if and only if every odd-degree polynomial over M has a root in M. Only odd degrees need a new idea.

**What the dimension theory does and does not say.** Essential dimension and resolvent degree bound parameters for families over higher-dimensional bases. Over an algebraic field like L they give no obstruction to a one-parameter family being enough. Their upper-bound techniques do transfer, and that is Workstream A.

## Workstreams

Five strands, A first because it yields theorems quickly. Write r for the number of real places of K and s for the number of complex pairs, so D = r + 2s.

### A. Classical Tschirnhaus over L

A trinomial tau is a point of K where the power sums p\_1, ..., p\_(D-2) all vanish. The first three can be killed over L by Bring's method whenever s is at least 2.

1. On trace-zero elements, p\_2 is the trace form, of signature (r + s - 1, s). Quadratic forms over a Euclidean field are classified by signature, so the quadric p\_2 = 0 contains L-rational linear spaces of projective dimension s - 1.
2. If s is at least 2, take an L-line on the quadric. The cubic p\_3 restricted to it has a real root, and cubics are solvable, so the root is in L.
3. Result: every such y is L-equivalent to a root of x^D + c\_4 x^(D-4) + ... + c\_D.

For D = 5 and r = 1 this is all of (SR-3): the real Bring–Jerrard form. For D = 5 and r = 3 the quadric is a sphere, its two rulings are complex conjugate, and the method stops. That is the first open case.

- [ ] Write Theorem A with proof: (SR-3) for D = 5, r = 1, and three-coefficient removal over L for s at least 2.
- [ ] Check whether the fourth coefficient can be removed over L for large D, following the hypersurface-intersection papers, while keeping track of realness.

### B. Obstruction hunt (Question 2)

Step 0 found the real-place obstruction. The working hypothesis from Conjecture P is that there is no other, so this strand tries to break that hypothesis at D = 5, r = 3.

- [ ] Describe the real locus of the genus 4 curve: number of components, and whether any component is forced to miss L-points.
- [ ] Compute Br(L). I expect Z/2, detected at the real place, which would leave Brauer–Manin with no content beyond real components.
- [ ] List which pairs (Galois group of the closure of L(y)/L, class of complex conjugation) arise from trinomials in low degree, and compare with all pairs allowed by Step 0.

### C. Tower form

The tower does not avoid point-finding: some step must be a trinomial root over a field F inside M whose Galois closure introduces the simple group A\_n. What the tower buys is the freedom to change K.

- [ ] Replace K by resolvent fields. For H a subgroup of S\_n, the fixed field K\_H generates the same closure; choose H so K\_H has few real places and its curve is more tractable. The sqrt(beta - c) trick is one instance.
- [ ] Use the degree-8 closure in the "larger base" variant to absorb any factor of degree at most 8 that appears along the way.
- [ ] Set up the induction: a real y of minimal odd degree over M not in M, and derive constraints on its Galois group over M(i).

### D. Auxiliary curves

Allowing tau whose minimal polynomial h is a proper factor of a trinomial of degree N > D gives one new curve per N, again by a dimension count. Each has its own real locus and low-degree maps.

- [ ] Compute the genus and gonality of the first few auxiliary curves for D = 5, r = 3 and look for one with a real map of degree at most 4 to P^1.

### E. Richer families (facts 4 to 7)

Each family replaces the trinomial in the one-step and tower forms, with fewer conditions on tau and a different real-root cap. Use the facts exactly as stated in the context section.

| Family | Conditions on tau in K | Real-root cap |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Trinomial (fact 2) | p\_1 = ... = p\_(D-2) = 0 | 3 for D odd, 2 for D even |
| Fact 4 | p\_1 = ... = p\_(D-4) = 0 | 5 for D odd, 4 for D even |
| Fact 7 (Chebyshev) | D - 4 conditions on K | None: p real roots when \|a\| + \|c\| < 1 |
| Fact 5 | Fixed degree 9; one condition short of a general nonic | Not determined here |
| Fact 6 | Fixed degree 10 | Not determined here |

The fact 4 cap follows from Rolle: the fourth derivative of x^D minus a cubic is a multiple of x^(D-4). The fact 7 and fact 5 entries are reported results that this plan has not re-derived; verify them before use.

**What this buys.** With fact 4 the target is a threefold instead of a curve, and (SR-3) relaxes to y with at most 5 (or 4) real conjugates. With fact 7 totally real fields are allowed directly, with no square-root lift. Fact 5 is the route for nonics with a nonreal root.

**What it does not buy.** The variety where p\_1, ..., p\_j vanish is Fano when j(j+1)/2 < D. With j = D - 4 that holds only for D at most 7, which fact 3 already covers. At D = 9 it is five conditions in P^8 and general type again. Reportedly, and not re-derived here, every family has a number of free parameters bounded independently of D, so none stays Fano as D grows, and a proof uniform in K would bound resolvent degree by a constant. Expect field-specific arithmetic.

- [ ] Restate Step 0 for each family: the right real-root cap and the corresponding corrected statement.
- [ ] D = 9 with fact 4: apply Workstream A to kill p\_1, p\_2, p\_3 over the base field, then study the residual locus p\_4 = p\_5 = 0.
- [ ] D = 9 totally real with fact 7: derive the D - 4 conditions on K explicitly, then look for a reduction to a low-dimensional target such as a P-rational line on an explicit surface.
- [ ] D = 9 with a nonreal root: write out what fact 5 leaves to prove.
- [ ] Run every point search over P as well as over L (see Base field).

## Test cases and computations (Question 3)

Degrees 5 to 8 say nothing new about P-constructibility, because fact 3 already covers them. The D = 5 and D = 6 rows are test beds for real solvable points as pure arithmetic, on curves one can compute with. The cases that matter for the goal are nonics: totally real ones, and those with P-constructible coefficients and at most two complex pairs.

| Case | Object | What to do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| D = 5, r = 1 | Genus 4 curve on a quadric with real rulings | Implement real Bring–Jerrard in Sage or Magma and output tau for a sample quintic. This is the template code for everything else |
| D = 5, r = 5 | No real points | Negative control: confirm numerically that no trinomial fits, matching Step 0 |
| D = 5, r = 3 | Genus 4 sextic curve on a sphere | Search plane sections whose binary sextic has solvable Galois group and a real root. From one L-point P, the tangent plane at P leaves a residual quartic, which is solvable, so one point may generate more |
| D = 6, r = 2 | Genus 49 curve on a K3 surface (the quadric–cubic intersection in P^4) | Use Workstream A to produce L-points on the K3, then test whether the quartic p\_4 can be met along L-rational curves of low degree |
| D = 9 or 10 | Genus 47,881 or 524,161 | No direct search on the trinomial curve. Work through facts 4, 5 and 7 (Workstream E) and the resolvent fields K\_H of Workstream C |

**Tasks.**

- [ ] Pick explicit polynomials: one S\_5 quintic for each of r = 1, 3, 5; one S\_6 sextic with r = 2; one S\_9 nonic with r = 1.
- [ ] For each, verify irreducibility over L by computing the Galois group and its derived series.

## Decision points

1. **After Step 0 and the literature pass.** If an expert reports that "L is PRC" is known, false, or equivalent to Q^solv being PAC, re-plan around that answer.
2. **After D = 5, r = 3.** A real solvable point is evidence for (SR-3) and gives a method to generalise. A proof that none exists kills the one-step form and leaves only the tower.
3. **If the tower form stalls.** Move effort to the richer families of Workstream E, using facts 4 to 7 only as stated.

My expectation: (SR-3) in full is out of reach of current methods because it sits above an open problem, the D = 5, r = 3 case is decidable with effort, and unconditional progress on P-constructibility is most likely to come from the tower form or a richer family.
