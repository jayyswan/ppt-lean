## 0ar. Session 67 (Nov 21): results

Topic: priority 1, scope (i): the cubic-arithmetic step that S66.5 requires of any global mechanism for V_A, and what it leaves for the quintic. **Mechanism named:** the Hasse principle for the Hermite–Joubert cubic X_A = {Tr y = Tr y³ = 0} ⊂ P(A) (a cubic in 9 variables with 126 nodes, S66.2), as the step that fails over 3-fields, followed by a transfer from X_A(ℚ) to V_A(ℚ). **Test that could fail:** (a) whether a theorem covers a nodal nonary cubic (S66.2 had found none); (b) whether X_A is everywhere locally soluble; (c) whether any transfer X_A → V_A works. **Result:** (a) and (b) pass: **Hooley's fourth nonary paper (Crelle 680, 2013) gives the Hasse principle for cubic hypersurfaces in P⁸ whose singularities are isolated ordinary double points**, and X_A has exactly 126 ordinary nodes [exact]; X_A has smooth ℚ_p-points at every unramified prime for every A [exact]. So the Hermite–Joubert problem for n = 10 is solved over ℚ up to local conditions at ramified primes, in contrast to 3-fields (S66.1). (c) fails for every transfer tried: the quintic condition is the whole remaining gap for scope (i).

**S67.1 (Hooley IV applies to X_A).** [source] (secondary: Heath-Brown's Royal Society memoir of Hooley, which states that Hooley IV, J. Reine Angew. Math. 680 (2013) 23–39, gives a nontrivial zero of a cubic form in n ≥ 9 variables with zeros everywhere locally whenever the singularities of the projective hypersurface are isolated ordinary double points; the same statement as Theorem H in Zhao, arXiv 1211.4215, and in arXiv 2610.08226. Hooley II–III (1991, 1994) needed the double points to be linearly independent, which 126 points in P⁸ are not; Hooley IV was not read) + [exact] (`nodes67.sing`, Singular over ℚ).
- **Sing(X̄) is 126 ordinary double points.** For X̄ = {Σz = Σz³ = 0} ⊂ P⁹ (z10 eliminated, a cubic F in 9 variables), the Jacobian ideal has affine dimension 1, degree (= sum of the Tjurina numbers) 126, and its radical has degree 126: 126 reduced points, each of Tjurina number 1, hence each an A1 singularity. (S66.2 had this by hand: the projective tangent cone at each node is a smooth quadric.)
- **Theorem (S67.1, given Hooley IV):** for every étale ℚ-algebra A of degree 10, **X_A(ℚ) ≠ ∅ as soon as X_A(ℚ_p) ≠ ∅ for every p** (real points are automatic, odd degree). For primitive A the nodes are not ℚ-rational (S65.1), so the point is smooth and a ≠ 0 generates A. The same for nonics: X_{K×ℚ} = {u ∈ K : Tr u³ = (Tr u)³} ⊂ P(K) is the same twisted nodal cubic.
- So the 3-primary input that S66.1 shows any global mechanism needs (a step that fails over some 3-field) exists over ℚ for the cubic part of V_A: **X_A satisfies the Hasse principle**, while over the 3-field of S66.1 it has no points. This is an unconditional replacement for Heath-Brown's 10-variable input of S66.2 with the trace condition included.

**S67.2 (local solubility of X_A; Hermite–Joubert for n = 10 over ℚ).** [exact] (`nodes67.py`, `xloc67.py`) + *unchecked* (assembly, short).
- Unramified p ≥ 7: Chevalley–Warning (1 + 3 < 10) gives #X(F_p) ≡ 1 mod p; for p ≥ 7 the singular F_p-points are exactly the Frobenius-stable nodes (bisections S with Frob(S) = S or S^c). Their number by cycle type (`nodes67.py`): 0 for (9,1), (7,3), (8,1,1), (7,2,1), …; 1 for (10), (5,5), (5,4,1), (5,3,2), (5,3,1,1), (5,2,2,1), (5,2,1,1,1), (5,1⁵), (4,3,2,1); otherwise ≢ 1 mod p except (4,2,2,2) at p = 7 and (2,2,2,1⁴), (2,1⁸) at p = 11. Every type with count ≡ 1 mod p has two equal cycles or an even cycle, which gives an explicit smooth ℚ_p-point (a = (x, −x, 0, …) on two isomorphic factors; a = x with Frob^c x = −x on a factor of degree 2c; a² non-scalar). The other types have a smooth F_p-point, which lifts.
- Unramified p = 2, 3, 5: pair/even-cycle points as above for every type except the two decic types with distinct odd cycles, (9,1) and (7,3) (nonic types: only (9,1), covered by S64.2 since V_K ⊂ X_K). Certified by line sections (`xloc67.py`: an integer line in {Tr = 0} whose restricted cubic is squarefree over ℚ with a ℚ_p-root gives a smooth ℚ_p-point): (9,1) at 2, 3, 5: 17, 20, 26 of 30 lines; (7,3) at 2, 3, 5: 19, 21, 26 of 30.
- **So X_A has smooth points at every real place and every unramified prime, for every A.** At ramified primes X_A ⊃ V_A, so every local point of S64.3–S64.4 and S66.4 is a point of X_A; in addition any factor L = M(π) with π^e ∈ M and e ∉ {1, 3} gives the point (π, 0, …, 0) (the S64.4(a) argument with only k = 1, 3), as does any pair of isomorphic factors or factor with an index-2 subfield.
- **Corollary (Hermite–Joubert, n = 10, over ℚ; given Hooley IV):** a decic field A has a generator a with Tr a = Tr a³ = 0 whenever its ramified completions carry points of X (in particular whenever V_A is everywhere locally soluble, e.g. by S64–S66 for every algebra checked); a nonic field K has u ∉ ℚ with Tr u³ = (Tr u)³ under the same condition. Over 3-fields this fails for some algebra (Reichstein 1999; Brassil–Reichstein), so this is a genuinely arithmetic statement. Not found in the literature searched (BR §9 treats p-fields under the Cassels–Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture).

**S67.3 (the quintic half: transfers from X_A to V_A tested, all fail).** *unchecked* (short arguments) + [numerical] (`nodeproj67.py`, identity to 10⁻¹⁵).
- (a) **Cubic last** (a variety Y → V_A over a rational base whose fibres are cubics in ≥ 9 variables with Hooley or Heath-Brown): a linear map into A has image in Q5′ = {Tr y = Tr y⁵ = 0} only on linear spaces in that quintic 7-fold, whose largest known members are the pairing P⁴'s (5 variables); a nonlinear map turns Tr y³ into a form of degree ≥ 6. Dead.
- (b) **ℚ-lines in X_A through a ℚ-point P:** they form the (1,2,3) complete intersection of directions {Tr v = Tr P²v = Tr Pv² = Tr v³ = 0} (dimension 4); on each the quintic is a binary quintic over ℚ with no forced root; forcing a 4-fold root needs the point of V_A first. Circular.
- (c) **Conic-bundle model:** a ℚ-line ℓ ⊂ X_A makes X_A a conic bundle over P⁶ and V_A a degree-10 cover of P⁶ (degree 8 if ℓ ⊂ V_A); a ℚ-point needs a fibre with a rational point, and the branch-locus trick (a unique double point in a fibre is rational) is circular (the branch locus is birational to the ramification locus inside V_A).
- (d) **Projection from a node N** (over the field F_N of N, degree 126 for S_10): N is a double point of both X and Q5′ (∇Tr y⁵ = 5·1 ∝ ∇Tr y at N, since N⁴ = 1), so lines through N in X_A meet V_A in 2N plus a residual cubic, and **V_A is birational over F_N to the nonic hypersurface −(20/3)c³ + 5cqT4 − q²T5 = 0 in P⁷** (directions v, c = Tr v³, q = 3Tr(Nv²), T4 = Tr(Nv⁴), T5 = Tr v⁵; the third point of X on N + κv is y = cN − qv and Tr y⁵ = q³·(that nonic), checked numerically). For primitive A no node, pair of nodes or triple of nodes is Galois-stable (no subgroup of index ≤ 3 stabilises a bisection), so this is not a ℚ-mechanism (and Colliot-Thélène–Salberger's three-conjugate-nodes theorem does not apply).
- (e) **p-field test of what is left:** over 2-fields every odd-degree form system of this size has points (as over ℝ), and the Brassil–Reichstein digit system for p = 5 (10 = 5 + 5: 5y1 + 5y2 = 5y1³ + 5y2³ = 5y1⁵ + 5y2⁵ = 0) has the solution (1, −1), so the valuation argument gives **no obstruction to V_E over p-fields for p ≠ 3** (*unchecked*: the digit point is the singular type-3 point; smooth 5-field points not proved). The only p-field obstruction is at 3 and is carried by the cubic, which S67.1 settles over ℚ. **What remains for V_A(ℚ) is therefore invisible to p-field tests**: a transfer from X_A(ℚ) to V_A(ℚ) must use global information (Hilbert-irreducibility type), not a further valuation-type step.

**S67.5 (status and decision).**

So X_A alone closes nothing.
