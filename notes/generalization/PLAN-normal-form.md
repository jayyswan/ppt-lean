> **Living plan.** Started 2026-11-27. This is a direction, not a result: the layer structure, the
> definition of a standard arc and the order of work are all expected to change as reductions are
> proved or fail. Each claim carries its label. Update the change log (§10) when something moves.

`SHEARED-SINES.md`, `notes/algebraic/SUMMARY.md` and `notes/reachability/` (`PLAN-routes.md`,

**[Computed]**, **[Heuristic]**, **[Open]**.

## 0. Goal

The aim is a **presentation of P**:

> P is the smallest subfield of ℝ containing ℚ and closed under a short list of named
> **primitives**, and every constructor of `Defs.lean` is proved to reduce to them.

That is the analogue of "constructible by ruler and compass ⇔ lies in a tower of quadratic
extensions". It is informative because it is a theorem that all the construction steps collapse
to one primitive. A single "`x ∈ P` iff `x` satisfies …" can then be written from the list. But
it only says something once the list is short, so the work is in the **reductions** (§4–§5), not
in the final formula.

**What success buys.**

- One case analysis for every future upper-bound or "this route adds nothing" argument: check
  the few primitives, not the 26 constructors.
- Equivalence of constructions: two routes to the same number become two normal forms to compare.
- A map of where genuinely new numbers enter, so research effort goes there.

**Non-goals and hard limits.**

- **Negative membership for transcendental numbers** ("γ ∉ P") is at the level of Schanuel's
  conjecture. The presentation will be used *positively* there.
- **For algebraic numbers** an honest iff is in reach, because Galois theory gives negatives.
  That is the existing `notes/algebraic/` programme. This plan treats it as one layer and does not
  redo it.

## 1. Proposed layers [Heuristic]

A first guess at the primitive list, as four layers. Each layer is fed by the ones before it, and
each can be iterated:

| layer | primitive | example | where it is studied |
|---|---|---|---|
| L1 algebraic | real roots of polynomials over the current field, of the kinds the algebraic engines reach | degree ≤ 8; solvable; trinomials (Theorem X engine) | `notes/algebraic/SUMMARY.md` |
| L2 integrals | `∫ R(t, w, y) dt` of an algebraic function `R` with coefficients in the current field, between endpoints in it | arc lengths of power-law towers, `F, E, Π` | `POWER-TOWERS.md`, `ThirdKindAll` |
| L3 inverses | the `t₁` solving `∫_{t₀}^{t₁} R = σ` | `arc_of_length` endpoints; `E⁻¹`; sheared-sine inverses | `SHEARED-SINES.md` §2, `POWER-TOWERS.md` §4 |
| L4 transcendental zeros | isolated real zeros of systems in `t, 2^t, sin t` and algebraic functions | Kepler, Dottie, `sin x = 2^x`, generalized Lambert | `GENERALIZATIONS.md` §7, `SHEARED-SINES.md` §4.3 |

Questions this table already raises:

- **Is L2 inside L3 ∪ L4?** A forward integral is the value at a known point. Probably keep it
  separate anyway, since L2 is where the classification (genus, periods) lives.
- **Do L3 and L4 merge?** An `arc_of_length` endpoint is a zero of `ℓ(t) − σ`, and `ℓ` satisfies
  an algebraic ODE, as `2^t` and `sin t` do. One "isolated zeros of a Pfaffian-type chain" layer
  may cover both (see the system in §6). Whether to merge is a presentation choice. Keeping them
  apart keeps the integrals visible.
- **Periods.** With algebraic data, L2 values of algebraic curves are Kontsevich–Zagier periods,
  and with `exp_two`/`sine` they are exponential periods. With P (transcendental) data they are
  "periods over the current field". This connection may give a usable invariant, but it does not
  give a negative by itself [Open].

## 2. Standard arcs (draft definition)

The curve-level normal form that the number-level one rests on.

A **standard arc** is `t ↦ Γ(t)` on `[t₀, t₁]`. Here:

- `Γ` is a **tower** `A_k ∘ Off_{h_k} ∘ ⋯ ∘ Off_{h_1} ∘ A_0` applied to a **base parametrization**
  `β`, as in `POWER-TOWERS.md` §1;
- all data (affine entries, offsets, any base parameters) and both endpoints are in the current
  field;
- `β` is one of: `(t, t^p)` with `p ∈ ℚ`; `(t, 2^t)`; `(t, sin t)`; `(cos t, sin t)`; a cubic
  Bézier with control points in the field; a polynomial graph of degree ≤ 6; a rectangle edge;
  or a warped glyph piece (text; see §7).

> **Conjecture N (curve normal form) [Open].** Every `PConstructibleCurve` is a finite union of
> standard arcs whose data lie in the subfield generated by the numbers used to build it.

Notes on the definition:

- Ellipses are `(cos t, sin t)` under an affine map. Lines and rectangles are affine images of a
  segment.
- Endpoints as *parameters* rather than plane points is deliberate. Crossings and `restrict` cut
  arcs at parameters that are roots of the L1/L4 equations. "In the current field" is meant to
  include those roots once they have been constructed.
- The theorem should carry a regularity side condition (C² away from finitely many points) so
  that offsets and lengths are defined. Offsets fold, so a standard arc need not be injective.

## 3. Constructor-by-constructor reduction table

Status of "this constructor preserves the normal form / reduces to the primitives":

| constructor | reduces to | status |
|---|---|---|
| `base_one`, `add`, `sub`, `mul`, `div` | field operations | trivial |
| `ellipse`, `rectangle`, `poly_graph`, `power_law`, `exp_two`, `sine`, `cubic_bezier` | base parametrizations of §2 | by definition |
| `translate_x/y`, `scale_x/y`, `rotate` | absorbed into the tower's affine part | [Lean] for general linear maps (`linearMap_PConstructibleCurve`, `rotate_PConstructibleCurve`) |
| `restrict` | cut standard arcs at window crossings: parameters solving `x(t) = c` or `y(t) = c`, an L1/L4 equation | [Proof sketch] |
| `arc_of_length` | a sub-arc, with new endpoint an L3 number | [Proof sketch] (`POWER-TOWERS.md` §5) |
| `offset` | adds a step to the tower | [Proof sketch]. Subtle part: `offset` takes the *whole* curve, and a union of arcs must be offset arc by arc, with the joins handled by `hseam`/`hC1` |
| `arc_length` | base integral + `Σ h_j` · ellipse arcs (Theorem T), i.e. L2 | [Proof; Computed] for power-law towers; same argument for any base |
| `inter_x/y` | isolated zero of `Γ₁(t) = Γ₂(s)`: L1 if both arcs are algebraic, L4 otherwise | [Proof sketch]; classification of the equations [Open] |
| `box_xmax/ymax` | an endpoint, a slope-critical point (`(N T)_x = 0`, algebraic in the slope), or an **offset cusp** (`1 − h κ = 0`) | [Proof sketch]. The cusp case is a genuine L1/L4 equation, not nothing |
| `text` | warped glyph pieces | [Open]: piecewise-bilinear images of circles and segments (`notes/warp/TEXT-WARP-THEORY.md`); the base list may need new entries |

Wanted, not yet stated:

- **Bézier and polynomial bases inside power laws?** A cubic Bézier is a sheared cubic up to
  reparametrization only in special cases. Find the exact overlap.
- **Offsets of `exp_two` and `sine` towers.** Theorem T applies unchanged. The base integrals
  are known; the inverses are partly catalogued.
- **Crossing equations.** Classify `Γ₁(t) = Γ₂(s)` by base pair, as `SHEARED-SINES.md` §4.3 does

## 5. Step 1 worked out: the power-law subsystem

Subsystem: `power_law`, affine maps, `offset`, `restrict`, `arc_of_length`, `arc_length`,
`box_*`, `inter_*`, and field operations.

> **Proposition N1 [Proof sketch].** In this subsystem every number is obtained by a finite
> triangular sequence of steps, each of one of three kinds, with data in the field generated
> by earlier steps:
>
> 1. **(L1)** a real root of an algebraic equation in `w = t^{1/k}` and `y_j = ‖N_j(1, v)‖`.
>    These come from crossings, window cuts, slope-critical points and offset cusps.
> 2. **(L2)** a value `c₀ 𝔅_p(r; v₀, v₁) + Σ_j c_j ℰ_j(v₀, v₁)` (`POWER-TOWERS.md` §2, §7).
> 3. **(L3)** a solution `v₁` of `c₀ 𝔅_p + Σ c_j ℰ_j = σ`.

Proof outline:

1. Curves are standard arcs over `(t, t^p)`, by §3's table restricted to this subsystem.
2. Lengths are L2, by Theorem T.
3. `arc_of_length` endpoints are L3.
4. Box extremes and crossings are L1. Within this subsystem the arcs are algebraic in `w`, `y`,
   so there is no L4.

**What is still missing to make N1 a proof:**

- Union-of-arcs bookkeeping for `offset` of a curve made of several arcs.
- Isolatedness: the crossing equations must not vanish identically on an arc. Analyticity gives
  this on each arc, but needs to be stated.
- Making the "current field" rigorous: the induction carries a field `K` together with the
  arcs, and each step adjoins one number.

## 6. The single-system form (for reference, not the goal)

Once the layers are fixed, one representation covers them all: a number is a coordinate of an
isolated real solution of

```
G_k(w, y, ℓ, e) = 0   (k = 1..m),     ℓ_i = ∫_{t₀ᵢ}^{t_i} λ_{D_i},   e ∈ {2^{t_i}, sin t_i, cos t_i}
```

where the `G_k` are polynomials over the current field. Each length `ℓ_i` enters only as
`ℓ_i − σ`. The functions form a chain where each derivative is algebraic in the earlier ones,
close to a Khovanskii Pfaffian chain. That framework gives finiteness of isolated zeros for
free, and it is the natural setting for any o-minimality-style upper bound [Heuristic]. This form
is close to listing the operations, so it is a fallback presentation, not the target. The target
is §1's short list with the reductions proved.

## 7. Risks and open questions

- **Text warps** may force base parametrizations that fit none of the layers cleanly (piecewise
  bilinear images of circles: quartic eggs, Gerono lobes). They are algebraic, so probably L1/L2
  over new curves, but the base list in §2 grows [Open].
- **L4 is large and unstructured.** Mixed crossings like `sin x = 2^x` have no known structure
  beyond "isolated zero". The plan accepts L4 as an irreducible primitive unless something
  better appears.
- **Is L1 exactly what the algebraic programme reaches?** Crossings of high-degree algebraic
  towers (offsets of power laws are algebraic curves of growing degree) may reach more than the
  current engines. Cross-check with `notes/algebraic/` before claiming the L1 row [Open].
- **Conventions.** `reachability/R12-convention.md` distinguishes conventions A and B for
  claims. The normal form should be stated under whichever convention the negatives use, and
  that needs checking.
- **Upper bound.** Whether the four layers are *all* of P is the content of Conjecture N plus
  §3's open rows. A proof would be the `→` direction below.

## 8. Lean shape

- Define `Described : ℝ → Prop` inductively, with one constructor per primitive (field
  operations, L1, L2, L3, L4), and `StandardUnion : Set (ℝ × ℝ) → Prop` for Conjecture N.
- **`←` (soundness):** `Described x → PConstructible x`. This is what the repo already does,
  one primitive at a time.
- **`→` (completeness):** `PConstructible x → Described x`, by one mutual induction over
  `PConstructible` and `PConstructibleCurve` (they are mutually inductive in `Defs.lean`), with
  the motive `StandardUnion S` on curves. Every reduction in §4 becomes a case of this induction.
  This direction is new, and it is where the plan's value is.
- Expect the `→` direction to need the regularity side conditions of §2 carried in the motive.

## 9. Order of work

1. **N1 on paper** (§5): close the three gaps. Rewrite `POWER-TOWERS.md` §5 as its statement.
2. **Lean: Theorem T and the power-law arc-length lemma** (`POWER-TOWERS.md` §9, items 1 and 4).
   They are reusable cases of the `→` induction.
3. **Add `exp_two` and `sine` bases.** This brings in L4; reuse `SHEARED-SINES.md` §4.3 for the
   crossing classification.
4. **Add ellipses, Béziers and polynomial graphs.** Mostly bookkeeping, plus the Bézier/power-law
   overlap question.
5. **Text.** Decide whether warped glyphs need new base entries.
6. **Prototype the `→` induction in Lean** on the power-law subsystem only, to learn what the
   motive has to carry before scaling up.
